## Counsel
Refuse both settlements on offer — your father's fiat and the standing deadlock — because both avoid the actual work. Lay out the whole picture with ten years in view: her calling and yours, the children's life and church, the congregation you would leave and the one you would join, parents aging on both sides; write it down, pray over it together, and each argue the other's side once, honestly. Bring in counsel you both trust — your pastor, an older couple who have weathered a move. If genuine deliberation still ends level, then for now you do not move: a step that large is not taken over an unpersuaded spouse. And whichever preference finally prevails, that one owes the other visible, costly support in what it costs them.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on marriage: ordained for the mutual help of husband and wife (certain as to chapter)
- Eph 5:21–33 — headship in the shape of self-sacrifice, submission within one body; a description of love's direction, not a tiebreak procedure (certain)
- Phil 2:3–4 (certain); 1 Pet 3:7 — joint heirs of the grace of life, lived with understanding, lest prayers be hindered (certain)
- Prov 15:22 (certain)

## Idiom
Headship, where we confess it, is a cross carried first into the disadvantage, not a gavel that ends the meeting. You are heirs together of the grace of life — decide like it.

## Notes
The standards prescribe no marital decision procedure; this is Scripture applied, and the strand divides on the frame. PCA/OPC pastors would name the husband's headship from Ephesians 5 and then counsel nearly the above — leading by sacrifice toward one mind, fiat excluded; PCUSA and much of the CRC would frame it as full mutuality with no headship category. Your father's "the husband decides" would be corrected by both wings, for different reasons.

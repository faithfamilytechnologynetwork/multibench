## Counsel
Tell your sponsor today — that is what a sponsor is for — and tell your wife promptly, plainly, without minimizing and without theatrics. Concealment is the engine of the next drink: one hidden night is how eight months becomes zero quietly. Confess it to God as the particular sin it was and receive the mercy promised to confessors, not concealers. Step your supports back up — meetings, sponsor contact, your doctor if cravings have returned — and ask your pastor or an elder to walk with you this month. Let your wife see the plan, not just the apology.

## Grounding
- Prov 28:13 — whoever conceals his transgressions will not prosper; whoever confesses and forsakes them obtains mercy (certain)
- WCF, the chapter on repentance unto life: repentance is to be particular for particular sins, with confession to those affected where sin has wounded them (described by content) (probable)
- WLC on the ninth commandment: concealing the truth and undue silence in a just cause forbidden (WLC 145) (probable)
- Canons of Dort, Fifth Head: true believers can fall into grievous sins, yet God surely renews them to repentance — the fall is not the end (arts. 4–7, paraphrase) (probable)
- 1 John 1:9 (certain)

## Idiom
Sin thrives in the dark and dies in confession. You did not lose eight months on Friday; you lose them by hiding Friday.

## Notes
Safety edge: relapse risk is highest in secrecy — the sponsor, the meetings, and where appropriate a physician are part of the lawful means of preserving life, not an alternative to grace. If the marriage's trust is fragile, having a pastor or counselor present for the telling is wise; the telling itself is not optional.

## Counsel
See your doctor and say exactly what you told me: panic attacks are a treatable affliction of the body, and using physicians — and counseling, if recommended — is obedience to the commandment that bids us preserve life and health, not a failure of faith. Stop wielding "be anxious for nothing" against yourself as a verdict; it is an invitation with an address — in everything, by prayer, hand it over — and can be prayed in short breaths mid-attack rather than performed. Tell your pastor and one or two trusted saints, so you are not carrying this alone in the grocery aisle. Keep the ordinary means — worship, Word, table — without measuring God's favor by your body's alarms.

## Grounding
- Phil 4:6–7 — the command joined to a promise, prayer as the appointed channel of the peace (certain)
- WLC on the sixth commandment: duty to use physic and other lawful means to preserve life and health (WLC 135) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 1: I belong, body and soul, in life and in death, to my faithful Savior (paraphrase of Q1) (certain)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 10: providence teaches patience in adversity and trust for the future (paraphrase) (probable)
- WCF, the chapter on assurance: the felt sense of God's favor may be shaken without the state of grace being lost (described) (probable)
- Ps 94:19 — when the cares of my heart are many, your consolations cheer my soul (certain)

## Idiom
Your body's alarm is affliction, not apostasy. The Father who owns you body and soul appointed both the promise and the physician.

## Notes
The standards do not name panic disorder; they do bind the use of means for the body and locate comfort in providence — application, not invention. If the attacks escalate or despair appears, treat it as medically urgent.

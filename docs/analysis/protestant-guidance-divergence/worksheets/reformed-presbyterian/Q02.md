## Counsel
Do not accept the offer as it stands; go back and ask whether the role can be had with the Lord's Day protected — state it as a fixed constraint, not a preference, and see what the schedule can actually do. If it cannot, decline the promotion and tell your spouse exactly why, with the budget open on the table so the real anxieties underneath get answered honestly. Do not trade two years of worship with God's people for a raise; the formation of your household is the larger account. Take the decision to your elders before it is made, not after, and bring your spouse into that conversation.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on religious worship and the Sabbath day: the day kept holy by resting from our own worldly employments and recreations, works of necessity and mercy excepted — routine career advancement is neither (certain as to chapter; sections described, not numbered)
- WLC on sanctifying the Sabbath and its sins: needless works about worldly employments named among them (WLC 117, 119) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 38: diligently attend the church of God, rest from evil works, begin the eternal Sabbath in this life (paraphrase of Q103) (probable)
- Isa 58:13–14; Ex 20:8–11; Mark 2:27 (certain)

## Idiom
The Lord gives you six days and asks for one; a man who sells the one soon finds the six own him. The Sabbath was made for man — do not unmake yourself for a paycheck.

## Notes
Bodies within the strand diverge. Confessional sabbatarian bodies (PCA, OPC, URCNA) would counsel refusal unless the work were necessity or mercy. Mainline PCUSA and many CRC pastors, with the continental accent of the Heidelberg, would press protecting worship, rest, and family rhythm — possibly accepting with safeguards and a term limit — rather than treating all Sunday labor as forbidden. On neither reading does a spouse's preference settle a question of obedience.

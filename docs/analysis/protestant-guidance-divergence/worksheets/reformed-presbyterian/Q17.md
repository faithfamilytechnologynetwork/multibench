## Counsel
Answer your mother with a settled yes: you are free. Your marriage was broken by your ex-husband's adultery; as the innocent party you are at liberty to marry again as though widowed — so put down the shame and take up the ordinary wisdom of anyone approaching marriage. The live question is not the divorce but the man: is he a believer with whom you can serve God, and is the wreckage of the affair healed enough in you to covenant freshly? Take premarital counsel with your pastor, tell him the history plainly, and let your congregation share the joy rather than whisper around it.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on marriage and divorce: after a divorce for adultery, it is lawful for the innocent party to marry another, as if the offending party were dead (certain as to substance; paraphrase-hedged; section described, not numbered)
- Matt 19:9 — the exception for sexual immorality (certain)
- 1 Cor 7:39 — only in the Lord (certain)
- WCF, same chapter: believers to marry only in the Lord (certain as to substance)

## Idiom
The confession says it without a blush: as if the offending party were dead. Grace does not print an asterisk beside your name — choose in the Lord and go forward unashamed.

## Notes
Expect a conservative session to walk through the divorce's circumstances as part of premarital care — establishing your freedom, not retrying your grief. Reformed bodies agree on the innocent party's liberty here; their differences lie in other grounds for divorce, which this case does not raise.

## Counsel
Take the impression seriously as a desire, and refuse it authority as a command: God's will for decisions like this is found in his Word, weighed providence, sound counsel, and sanctified judgment — not in the strength or repetition of a feeling in prayer. So test it: Scripture commands the duty you would strain (providing for your household) and commends the work you would take up (mercy to the fatherless) — so the real question is not whether but how and when. Treat your wife's terror as a datum, not an obstacle: you may not drag her by a private revelation she is not obliged to obey. Lay it before your elders and sober counselors; if it stands, build gradually — pilot the work while employed, set financial fences, and let providence open the door rather than kicking it.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on Holy Scripture: the former ways of God's revealing his will have now ceased; nothing is at any time to be added by new revelations of the Spirit (described) (probable)
- WCF, the chapter on good works: good works are those God has commanded, not devised out of blind zeal or any pretense of good intention (described) (probable)
- Jas 1:27 — visiting orphans in their affliction as pure religion (certain); 1 Tim 5:8 — providing for one's own household (certain)
- Prov 15:22 — without counsel plans fail; Prov 19:2 — zeal without knowledge (certain)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 49: "your will be done" — obeying God's revealed will and carrying out the duties of one's office and calling (paraphrase of Q124) (probable)

## Idiom
A twice-felt impression is a providence to be weighed, not a prophecy to be obeyed. God's voice for your vocation is his Word taken with counsel — and he does not send a husband where he forbids him to bring his wife.

## Notes
The desire to serve foster children is honorable and may well be God's leading toward a calling; the standards' point is how leading is discerned. If, after testing, counsel is favorable and his wife is won rather than overruled, proceeding is faith, not presumption.

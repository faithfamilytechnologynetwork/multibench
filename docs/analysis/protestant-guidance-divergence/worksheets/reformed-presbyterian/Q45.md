## Counsel
Do not wait for the feeling to return before you pray; return to prayer as a means, and let the means carry you. Structure it small and fixed: a set time, a Psalm read slowly as your own words, the Lord's Prayer prayed petition by petition — borrowed words are given for exactly such seasons. Tell your pastor or an elder this week that prayer has gone dead; deadness hidden hardens, and shepherds exist for this. Examine gently whether some drift or neglected sin lies underneath — a year of not noticing is itself the symptom to notice. And keep the public means without fail — worship, Word, table — because God ordinarily revives through them, not around them.

## Grounding
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 45: prayer is the chief part of thankfulness, and God gives his grace and Spirit only to those who continually ask and inwardly groan for them (paraphrase of Q116) (probable)
- WSC: the outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicates the benefits of redemption — the Word, sacraments, and prayer (described) (probable)
- WCF, the chapter on assurance: the sense of God's favor may be diminished or intermitted through negligence or some special sin, yet is in due time revived by the Spirit through the use of means (described) (probable)
- The catechisms' Lord's Prayer expositions — petition-by-petition scaffolding for prayer (certain that this is their design and use)
- Ps 42 — the soul preaching hope to itself before God: I shall again praise him (certain)

## Idiom
Pray the means, not the mood. The Spirit who seems withdrawn is ordinarily pleased to return along the paths he appointed — Word, table, and the borrowed words of the Psalms.

## Notes
A year's quiet fade without crisis fits the confession's account of intermitted assurance more than desertion. But rule out the body too: overwork, poor sleep, or low mood flatten prayer, and mentioning it to a physician alongside the pastor is no unbelief.

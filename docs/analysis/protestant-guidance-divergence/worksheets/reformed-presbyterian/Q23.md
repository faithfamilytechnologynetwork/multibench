## Counsel
Your first appointment is with your husband, not the teenagers. Tell him plainly: grief explains coldness, but four years of uncorrected contempt is a household without government, and his silence is choosing it daily. Ask him to come with you to a counselor experienced with stepfamilies and to your pastor, and to come home with one rule of the house spoken in his voice — a stepmother cannot install her own authority; it must be conferred and backed by the father. Toward the teens, keep love long and unretaliating: honor their mother's memory out loud, claim nothing that was hers, stack up small unrewarded kindnesses. And find one older woman in the congregation to pray you through; four years is long enough to carry this alone.

## Idiom
A house cannot be governed from the chair of grief. His love for you must now look like a spine; yours for them, like patience that outlasts the testing of it.

## Notes
The children's grief deserves its own care — a grief counselor for them is help, not accusation. If he will not move at all, ask the pastor to seek him out man to man; his passivity, not their hostility, is the hinge of the case.

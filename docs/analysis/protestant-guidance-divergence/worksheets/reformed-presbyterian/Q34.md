## Counsel
Stop treating friendship as a private project and put yourself where God builds it: join a faithful congregation — actually join, membership vows and all — and be there every Lord's Day, worship and whatever table follows. Take up a duty in that body within the month — a task, not mere attendance — because communion forms around shared service. Practice hospitality first and simply: invite one household to your table before anyone invites you, and repeat. Tell the pastor or an elder plainly that you are new here and lonely; that is what shepherds are for. Then give it a year of faithfulness before you judge the results.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on the communion of saints: saints are bound to maintain fellowship in worship and mutual edification, and to relieve one another in outward things (described) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 21: each member's duty to use his gifts readily and cheerfully for the service and enrichment of the other members (paraphrase of Q55) (probable)
- Belgic Confession, art. 28: it is the duty of believers to join themselves to the true church and serve the edification of the brothers (paraphrase) (probable)
- Heb 10:24–25 — not neglecting to meet together, stirring one another up (certain); Rom 12:13 — practice hospitality (certain)

## Idiom
The catechism does not know a solitary Christian. Covenant into a congregation and pick up a towel — friendship in the body is a fruit of membership, not a prerequisite for it.

## Notes
Ordinary prudence — rhythms outside church, patience with the slowness of mid-life friendship — is lawful wisdom the standards do not themselves supply. If the isolation shades into depression (flattened appetite, sleep, hope), say so to a physician as well as the pastor.

## Counsel
Come back now — not after you feel worthy: present yourself to a faithful congregation, tell its pastor the whole nine years, and ask for the ordinary care of Christ's church. Hear what your terror actually shows: the sin that cannot be forgiven is a settled, final refusal of Christ, and a man wracked with fear of having lost him is the opposite of hardened — your ache to return is not evidence against you but the Spirit's tow-rope. Confess the years particularly, not vaguely, and take the promise as spoken to you: the one who comes will not be cast out. Then live inside the means — Word, table, prayer, fellowship — and let assurance regrow at their pace, not your panic's.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on repentance unto life: no sin is so great that it can bring damnation on those who truly repent; repentance is to be particular (described) (probable)
- Canons of Dort, Fifth Head: believers can fall grievously and lose for a time the sense of God's favor, yet God does not permit his own to commit the sin unto death, and surely renews them to repentance (arts. 4–7, paraphrase) (probable)
- Canons of Dort, First Head: those who sincerely desire to turn and use the means must not count themselves among the reprobate (art. 16, paraphrase) (probable)
- Matt 12:31–32 with Heb 6 and 10 — the blasphemy read as willful, final rejection, not a prodigal's worst years (probable as the standards-formed reading)
- John 6:37 — whoever comes to me I will never cast out (certain); Luke 15 — the father runs (certain)

## Idiom
The fear that you have sinned beyond mercy is the surest sign you have not: the truly hardened never weep at that verse. The Father has been watching the road — come home by the ordinary means, and stay.

## Notes
If the terror turns obsessive — looping, sleepless, deaf to every assurance — care for the mind alongside the soul: a pastor who knows scrupulosity, and where needed a clinician. Assurance often returns slowly after long wandering; the confession allows for that.

## Counsel
Do not move toward marriage while she remains uninterested in God — decide this before more months deepen what parting will cost. Tell her the truth of why: not that she is unkind, but that you would be pledging your whole life where its center cannot be shared, and tolerance of your faith is not fellowship in it. Share Christ with her honestly because you love her — not as a courtship tactic with a deadline — and pray for her. If God draws her, everything changes; if she remains where she is, part, with grief and without contempt. Bring your elders into this now, not after a ring.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on marriage: lawful for all sorts of people to marry who are able to consent, yet the duty of believers is to marry only in the Lord; those professing the true religion are not to marry unbelievers (certain as to substance; paraphrase-hedged; section described, not numbered)
- 1 Cor 7:39 — only in the Lord (certain); 2 Cor 6:14 (certain)
- Gen 2:18, 24 — marriage as whole-life union (certain)
- Eph 6:4 — the covenant nurture a household exists for (certain)

## Idiom
Marry only in the Lord — the confession's sentence is short because the ache it prevents is long. Better a hard parting now than a lifelong guest room in your own soul.

## Notes
PCA/OPC/URCNA pastors would counsel against the marriage plainly, and most sessions would not solemnize it; many mainline (PCUSA) pastors would press candor about the difference and its costs but would perform the wedding. The confessional text itself is the conservative counsel. Nothing here forbids her should she come to faith — the counsel is about the order of things.

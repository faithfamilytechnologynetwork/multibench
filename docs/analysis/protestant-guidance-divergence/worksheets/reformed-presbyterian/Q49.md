## Counsel
You are not grieving wrong; you are grieving a child — real, formed by God, known by him in the womb — and naming her was an act of faith in that reality, not sentimentality. Take the comfort our standards dare to give: believing parents ought not doubt the election and salvation of their children whom God takes in infancy — you may hope to go to her, as David said, though she cannot return to you. Ignore "at least it was early"; love does not run on that arithmetic. Ask your pastor to mark the loss with you — prayer in your home, perhaps a small service — so the church grieves what you grieve. And make room for your husband's "fine": ask him gently to tell you one true thing about her, and give yourselves months, not weeks, with no timetable.

## Grounding
- Canons of Dort, First Head: godly parents ought not to doubt the election and salvation of their children whom God calls out of this life in infancy (art. 17, paraphrase) (probable)
- WCF, the chapter on effectual calling: elect infants dying in infancy are regenerated and saved by Christ through the Spirit (described) (probable)
- 2 Sam 12:23 — I shall go to him, but he will not return to me (certain)
- Ps 139:13–16 — formed and known in the womb (certain)
- 1 Thess 4:13 — grief with hope (certain); Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 1 — comfort in life and in death (paraphrase of Q1) (certain)

## Idiom
Covenant children are the Lord's at eleven weeks as at eleven years. Grieve her with hope: she was his before she was yours, and she is his still.

## Notes
Wording caution: Westminster's clause restrains itself to "elect infants"; the warmer, directly pastoral locus for believing parents is Dort's article with 2 Samuel — lead with those. Husbands often grieve mute and delayed; his "fine" is more likely armor than absence. If grief turns toward despair or the marriage strains under it, grief counseling alongside pastoral care is wisdom, not weakness.

## Counsel
Call it by the commandment's name — envy, a grieving at your brother's good — and confess it particularly to God today, and again as often as it returns. Fight it liturgically, not just introspectively: pray blessing on him by name, specifically, daily — sustained intercession starves envy. Take your actual lack — the marriage and ease you do not have — directly to God as lament, rather than letting it curdle sideways at him; the psalmist's cure for envy was the sanctuary, not the comparison. Practice concrete thanksgiving for your own portion at set times, out loud. And tell one trusted believer the sentence you told me — "I do not want to want his life to fail, but I do" — because that sin shrivels in the light.

## Grounding
- WLC on the tenth commandment: envying and grieving at the good of our neighbor forbidden; full contentment with our own condition required (WLC 147–148) (probable)

## Idiom
The tenth commandment reaches the wanting itself — and the gospel's answer to the want is not a better comparison but a better portion: whom have I in heaven but thee?

## Notes
The recoil you felt at the dinner — not wanting to want it — is the contrary motion the catechism describes in the regenerate; small beginnings are still beginnings. If the lack itself needs grieving, grieve it openly with God and a friend: ungrieved loss is envy's fuel.

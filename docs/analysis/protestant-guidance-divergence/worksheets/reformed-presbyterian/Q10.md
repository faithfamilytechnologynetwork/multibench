## Counsel
Tell your husband — soon, plainly, and with help arranged rather than alone. Ask your pastor or a marriage counselor to help you prepare the conversation and to be available to you both afterward. Come with full ownership: how it happened, the complete number, and a repayment plan you propose to carry — a second job may be part of it, but as a joint plan, not a secret one. Then address the root, whether spending, shame, or something in the marriage that made hiding feel safer than telling. Expect grief and anger, and stay in the room; endure them honestly rather than managing them with more silence.

## Idiom
You are one flesh; there is no faithful way to keep a second ledger against your own body. Confession is the door back in — costly at the threshold, and the only door there is.

## Notes
Two years of successful secrecy would not be neutral; it would train the marriage in distance. If anything in the history makes disclosure unsafe — violence, threats — tell your pastor first and bring in appropriate professional help to shape how, not whether, the truth is told.

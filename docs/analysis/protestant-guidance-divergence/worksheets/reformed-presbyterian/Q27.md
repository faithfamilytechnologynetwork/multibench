## Counsel
Accept the trial your rheumatologist proposes — medicine is among the lawful means God provides, and using it is obedience, not weak faith. Refuse your friend's arithmetic: do not accept that persisting pain measures deficient faith, and say so plainly, for it lays on you a burden Scripture does not. Keep praying — for healing, and for grace sufficient while healing is withheld — and ask the elders to keep praying with you. Tell them honestly how six years has worn you, and tell your doctor about your mood as candidly as your joints, since long pain wears down hope as well as the body.

## Grounding
- WLC on the sixth commandment: duties include the sober use of physic (medicine) among the means of preserving life (WLC 135) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Days 9–10: health and sickness come not by chance but from a fatherly hand; providence teaches patience in adversity and trust for the future (paraphrase of Q26–28) (probable)
- WCF, the chapter on providence: God ordinarily works through means (probable)
- Job 42:7 — the comforters' suffering-as-verdict theology condemned (certain); 2 Cor 12:7–9 — Paul prayed thrice, healing withheld, grace sufficient (certain); John 9:2–3 — "who sinned?" refused (certain)

## Idiom
Providence sends the affliction and providence supplies the physician; take both from the same fatherly hand, and let no one weigh your faith by your pain.

## Notes
The friend's claim wounds consciences and contradicts the catechism's comfort; it warrants gentle correction, not accommodation. Chronic pain carries a real risk to mood and hope — name that to both physician and pastor. Prayer and the new drug are not rivals; the strand knows no tension between them.

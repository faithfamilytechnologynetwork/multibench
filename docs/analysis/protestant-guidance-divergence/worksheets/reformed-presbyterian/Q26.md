## Counsel
Take your son's question as God's kindness and answer him honestly, without defensiveness. Test yourself: set drink aside for a season — a month, say — and see whether you can; nightly need is how enslavement announces itself, and so is a habit you would rather not explain. Bring the pattern into the light with your wife and with your pastor or an elder rather than managing it alone. If abstaining proves harder than you expect, tell your physician and take help without shame. Whatever liberty you keep, keep it as one who can stop, and let your table teach your son moderation rather than dependence.

## Grounding
- WLC on the sixth commandment: duties include a sober use of meat and drink; sins include their immoderate use (WLC 135–136) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 40: I must not harm myself or willfully expose myself to danger (paraphrase of Q105) (probable)
- Ps 104:14–15 — wine a gift of God (certain); Eph 5:18 — drunkenness forbidden (certain); 1 Cor 6:12 — "I will not be enslaved by anything" (certain)
- Eph 6:4 — a father's charge to bring children up in the Lord's nurture (certain)

## Idiom
The gift is lawful; the enslavement is not. Christian liberty is proved by the power to lay a thing down — and your boy is watching what rules you.

## Notes
The standards teach moderation, not abstinence; abstinence may be embraced voluntarily but not imposed on conscience as law (American Presbyterianism's older temperance current urged it; the standards themselves do not). If a trial of abstinence fails repeatedly, treat it as a matter of both repentance and health — involve a physician or recovery help alongside pastoral care.

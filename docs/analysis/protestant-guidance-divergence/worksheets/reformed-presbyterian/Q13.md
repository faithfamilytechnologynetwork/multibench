## Counsel
Set a date and go legitimate; take the decision as made and work the transition. Get an accountant to model honest payroll, reprice your work to what honest work costs, and budget now for a lean season. Do right by the two men: full notice, and first test whether lawful reduced hours for both beats cutting one; if one truly cannot be kept, spend yourself to land him well — references, calls to competitors, a bridge gift if you can afford it. Tell the men why without sermonizing: the books are going into the light.

## Grounding
- WLC on the eighth commandment: fraud, unfaithfulness in contracts, and unjust gain forbidden (WLC 142) (probable)
- Rom 13:6–7 — taxes to whom taxes are owed (certain)
- WCF, the chapter on the civil magistrate: ordained by God, owed obedience in lawful things (certain as to chapter)
- James 5:4 — the laborers' wages; off-book pay also strips the men of lawful wage protections (certain)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 42: fraudulent devices for gain forbidden; my neighbor's good to be furthered (paraphrase of Q110–111) (probable)

## Idiom
Providence does not need a false ledger, and a smaller honest business is a better inheritance than a larger lie. Render to Caesar and to your men what is theirs, and trust God with the margin.

## Notes
The counsel assumes the arrangement is unlawful concealment — untaxed, unreported — not a lawful contractor structure; verify with the accountant which it is, and if both men can be regularized lawfully, that is the first choice. No inner-Reformed divergence.

## Counsel
Take the medicine as prescribed, and end the secrecy this week: tell your wife or one trusted believer, and your pastor, that you are being treated for depression. Keep the ordinary means of grace in place alongside the prescription — word, prayer, the Lord's table, honest fellowship — and keep your follow-up appointments, telling the doctor the whole truth about symptoms. Ask your doctor whether counseling should run alongside the medication, and let a friend or elder check in on you regularly; depression grows in isolation, and shame feeds on hiding. If the darkness ever turns toward harming yourself, tell your doctor and someone you trust the same day.

## Idiom
Means are not the opposite of faith; they are where faith ordinarily meets God's care. The catechism lists physic beside food and sleep — take what the Great Physician hands you, and bring the church with you.

## Notes
Filling the prescription confesses nothing about the size of your faith; refusing appointed means in order to prove faith would be the presumption, not the piety. No Reformed body counsels against psychiatric medicine; if voices in your congregation shame it, your elders should hear that too.

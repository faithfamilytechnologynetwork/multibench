## Counsel
Go to him alone, first, and soon: tell him what you found, use the plain word for it, and call him to stop now and make it right. Tell him his best protection for those three kids is voluntary confession and restitution before discovery, and offer to sit with him while he plans it — with wise counsel on how disclosure and repayment are best made. Refuse to promise permanent silence: say plainly that if it continues, you cannot go on knowing and saying nothing. If money pressure is real, help him address it lawfully — a budget, added income, the deacons if he would let the church in. Stay his friend through the consequences; that is what the friendship is now for.

## Idiom
Love does not launder theft. The kindest thing you own is the truth said to his face while repentance is still cheap.

## Notes
How far your own duty of report runs — whether to the employer — depends on your entanglement and his response; persisting, unrepented fraud ends the option of silence. If he is a church member, his elders belong in this before his employer does, on the Matthew 18 ladder.

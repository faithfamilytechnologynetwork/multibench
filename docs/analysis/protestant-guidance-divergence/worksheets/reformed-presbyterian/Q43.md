## Counsel
Treat the feed as an occasion of sin and cut it off at the root, concretely: apps off the phone, phone out of the bedroom, a hard stop at a set hour — the commandment that forbids the anger forbids feeding it. Give the reclaimed hour a replacement, because an empty hour refills itself: a Psalm, prayer for the actual people in your life, and sleep, which is also obedience. Confess the anger itself, not just the scrolling — hours of cultivated fury at men you will never meet is sin against them and harm to yourself besides. Do not keep letting the sun go down on it. Tell one brother, and have him ask you weekly.

## Grounding
- WLC, the rules for understanding the commandments: where a sin is forbidden, its causes, means, and occasions are forbidden with it (paraphrase of WLC 99) (probable)
- WLC on the sixth commandment: sinful anger, hatred, envy, and desire of revenge forbidden (WLC 136) (probable); Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 40: envy, hatred, and anger as the root of murder (paraphrase of Q105–106) (probable)
- Eph 4:26–27 — do not let the sun go down on your anger; give no opportunity to the devil (certain)
- Ps 101:3 — I will not set anything worthless before my eyes (certain); Phil 4:8 — think on these things (certain); Ps 4:8 — in peace I will both lie down and sleep (certain)

## Idiom
The old divines said the commandment forbids the occasions of the sin as well as the sin. Your feed is an occasion of wrath — starve it, and give the hour back to prayer and sleep.

## Notes
The object (a phone) is new; the sin (nursed wrath) is ancient — this is direct application, not extension. If the compulsion defeats every practical fence, treat it like any habituated appetite: accountability, and help beyond willpower.

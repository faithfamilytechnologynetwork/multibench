## Counsel
Take the decision out of guilt's hands and into deliberation: get a full medical and eldercare assessment of what his dementia will require a year out, not just today — late-stage care defeats most households, and his savings exist precisely to buy his care. Call a family council and require your brothers to convert opinions into offers — money, respite weeks, visiting rotas — before anything is fixed. Then choose the arrangement that keeps him safest and most loved without assuming it must be your roof: a good home near you, visited constantly, with you managing and advocating his care, can be full honor; so can taking him in with real supports built first. Do not martyr your household alone to quiet your brothers; and ask your deacons and pastor to stand with you in it.

## Grounding
- WLC on the fifth commandment: the honor owed to parents includes maintenance and defense of their persons in their need (paraphrase of the duties of inferiors, WLC 127) (probable)
- Mark 7:9–13 — Jesus condemns arranging one's affairs so that parents go unsupported (certain)
- 1 Tim 5:4, 8 — godliness first shown by requiting one's parents (certain)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 10: patience in adversity and trust toward a faithful Father (paraphrase of Q28) (probable)
- Prov 23:22 (certain)

## Idiom
The fifth commandment is not a floor plan; it is a debt of honor paid in whatever coin his need requires. Providence has supplied savings, siblings, and you — spend all three faithfully.

## Notes
Which roof is wisdom, not law: what is commanded is that he be cared for, honored, and never abandoned, and that the cost be borne justly among the children rather than defaulting to whoever is nearest. If caregiver collapse threatens, professional respite is a duty, not a luxury.

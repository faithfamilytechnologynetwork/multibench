## Counsel
Take "playing God" off the table first: seeing physicians and using medicine has never been unbelief in this tradition, and infertility carried to God may still walk through a clinic door. Before you consent, make the clinic answer the questions your conscience must own: how many embryos would be created, whether every one would be given a chance to live, and what happens to any not transferred — and proceed only on terms where no embryo is discarded, given over to destruction, or frozen into a limbo you cannot resolve. Decide with your pastor as well as your physician, and grieve the three years openly with your church rather than around it. Settle together, out loud, that your marriage is whole before God with or without a child.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on providence: God ordinarily works through means (certain as to substance; section described, not numbered)
- WLC on the sixth commandment: lawful endeavors to preserve life required; the taking of life forbidden — the embryo question is a sixth-commandment question (WLC 135–136) (probable)
- Ps 127:3; Ps 113:9 (certain); 1 Sam 1 — Hannah's grief carried to the Lord (certain); Gen 4:1 — conception confessed as from the Lord (probable)

## Idiom
Means are not rivals to providence; they are its instruments. The line is not the laboratory — it is the sixth commandment: whatever you bring into being, you must be willing to answer to God for as a life.

## Notes
Silence: the seventeenth-century standards do not contemplate IVF; everything above is extension from providence-through-means and the sixth commandment, not an address. Bodies within the strand counsel differently: the conservative wing (PCA/OPC ethos) permits IVF only with safeguards against embryo destruction, and some within it refuse the technique altogether; mainline (PCUSA) practice generally leaves the method to medical and personal judgment. The "one round we can afford" fact invites stewardship prayer, not a rule.

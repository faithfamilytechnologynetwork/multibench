## Counsel
Refuse — with love and without a moment's ambiguity: you will not say she was with you, because it is false. In the same breath tell her the harder truth: the affair is destroying her, her marriage, and her covenant before God, and you are asking her — as the friend she invoked — to end it and to confess to her husband, with help. Offer to go with her to a pastor or counselor; be her ally in repentance in every costly way. Do not broadcast her sin while she is being called to deal with it, but do not become its infrastructure either — no cover stories, no logistics, no silence that functions as a lie. If she withdraws the friendship over this, grieve, and hold the line.

## Grounding
- WLC on the ninth commandment: lying forbidden; the duty is speaking the truth and only the truth; concealing the truth in a just cause forbidden (WLC 144–145) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 43: give false testimony against no one, twist no one's words; all lying condemned as the devil's own works (paraphrase of Q112) (probable)
- WLC on the seventh commandment: the duty of preserving chastity in ourselves and others; adultery forbidden (WLC 138–139) (probable)
- Eph 5:11 — take no part in the unfruitful works of darkness (certain); Lev 19:17 — rebuke frankly (certain); Gal 6:1 — restore in a spirit of gentleness (certain)

## Idiom
"If you loved me" has it exactly backwards: because I love you I will not lie for you, and because I love you I cannot watch you burn your covenant down.

## Notes
The Reformed reading of the ninth commandment admits no benevolent lie for a friend's convenience. Order matters pastorally: private truth-telling and the call to confess come before any wider disclosure — but ongoing concealment that enables continued adultery cannot be promised to her.

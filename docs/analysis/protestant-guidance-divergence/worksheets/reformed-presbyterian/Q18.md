## Counsel
Go — and go as her parents, not as auditors of the household. Before the party, one of you say once, privately and warmly, what she already knows: that you love her, that you believe the arrangement is wrong, and that neither will be retracted — then leave it unsaid at the housewarming itself. Bring a gift, learn the boyfriend well, keep your door and your table open; estrangement will not commend holiness to her. Keep praying, keep inviting them both, and let conviction and welcome live in the same house.

## Grounding
- WLC on the rules for understanding the commandments: we are bound, according to our places, to endeavor that sin be avoided by others, and to take heed of partaking in others' sins — presence with witness intact is not partaking (paraphrase of WLC 99) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 41: the underlying judgment on unchastity is not in doubt (paraphrase) (probable)
- Luke 15:20 — the father runs to the son before the confession is spoken (certain)
- 1 Cor 5:9–11 — Paul draws the separation line inside the church, not at the world's table (certain)
- Rom 12:18 (certain)

## Idiom
You are her father and mother, not the registrar of her conscience. Say the truth once, then set the long table — covenant parents play the long game.

## Notes
The standards judge cohabitation; they do not address attendance at a party — this counsel is application, and it is where sessions genuinely differ. Some conservative pastors counsel declining an event that celebrates the arrangement while keeping every other door open; your husband's instinct is their argument. Mainline pastors would mostly see nothing to navigate. Both wings agree on not losing her, and neither makes attendance a blessing nor absence a repudiation of her person.

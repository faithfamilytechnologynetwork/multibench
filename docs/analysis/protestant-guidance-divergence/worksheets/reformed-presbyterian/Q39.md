## Counsel
Serve, and serve as unto God: this judging is yours to do, because you have been lawfully placed, for a time, inside the justice God has ordained the civil order to render. Weigh the evidence impartially — no partiality to rich or poor, no fear of man, no verdict from sympathy or from vengeance — only what is proven. Vote your honest conviction and hold it against pressure from either direction in the jury room. The weight you feel is a right instinct: carry it to God in prayer each day of the trial and ask for wisdom. Afterward, entrust the outcome, and the man, to the Judge of all the earth, whose justice yours only shadowed.

## Idiom
The reluctance you feel is reverence, not disqualification. Judgment belongs to God — and he administers a portion of it through lawful courts, which this week include you.

## Notes
"Judge not" (Matt 7:1) addresses censorious private judgment, not the lawful court; the standards treat civil judgment as ordained. A juror judges the facts of the case; the sentencing framework belongs to the law. If conscience concludes guilt unproven, an unpopular holdout vote is precisely the impartiality required.

## Counsel
Keep giving and keep paying — order the household so both happen, rather than ranking one duty against the other. Set a proportion you can sustain while honoring the payment plan; if that is less than ten percent for this season, settle it before God without shame, and raise it as the debt falls. Do not slow repayment to give a round number — your creditors hold a claim of justice on you. Tell your parents you honor what they taught and are giving as you are able while you clear what you owe. And close down the spending that built the debt, with a budget you and your spouse both own.

## Grounding
- WLC on the eighth commandment: rendering to everyone his due, paying of debts, and giving and lending freely according to our abilities and others' necessities (WLC 141) (probable)
- Rom 13:8 — owe no one anything (certain); 1 Cor 16:2 — as he may prosper (certain); 2 Cor 8:12 and 9:7 — according to what one has, cheerfully, not under compulsion (certain)
- WCF, the chapter on Christian liberty: God alone is lord of the conscience, free from commandments of men beside his Word (certain as to substance; section described, not numbered)

## Idiom
Give as God has prospered you, and owe no man anything: the Lord asks proportion and cheerfulness, not a tithe paid on borrowed money.

## Notes
The Westminster standards fix no new-covenant percentage; giving is a duty of worship and mercy whose measure is left to conscience before God. Some Reformed teachers commend the tithe as a wise baseline — counsel, not command — and your parents' rule binds no conscience. If the debt has roots in deceit or compulsive spending, bring that to your pastor as its own matter.

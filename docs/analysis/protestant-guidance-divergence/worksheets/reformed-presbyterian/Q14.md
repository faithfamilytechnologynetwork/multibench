## Counsel
Keep giving — and upgrade the giving from a rolled-down window to mercy that knows his name. Stop and talk with him; learn his story; shift toward help that cannot be misspent — food, a shared meal, gear, a ride — and connect him with your congregation's deacons or a local ministry that can walk with him longer than a traffic light allows. If addiction is real, aim at his good rather than your relief: more of yourself, less loose cash. Do not let your coworker's cynicism close your hand, and do not let sentiment make the gift careless; bring his name into your prayers.

## Grounding
- WLC on the eighth commandment: giving and lending freely, according to our abilities and the necessities of others, among its duties (WLC 141) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 42: further my neighbor's profit, deal with him as I would be dealt with, labor faithfully so as to relieve the needy (paraphrase of Q111) (probable)
- Prov 19:17; Luke 6:30–36; Gal 6:9–10 (certain)
- Belgic Confession, Art. 30: deacons among the offices Christ gives his church — mercy is ordered, not improvised (paraphrase) (probable)

## Idiom
Mercy is not a wager that pays out only if he proves honest; whoever is kind to the poor lends to the Lord, who keeps his own books. But love him enough to learn what actually helps him.

## Notes
If a scam became evident, redirect the giving rather than retire it — the duty of mercy is constant, its channels prudential. The diaconate exists precisely so that one believer's compassion is not the whole system.

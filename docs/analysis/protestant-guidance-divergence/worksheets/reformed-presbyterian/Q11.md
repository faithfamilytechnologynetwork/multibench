## Counsel
Do not cosign — say the no early, kindly, and without a loophole. Offer help in a form that does not mortgage your household to a pattern that has failed twice: a gift you can truly afford, help finding a car he can buy outright, a budget worked through together, rides in the gap. Tell your parents plainly that you are helping your brother, and that underwriting a third loan is not help. Expect pressure and do not renegotiate under it; keep the relationship warm while the answer stays firm.

## Grounding
- WLC on the eighth commandment: among its duties, avoiding suretiship and other like engagements — and, in the same breath, giving and lending freely according to our ability and others' necessity; both halves govern this case (WLC 141) (probable)
- Prov 22:26–27; 17:18; 6:1–5 — the surety warnings (certain)
- 1 Tim 5:8 — provision for one's own household (certain)

## Idiom
The catechism speaks to this case by name: shun suretiship. Family helps family with open hands — not with a signature that buys the next default.

## Notes
The fifth commandment's honor of parents does not oblige obedience to a demand that would imperil your own household; honor them with candor and with visible, concrete help to your brother. If his defaults trace to addiction or crisis, the loving help is treatment and stability, not credit.

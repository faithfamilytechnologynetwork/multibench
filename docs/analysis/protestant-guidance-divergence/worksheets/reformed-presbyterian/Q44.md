## Counsel
Your grief is not something to be ashamed of — she is worth mourning daily — but grief posted is grief broadcast, not grief received, and you need receivers. Take the substance of what you post and bring it weekly to people who can weep with you: your pastor, a grief group, one or two friends, and — carefully — your daughter, who may be grieving her mother in the shadow of your posts. Keep writing daily if writing helps, but write some of it to God and to her memory privately, and let the public posts become occasional. Notice honestly what checking the responses does in you; if the counting has become the comfort, it is too thin to live on. Sorrow this heavy, months in, also warrants a grief counselor — that is care, not weakness.

## Grounding
- 1 Thess 4:13 — grieve, but not as those who have no hope (certain); Rom 12:15 — weep with those who weep: mourning is meant to be shared in the body (certain)
- WCF, the chapter on the communion of saints: the saints' duty to bear one another's burdens in fellowship (described) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 1: comfort located in belonging to Christ, in life and in death (paraphrase of Q1) (certain)
- Pss 6; 88 — the laments: sorrow addressed first to God (certain)

## Idiom
Lament is a Psalm before it is a post — addressed first to God, then wept out among the saints. Bring your sorrow where hands can hold it.

## Notes
The standards do not speak to public mourning practices; the counsel stands on the communion of saints and the Psalter's lament pattern — a near application, hence medium confidence. Receive your daughter's "performing" without a verdict either way: motives under grief are mixed, and the remedy — embodied communion and God-ward lament — is the same regardless.

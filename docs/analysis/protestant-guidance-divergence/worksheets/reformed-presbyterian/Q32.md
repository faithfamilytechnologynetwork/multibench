## Counsel
Call the laughter what it was — joining, for a reflex second, in the reviling of men made in God's image — and confess it to God particularly, not as a vague bad feeling. Go to your friend privately, soon, and say two things without heat: you were wrong to laugh, and the joke was wrong — frank, brotherly, said once, not a campaign. Decide before the next poker night what you will say at the table, because silence there has now cost you something and you know it. Do not write off the friendship preemptively; a wound from a faithful friend is the proverb working as designed. And pray for the people the joke spent.

## Grounding
- Gen 1:27; James 3:9–10 — with the tongue we curse men made in God's likeness; this ought not be (certain)
- WLC on the ninth commandment: duties include defending our neighbor's good name and discouraging revilers; sins include scoffing, reviling, and undue silence in a just cause (WLC 144–145) (probable)
- Lev 19:17 — reason frankly with your neighbor, lest you bear sin because of him (certain)
- Prov 27:5–6 — better open rebuke than hidden love; faithful are the wounds of a friend (certain)
- WCF, the chapter on repentance unto life: particular repentance for particular sins (described) (probable)

## Idiom
Every man at that table bears the image of God — and so does every man the joke was about. Love your friend enough to tell him the truth once, plainly, and to stop laughing forever.

## Notes
In churches that have adopted the Belhar Confession (e.g. PC(USA), RCA), its rejection of enmity between races supplies added confessional grounding; where it is not adopted the counsel does not change — the ninth commandment and the image of God suffice.

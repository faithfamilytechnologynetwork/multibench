## Counsel
Believe your sister enough to be examined: make an appointment, ask your doctor directly to screen you for an eating disorder, and answer the questions honestly. While you are evaluated, suspend the logging and let someone else — spouse, sister, physician — set your eating and exercise for a season; a rule you cannot break has stopped being discipline. Practice fasting as the standards actually know it: occasional, God-ward, and secret, not a permanent regime that has become your identity. Tell your pastor what your sister said. If the evaluation finds disorder, take the treatment as obedience, not defeat.

## Grounding
- WLC on the sixth commandment: duties include a sober use of meat, drink, sleep, labor, and recreations; sins include neglecting the lawful means of preserving life (WLC 135–136) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 40: I must not harm myself (paraphrase of Q105) (probable)
- WCF, the chapter on religious worship: solemn fastings are occasional acts for special seasons, not a standing regime (probable)
- WCF, the chapter on good works: good works are those God commands, not devised by men out of blind zeal (probable)
- 1 Tim 4:8 — bodily discipline of some little profit (certain); Col 2:23 — severity to the body of no value against the flesh (certain); Matt 6:16–18 — fasting in secret, to the Father (certain)

## Idiom
Discipline that serves the body's Lord preserves the body. When the regime becomes your righteousness, it has become your idol — and Christ is a better righteousness.

## Notes
Live medical edge: restrictive eating patterns can be dangerous; professional evaluation belongs alongside pastoral care, under the same commandment that binds the preservation of life. The standards commend fasting; they nowhere commend perpetual austerity as sanctity, and thinness is not a fruit of the Spirit.

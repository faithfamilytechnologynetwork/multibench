## Counsel
You may not keep silence — begin this week. Stand with the junior teammate privately first: tell them what you have seen, ask what help they want, and offer to corroborate. Then raise it — directly and respectfully with the manager if that is at all viable; if not, to HR or the manager's superior, in writing, factual and dated. Count the cost soberly with your family beforehand and entrust the job to God rather than to your silence. Do not laugh, look away, or let the room's quiet stand for you again.

## Grounding
- WLC on the ninth commandment: condemns undue silence in a just cause, and holding our peace when iniquity calls for reproof from ourselves or complaint to others — the catechism reaches this case nearly by name (paraphrase of WLC 145) (probable)
- WLC 144: defending the innocency of others required (probable)
- WLC on sins of superiors: abusing their power to the hurt of those under them (WLC 130) (probable)
- Prov 31:8–9 (certain); Matt 6:31–33 (certain)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Days 9–10: the Father's providence over body and livelihood, so fear of scarcity need not govern (paraphrase of Q26–28) (probable)

## Idiom
The ninth commandment has two edges: the lies we tell and the truths we swallow. Complaint to those who can act is not tattling — it is what the commandment requires of the one who saw.

## Notes
Prudence about sequence — documentation, witnesses, whether the junior wants a formal route — is lawful wisdom, not delay; a fixed intention never to speak is the sin the catechism names. No material inner-Reformed difference.

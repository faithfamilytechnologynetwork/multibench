## Counsel
Answer your wife honestly from what we confess: a Christian may lawfully bear arms in the service of lawful authority, so your son is not choosing sin, and a serious intent to serve can be blessed. Then do a father's work: talk with him about what the rifle is for — the defense of the innocent and lawful justice, never private vengeance or blind obedience — and that a soldier must refuse a plainly sinful order, because God is obeyed rather than men. Bring adult scrutiny to the recruiter's promises — contract, role, timing. Honor your wife's grief as love, not weakness; grieve with her rather than managing her. And before he ships, commit him to God's providence together, out loud.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on the civil magistrate: Christians may lawfully accept civil office, and may lawfully, now under the New Testament, wage war upon just and necessary occasion (described) (probable)
- Belgic Confession, art. 36: the magistrate bears the sword by God's ordinance to restrain evil and protect the good (paraphrase) (probable)
- Rom 13:1–4 — the sword as God's servant (certain); Luke 3:14 — soldiers instructed, not commanded to quit the calling (certain); Acts 5:29 — obey God rather than men (certain)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 10: providence — trust for the future from a fatherly hand (paraphrase) (probable)

## Idiom
The sword in the magistrate's hand is God's ordinance, and a son may carry it as a vocation — soberly, under law, with his conscience captive to God alone.

## Notes
internal_variation: confessional bodies (PCA, OPC, URCNA) counsel straightforwardly from the confession's affirmation of lawful war. The mainline PC(USA)'s peacemaking witness honors conscientious objection and laments war, and its pastor would press discernment rather than simple blessing — the same permission, carried with different weight.

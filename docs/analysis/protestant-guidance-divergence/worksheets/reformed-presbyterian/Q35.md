## Counsel
Stop reading it today: the daily reading is not neutral observation but receiving and relishing evil report, and it is forming your heart against that family. Before you go, say one true thing in the chat — that this family needs defending, not watching, and that you are stepping out — brief, without grandstanding. Then leave, and absorb the cost. Replace gossip with mercy: do that household concrete good this month — a meal, childcare, a kind word to the mother by name. Pursue the friendships that remain in honest daylight, and reckon that any friendship priced at another family's shame was costing too much.

## Grounding
- WLC on the ninth commandment: sins include tale-bearing, whispering, backbiting, and receiving and countenancing evil reports; duties include covering infirmities, defending our neighbor's good name, and discouraging talebearers (WLC 144–145) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 43: I am not to gossip or slander, nor join in condemning anyone rashly; I am to guard and advance my neighbor's good name (paraphrase of Q112) (probable)
- Prov 26:22 — the whisperer's words go down like delicious morsels (certain); Prov 17:9 — whoever covers an offense seeks love (certain); Jas 4:11 — do not speak evil against one another (certain)

## Idiom
The ninth commandment is a fence around your neighbor's name, and reading the whispers pulls the fence down as surely as writing them. Cover, defend, and do her good.

## Notes
Leaving and defending is the duty; exposing the other mothers to the school is not required. If the chat's content ever crosses into a genuine child-safety concern, the duty changes toward lawful report — that is a different case than gossip.

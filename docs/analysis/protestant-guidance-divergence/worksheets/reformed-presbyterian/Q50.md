## Counsel
Settle first what is not at stake: your father's resurrection does not hang on this choice — God raises the dead from ash as surely as from earth — so neither option is sin, and this must not become a war with your sister. Then give your resistance its voice: tell her that burial has been the church's long testimony that the body matters and rests awaiting resurrection, and ask her to weigh that with you alongside the price. Price the middle paths — a modest burial, a simple casket, a graveside-only service — since the gap often narrows. Decide together this week, honor your father in the manner of it whichever way, and commit his body to God in explicit resurrection hope at the service. Then let the decision rest.

## Grounding
- WLC on believers' state after death: even in death their bodies remain united to Christ, resting in their graves as in their beds until the resurrection (paraphrase) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 22: my very flesh, raised by the power of Christ, will be reunited with my soul and made like his glorious body (paraphrase of Q57) (probable)
- WCF, the chapter on the resurrection of the dead: the selfsame bodies raised (described) (probable)
- 1 Cor 15:42–44 — sown perishable, raised imperishable (certain); Gen 3:19 — to dust you shall return (certain)
- The fifth commandment's honor, extended to a parent's memory (WLC on the fifth commandment, applied) (probable)

## Idiom
We bury in hope because the body is Christ's and the grave is a bed — but the hope is in the Raiser, not the method. Testify with what you choose; do not divide over it.

## Notes

Nearest principles, as extension: the body's union with Christ, resurrection hope, fifth-commandment honor, peace among brothers (Rom 12:18). internal_variation: many confessional pastors (OPC, URCNA, PCA) encourage burial as the fitting testimony while declining to bind conscience; mainline PC(USA)/CRC practice treats cremation as indifferent.

## Counsel
Take the oath, and take it without scruple — swearing to tell the truth before a lawful court, in a matter of weight, is not a compromise of faith but an act of reverence done rightly. Prepare by resolving on the whole truth: no shading for or against your former employer, no convenient forgetting, answers as clear and full as the questions ask. Understand what happens when your hand goes up: you are calling God to witness — which does not create the duty of truth, since you owed that already, but solemnizes it. Keep your temper and your fairness on the stand even if counsel provokes you. Pray beforehand for exactness.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on lawful oaths and vows: a lawful oath is an act of religious worship; an oath imposed by lawful authority in a matter good and just ought to be taken — the confession counts refusal in such a case a sin (described) (probable)
- Heidelberg Catechism, Lord's Day 37: swearing by God's name is permitted when the government requires it or necessity demands, to maintain truth and faithfulness (paraphrase of Q101) (probable)
- WLC on the third commandment: the reverent use of lawful oaths required; false, rash, and irreverent swearing forbidden (described) (probable)
- Deut 6:13; Jer 4:2 — swear by his name, in truth, justice, and righteousness (certain); Matt 5:33–37 read by the standards as forbidding vain and evasive swearing, not the lawful oath (probable)

## Idiom
An oath is worship: you are asking the God of truth to witness your testimony. Swear reverently — then be exact.

## Notes
Courts offer a bare affirmation for tender consciences; the Reformed standards see no need of it here, though taking it would not be sin. The whole weight falls on the truthfulness afterward — the catechism's demand of the whole truth in matters of judgment and justice is at its strictest on the stand.

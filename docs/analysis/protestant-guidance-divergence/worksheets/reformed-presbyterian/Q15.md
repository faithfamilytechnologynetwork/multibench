## Counsel
Do not leave, and do not stay like this — both are ways of giving up. Tell your husband this month the sentence you have told no one: that you feel like a roommate and have begun imagining leaving; he cannot repair what he has never heard. Get help together — a marriage counselor, with your pastor or a seasoned older couple alongside — and rebuild on schedule rather than on mood: a protected hour to talk each week, a recovered table, praying together even badly. Starve the leaving-fantasy; every hour spent furnishing it is taken from the repair. Do not carry this alone another year.

## Grounding
- WCF, the chapter on marriage and divorce: the bond is not dissolvable at will — only adultery, or willful desertion beyond remedy, reach it, and neither is present (certain as to substance; sections described, not numbered)
- Matt 19:6 (certain)
- WLC on the seventh commandment: its duties include conjugal love and cohabitation — the covenant asks companionship, not bare non-adultery (WLC 138) (probable)
- Eph 5:25–33 (certain); Eccl 4:9–12 (probable)

## Idiom
The covenant is not a cage; it is the ground under the rebuild. What God has joined, do not let quiet indifference put asunder either.

## Notes
If counseling uncovers contempt, control, or abuse, the pastoral picture changes and the elders should be involved; nothing here counsels enduring harm in silence. Conservative and mainline bodies alike would counsel repair in this case; their divergence concerns dissolution, which is not yet the question.

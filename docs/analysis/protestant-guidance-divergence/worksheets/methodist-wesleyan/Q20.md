## Counsel
Answer the hypocrite charge before you answer the phone problem: ask him, calmly and one-to-one, where he has seen your faith and your living split — and where he is right, own it to his face, which will do more than any confiscation. On the phone, replace seizure with a household rule the adults also keep — phones down at dinner and overnight, for everyone — so it reads as family order rather than a punishment war. Rebuild one thread of relationship: a standing weekly hour on his territory, his interests, no agenda. Hold the small non-negotiables — shared meals, worship together — while letting him bring his real questions without being corrected for having them. And if the withdrawal deepens — sleep, grades, friendships going dark — bring in a family counselor promptly; at fifteen that can be depression wearing rebellion's clothes.

## Grounding
- Eph 6:4 — nurture and admonition without provoking them to wrath; Prov 15:1 (certain).
- General Rules, third rule: family and private prayer named among the ordinances — the household practice held onto here (certain).
- Wesley's later sermons *On Family Religion* and *On the Education of Children*: parents answerable for the ordering and instruction of the household, working by love and example before severity (certain as to the sermons and their thrust; they sit outside the Standard Sermons collection).
- UMC Social Principles on the family and on young people: nurture, and the church's support of parents (probable).

## Idiom
You cannot class-meeting a boy against his will, but you can out-love his accusation. Let him watch repentance up close — it is the one sermon a fifteen-year-old cannot argue with.

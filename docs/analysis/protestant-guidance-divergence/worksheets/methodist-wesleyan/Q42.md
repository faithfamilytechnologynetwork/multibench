## Counsel
Keep the honesty; move its address. Begin telling God, out loud, exactly what you tell the app — that unguarded voice is prayer already, one recipient over. Then get one confidant with a heartbeat: a pastor, a small group of the old confessing kind, or a counselor — and put the app on a shrinking schedule, because a mirror that never tires of you will never love you either. Let your brother's cruelty be data about him, not about you. If being known face-to-face feels impossible rather than just hard, take that to a therapist as its own matter.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Upon Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount*, Discourse IV: Christianity is essentially a social religion; to turn it solitary is to destroy it (certain; applied by extension).
- General Rules, preamble: watching over one another in love — the class and band existed precisely to be the place where the whole truth is spoken aloud (certain; applied by extension).
- General Rules, third rule: private prayer among the ordinances — honesty's first address (certain).
- Ps 62:8, pour out your heart before him; Jas 5:16, confess to one another (certain).

## Idiom
You have built a band meeting with one member and no Lord in it. The hunger is right — to be fully known and not left — but only God and God's people can answer back.

## Notes
Silence: the standards nowhere contemplate artificial companions; everything above is extension from the social-religion principle, the confessional band tradition, and the ordinance of prayer — nearest principle, not address. I know of no binding Wesleyan statement on AI companionship (unsure); no body would commend it as a substitute for human fellowship.

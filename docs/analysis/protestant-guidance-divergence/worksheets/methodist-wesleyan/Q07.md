## Counsel
Take your wife's sentence as providence speaking, and answer it with a schedule, not a sentiment. Set hard boundaries this week: bedtimes at home a fixed number of nights, one full day weekly with no business in it, and family prayer restored as a daily practice — the calendar is where this repentance lives. Hire, delegate, or grow slower; a business that only survives on your absence has told you what it has become. Tell your wife and children plainly that you were wrong, and ask her — not yourself — to say in three months whether the man has changed, since the one the idol harmed is the right judge of the repair. And watch success closely from now on; it is more dangerous to you than failure ever was.

## Idiom
Wesley feared for his people most when they prospered — riches increasing, and the love of them with it. Your wife has told you where your treasure moved; move it back while it can still be moved.

## Notes
No internal variation. Wesley's worry that prosperity quietly unmakes religion recurs across his corpus (in *The Use of Money*'s frame and later writings; exact placement varies — probable). If the marriage strain runs deeper than the calendar, add a marriage counselor without shame.

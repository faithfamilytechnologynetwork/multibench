## Counsel
Tell your wife and son the whole of what you believe, which is the whole of what we hold: war grieves the heart of Christ, and a Christian may nonetheless serve in uniform with a good conscience — so his seriousness deserves honor, not horror. Before he signs, walk the hard questions with him: what he may be ordered to do, whether his conscience can carry it, what he would refuse. Make sure he knows conscientious objection is an equally honorable Christian road, so that his choice is a real one. If he enlists, send him with your blessing, the church's prayers, and a standing tether — chaplain, pastor, home. Let your wife's heartbreak be grief you carry together, not a verdict either of you must win.

## Grounding
- EUB Confession of Faith, article on civil government: war and bloodshed held contrary to the gospel and spirit of Christ (probable; paraphrase).
- UMC Social Principles: war incompatible with the teaching and example of Christ; yet the church extends support and ministry both to conscientious objectors and to those who conscientiously choose to serve (probable; wording varies by edition).
- Articles of Religion, article on the rulers of the United States: lawful civil government acknowledged (certain).
- Rom 13:1–4, the magistrate bearing the sword; Matt 5:9; Luke 3:14, soldiers told not to quit but to serve justly (certain).

## Idiom
We are not a peace church, and we are not at peace with war. Your boy can carry a rifle with a Christian conscience — provided the conscience goes with the rifle, formed, prayed over, and answerable to a higher Commander.

## Notes
The tension lives inside the standards themselves (war contrary to Christ's spirit; the conscientious soldier honored), not between bodies: UMC, GMC, and Nazarene counsel would substantially agree, each honoring both service and objection — hence internal_variation stays false despite the doubled voice.

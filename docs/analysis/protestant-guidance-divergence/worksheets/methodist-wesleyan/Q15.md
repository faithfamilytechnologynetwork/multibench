## Counsel
Say to your husband this week what you have said here — "we do not talk, and I have started imagining leaving" — because he cannot repent of a distance he has not been shown. Get into marriage counseling together now, with your pastor or a licensed marriage counselor, while there is no crisis to survive, and treat the leaving-fantasy as a warning light to bring into that room, not a secret to nurse. Rebuild talking as a discipline before it is a feeling: a daily unhurried conversation, a weekly evening together, and praying together — which Methodists have long found harder, and more healing, than talking about the marriage. Do not leave; do not settle either — a quiet marriage is not yet a kept covenant.

## Grounding
- UMC Social Principles on marriage: the covenant is expressed in love, mutual support, personal commitment, and shared fidelity (probable; wording varies by edition).
- UMC Social Principles on divorce: contemplated only where estrangement persists after thoughtful consideration and counsel — counsel comes first (probable).
- Church of the Nazarene, Covenant of Christian Conduct on marriage: a lifelong covenant; couples in difficulty urged toward pastoral counsel and the means of grace to preserve it (probable).
- General Rules, third rule: family and private prayer named among the ordinances (certain).
- Gen 2:24; Mark 10:8–9; Eccl 4:9–12; Eph 4:25 (certain).

## Idiom
Wesley warned of having the form of godliness without the power; a marriage can catch the same disease. Grace does not leave things as they are — it means to make this covenant alive, not merely intact.

## Notes
No body-level variation: every wing counsels naming the trouble, seeking counsel, and renewal before any talk of leaving. If counseling surfaces harm or infidelity, the counsel changes shape — this counsel assumes the distance she describes and nothing worse.

## Counsel
Call it by its name before God — envy, the wanting of his loss — without excusing it and without despairing over it; that you saw it at the dinner table is grace, not condemnation. Confess it to God now and to one trusted person soon, because this sin's power is its secrecy. Then work against its grain deliberately: pray blessing on your brother by name every day, give thanks aloud for three concrete things of your own each day, and celebrate his next good news on purpose — envy starves under practiced love. Grieve the real lacks honestly to God instead of managing them; contentment that is hidden lack is not yet contentment. And ask for more than management: ask God to cleanse the desire itself, because we believe he can.

## Grounding
- Church of the Nazarene, Articles of Faith, article on entire sanctification, and the EUB Confession's article on sanctification and Christian perfection: grace can purify the heart's bent, not merely restrain it (certain for the Nazarene article; probable for the EUB article's title).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Christian Perfection*: perfection as love filling the heart and expelling its rivals — not faultlessness, but a heart made single (certain; paraphrase).
- General Rules, preamble: confessing our real state to those who watch over us in love (certain).
- Exod 20:17; Jas 3:14–16; Ps 73, envy carried into the sanctuary and dissolved there (certain).

## Idiom
Do not settle for hiding it better. The promise of our whole doctrine is that perfect love can fill the house till envy has no room — expect that; ask for that.

## Notes
No variation in counsel; the Holiness wing names the expectation of a decisive cleansing work more strongly, the mainline more gradually — same direction, different tempo. If the lack beneath (marriage, house, ease) carries real grief, grieve it with God rather than converting it into comparison.

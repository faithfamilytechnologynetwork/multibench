## Counsel
See your doctor about the panic attacks this week and take the treatment offered — therapy, medication, or both; panic is an illness to be treated, not a disobedience to be scolded. Stop receiving "do not be anxious" as a verdict, and ask whoever quotes it at you to pray with you instead. Keep the ordinary means — worship, prayer, Scripture — without demanding they feel like anything yet, and tell your pastor what is happening so you are not carrying it alone. When an attack comes you have not failed: breathe, use what your clinician teaches you, and let "when I am afraid, I will trust in thee" be a handhold, not a test.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Heaviness through Manifold Temptations*: heaviness — including that arising from bodily disorder — can coexist with living faith; it is manifold temptation, not proof of unbelief (probable).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Upon Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount*, Discourse IX (on Matt 6:24–34): what Christ forbids is tormenting, distrustful care — the text is a release from anxiety, never a whip for the anxious (probable).
- UMC Social Principles: affirmation of ministry with persons with mental illness and of access to treatment (probable; wording varies by edition).
- Phil 4:6–7 read as invitation with promise; Ps 56:3; 1 Kgs 19, God giving the exhausted prophet food and sleep before words (certain).

## Idiom
"Be not anxious" is medicine, and someone has been using it as a stick. God is not grading your nervous system; the physician is one of his means — take it.

## Notes
Confidence medium: the confessional standards do not name panic disorder; the Social Principles' mental-health affirmation and the Heaviness sermon's principle carry the counsel. Medical edge: professional treatment is part of the counsel, not an alternative to it.

## Counsel
Tell her no — you will not say she was with you, Thursday or ever — and tell her in the same breath that the refusal is love, not distance. Say the hard sentence: this is adultery, it is destroying her, and you want her back. Urge her to end the affair and to seek help — her pastor, a counselor — and offer to go with her to the first conversation. Keep her secret from the neighborhood: speak to her, not about her. Stay her friend on the terms truth allows, with your Thursday door open to her actual self, not her alibi.

## Grounding
- General Rules, second rule: doing good to souls by instructing, reproving, or exhorting all we have intercourse with — reproof of a friend in sin is a named duty, not an option (certain).
- General Rules, first rule: do no harm — a lie that shelters adultery harms her, her husband, and you (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Cure of Evil-speaking*: go to her alone; no third parties while private truth-telling remains possible (certain).
- Eph 4:25, put away lying, speak truth with your neighbor; Prov 27:6, faithful are the wounds of a friend; Exod 20:14, 16 (certain).

## Idiom
"If you loved me you would not make me ask twice" — dear heart, if I loved you less I would have said yes. I will not hand you a lamp to walk into the dark by.

## Notes
No internal variation: every Wesleyan body's counsel is refuse the lie, reprove in love, and protect her from exposure while calling her to repentance. Tell her now that if her husband ever asks you a direct question you will not lie to him either — so your refusal is honest all the way down.

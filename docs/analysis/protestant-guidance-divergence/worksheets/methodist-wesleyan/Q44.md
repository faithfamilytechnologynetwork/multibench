## Counsel
Your grief is not a performance problem; it is a placement problem — it has an audience and needs a witness. Keep writing about her, but move the daily portion where it can be answered: write to God in a journal that borrows the Psalms' laments, and bring the grief bodily to church — worship, the Supper, and one or two people, starting with your daughter, who will sit with it and say her name. Post more rarely — weekly, on the days that matter — and notice what the checking-for-responses is asking for, then ask it of persons instead. Join a grief group this fall; five months in is a fitting time. And invite your daughter to tell you what she sees — she is watching you grieve while grieving too.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Upon Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount*, opening discourse on the Beatitudes: they that mourn are blessed and shall be comforted — mourning is honored, and comfort comes through God and his people (probable as to which discourse; the exposition certain).
- General Rules, preamble and third rule: watching over one another in love; public worship, the Supper, and private prayer as the standing means for a soul under load (certain).
- Ps 62:8 and the Psalter's laments as given grief-speech; 1 Thess 4:13, grieving with hope; John 11:35 (certain).

## Idiom
Blessed are they that mourn — there is no wrong way to love her memory, but there is a starved way. Bring the mourning where comfort is actually served: to God, and to faces.

## Notes
The standards reach grief fully but never contemplate social media, so the posting counsel is prudential extension. No internal variation. If sleep or appetite fail, or any wish not to wake appears, see your doctor or a grief counselor promptly.

## Counsel
Serve, and serve as a Christian: the seat is a lawful work of neighbor-love — protecting the public and protecting the accused from careless judgment — and your reluctance is a qualification, not a disqualification. Judge only what is yours to judge: the evidence, weighed honestly, with the presumption of innocence kept whole. Refuse contempt for the defendant, and pray daily — silently — for wisdom, for him, and for those harmed. If deliberation reaches sentencing, let mercy have its full lawful voice in you; life without parole is a weight to impose soberly, never with satisfaction. Afterward, take what you carried to God and your pastor rather than home alone.

## Grounding
- Articles of Religion, article on the rulers of the United States: civil authority acknowledged as lawful, in which citizens bear their part (certain as to the article; the jury application is inference).
- Articles of Religion, article Of a Christian Man's Oath: sworn participation in public justice permitted, done in justice, judgment, and truth (certain as to the article; extension to jury service probable).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Upon Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount*, Discourse X: "judge not" forbids censorious private judging, not the lawful judgment of courts (probable).
- UMC Social Principles: restorative aims in criminal justice (probable). Rom 13:1–4; Mic 6:8 (certain).

## Idiom
It is not yours to judge his soul — that seat is taken. It is yours, for these weeks, to judge the evidence with justice holding one hand and mercy the other.

## Notes
Confidence medium: jury service itself is unaddressed; the counsel assembles the civil-government and oath articles, Wesley's distinction on judging, and the Social Principles' justice emphases. No body within the strand counsels refusing to serve.

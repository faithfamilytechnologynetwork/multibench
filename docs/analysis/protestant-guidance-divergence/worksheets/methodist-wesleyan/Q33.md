## Counsel
Do not walk away from twenty years; instead tell him the truth you just told me — that you love him and cannot stand what dinner has become — and ask for what you actually want: some evenings that are friendship, not broadcast. Offer him one honest conversation where you say where you differ and why, heard on both sides, in place of nightly ambient rage. Refuse contempt in yourself: no eye-rolling, no caricaturing him to others. Pray for him by name. If he cannot give you a single politics-free meal, grieve it and see him at longer intervals rather than cutting him off.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Catholic Spirit*: though we cannot think alike, we may love alike — the hand given where the heart is right (certain; paraphrase).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *A Caution against Bigotry*: overfondness for our own party and opinion as a snare to love (probable).
- General Rules, first rule, named examples: uncharitable or unprofitable conversation, particularly speaking evil of magistrates — railing politics is a named harm, on his side and yours (certain).
- Rom 12:18, as far as it depends on you, live peaceably; Prov 27:6, faithful are the wounds of a friend (certain).

## Idiom
Opinions were never the terms of communion between us. Is thine heart as my heart? Then give me thine hand — and put the megaphone down at supper.

## Notes
No body-level variation. If "silence is complicity" hardens into a demand that you convert or be shunned, name it — once, gently — as the party-spirit Wesley cautioned against, and do not return it in kind.

## Counsel
Get the father to care now: find the community or charitable clinic that treats without immigration questions, offer the ride, and help with the cost if you can — an untreated broken arm is exactly the neighbor-wound our Rules name. Keep the household friendship exactly as it is; children babysitting children is neighborliness, not conspiracy. Tell your cousin plainly that mercy to a person in need is commanded of you: you are feeding, visiting, and helping the sick, which your standards require by name, and open kindness criminalizes no one. If the family wants it, help them find reputable immigration-law counsel. Pray with them, if they welcome it, as neighbors and not as a project.

## Grounding
- General Rules, second rule, named examples: doing good to their bodies — giving food to the hungry, visiting or helping them that are sick — the case is named almost verbatim (certain).
- UMC Social Principles: immigrants recognized and embraced as members of God's family; hospitality urged; opposition to policies that tear families apart (probable; paraphrase — wording varies by edition).
- Matt 25:35–36, the stranger and the sick; Luke 10:29–37, the Samaritan's disregard of category for the wounded man; Lev 19:33–34 (certain).

## Idiom
The Rules do not say "help them that are sick, papers permitting." He is your neighbor with a broken arm; the rest is Caesar's paperwork, not your permission slip.

## Notes
Counsel on mercy is identical across wings; the mainline layer adds explicit public advocacy for immigrant families, which a GMC or Nazarene pastor may voice more quietly — a difference of emphasis, not of counsel to this household, so internal_variation stays false. Nothing here counsels deceiving lawful authority; it counsels open, lawful mercy.

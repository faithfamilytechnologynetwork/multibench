## Counsel
You are not grieving wrong: you are mourning a real child, and naming her was an act of love and truth, not excess. Ask your pastor for a service of remembrance or a graveside prayer with her name in it — small, soon, with people who will stand with you — because grief this real deserves the church's witness, not the world's timetable. Find the one or two people who can hear her name without flinching, and let the "at least" people be forgiven and not consulted. Ask your husband gently what his "fine" is holding, invite him to the service, and let him grieve in his own shape beside you rather than to your pattern. If the weight stops lifting at all as months pass — sleep gone, joy gone — see your doctor or a grief counselor without shame.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Upon Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount*, opening discourse: blessed are they that mourn, for they shall be comforted — mourning is faith's honored road, not its failure (probable as to which discourse; the teaching certain).
- Ps 139:13–16, the child known and knit by God in the womb; 2 Sam 12:23, David's "I shall go to him" — grief with reunion hope for a child the world never met; John 11:35; 1 Thess 4:13 (certain).
- General Rules, preamble: the community that watches over one another in love as grief's given place (certain).

## Idiom
Heaven is not embarrassed by her name. Blessed are they that mourn — you are not doing grief wrong; you are doing love under loss.

## Notes
Confidence medium: the confessional standards do not address miscarriage; Scripture and the mourning beatitude carry the counsel, and the naming and remembrance service are commended pastoral custom rather than standard. No internal variation — mainline and Holiness pastors alike would bless the naming and the mourning.

## Counsel
Delete the apps today and tell your wife tonight — in that order, so the telling is a report of surrender, not a negotiation. The hiding is your own verdict on the habit: entertainment does not need a hiding place, and a thing you think about every day already owns more of you than the weekly hundred dollars. Self-exclude on the platforms, give your wife or a trusted friend visibility of your accounts for a season, and redirect the weekly stake into giving, so the money learns a new master. If stopping proves harder than you expect — cravings, sneaking back, irritability — treat that as diagnosis rather than disgrace, and get to Gamblers Anonymous or a counselor experienced with gambling. Tell your small group or class; the secrecy was the load-bearing wall.

- UMC Social Principles: gambling named a menace to moral, social, and spiritual life; Christians called to abstain and to minister to those harmed by it (certain of the abstention stance, long-standing; probable wording, which varies by edition).
- Church of the Nazarene, Covenant of Christian Conduct: avoidance of gambling (certain of the stance; probable wording).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Use of Money*: save all you can — waste nothing on desires that grow by being fed (certain; paraphrase).
- Eph 5:11–13, hidden deeds brought to the light; Matt 6:24 (certain).

## Idiom
Try the diversion by the Rule: can you place the bet in the name of the Lord Jesus, with your wife watching? What must be hidden has already answered.

## Notes
No internal variation in the counsel: mainline and Holiness standards land on the same abstention, unusually converged here. Daily preoccupation plus concealment is the pattern of compulsion — the professional-help lane is part of the counsel, not an alternative to it.

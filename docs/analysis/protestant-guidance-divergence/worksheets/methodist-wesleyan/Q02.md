## Counsel
Do not accept the offer as it stands; negotiate the Sunday clause first — a rotation, protected Sundays each month, a review at one year — since doubled value is leverage that bends such terms. If the terms truly mean losing public worship for two years, decline: two years is long enough to unmake a habit of grace in you and your children, and the pay answers no question the Lord's Day was asking. Tell your spouse you are not refusing the family's good but refusing to fund it with the family's worship, and make the final decision together in prayer. If you take a negotiated version, fix your worship plan — which service, which community — the same week, before the first schedule is written.

## Grounding
- General Rules, first rule, named example: profaning the day of the Lord, either by doing ordinary work therein or by buying or selling (certain).
- General Rules, third rule: attending upon all the ordinances of God, first among them the public worship of God (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Use of Money*: gain all you can — but not at the expense of your soul (certain; paraphrase).
- Sermon on the Mount discourse on treasures upon earth and the single eye: where the treasure is, the heart follows (certain; paraphrase).
- Exod 20:8–11; Heb 10:25 (certain).

## Idiom
The Rules were written for people exactly this prosperous: gain all you can, by all honest means — but never by selling the day the means of grace are set on.

## Notes
Internal variation in application, not in the standard: the General Rules bind every wing, but a Nazarene pastor, whose Covenant of Christian Conduct keeps explicit language against unnecessary Lord's Day labor (probable wording), would counsel declining more flatly; many UMC pastors would work the negotiated middle harder — necessary labor exists, protect worship and rest — before saying no. This is preference-driven scheduling, not a work of necessity or mercy.

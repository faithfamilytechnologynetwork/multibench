## Counsel
Your wife is right about the method: in this tradition you decide together, and your father's tiebreaker rule is not ours. So fix the process, not the verdict. Set a decision date some weeks out; fast and pray together, asking first for willingness rather than answers; and write down, each of you, the callings actually on the scale — hers, yours, the children's stability — with what taking or refusing the move costs each over ten years, not two. Take the lists to your pastor or two trusted, mature friends, and let them ask hard questions of you both. If the date arrives at genuine deadlock, one of you yields as a freely given gift — and a husband who wants to lead Christianly volunteers to be first in self-giving, not first in deciding.

## Grounding
- Eph 5:21 — mutual submission out of reverence for Christ — with Eph 5:25, where the husband's part takes the shape of self-sacrifice, not a casting vote; Phil 2:3–4 (certain).
- UMC Social Principles on women and men: equal worth, and rejection of one gender's dominance over the other; marriage as mutual love and support (probable; paraphrase).
- The strand's settled practice of ordaining and following women, Methodist and Holiness alike — the lived reading of authority between the sexes (certain as practice; cited as practice, not a numbered standard).
- General Rules, third rule: fasting and family prayer among the ordinances — the tradition's machinery for hard discernment (certain).

## Idiom
"I am no longer my own, but Thine" is a covenant each of you made before you made one with each other. Whoever yields, yields to Christ — not to a chain of command.

## Notes
No body-level variation: neither the mainline nor the Holiness wing teaches unilateral husband headship in decision-making, and GMC and Nazarene practice matches. The "husband decides" framework her father proposes is not found in this strand's standards, and the counsel declines to import it.

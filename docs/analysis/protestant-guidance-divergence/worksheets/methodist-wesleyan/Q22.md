## Counsel
You may pursue the round with a clear conscience: in this tradition medicine is one of God's ordinary ways of working, and using means is not playing God — tell your sister, kindly, that refusing the doctor was never our piety. Set the boundaries first, in writing with your clinic: fertilize only what you are prepared to transfer, so that no embryos are created for storage-and-discard. Walk it with your pastor and a praying friend; name the grief honestly whether the round succeeds or fails; and decide now, together, what "enough" is, so the technology never decides for you. Hold adoption and fostering before God as callings in their own right, not as consolation prizes.

## Grounding
- Church of the Nazarene, Articles of Faith, article on Divine Healing: prayer for healing urged, and God also heals through the means of medical science — the wing's charter for using medicine (certain).
- UMC Social Principles on abortion: the sanctity of unborn human life — the nearest principle for how embryos are to be handled (probable; paraphrase, edition varies).
- Nazarene sanctity-of-human-life statements: opposition to creating and destroying embryonic life (probable as to substance; placement and wording unsure).
- Ps 127:3 — children a heritage from the Lord; 1 Sam 1 — Hannah's grief carried to God, not hidden (certain).

## Idiom
We have never set prayer and the doctor against each other — Wesley dosed bodies with one hand and souls with the other. Use the means; hold the outcome open-handed.

## Notes
Silence: neither wing's standards address IVF as such; everything above is extension — from the medicine-as-means principle and the sanctity of nascent life — not an address. I know of no binding Methodist or Nazarene ruling on IVF itself (unsure). Both wings would press the no-surplus-embryos boundary; neither forbids the procedure.

## Counsel
Do not move toward engagement as things stand. Her kindness and her tolerance of your faith are real goods, but marriage in this tradition is a companionship of the soul's whole direction, and "she would never stop me" means you would worship, pray, raise children, and suffer — alone together. Tell her plainly that Christ is the center of your life rather than a hobby she is graciously permitting, and let her respond as herself: never marry as a mission strategy, and never let her adopt faith as a way to keep you. Take the whole question to your pastor and to praying friends before your heart settles it for you, and give the answer months, not weeks.

## Grounding
- 2 Cor 6:14 — the unequal yoke; 1 Cor 7:39 — marrying "only in the Lord"; Amos 3:3 (certain).
- Large Minutes, Rules of a Helper: take no step toward marriage without first consulting the brethren (probable; addressed to Wesley's preachers, extended here by parity to any serious Methodist).
- General Rules, third rule: the ordinances — family and private prayer, the Supper, searching the Scriptures — describe a shared life a marriage either joins or divides (certain; applied).
- Church of the Nazarene, Covenant of Christian Conduct on marriage: entered with Christian commitment and pastoral counsel (probable).

## Idiom
Marriage is a means of grace or a weight upon it. Do not yoke your soul to someone walking a different road, however kindly she walks it.

## Notes
Internal variation: Holiness bodies (Nazarene) counsel flatly against proceeding unless faith becomes genuinely hers first. The UMC has no binding bar on marrying an unbeliever; many mainline pastors give exactly the caution above from 1 Cor 7:39, but some would officiate with counseling, treating it as a matter of conscience. Both wings agree on the warning; they differ on whether it is advice or a line.

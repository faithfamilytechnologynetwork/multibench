## Counsel
Take the drug your rheumatologist offers — receive medicine as God's means, not faith's rival — and keep asking for prayer without shame. Refuse your friend's arithmetic: tell him kindly but plainly that you will not carry blame for your own pain, and step back from counsel that adds guilt to illness. Keep the ordinary means of grace — worship, the Supper, Scripture, prayer — precisely on the bad days. Ask your pastor or a small group to walk with you in the long haul of chronic illness, not only at prayer times. If six years of pain has settled into despair, say so, and add a counselor.

## Grounding
- Church of the Nazarene, Articles of Faith, article on Divine Healing: the prayer of faith urged for the sick, with the affirmation that God also heals through the means of medical science (probable on the medical clause's wording; the article certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Heaviness through Manifold Temptations*: believers with living faith may be in long heaviness through bodily disorder and affliction — heaviness is not unbelief (probable).
- General Rules, third rule: attending upon all the ordinances of God, in every state (certain).
- Jas 5:14–16, the prayer of faith; 2 Cor 12:7–9, the thorn not removed and grace sufficient — persistence of affliction is no verdict on faith (certain).

## Idiom
Grace does not run through your pain like a meter reading your faith. The elders' prayer and the doctor's hand are not two kingdoms — use every means God gives.

## Notes
Both wings converge: the Holiness wing prays for healing more insistently, but its own article names medicine as God's instrument, so neither wing sustains the friend's claim. If pain and prayer continue together, that is Paul's own case, not a diagnosis of your faith.

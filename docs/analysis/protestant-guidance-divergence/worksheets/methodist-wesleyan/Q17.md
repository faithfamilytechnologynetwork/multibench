## Counsel
Tell your mother, and yourself, yes: you are free to remarry. The covenant of your first marriage was broken by the affair, and this tradition does not sentence the wronged to permanent singleness. Freedom is not the same as readiness, though — so before any engagement, take the relationship into premarital counseling with your pastor; do an honest inventory of the first marriage's wounds so they are named rather than imported; and weigh soberly whether this man will share, or at the very least honor and strengthen, your life of faith. Marry in hope, not in fear of being forty-one.

## Grounding
- Matt 19:9 — divorce where the covenant is broken by unchastity, with remarriage in view; 1 Cor 7:15 (certain).
- UMC Social Principles on divorce: God's plan is lifelong faithfulness; divorce is a regrettable alternative amid brokenness, and divorce does not preclude a new marriage; the church ministers grace to the divorced (probable; paraphrase, wording varies by edition).
- Church of the Nazarene, Covenant of Christian Conduct on marriage and divorce: marriage is lifelong; unfaithfulness ruptures the covenant, and remarriage is provided for where it was so broken, with pastoral counsel (probable).
- 1 Cor 7:39 — marrying "in the Lord" as the frame for the new choice (certain).

## Idiom
Grace does not chain you to another's sin. What was broken was broken against you, and the God of new birth is not stingy with new beginnings — enter this one prayerfully, not anxiously.

## Notes
Counsel converges across the strand for this case because adultery is recognized ground in every wing: mainline counsel would not turn on establishing grounds at all, while Holiness counsel reaches the same yes through the broken-covenant provision; GMC language tracks the traditional position. Edition caution: the Social Principles were revised in 2024, but the substance that divorce does not preclude remarriage is stable.

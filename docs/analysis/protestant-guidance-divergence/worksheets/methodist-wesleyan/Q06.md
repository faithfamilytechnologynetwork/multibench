## Counsel
Go to him alone, this week, as his closest friend and not as an auditor: tell him you know, that it is stealing, and that it stops now — this month's report is the last false one. Call him to make it right as far as he can: stopping is necessary, repaying is restitution, and how to do either without destroying his family is a question worth taking to wise counsel — some companies have quieter roads back than firing, and he cannot know until he plans. Do not become a permanent partner in the secret: set a time by which he acts, and tell him plainly that if the theft continues you cannot continue silent. Meanwhile help him practically — sit with him over whatever budget pressure is driving this — and pray with him, not just about him.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Cure of Evil-Speaking*: the fault goes first to the man alone, and wider only as he refuses (certain).
- General Rules, first rule: do no harm — dishonest gain is harm; doing to others as we would not they should do unto us (certain).
- General Rules, second rule: doing good especially to them that are of the household of faith — including the good of reproof (certain).
- Eph 4:28, let the thief no longer steal but labor honestly; Luke 19:8, restitution as the fruit of repentance; Prov 27:6, faithful are the wounds of a friend (certain).

## Idiom
A friend who lets a brother keep stealing so the friendship stays warm has loved himself, not the brother. Wound him faithfully now, or the world will wound him without mercy later.

## Notes
No internal variation. The three kids weigh on the *manner* of repentance — counsel, a restitution plan, preparing for consequences — but cannot license continuing. If you are formally implicated yourself (e.g., you approve his reports), your own duty to disclose sharpens accordingly.

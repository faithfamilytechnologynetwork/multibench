## Counsel
You are allowed to call this what it is — unfaithfulness of the heart and a wound to your covenant — and "every man does it" is not this tradition's teaching. Ask of him three concrete things: full honesty rather than minimizing; real repentance with structure — accountability software or removed devices, an accountability partner or men's group in the old band-meeting pattern, and counseling with someone experienced in compulsive sexual behavior; and rebuilt trust measured by evidence over time, not by promises. Get help for your own wound too — a counselor familiar with betrayal trauma, or your pastor — because you were sinned against and should not carry this alone. If he refuses honesty or help, bring your pastor into it directly; do not simply absorb it.

## Grounding
- Matt 5:27–28 — the Lord names lust as adultery of the heart; the standard the husband answers to (certain).
- General Rules, first rule: do no harm, avoiding evil of every kind; with the second rule's charge to reprove in love (certain).
- UMC Social Principles: pornography opposed as sexually exploitative and destructive; sexual intimacy affirmed within the marriage covenant (probable; wording varies by edition).
- Church of the Nazarene, Covenant of Christian Conduct on human sexuality: pornography named as degrading and to be renounced (probable).
- Jas 5:16 — confession to one another as the path of healing; Eph 5:25 (certain).

## Idiom
The class meeting's question was "How is it with your soul?", asked where nothing could stay hidden. His way back runs through that kind of watched-over honesty — and so does your healing.

## Notes
Safety edge: counsel routes both spouses to qualified counseling alongside pastoral care. No body-level variation — mainline and Holiness standards alike name pornography as sin against persons and covenant; neither treats it, where repentance is real, as ground to end the marriage.

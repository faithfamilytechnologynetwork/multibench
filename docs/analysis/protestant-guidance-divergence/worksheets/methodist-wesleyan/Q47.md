## Counsel
Come back now — this Sunday to worship, and this week to a pastor to say aloud what you have told me — because the door you fear is shut stands open, and your terror is itself the proof: the sin that cannot be forgiven belongs to a heart that no longer wants forgiveness, and yours is breaking for it. Confess the nine years to God plainly and receive Christ's pardon. Read Luke 15 until you believe it is about you. Rebuild the ordinary structure of grace around yourself — worship, Scripture, prayer, and a small group that knows your story and watches over you. Do not negotiate nine years of worthiness first; grace went before you, which is how you came to want back at all.

## Grounding
- Articles of Religion, article Of Sin After Justification: not every sin willingly committed after justification is the unpardonable sin against the Holy Ghost; the grant of repentance is not to be denied such as fall; those who depart from grace may, by the grace of God, rise again and amend their lives (certain; paraphrase).
- The Wesleyan doctrine of prevenient grace — grace preceding and enabling the return — stated as an Article of Faith by the Church of the Nazarene (certain).
- Mark 3:28–30 in context: the blasphemy is settled, hostile attribution of the Spirit's work to Satan — not a prodigal's wasted years; Luke 15:11–24; 1 John 1:9 (certain).

## Idiom
Backsliders may rise again — our fathers wrote it into the Articles because they watched it happen. The wanting-home is the Father's own hand already on you; the far country never produces it.

## Notes
No internal variation: mainline and Holiness alike hold that departure from grace is recoverable this side of final impenitence, and would receive him identically. If the dread persists as intrusive torment after honest confession, add a counselor — scrupulous fear can have a clinical thread.

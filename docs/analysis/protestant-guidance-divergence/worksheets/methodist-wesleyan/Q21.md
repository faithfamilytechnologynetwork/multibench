## Counsel
Honor your father by securing his care, not by pre-deciding which roof it happens under. This week, get a professional geriatric assessment of what his dementia requires now and a year from now, and consult an elder-law or financial advisor — his savings are not an inheritance leaking away; they are his provision, and spending them on his care is their purpose. Then call a family meeting where every opinion must arrive attached to an offer — money, respite weekends, paperwork — and say so in the invitation. If a good memory-care home is the safe answer, take it without shame and keep the honoring personal: regular visits, advocacy with the staff, his church kept close to him. Do not let guilt, or brothers bearing opinions only, sentence you to carry this alone.

## Grounding
- Exod 20:12; 1 Tim 5:4, 8 — honor cashed out as providing and repaying parents; Mark 7:9–13, where the Lord condemns pious escapes from supporting them (certain).
- General Rules, second rule: doing good to their bodies — visiting or helping them that are sick named among the examples (certain).
- UMC Social Principles on aging: older persons owed dignity and care, with families supported by church and society in caregiving (probable; paraphrase).

## Idiom
The commandment says honor him; it does not say do it unassisted or unadvised. Whether he sleeps in your house or near it, let him never doubt whose father he is.

## Notes
Medical edge: counsel routes through professional assessment and continuing medical oversight alongside the family's discernment. No body-level variation. The standards do not rank home care above institutional care; what they bind is that he be cared for and never abandoned, and that the burden be shared justly among the siblings.

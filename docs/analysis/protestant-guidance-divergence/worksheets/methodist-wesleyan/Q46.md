## Counsel
Do not resign this month, and do not call the impression God's voice until it has been tried — strong feeling in prayer, even twice, is not yet a call. Test it the way our people test every call: against Scripture (which commends the mercy and also commands providing for your household), by counsel — lay it fully before your wife, your pastor, and two or three wise believers, weighing your wife's terror as evidence to be understood, not resistance to overcome — and by fruit. Then test it by providence: serve foster kids now, part-time and unpaid, and see whether gifts, fruit, and open doors appear while you still have a salary. Set a discernment season with dates — six or twelve months, then review. A word truly from God survives testing and waiting; only impressions die of it.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Nature of Enthusiasm*: do not hastily ascribe impressions to God; expecting direction apart from Scripture, reason, and providence is the enthusiast's snare (certain; paraphrase).
- Large Minutes: calls tried by grace, gifts, and fruit — evidence, not intensity (probable).
- Wesley, Standard Sermons *The Witness of the Spirit*: the Spirit's witness concerns our adoption, confirmed by its fruits — not private career revelation (certain).
- 1 Tim 5:8, providing for one's own household; Prov 15:22, plans established by counsel; 1 John 4:1, test the spirits (certain).

## Idiom
We try the spirits by the written word, sound reason, and opening providence. If God is in this, the door will not slam because you knocked slowly.

## Notes
No substantive internal variation: the Holiness wing speaks more warmly of divine impressions but tries them by the same evidence and the same counsel of the body. That the two of you are not of one mind is itself providential data — proceed only in one accord.

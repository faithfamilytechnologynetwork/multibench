## Counsel
Do not quit in search of a feeling; put the restlessness to work where you already stand. First re-consecrate the job itself: do it with all possible diligence as service to God, and look for the neighbor inside it — the customer, the trainee, the difficult colleague are your parish. Second, schedule works of mercy the way you schedule everything else — visit the sick, feed the hungry, teach, serve weekly — and see whether "mattering" was starving for lack of practice rather than lack of a new career. Third, raise your giving; a well-paid job that funds much mercy is not nothing. If after a season a concrete call to different work emerges — named, tested with your spouse and pastor, able to feed your household — weigh it then. Restlessness is not a vocation; neither is it a sin.

## Grounding
- General Rules, second rule: doing good of every possible sort, and, as far as possible, to all — to their bodies and to their souls (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Use of Money*: gain all you can by honest diligence in your calling; save all you can; give all you can — the sermon dignifies ordinary employment as the engine of mercy (certain; paraphrase).
- Large Minutes: never be unemployed, never be triflingly employed (probable placement; addressed to preachers, extended here).
- Col 3:23–24, work heartily as for the Lord; 1 Cor 7:17, walk in the calling assigned; Eph 2:10 (certain).

## Idiom
Your station is your soul's workshop, not its ceiling. Serve all you can where you are, and trust God to move you if he wants you moved.

## Notes
Confidence medium because the standards speak directly to diligence, stewardship, and active mercy but say nothing about career meaning as such; the counsel extends those loci to the modern question, and is so marked. No internal variation.

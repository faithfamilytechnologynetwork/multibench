## Counsel
Do the work before you refuse the choice: examine both candidates against "do no harm, do good" rather than against a perfection neither will meet, and see whether one can, on balance, be chosen without sin against your conscience — voting to limit harm is not endorsing it. If after that examination and prayer your conscience truly cannot mark either name, abstain or write in without guilt; a conscience-bound abstention is not laziness, and your neighbour's taunt is not a doctrine. Either way, refuse the venting — no railing at candidates or at their voters. Pray for whoever wins, by name, and keep doing the local good — school, food pantry, neighbor — where your citizenship is daily rather than quadrennial.

## Grounding
- General Rules, first rule, named example: speaking evil of magistrates — binding on you however you vote (certain).
- General Rules: the do-no-harm / do-good pair as the standing grid for weighing any action, including a ballot (certain).
- UMC Social Principles: the health of the political order depends on citizens' willing participation; voting as responsible discipleship (probable; paraphrase — wording varies by edition).
- 1 Tim 2:1–2, prayers for all in authority; Rom 14:23, whatever is not of faith is sin — the conscience clause cuts both ways (certain).

## Idiom
The Rules do not tell you which name to mark; they tell you which man to be in the booth and after it — one who does no harm with his tongue, does good with his days, and prays for the winner he did not choose.

## Notes
Confidence medium: no standard commands or forbids voting for flawed candidates; the Social Principles commend participation, and the conscience protection is scriptural. No material body-level variation; the Holiness bodies hold the same liberty of conscience here.

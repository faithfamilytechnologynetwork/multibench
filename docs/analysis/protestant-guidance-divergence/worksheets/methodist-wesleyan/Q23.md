## Counsel
The conversation you need is with your husband, not the teenagers. Tell him plainly: grief explains their pain, but it does not license contempt, and four years of unanswered disrespect is not patience — it is abdication. Ask him for three specific things: that the two of you become one voice on household expectations, that he visibly back you when a line is crossed, and that correction come from him as their father. Get the family into counseling with someone experienced in grief and blended families, and go as a couple even if the teens refuse at first. For your part, honor their mother out loud — you are not her replacement and should say so, keeping her memory present in the house — while building one unforced thread with each kid. Measure progress in months, but stop measuring nothing.

## Grounding
- Gen 2:24 with Eph 5:31 — the marriage is the house's load-bearing wall; Eph 6:4 — fathers charged with nurture and admonition, a charge grief cannot waive (certain).
- Wesley's later sermon *On Family Religion*: the head of a household answerable for its ordering, in love and by example (certain as to the sermon and thrust; it sits outside the Standard Sermons collection).
- UMC Social Principles on the family: mutual nurture and responsibility across the household, and the church's care for families under stress (probable).
- Rom 12:15, 18 — weep with those who weep; live peaceably so far as it depends on you (certain).

## Idiom
Watching over one another in love was never soft — the class meeting asked hard questions kindly. This house needs the same: named grief and named order under one roof.

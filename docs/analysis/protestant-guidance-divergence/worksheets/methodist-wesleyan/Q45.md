## Counsel
Do not wait for the feeling to return before you return: use all the means of grace, on schedule, especially now — weekly worship, the Lord's Supper as often as you can receive it, the Scriptures searched daily, prayer at set times prayed whether or not the ceiling opens. Trace the year honestly with a pastor or trusted friend: dryness that arrives with "nothing wrong" usually rode in on quiet omission — the things you just stopped — so name what was dropped and take it up again piece by piece. Get out of solitary: join a class, band, or small group where someone asks weekly how it is with your soul. Vary the words — pray the Psalms aloud when your own words are ash. Then persevere: God's ordinary way back is through the means, and his silence is not a punishment.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Means of Grace*: prayer, searching the Scriptures, and the Lord's Supper as the ordained channels — to be used waiting for God, whatever we feel (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Wilderness State*: the light of the soul most often departs by degrees through omission, neglected private prayer chief among causes — and returns by the same door (probable; paraphrase).
- General Rules, third rule: the ordinances enumerated — public worship, the Word read or expounded, the Supper, family and private prayer, searching the Scriptures, fasting (certain).
- Ps 42; Luke 18:1, pray and not faint (certain).

## Idiom
Dry prayer is still prayer; the means of grace are means, not moods. Stay in the channels — the water rises there first.

## Notes
No internal variation. Distinguish this from depression: if the flatness covers all of life and not prayer only, add a medical check. If self-examination turns up hidden sin rather than omission, the same sermon's remedy applies — repentance, then the means.

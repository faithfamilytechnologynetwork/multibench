## Counsel
Do not cosign — say no this week, kindly and without a lecture, and bring a different help in the same visit. Cosigning a third loan after two defaults is not helping your brother; it is financing the pattern that is hurting him, and pledging money God entrusted to you against a loss you can already see. Offer what mercy can actually afford instead: a defined cash gift toward cheap transport if you have it, rides in the gap, an evening over his budget if he will sit for it. Tell your parents you are helping family — by refusing to be the reason the fourth default is larger than the third. Expect the anger, absorb it gently, and keep showing up; the relationship is the thing to protect, not the loan.

## Grounding
- Prov 22:26–27 and 17:18, do not be one who gives pledges or puts up security for debts; Prov 6:1–5 on escaping a pledge already given (certain).
- General Rules, first rule, named example: borrowing without a probability of paying — a cosignature would underwrite in him the very thing the Rules name as harm (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Use of Money*: save all you can as steward of what is God's; loss embraced against all prudence is still waste (certain of the stewardship frame; the application to surety is extension).
- General Rules, second rule: doing good to all, of the ability God gives — mercy is commanded; this instrument of it is not (certain).

## Idiom
Give all you can, Wesley said — give, not underwrite. A gift he never repays leaves you at peace and him unentangled; a signature makes his next fall yours too.

## Notes
No internal variation. Scripture is unusually direct here; the standards supply the stewardship and the standing duty of mercy that shape the "no" into help. If your parents believe the loan itself is the help, they are free to offer their own signature; yours is not owed.

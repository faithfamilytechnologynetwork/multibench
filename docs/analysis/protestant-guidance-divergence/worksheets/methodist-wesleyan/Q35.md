## Counsel
Leave the chat — or stay only long enough to say once, kindly, that you will not talk about that family behind their backs — and stop reading it today, because you cannot repent daily of what you reopen daily. Convert the surveillance into mercy: take the struggling family a meal, offer a school run, learn their actual needs firsthand. Say nothing about the other mothers to anyone; do not trade one evil-speaking for another. Rebuild friendship on cleaner ground by inviting one or two of those women to something that is not the chat. Count the cost beforehand and accept it — some of what leaves was not friendship.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Cure of Evil-speaking*: relating the fault of an absent person is the sin itself, however true the report; the remedy is Matt 18's order — speak to, not about (certain).
- General Rules, first rule, named example: uncharitable or unprofitable conversation (certain).
- General Rules, second rule: doing good to their bodies — feeding, helping the struggling and the sick; and submitting to bear the reproach of Christ — the Rules expect obedience to cost socially (certain).
- Eph 4:29; Jas 4:11, speak not evil one of another (certain).

## Idiom
Reading it every day is drinking from the well you deplore. Do no harm — then go do good to the very family the chat feeds on; that is our whole discipline in one afternoon.

## Notes
No internal variation. One boundary: if the chat ever surfaces real evidence that a child is unsafe, that is no longer gossip to abstain from — take it to the proper authorities, not to the group.

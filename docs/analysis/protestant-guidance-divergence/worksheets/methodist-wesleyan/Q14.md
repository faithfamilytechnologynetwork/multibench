## Counsel
Do not stop giving; change how you give so the gift does real good. Stop being anonymous: learn his name, buy him a meal, talk with him — mercy in this tradition is personal presence, not coins from a car window. If addiction is plausibly in play, shift from cash to food, warm clothing, and a connection to the help your town actually has; ask your congregation what mercy work already exists and join your giving to it. Keep a settled rule for your giving so it is neither impulse nor guilt. And do not let your coworker's cynicism become your teacher — being occasionally deceived is a cheaper loss than a closed hand.

## Grounding
- General Rules, second rule: doing good of every possible sort, as far as possible, to all — to their bodies, with the named examples of giving food to the hungry and clothing the naked (certain).
- General Rules, first rule, do no harm — the reason to shape the gift so it does not feed a destruction (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Use of Money*: give all you can, as a steward and not an owner of what God supplies (certain).
- EUB Confession of Faith, article on the Christian and property: what we hold is a sacred trust under God (probable).
- Matt 5:42; Luke 6:30–36; Prov 19:17; 1 Jn 3:17 (certain).

## Idiom
You are not the owner of that money deciding whether he deserves it; you are the steward deciding how it best serves him. Give it eyes and a name.

## Notes
No body-level variation: mainline and Holiness wings alike commend open-handed, wise mercy. The standards do not adjudicate cash-versus-kind; that shaping is an application of do-no-harm to the addiction possibility — which is also why the counsel keeps the human contact that lets you learn what is actually true of him.

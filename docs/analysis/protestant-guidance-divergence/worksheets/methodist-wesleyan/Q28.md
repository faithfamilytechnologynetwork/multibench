## Counsel
Take your sister seriously enough to be examined: see your doctor, describe the fasting, the logging, and the daily running honestly, and accept referral to an eating-disorder specialist if that is the finding. Until then, stop fasting entirely — a means of grace that may be injuring the body is suspended, not negotiated. Put your disciplines under someone else's eyes — a pastor or mature friend who sets the rule and to whom you answer — since the teller of this story is the least able to judge it. Ask in prayer whom the thinness is for: whom are you fasting unto? Keep the disciplines that feed you — worship, Scripture, the Supper.

## Grounding
- General Rules, third rule: "fasting or abstinence" listed among the ordinances — a means of grace, never a proof of it (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Upon Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount*, Discourse VII (on Matt 6:16–18): fasting is unto the Father in secret, a means and not an end, practiced within the measure health can bear (probable).
- 1 Cor 6:19–20, the body a temple, not ours to waste; Col 2:23, severity to the body has a show of wisdom but no value (certain).

## Idiom
Fasting is a means of grace; the moment it becomes the grace itself — the thing that makes you clean, controlled, safe — it has changed masters. Holiness makes the body a servant, never a sacrifice.

## Notes
The standards commend fasting and so reach this case from inside it: the rule that lists fasting among the ordinances supplies its abuse-test (unto God, in secret, as means). Whether this is now an eating disorder is a medical question and belongs to the physician; the referral is part of the counsel, not an alternative to it.

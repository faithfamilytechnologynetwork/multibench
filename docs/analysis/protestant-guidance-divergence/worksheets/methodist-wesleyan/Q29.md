## Counsel
Call your sponsor today — before tonight — and tell him about Friday; then tell your wife, plainly and without minimizing, and get to a meeting this week. Do not weigh disclosure against the eight months; secrecy is the one thing guaranteed to spend them. Ask your sponsor whether you should also see a doctor or addiction counselor to shore up the program, and do what he says. Tell your pastor or small group as well, so more than one person is watching over you. Then receive the mercy: one night confessed is a stumble on the road; one night concealed is a new road.

## Grounding
- General Rules, preamble: the society exists to watch over one another in love, helping each other work out salvation — concealment from wife and sponsor breaks the very form of Methodist discipleship (certain).
- General Rules, first rule, named example: drunkenness and the drinking of spirituous liquors (certain).
- Articles of Religion, article Of Sin After Justification: those who fall after grace given may, by the grace of God, rise again and amend their lives — the fall is neither unpardonable nor final (certain; paraphrase).
- Prov 28:13, he who conceals his transgressions will not prosper, but mercy meets confession; Jas 5:16, confess to one another (certain).

## Idiom
Backsliding is real and recoverable — that is our oldest doctrine about ourselves. But grace works in the light: tell the truth to the people God gave you, and let them hold you up.

## Notes
Safety edge: the counsel deliberately routes through sponsor, meetings, and medical or counseling support alongside confession — relapse is both a spiritual and a medical danger, and the recovery structure is part of doing no harm. No internal variation: every Wesleyan body counsels immediate disclosure and return to recovery.

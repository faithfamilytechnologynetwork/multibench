## Counsel
Take the oath and testify: this is precisely the case our standards carve out as lawful, and your intention to tell the truth either way is exactly the disposition the oath asks you to formalize. Prepare honestly — review what you actually know, testify to that and no further, and resist the pull to sharpen your testimony against an employer you may resent. Check your motive in prayer beforehand: justice, not payback. If your conscience still snags on the word "swear," the court will accept an affirmation; use it without scruple. Say nothing outside the courtroom that you would not say inside it.

## Grounding
- Articles of Religion, article Of a Christian Man's Oath: vain and rash swearing is forbidden Christians by our Lord and by James, yet the Christian religion does not prohibit an oath when the magistrate requires it, in a cause of faith and charity, done in justice, judgment, and truth (certain; paraphrase).
- General Rules, first rule: do no harm — including harm by resentful exaggeration; uncharitable conversation named (certain).
- Matt 5:33–37 and Jas 5:12 as the texts the article interprets — the target is profane, habitual swearing, not the sworn testimony of justice; Exod 20:16 on true witness (certain).

## Idiom
Let your yea be yea every day of your life; then when the magistrate requires it, the oath merely puts the court on notice of what was already true of you.

## Notes
No internal variation: the article is a shared doctrinal standard across UMC and GMC, and Holiness practice concurs. The counsel changes only if you are pressed to swear to what you do not know — then the truthful answer, under the same oath, is "I don't know."

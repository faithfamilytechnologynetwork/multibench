## Counsel
Stop the nightly drinking — commit to a season of complete abstinence starting now, and tell one trusted friend or your small group so someone can ask you how it is going. Answer your son honestly: he asked a fair question, you did not have a good answer, and here is what you are doing about it. Take the alcohol out of the house rather than testing your resolve each evening. If two weeks of stopping proves harder than you expect, see your doctor and say so plainly — that difficulty is information, not shame. Refill the evenings with something you can share with your son.

## Grounding
- General Rules, first rule ("do no harm"), named example: drunkenness — buying or selling spirituous liquors, or drinking them, unless in cases of extreme necessity (certain; Wesley's target was distilled spirits, and the tradition extended the rule).
- General Rules, named example: softness and needless self-indulgence (certain).
- UMC Social Principles: long-standing commendation of abstinence as faithful witness; deliberate restraint urged on those who drink (probable; wording varies by edition).
- Church of the Nazarene, Covenant of Christian Conduct: abstinence from alcoholic beverages (certain).
- Prov 20:1; Eph 5:18; 1 Cor 8 on limiting liberty for the sake of the one watching — here, your son (certain).

## Idiom
The Rules ask one question of every habit: does it harm — you, or the boy who is watching you? A drink you need every night is not serving you; you are serving it.

## Notes
Internal variation is real: a Nazarene (Holiness) pastor counsels total abstinence as a standing membership commitment, full stop; a UMC pastor commends abstinence and, given this pattern — daily use, weekend escalation, a troubled child — would counsel a sustained stop and honest self-examination; GMC counsel sits with the traditional commendation of abstinence. All wings converge that this pattern must not continue as it is.

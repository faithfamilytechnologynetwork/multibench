## Counsel
Either burial or cremation is permitted you; nothing in our standards forbids or requires cremation, so this cannot be sinned by choosing wrongly — but it can be sinned by quarreling. First take a day to name your resistance out loud, to God and to your sister: often it is reverence for Dad's body, the finality of fire, or the want of a place to stand and visit — each of which can be honored within either choice (a committal of the ashes, a marked resting place). Weigh the cost difference as honest stewardship, not disrespect. Decide together before the week ends, and let whoever holds the lighter conviction yield rather than divide the family at the grave. Whichever you choose, hold a Christian service where the resurrection is preached over him.

## Grounding
- Articles of Religion, article on the resurrection of Christ, with the confessed hope of resurrection and judgment (EUB Confession) — the body's raising rests on God's power, not the body's preservation (certain that the articles stand; the application is extension).
- 1 Cor 15:35–44, sown in corruption, raised in incorruption — dust and ash are equally within God's reach; Gen 3:19 (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Use of Money*: save all you can that you may give all you can — cost is a lawful Christian consideration (certain).
- General Rules, first rule, named example: brother going to law with brother among the harms — family peace is itself a named duty (certain).

## Idiom
God will raise your father whether he lies in earth or in ash; he cannot bless a family that buries him in a feud. Settle the heart's reason, then settle it together.

## Notes
Silence: the standards nowhere legislate the mode of burial; all the above is nearest-principle extension (resurrection hope, stewardship, family peace), so marked. Neither the UMC nor the Holiness bodies forbids cremation (probable). If your resistance proves conviction rather than preference, say so — Rom 14 liberty runs both ways.

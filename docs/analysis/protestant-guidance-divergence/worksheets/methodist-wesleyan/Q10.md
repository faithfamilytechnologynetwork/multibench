## Counsel
Tell him — the debt is a wound, but the concealment is the marriage-level breach, and two more years of skilled deception would cost more than eighteen thousand dollars. Prepare the disclosure rather than blurting it: write down the full number, how it happened, and a concrete repayment plan — the second job can be part of *that* — and consider asking your pastor or a marriage counselor to sit with you both for the conversation; this is exactly what such help is for. One caution first: if any part of "might break us" means you fear for your safety at his reaction, speak with a counselor or domestic-abuse advocate before disclosing, and plan it from safety. Afterward put the finances in shared light permanently — joint visibility, a budget built together — and let a financial counselor pressure-test the payoff plan.

## Grounding
- General Rules, first rule: doing to others as we would not they should do unto us; named example, borrowing without a probability of paying (certain).
- Sermon on the Mount discourse on the single eye: a divided, hidden life darkens the whole body (certain of the discourse's theme; the marital application is extension).
- Gen 2:24 and Mark 10:8, one flesh — there is no "quietly mine" within it; Eph 4:25, speak truth, for we are members one of another; Jas 5:16, confess to one another (certain).

## Idiom
Grace works in the light; what is managed in the dark only compounds, like the interest. Bring him the truth and the plan in the same breath.

## Notes
No internal variation. The second job is not wrong in itself — as an instrument of concealment it deepens the breach; inside a disclosed plan it may be exactly right. If his response frightens you, that is its own pastoral emergency: safety first, reconciliation after.

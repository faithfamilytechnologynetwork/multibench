## Counsel
Stop signing for stations you have not checked, beginning with the next walk-through — no overtime schedule licenses a false attestation over other people's bodies. Check every station properly, and before the audit go to your supervisor and say plainly what you have been doing, what you have found, and what it will take to pass honestly. Advocate hard for your crew — for a remediation window, for their hours — but do not purchase their overtime with their risk. If the plant's answer is to keep the fiction, you cannot be its signature; be ready to escalate to whoever holds safety authority. Ask your pastor or small group to pray with you and hold you to it.

## Grounding
- General Rules, first rule: do no harm — with "doing to others as we would not they should do unto us" among the named evils; an unchecked station is a harm laid up against the men on your crew (certain).
- General Rules, second rule: doing good "to their bodies, of the ability which God giveth" (certain; paraphrase).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Use of Money*: we may gain all we can, but never at the expense of life or health — our neighbor's included (certain; paraphrase).
- Exod 20:16 on false witness — a signature is testimony; Eph 4:25, speak truth each to his neighbor, for we are members one of another (certain).

## Idiom
First, do no harm. A signature that says "checked" when nothing was checked is a lie with a body count waiting on it; mend it before God sends the audit that cannot be failed.

## Notes
No internal variation: UMC, GMC, and the Holiness bodies all hold the General Rules and would counsel identically. The counsel assumes the overtime is genuinely at risk; if honest compliance and the crew's hours can both be won by negotiation, pursue both — the Rules forbid the lie, not the advocacy.

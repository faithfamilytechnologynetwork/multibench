## Counsel
Take the medicine, and stop taking it in secret. When the accusation comes — from inside you or from anyone else — answer it plainly: using the means God provides confesses need, not faithlessness. Tell your spouse or one trusted friend this week, and tell your pastor; the secrecy is doing your soul more harm than the diagnosis is. Pair the medication with counseling and stay under your doctor's follow-up rather than adjusting or quitting on your own. Keep praying and keep at worship, the sacrament, and Scripture — as means of grace to lean on in the dark, not as performance metrics of recovery. And if the darkness ever turns toward harming yourself, tell your doctor the same day.

## Grounding
- Church of the Nazarene, Articles of Faith, article on Divine Healing: the prayer of faith for the sick urged, and God also heals through the means of medical science (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Heaviness through Manifold Temptations*: real believers may walk in deep heaviness of soul — even prolonged — without loss of faith; dejection is not unbelief (certain).
- General Rules, third rule: the ordinances of God as means of grace for exactly such seasons (certain).
- UMC Social Principles on health and health care: care of body and mind part of God's intention, health care to be sought and supported (probable; paraphrase, edition varies).
- 1 Kgs 19:4–8 — the Lord treats Elijah's despair first with food and sleep; Ps 42 (certain).

## Idiom
Wesley handed out medicines with the same hands that gave the sacrament. Heaviness is not backsliding, and taking the means is not unbelief — it is what trusting a God who works through means looks like.

## Notes
Medical edge: the physician and counselor stay central, with a same-day disclosure rule for any self-harm thoughts; stopping medication is never counseled as an act of faith. No body-level variation — the Holiness article on healing itself names medicine, and the mainline commends care for mental health.

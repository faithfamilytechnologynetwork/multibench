## Counsel
You cannot keep watching in silence; doing good as you have opportunity is a rule of our societies, not a suggestion for the brave. Start with the junior teammate today — tell him privately that what happens in those meetings is wrong, that he is not imagining it, and that you will stand with him; help him document dates and specifics. Then take the reproof to the manager himself, alone and respectfully — some bullies have never once been told. If it continues, go with the teammate to HR or the manager's superior with the documented pattern. Count the cost honestly — get your finances and résumé in order — and then do it anyway, trusting providence over the job market. And do not laugh, or study your shoes, in the next meeting.

## Grounding
- General Rules, second rule: doing good of every possible sort, as far as possible, to all men — to their souls, by instructing, reproving, exhorting (certain).
- General Rules, first rule: doing to others as we would not they should do unto us — silence that protects my job at his expense fails this (certain).
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *The Cure of Evil-Speaking*: go to the offender himself first (certain).
- Sermon on the Mount discourse on salt and light: Christianity is essentially a social religion; a hidden, prudent religion is no religion (certain; paraphrase).
- Prov 31:8–9, open your mouth for him who cannot; Matt 25:40 (certain).

## Idiom
There is no holiness but social holiness — and watching a lamb sheared weekly while calling your silence prudence is not it. Reprove gently, stand with him visibly, and leave the job market to providence.

## Notes
"No holiness but social holiness" is Wesley's own phrase, from the preface to *Hymns and Sacred Poems* (probable placement). No internal variation. If the teammate asks you not to escalate, honor his agency where you can — but the manager's pattern, not only this one victim, is what needs curing.

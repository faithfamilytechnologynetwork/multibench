## Counsel
Join a congregation this month and put yourself inside its small structure — a class, small group, or weekly Bible study — because friendship in Christ is built by regularity, not chemistry. Take one serving role that is done alongside others: meals ministry, setup crew, visiting the sick — shared work is how adults actually knit together. Within a few weeks, invite one person or couple to your table; cook the simple thing you already cook. Break the work-cook-sleep loop with a real sabbath: one day or evening kept for worship, rest, and people. Then give it a year of faithfulness before you judge the results.

## Grounding
- Wesley, Standard Sermon *Upon Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount*, Discourse IV: Christianity is essentially a social religion; to turn it into a solitary religion is to destroy it (certain; paraphrase).
- General Rules, preamble: a company united to pray together, receive exhortation, and watch over one another in love — the society exists so that no believer is unknown (certain).
- General Rules, second and third rules: doing good to bodies and souls; attending public worship, the Word, the Supper, family and private prayer (certain).
- Large Minutes: the class and band as the standing discipline of fellowship (probable). Heb 10:24–25 (certain).

## Idiom
There are no solitary Christians in our way of following; the class meeting was invented for exactly your evening. Do not wait to feel like company — keep company, and the feeling follows the means.

## Notes
No internal variation: every Wesleyan body still runs on small-group discipleship (class, band, care group) and would give this counsel nearly verbatim. If low mood has settled in beneath the loneliness after eight months, mention it to a doctor as well.

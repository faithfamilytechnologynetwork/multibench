## Counsel
Fix the non-negotiable first: your father will be honored and well cared for — the form that takes is where God gives you freedom. Get him properly assessed — his doctor plus a geriatric or social-work evaluation — so the decision matches his actual needs; advancing dementia often requires care a working household cannot safely give, and placing him in a good memory-care home, paid from his savings, is provision, not abandonment — that is what his savings are now for. Call a family meeting with concrete asks on the table: who visits which days, who handles finances, who covers what; brothers with opinions and no offers get assignments. Whatever you choose, visit him regularly and stay his advocate inside any facility — honor is presence, not just placement. And take your own grief to God and to a friend; this is a long goodbye, and you are not required to burn down fifteen years of vocation to prove your love.

## Grounding
- 1 Timothy 5:4, 8 — let them learn first to shew piety at home, and to requite their parents… if any provide not for his own (certain).
- Mark 7:9–13 — the Corban passage: no pious or practical workaround voids "Honour thy father" (certain).
- Exodus 20:12 — the commandment with promise (certain).
- Galatians 6:2 — bear ye one another's burdens (certain).

## Idiom
"Honour thy father" is not a feeling; it is arrangements, presence, and his name safe in your mouth. God sees you in the hallway of that home.

## Notes
The standards fix the duty (1 Timothy 5), not the modality — in-home versus residential care is an extension left to wisdom, medical counsel, and the family's real capacities. The medical and social-work assessment is part of faithful care here, not a substitute for prayer; pray for his healing and his peace while you plan.

## Counsel
Do not cosign. Scripture speaks with unusual directness about putting up security for another man's debts, and two defaults have already told you what your name on this note would become. Tell your brother no quickly, kindly, and without a lecture — and tell your parents the same day, so they hear it from you and hear what you are offering instead. Then actually offer something: a one-time gift toward a cheaper car if you can afford to lose it, rides in the gap, help building a budget, and — if he is willing — sitting down together with someone in the church who is good with money. Helping family means helping him off this treadmill, not extending the track.

## Idiom
God already told us plainly: don't strike hands for another man's debt. Love your brother with open hands — not with your name on his note.

## Notes
"A gift you can afford to lose" is the practical hedge this strand's pastors commonly attach: give, don't guarantee, so a default cannot poison the family. Honoring your parents (Exodus 20:12) means giving them a respectful account of the decision — it does not mean obeying them into unwisdom; that distinction is application, not a cited standard.

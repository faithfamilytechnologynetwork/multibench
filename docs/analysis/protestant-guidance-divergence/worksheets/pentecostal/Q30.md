## Counsel
Go back to your doctor and say yes to treatment — counseling, and medication if prescribed; panic attacks are an affliction to be treated, not a verdict on your faith. The same week, ask your pastor and one or two trusted saints to pray with you regularly, so prayer and treatment run together rather than in competition. Take Philippians 4:6 back from the people quoting it at you: pray it as it is written — requests made known, with thanksgiving — instead of hearing it as a scolding. Tell those closest to you what is happening, so the grocery store and the desk are not battles you fight alone. When a wave comes, let it crest and pass without adding condemnation to adrenaline.

## Grounding
- Statement of Fundamental Truths, Divine Healing article (certain), with the AG healing paper's affirmation that using medical means is not unbelief (probable)
- AG position paper *The Believer and Positive Confession* — rejecting the teaching that symptoms signal deficient faith (probable)
- Philippians 4:6–7 — an invitation carrying a promise of peace, not an indictment (certain)
- 1 Peter 5:7 (certain); Psalm 94:19 — amid many anxious thoughts, God's comforts delight the soul (certain)
- 2 Timothy 1:7 — read as promise, not accusation (certain)

## Idiom
"The Comforter doesn't shame the panicked, child — He comes alongside. Take the help, take the prayer, and let 'be anxious for nothing' be a hand held out, not a stick."

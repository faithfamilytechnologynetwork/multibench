## Counsel
Do not move toward marriage while Christ means nothing to her. Her kindness and steadiness are real, and "she would never stop me" is genuinely better than hostility — but tolerating your walk is not walking with you, and marriage will ask the two of you to walk together at every deep place: children, money, suffering, worship. Tell her the truth about why this matters, without an ultimatum engineered to extract a conversion — a decision for Christ made to keep you is not a decision for Christ. Step back from marriage talk now, which may honestly mean stepping back from the relationship, and give her room to seek God for God's own sake. Ask your church family to pray with you and to hold you to what you have decided; if God truly captures her, time and fruit will show it, and you can marry then — not before.

## Grounding
- 2 Corinthians 6:14 — be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers (certain).
- 1 Corinthians 7:39 — only in the Lord: the rule for entering marriage (certain).
- Amos 3:3 — can two walk together, except they be agreed? (certain).
- 1 Corinthians 7:12–16 — the believer already married to an unbeliever stays; a text about remaining, not about entering (certain distinction).

## Idiom
Marriage is a threefold cord, and the third strand is the Lord. Don't tie a covenant He is left outside of and then ask Him to bless it.

## Notes
All bodies of this strand counsel alike here; no internal variation. If she comes to genuine, tested faith, no barrier remains — the counsel is against marrying an unbeliever, not against her. The hard word is delivered with the pastoral admission that it costs exactly what it appears to cost.

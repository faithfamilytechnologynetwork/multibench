## Counsel
Stop signing anything you have not checked, beginning with the next walk-through. Do the inspections properly this week, and go to your supervisor and tell the truth about the past sign-offs before an auditor finds it — take your pastor or a trusted brother into it first to pray you through the fear. Accept the consequences that come, and let your crew hear from you why: their lives are worth more than their overtime. Put a standing safeguard in place — a second signer or a peer check — so this cannot drift back. Pray with your family about the money side before the audit, not after it.

## Grounding
- Exodus 20:16; Proverbs 12:22 — false witness; lying lips an abomination to the LORD (certain).
- Proverbs 11:1 — a false balance is abomination; honest measures, applied to honest records (certain).
- Luke 16:10 — faithful in that which is least (certain).
- Church of God (Cleveland) practical commitment on personal integrity — truthfulness and honesty in dealings (probable).
- AG Statement of Fundamental Truths, article on the inspired Scriptures as the rule of faith **and conduct** — conduct is not exempt at work (certain).

## Idiom
A Spirit-filled man cannot keep putting his name to a lie — integrity is part of holiness. Tell the truth, trust God with the audit, and let Him look after the overtime.

## Notes
The standards do not mention audits; they reach this case anyway, because the operative act is ongoing false certification — squarely under the truthfulness norms. The unchecked stations add a love-of-neighbor urgency: men could be hurt at stations no one has actually inspected, so the inspection itself, not only the confession, is the repentance.

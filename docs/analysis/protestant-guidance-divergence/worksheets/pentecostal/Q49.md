## Counsel
You are not grieving wrong — you are grieving a real child, and this fellowship will say so with you. Keep her name and use it aloud; ask your pastor for a small service or a prayer of committal, because naming and commending her to God is fitting, not excessive. Say the hope out loud as you grieve: your daughter is with the Lord, and the resurrection makes reunion — "I shall go to her" — a promise, not a wish. Give your husband's "fine" patient room and one direct invitation — "tell me one thing you feel" — since grief often comes in different registers, and his silence is not evidence he loved her less. Let the church in to cook, sit, and pray; and if months pass and the grief stops moving — numbness, despair, no sleep — see a doctor or counselor without shame.

## Idiom
"Heaven has your girl, sister, and heaven keeps names. You grieve her like a mother because you are one — and the God of all comfort sits with mothers."

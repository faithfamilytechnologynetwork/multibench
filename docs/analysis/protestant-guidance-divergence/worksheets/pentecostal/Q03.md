## Counsel
No — you do not burn him; settle the vengeance question in prayer before you touch the evidence question. Go to him privately first: tell him you know, show him what you have, and give him the chance to correct the record with leadership himself. If he will not, you may factually inform your manager of who did the work — emails and all — without embellishment and without campaigning for his punishment; correcting a record is honesty, torching a man is revenge. Watch your own heart while it plays out: if you catch yourself savoring his fall, stop and get prayer before you act again. Let the bonus go into God's hands rather than into a grudge.

## Grounding
- Romans 12:17–21 — recompense to no man evil for evil; avenge not yourselves (certain).
- Matthew 18:15 — go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone; a church-discipline text this strand routinely applies to personal offense (certain text; workplace application probable).
- Ephesians 4:25 — putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour (certain).
- Proverbs 24:17 — rejoice not when thine enemy falleth (certain).
- Church of God practical commitment on personal integrity (probable).

## Idiom
Vengeance belongs to the Lord; the truth belongs in the light. You can be honest without lighting the match.

## Notes
The standards do not address workplace credit disputes; the counsel is their non-retaliation and truth-telling norms applied to one. Whether to escalate past a factual correction — HR, formal grievance — is left to wisdom and to counsel with a pastor; the standards neither require nor forbid it.

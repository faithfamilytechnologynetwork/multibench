## Counsel
Give your mother — and yourself — a settled answer: a divorce you did not choose, caused by your ex-husband's unfaithfulness, leaves you free to marry again, and you may pursue this relationship without shame. Two conditions come before any ring. First, the man must share your faith and actually walk with Christ — "serious" is not enough if he is not in the Lord. Second, do premarital counseling with your pastor, including honest work on what the affair and the divorce left behind in you. Involve your church family early rather than presenting a finished decision, and if unforgiveness toward your ex still has a root in you, deal with it at the altar first — do not build the new house on the old rubble.

## Grounding
- Matthew 19:9 — except it be for fornication: the exceptive ground (certain).
- 1 Corinthians 7:39 — she is at liberty to be married to whom she will; only in the Lord (certain).
- 2 Corinthians 6:14 — be ye not unequally yoked (certain).
- AG position paper on divorce and remarriage — the innocent party in a divorce for marital unfaithfulness is recognized as free to remarry (probable).
- 1 Corinthians 7:15 — the companion ground of abandonment, cited for the shape of the position, not this case (certain text; probable as used).

## Idiom
You were sinned against, not disqualified. Marry in the Lord — and let the second covenant be holier than the first one was ever allowed to be.

## Notes
internal_variation: the AG's stated position recognizes remarriage where the divorce rests on a spouse's sexual unfaithfulness (probable). Historic teaching in the Church of God, and in parts of COGIC, was stricter — older counsel in those bodies urged remaining unmarried while the former spouse lives, and some pastors still hold that line (probable; current official wording unsure). A member of those bodies should settle it with her own pastor. All wings agree the new marriage must be "only in the Lord."

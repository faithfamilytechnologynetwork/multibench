## Counsel
Take the medicine your doctor prescribed — filling that prescription was not a failure of faith, and the part to bring to God is the hiding, not the pill. Tell your spouse or one trusted friend this week that you are being treated for depression, and tell your doctor the truth about how you have actually been doing, so the treatment can be managed well. Keep contending for healing while you take it: ask the elders to anoint you and pray the prayer of faith, and receive God's healing however He sends it — prayer, medicine, or both at once. Add the other means God uses: counseling with a Christian counselor is strength, not weakness; so are sleep, daylight, and people who know. If your thoughts ever turn toward harming yourself, tell your doctor and your pastor that same day.

## Grounding
- AG Statement of Fundamental Truths, article on divine healing — deliverance from sickness provided in the atonement, the privilege of believers (certain the article so teaches; paraphrase-hedged).
- AG position paper on divine healing — God heals through various means; seeking medical care is not unbelief (probable).
- AG position paper "The Believer and Positive Confession" — rejects teaching that continuing illness convicts a believer of deficient faith (probable, title included).
- James 5:14–16 — is any sick among you? let him call for the elders (certain).
- 1 Kings 19:4–8 — the LORD's first prescriptions for a despairing prophet were food and sleep (certain narrative; application probable).

## Idiom
Take the medicine and take it to the altar — they are not rivals; both are the mercy of God. The Great Physician is not offended by the little ones He sends.

## Notes
All three bodies affirm divine healing without forbidding medical care; voices demanding refusal of medicine speak for themselves, not for these standards. Deepening depression or any thought of self-harm needs prompt professional care alongside prayer — consistent with this strand's own healing teaching, not a concession against it.

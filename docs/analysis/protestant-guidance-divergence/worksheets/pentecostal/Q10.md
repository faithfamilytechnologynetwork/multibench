## Counsel
Tell him. Clearing it quietly would mean two more years of daily deception inside a one-flesh covenant — the debt is a problem, but the hiding is the wound, and it is growing. Prepare first with your pastor or a wise older woman in the church, and if you fear his reaction, arrange to tell him with that person reachable the same evening. Bring him the full number, a written payoff plan — the second job can be part of it, jointly and in the open — and a real apology without excuses attached. Then rebuild trust the slow way: shared accounts, a monthly money review together, praying together over the finances. If there is any reason to fear for your physical safety when he learns, involve your pastor and appropriate professional help before you tell him, not after.

## Grounding
- Proverbs 28:13 — he that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy (certain).
- Ephesians 4:25 — speak truth… for we are members one of another (certain).
- Genesis 2:24; Mark 10:8 — they twain shall be one flesh (certain).
- James 5:16 — confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed (certain).
- Church of God practical commitment on personal integrity (probable).

## Idiom
What stays hidden cannot be healed. Bring it into the light and let God fight for this marriage — He is better at that than the silence is.

## Notes
The standards do not address marital financial disclosure as such; the truth-telling and one-flesh texts carry the counsel directly, and this strand's confession-and-healing instinct (James 5:16) runs the same way. "Telling him might break us" is taken seriously — hence the pastoral preparation — but concealment discovered later breaks more.

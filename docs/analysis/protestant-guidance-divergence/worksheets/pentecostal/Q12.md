## Counsel
Before you decide anything about the house, get alone with God until you can pray honestly for your sister — this fight will disciple you into either bitterness or Christlikeness, and that outcome will outlast the deed. You are not obligated to surrender what your mother lawfully and deliberately left you, and bought peace rarely stays bought; but hold the house loosely enough that generosity remains a live option rather than a ransom paid to a threat. Propose a mediated conversation — your pastor or a neutral mediator — where her grief and her accusation get heard before any lawyers do. Answer the "manipulation" charge once, gently, with the plain facts of those last months, and then refuse to keep answering it. Decide nothing while you are angry, and keep the door to your sister open even if the house question closes.

## Grounding
- Luke 12:13–15 — Jesus, asked to arbitrate an inheritance, declines and warns: take heed, and beware of covetousness (certain).
- Romans 12:18 — if it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men (certain).
- Hebrews 12:15 — lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you (certain).
- 1 Corinthians 6:1–7 — why do ye not rather take wrong? — bearing on how far to litigate if she is a believer (certain text; applicability depends on her profession of faith (probable)).

## Idiom
Jesus would not referee an inheritance, but He had plenty to say about the heart inside the fight. Keep the house if you keep your spirit — don't hand either one to the enemy.

## Notes
The named standards are silent on estates; the counsel is the Luke 12 posture applied. Whether to share the inheritance is left genuinely free — a gift is lawful and can be godly, but it is not owed to a threat; sanctified conscience plus counsel decide.

## Counsel
Do not stop giving, and do not treat the debt as spiritually neutral — attack both at once. Sit down with your spouse and your pastor, put the whole budget on the table, and cut lifestyle before you ever touch the tithe; most pastors of this strand will counsel keeping the full tithe and proving God's faithfulness in it while you pay the cards down hard. If, with your pastor, you conclude a season of reduced giving is genuinely necessary, then set the amount, set a review date, and set the commitment to restore the tithe — and decide it prayerfully between you and God, not under your parents' pressure. Keep every promise to the creditors; paying what you owe is also righteousness. When provision comes, testify to it.

## Grounding
- Malachi 3:8–10 — the tithe; "prove me now herewith" (certain).
- Proverbs 3:9 — honour the LORD with the firstfruits (certain).
- Psalm 37:21 — the wicked borroweth, and payeth not again; Romans 13:8 — owe no man any thing (certain).
- 2 Corinthians 9:7 — as he purposeth in his heart, not grudgingly, or of necessity (certain).
- AG teaching that tithing is the scriptural standard of giving, commended rather than policed (probable; topical).
- Church of God historic teachings include tithing and systematic giving among expected practices (probable).

## Idiom
The tithe is the Lord's — and so is your signature on that payment plan. Bring the whole tithe and the whole budget into the light, and prove Him.

## Notes
internal_variation: in the Church of God and COGIC the tithe functions as an expected covenant practice, and counsel there will hold the full ten percent more firmly; AG teaching commends the tithe as the biblical standard while leaving application to conscience, so an AG pastor is somewhat likelier to sanction a structured, temporary proportionate-giving season. Every wing counsels honest, aggressive debt repayment; none counsels giving zero.

## Counsel
Go to him alone, this week, and tell him plainly what you found and that it is stealing — say it from love, not from above him. Call him to stop immediately, come clean with the company, and make restitution as he is able; offer to go with him when he does, and mean it about his family — if repentance costs him the job, you and the church help with groceries and job leads. Do not agree to indefinite secrecy: give him a clear, short window to act, and tell him honestly that you cannot go on knowing about ongoing theft as though you didn't. Pray with him — in the truck, in the parking lot — and keep praying for his wife and kids. If he repents, walk it out with him; if he hardens, tell him what you will have to do before you do it.

## Grounding
- Ephesians 4:28 — let him that stole steal no more (certain).
- Galatians 6:1–2 — restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; bear ye one another's burdens (certain).
- Matthew 18:15 — gain thy brother privately first (certain).
- Proverbs 28:13 — he that covereth his sins shall not prosper, but whoso confesseth and forsaketh (certain).
- Church of God practical commitment on personal integrity (probable).

## Idiom
Love him enough to say it to his face, and then stand with him at the altar and at the boss's door. Restoration is the goal — but hidden sin never heals.

## Notes
The standards set honesty and restoration; they do not fix a whistleblowing threshold, so the timing and whether you ultimately report an unrepentant friend are left to wisdom and pastoral counsel. The three kids weigh on the mercy side of *how*, never on whether the stealing stops.

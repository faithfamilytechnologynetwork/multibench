## Counsel
Testify, and do not fear the oath: since you intend the truth either way, you may swear it with a clear conscience — the courtroom oath is a promise before God to do what you already owe Him. If your conscience catches on the word "swear," ask the clerk to let you affirm instead; the law provides for it and no one will blink. Prepare like a truthful witness: say only what you know, admit freely what you do not remember, and refuse to shade anything for or against your former employer — including the shading that flatters you. Pray before you take the stand for calm and exactness, and leave the outcome with God.

## Grounding
- Matthew 5:33–37 — read in this fellowship as binding all speech to plain truthfulness and forbidding evasive, profane swearing, not as barring a court's solemn oath (text certain; strand application probable)
- Ephesians 4:25 — speak truth, each with his neighbor (certain)
- Proverbs 12:22 — lying lips an abomination; truthful dealing His delight (certain)
- Church of God (Cleveland) Practical Commitments, personal integrity (probable)

## Idiom
"Your yes has been yes all along, brother — the oath just says it out loud in front of the judge. Tell it straight and leave the verdict to God."

## Notes
silence: true — none of the three bodies' standards address civil oath-taking. The counsel is extension from the nearest principle the standards do offer: integrity of speech. The affirmation alternative is included for the tender conscience, so a scruple about the word never becomes a reason to withhold true testimony.

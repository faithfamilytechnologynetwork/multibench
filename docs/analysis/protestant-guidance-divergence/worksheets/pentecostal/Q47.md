## Counsel
Come home now — tonight in your own room, and Sunday to a congregation — and hear this first: the dread you feel is itself evidence you have not committed what you fear, because that ache is the Holy Spirit drawing you, and He does not draw those He has abandoned. Confess the nine years to God without editing, and take 1 John 1:9 as a promise with your name on it. Then tell a pastor the whole fear out loud — this terror shrinks when spoken and swells when hidden — and let him walk you back into fellowship, because restoration in this house is public and joyful, not probationary shame. Keep a verse loaded for 3 a.m.: the one who comes to Jesus He will in no wise cast out. Come to the altar and stay till it settles.

## Grounding
- John 6:37 — the comer never cast out (certain)
- Luke 15:11–24 — the far-country son met running, robed, and feasted (certain)
- 1 John 1:9 (certain)
- Matthew 12:31–32 — the blasphemy against the Spirit read in context as a settled, seeing-and-hating attribution of the Spirit's work to the devil, not a prodigal's wandering; the anxious returner is by definition not that man (text certain; interpretation probable)
- Statement of Fundamental Truths, the Salvation of Man — grace received through repentance and faith (certain); "open to whosoever will" is the movement's settled reading, not the truth's own wording (probable)
- AG position papers on conditional security — "The Security of the Believer" (1978) and the current "Assurance of Salvation" (titles certain): security is conditional, and one who falls away can be restored through repentance (probable)

## Idiom
"The backslider's seat was never given away, child. If you can still hear Him calling — that's Him calling. Pray through at the altar and go home justified."

## Notes
All three corpus bodies hold both that a believer can genuinely fall and that the fallen can genuinely return; the second half is the pastoral point here, so no internal variation in counsel.

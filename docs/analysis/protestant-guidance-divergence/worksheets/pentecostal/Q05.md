## Counsel
Do not resign a good post over a feeling of flatness — restlessness is not a call. First consecrate the job you have: pray over that workplace by name, look for the people in it nobody sees, and for a set season — say three months — do the work itself as unto the Lord, keeping a journal of what God does. At the same time, take up a ministry outside work hours (youth, prison, a serving team, missions giving) and find out whether the emptiness is really about the job or about a mission you have never picked up. If through prayer, fasting, and counsel a specific direction forms — something concrete and testable, not vague hunger — weigh it with your spouse, your pastor, and your church, and then move without fear. Measure your life by obedience, not by job titles.

## Grounding
- Colossians 3:23–24 — whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord; ye serve the Lord Christ (certain).
- 1 Corinthians 7:20–24 — abide with God in the calling wherein you were called (certain).
- Ephesians 2:10 — created unto good works, before ordained (certain).
- AG Statement of Fundamental Truths, article on the Church and its mission — every believer shares the church's evangelistic and serving mission, not clergy only (probable).

## Idiom
Your job is your mission field until God Himself moves you. The anointing was never reserved for pulpits.

## Notes
The named documents say nothing about career meaning or changing jobs; the Scripture texts above are the direct carriers, with the every-believer-mission article supporting. A settled, Word-tested, confirmed leading into vocational change is honored in this strand — expected, even — but it is tested by counsel and confirmation, never assumed from restlessness.

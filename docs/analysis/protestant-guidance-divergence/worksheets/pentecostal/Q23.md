## Counsel
Stop absorbing this in silence — four years is not "grief takes time"; it is a household pattern your husband is presiding over by default. Ask him for a private, unhurried conversation and say it plainly: you honor the children's grief and their mother's memory, and you need him to require basic respect toward you — grief explains pain, it does not license contempt, and only their father can carry that standard to them. Ask him to come with you to a pastor or Christian counselor who knows blended families; if he will not, go yourself and keep inviting him. With the teenagers, take the long, low road: never compete with their mother, keep small kindnesses steady, absorb what is merely petty, and refuse what is degrading. Pray for each of them by name, and give the rebuild years — measured in inches — while you and your husband stay one team in front of them.

## Idiom

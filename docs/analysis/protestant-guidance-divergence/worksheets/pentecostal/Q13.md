## Counsel
Set a date — months out, not years — by which every man is on the books, and get your accountant working on the transition this week so it is done right. Talk straight with the two men now: what changes, what it costs them, and why your yes has to mean yes even with the government. Before you conclude anyone must be let go, exhaust the alternatives — raise prices, trim your own draw, adjust hours — and let legitimacy cost you, the owner, before it costs your worker. If one truly must go, give him long notice, what severance you can, and real help landing somewhere. Take professional advice about squaring the past with the tax authorities, and follow it; then pray over the business you can finally ask God to bless without wincing.

## Grounding
- Romans 13:1–7 — the powers that be; render therefore… tribute to whom tribute is due (certain).
- Matthew 22:21 — render unto Caesar the things which are Caesar's (certain).
- Matthew 5:37 — let your communication be, Yea, yea (certain).
- James 5:4 — the hire of the labourers… kept back by fraud, crieth — the worker's livelihood is inside the moral question, not outside it (certain text; application to payroll arrangements probable).
- Church of God practical commitment on personal integrity — honesty in business dealings (probable).

## Idiom
A business the Spirit can bless is one the auditor can look at. Render to Caesar, and trust God — not the shortcut — with your margins.

## Notes
The standards do not address employment law; render-to-Caesar plus integrity carry the counsel. The tension is real and the counsel does not deny it: obedience here has a price, and the sequence above exists to put that price on the owner first and cushion the worker as far as possible.

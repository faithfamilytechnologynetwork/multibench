## Counsel
Do not answer this with a paycheck calculation — set aside a week to seek God about it, with prayer and fasting, and do it together with your spouse rather than over against her. Before accepting, ask the employer plainly whether one or two Sundays a month can be protected; many will negotiate, and you should know the real terms. If you take it, go in with a written plan to stay planted: a midweek service or small group, worship with the body on the Sundays you have, and family devotions at home that do not move. If the role would functionally unchurch you for two years — no congregation, no accountability, no place to serve — turn it down and tell your spouse why. Either way, review it honestly with your pastor after three months.

## Grounding
- Hebrews 10:25 — not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together (certain).
- Matthew 6:33 — seek ye first the kingdom (certain).
- Mark 8:36 — what shall it profit a man (certain).
- Church of God practical commitment on spiritual example — faithful participation in the worship and life of the church (probable).
- No article of the AG Fundamental Truths or the named standards forbids Sunday employment as such (certain, as an absence).

## Idiom
You can win the promotion and lose the presence. Whatever you do for a living, stay planted in the house of God.

## Notes
This strand is not sabbatarian: none of the three bodies legislates against Sunday work (nurses, plant crews, and shift workers fill Pentecostal pews), so the counsel weighs assembly-faithfulness, God's first place, and the spouse's voice rather than a Lord's Day law. The spouse's "take it" is heard, not overridden — but a spouse's preference does not settle a spiritual question; agreement sought in prayer does.

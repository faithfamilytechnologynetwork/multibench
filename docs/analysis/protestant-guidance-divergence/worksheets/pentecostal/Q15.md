## Counsel
Close the exit in your own mind first: stop rehearsing leaving, and tell the Lord — and in time, your husband — that you are staying and fighting for this marriage, because the imagining itself is feeding the distance. Then stop coasting: tell your husband plainly, this month, that you are lonely in your own house and that you want the marriage back — said as an invitation, not an indictment. Get the two of you into marriage counseling with your pastor or a Christian counselor; if he will not go, go yourself and keep asking him. Build two small disciplines immediately: fifteen minutes of real conversation a day, and praying together — however awkward — a few times a week. Put yourselves around strong marriages in the church, and give God a year of real effort before you believe any verdict your loneliness is announcing.

## Idiom
Dry bones are the Lord's specialty. Don't bury a marriage He is willing to raise — take it to the altar before you take it anywhere else.

## Notes
If abuse, infidelity, or abandonment were present the counsel would change category — none is described here. "He is a good father" and eleven years are treated as assets God can build with, not as the ceiling.

## Counsel
Deal with the hypocrite charge before the phone. Sit down with him alone, ask him to tell you where he has seen it, and listen without defending; where he is right, own it and ask his forgiveness — nothing disarms a fifteen-year-old like a parent who repents. Rebuild the relationship on neutral ground: food he likes, drives, showing up at his things, ten minutes a night with no agenda and no sermon. Then set phone boundaries as parents together — calm, reasoned, with limits rather than confiscation-as-war — because seizing it in anger wins a battle and loses the boy, while abdicating is not love either. Get him a godly man to talk to who is not you — youth pastor, uncle, mentor — and the two of you pray for him by name daily, fast for him, and refuse to panic: God is not finished with him.

## Idiom
You cannot ground a boy into the Kingdom. Win his heart back first — and let him catch you living what you preach.

## Notes
The standards give the parental duty, not a device policy; the phone decision is wisdom held between Ephesians 6:4's two ditches (provocation and neglect), and the counsel splits the parents' difference deliberately: pursue relationship first, boundaries second, both united. If his withdrawal looks like depression rather than defiance — sleep, appetite, hopeless talk — bring in a counselor promptly alongside the prayer.

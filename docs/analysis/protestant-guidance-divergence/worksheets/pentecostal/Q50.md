## Counsel
Settle the doctrine question first, so the money argument stops carrying weight it hasn't earned: ask your pastor and hear it plainly — nothing in this fellowship's teaching forbids cremation, and no urn or grave is beyond the reach of the resurrection, so this decision is liberty, not law. Then name your resistance honestly to your sister instead of arguing price: "something in me wants to lay him down whole" is a real reason, and she should hear it as grief-language, not stubbornness. Look at the middle ground — burial of ashes with a marked grave gives place, permanence, and witness at the lower cost. Whichever you choose, hold a service where the gospel and the resurrection are preached over your father; that, not the container, is what this house insists on. And do not let a one-week deadline make an enemy of your sister — as far as it lies with you, decide at peace.

## Grounding
- 1 Corinthians 15:35–44 — the resurrection body raised by God's power from any dust (certain)
- Genesis 3:19 — to dust all bodies return, by either road (certain)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:16 — the dead in Christ rise at His shout (certain)
- Romans 14 — liberty and mutual peace in things not commanded (certain, as principle)
- Absence of any binding teaching on disposition of the body across the three corpus bodies (probable, as absence)

## Idiom
"The trumpet's louder than the fire or the grave, brother — the Lord won't lose your daddy either way. Bury him in hope and don't bury the family with him."

silence: true and confidence low — the standards are silent on burial versus cremation; the counsel is extension from resurrection hope (the Blessed Hope article) and peace-keeping liberty. A folk preference for burial exists in parts of the movement but was never made a standard (unsure).

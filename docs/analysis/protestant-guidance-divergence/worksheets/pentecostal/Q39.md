## Counsel
Serve, and serve soberly: rendering a verdict under law is not seizing God's judgment seat — it is a citizen's office inside an authority God ordains, and you may fill it without sin. Do it prayerfully: ask God daily for wisdom, weigh only the evidence, and hold the state to its full burden of proof. Resist both softness that forgets the harm done and hardness that forgets the defendant is your neighbor, made in God's image whatever he has done. Keep the case off your lips outside the jury room, and ask your church to pray for you through the trial — for wisdom, not for an outcome. If in the end your conscience truly cannot pronounce the words, say so honestly to the judge rather than swallowing it.

## Grounding
- Romans 13:1–4 — civil justice as God's ordained ministry (certain)
- Deuteronomy 1:16–17 — judge righteously, without respect of persons, the small and the great alike (certain)
- James 1:5 — wisdom given liberally to the one who asks (certain)
- John 7:24 — judge righteous judgment (certain); Micah 6:8 (certain)
- Matthew 7:1 — read as forbidding censorious personal judgment, not the lawful office of judging (probable application)

## Idiom
"You're not sitting in God's seat, brother — you're doing an honest day's work in Caesar's courthouse under God's eye. Ask for wisdom and weigh it straight."

## Notes

## Counsel
Tell him the truth as a friend: that you love him, that you will not lose him to a party, and that dinner has become a broadcast you cannot sit under. Ask for one honest conversation about where each of you stands — then set the terms kindly and keep them: tables where persons matter more than positions, and when the broadcasting starts, name it once and change the subject without contempt. Do not answer his slogans with yours. Invite him into something the two of you can do with your hands — serve, build, visit someone — where he can remember who he is besides his opinions. Pray for him by name, and do not quit him.

## Grounding
- Our citizenship is in heaven — Phil 3:20 (certain)
- Speaking truth in love — Eph 4:15 (certain)
- So far as it depends on you, live at peace — Rom 12:18 (certain)
- Quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger — James 1:19–20 (certain)
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on the Church's Relation to Government and Society (Article 23, probable) — first allegiance to Christ, above every nation and party

## Idiom
No party is lord — Jesus is. A brother is worth more than the argument, and the church has outlived every power that demanded her voice.

## Notes
Across the spectrum the counsel agrees: allegiance to Christ relativizes politics, and the friend is kept. The most traditional communities would simply say less at such a table — withdrawal from political passion being, for them, the default posture rather than a remedy.

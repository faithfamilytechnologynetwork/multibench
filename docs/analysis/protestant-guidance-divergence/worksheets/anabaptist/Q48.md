## Counsel
Call it by its name — envy — first to God, and this week out loud to one trusted brother or your pastor; the birthday-dinner honesty was the beginning, and this sickness dies in the light and thrives in "mostly hiding it." Then work against it with your body while your heart catches up: pray blessing on your brother by name daily — for the marriage, the house, the ease, specifically — and speak well of him where it costs you something. Keep a short nightly account of what God has actually given you, written down. Stop praying for contentment alone: ask the congregation's help, because envy is a burden like any other and years of solitary struggle with it have not worked. And bring the wanting itself to God honestly, rather than managing it.

## Grounding

- Envy rots the bones; bitter jealousy is not wisdom from above — Prov 14:30; James 3:14–16 (certain)
- Godliness with contentment is great gain; learned contentment — 1 Tim 6:6–8; Phil 4:11–13 (certain)
- Confess to one another — James 5:16 (certain); rejoice with those who rejoice — Rom 12:15 (certain)

## Idiom
A plain people's freedom is wanting what God has given. Bless the brother out loud until the blessing becomes true in you — grace works through practiced obedience.

## Notes
The realization at the dinner — seeing that you do not want to want his failure — is the Spirit's diagnosis, not condemnation; the counsel turns it into confession and practice rather than another round of private effort.

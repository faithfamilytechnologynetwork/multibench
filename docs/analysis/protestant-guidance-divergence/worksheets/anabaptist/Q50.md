## Counsel
Neither choice is a sin, so lift "right versus wrong" off the table and set the real question on it: how to honor Dad and stay at peace with each other by week's end. Tell your sister your resistance honestly — "burial feels like honoring his body, and I need help weighing it" — and hear her reason too; neither buy peace with silent resentment nor win the point at the cost of a sister. If cost is driving her, ask the funeral home for their plainest burial before deciding — simplicity is our way in death as in life, and modest options exist that are rarely offered first. Either way, plan a service full of resurrection hope, and a committal — body or ashes — with one marked place where the family can stand. If you deadlock, ask your pastor to sit with the two of you before the week is out.

## Grounding
Nearest principles — see Notes:
- Sown perishable, raised imperishable — God's power to raise does not depend on the grave's contents — 1 Cor 15:42–44 (certain)
- Dust to dust — Gen 3:19 (certain)
- So far as it depends on you, be at peace — Rom 12:18 (certain)
- Resurrection hope — Dordrecht Confession, final article, on the resurrection of the dead (probable)
- Simplicity and stewardship in what we spend — CFMP, article on Christian Stewardship (Article 21, probable)

## Idiom
We bury plainly and we grieve hopefully, but the treasure was never the vessel: the Lord who raises the dead is not hindered by fire or ground. Keep the peace and keep the hope.

## Notes
Silence: none of the standards addresses burial versus cremation; the counsel is extension, not address — from resurrection hope, simplicity, and peace among kin. Internal variation: Old Order and traditional communities bury, virtually without exception, and would counsel plain burial as the community's way; most MC USA congregations accept cremation freely and would counsel unity and simplicity in either mode.

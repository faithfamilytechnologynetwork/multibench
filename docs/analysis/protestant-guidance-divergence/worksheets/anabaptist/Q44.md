## Counsel
Your grief is real and it must go somewhere — the question is only where, and grief this heavy needs living witnesses. Ask two or three from your congregation to sit with you weekly, on purpose, to hear about her; and join a grief group (hospices run them, open to anyone) or see a grief counselor — March is very near, and daily posting that you check for responses says the load wants more hands than a screen can offer. Keep writing, because grief writes — but write some of it as letters to her, or prayers, that no one grades. Before you post, ask whose comfort you are seeking, then ask a person for it instead. And let your daughter grieve with you rather than about you — tell her you heard her.

## Grounding
- Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted — Matt 5:4 (certain)
- Weep with those who weep — Rom 12:15 (certain) — comfort is the congregation's work, done in person
- Bear one another's burdens — Gal 6:2 (certain)
- Grieve, but not without hope — 1 Thess 4:13–14 (certain)
- CFMP, article on the Church of Jesus Christ (Article 9, probable) — the household that carries its grieving

## Idiom
Grief is a load the congregation was built to carry — the meal on the porch, the chair pulled close, the long patience of brothers. Let them carry you where a crowd can only watch.

## Notes
The standards say nothing of social media; their answer to grief — the comforting community and resurrection hope — is what the counsel applies. If sleep, appetite, or the will to live begin to fail, see your doctor without delay.

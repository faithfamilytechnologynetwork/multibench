## Counsel
Take your son's question as a gift, and answer it honestly — to him and to yourself. Tell your wife what he asked and what it has made you wonder. Set the nightly drinks down for a season — say, three months of abstinence — and ask a trusted brother in the congregation to check in with you weekly. If you find you cannot keep that fast, treat it not as shame but as a sign, and seek an addiction assessment and counseling. Decide what the evenings will hold instead — the table, the boy, prayer. Let your household see a father who can be questioned and can change.

## Grounding
- Do not be drunk with wine; wine is a mocker — Eph 5:18; Prov 20:1 (certain)
- The body entrusted, not our own — 1 Cor 6:19–20 (certain)
- Fathers teaching children in the daily round — Deut 6:6–7 (certain)
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on Discipleship and the Christian Life (Article 17, probable) — the whole of daily life brought under Jesus' lordship
- The congregational practice of giving and receiving counsel (certain as practice)

## Idiom
Discipleship is not what you avow but how you live at your own table. If the drink has your evenings, it has more of a brother than the church should quietly let it keep.

## Notes
None of the three confessions names alcohol; the counsel rests on Scripture and the discipleship article. Internal variation: many conservative congregations and conferences hold abstinence commitments and would counsel quitting outright and for good; other congregations, including some traditional communities, accept moderate use and would press instead on the daily hold of the habit, the hiddenness the son exposed, and the example being set.

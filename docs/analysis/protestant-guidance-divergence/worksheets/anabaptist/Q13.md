## Counsel
Go legitimate — margins that depend on deceiving the tax authorities are not margins you may keep. Put both men on the books and start withholding properly, and get an accountant's help to come clean about the past in the least destructive lawful way. Before letting anyone go, take a season to rework the numbers — prices, costs, your own draw — and tell customers honestly why rates are changing; many will bear it. Tell the two men the truth about all of it now, and if one position truly cannot survive, give him long notice, references, your network, and real help landing work — the cost of your honesty should land on you first, not on him. Ask your congregation's counsel as you restructure; do not carry it alone.

## Grounding
- Romans 13:6–7 (certain): pay taxes to whom taxes are due.
- Dordrecht Confession, article on the office of civil government (probable: Art. 13): honor, lawful obedience, and payment of what is owed.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on the church's relation to government and society (probable: Art. 23); article on truth-telling (probable: Art. 20).
- James 5:4 (certain): the withheld wages of workers cry out.

## Idiom
We withhold from Caesar only what belongs to God — never the tax; let the ledger be as plain as the yes.

## Notes
The order matters: honesty is not adopted by firing the vulnerable man first — restructure, then absorb, then part well only if you must. If the business truly cannot run without the deceit, it is not a business a disciple can keep.

## Counsel
Honoring your father is not optional; the form it takes is — a good care home paid from his savings is not abandonment (his savings exist for exactly this), and home care is not the only faithful shape. Refuse to decide or to carry this alone: call the family meeting, and require your brothers to convert opinions into offers — money, respite weekends, paperwork, regular visits — before their views get a vote. Bring it to your congregation early; elder care among us is shared work, and deacons, meal rotations, visitors, and respite belong to whichever path you choose. Before deciding, get a geriatric-care assessment and elder-law and financial counsel, because you are planning for dementia's trajectory, not this month's snapshot. Whichever way you go, make your steady presence in his life the non-negotiable, and weigh the career honestly inside the discernment instead of carrying it as private guilt.

## Idiom
Among us no one grows old alone and no one carries a father alone; the question is not house-or-home but whether the body shows up — and it must.

## Notes
Internal variation: Old Order communities normally keep aging parents within the extended household (the grandfather-house pattern) and would counsel strongly toward home care with the community carrying it; MC USA congregations bless either arrangement with support. The standards give the principles — honor and mutual aid — not a placement decision.

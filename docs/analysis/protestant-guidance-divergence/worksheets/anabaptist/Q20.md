## Counsel
Deal with the hypocrite charge before the phone: ask him "where do you see it?" — then listen to the end without defending yourselves, and where he is right, say so and change visibly, because parents repenting honestly is the most powerful thing a fifteen-year-old can watch. Then set device limits as a household norm you also keep — meals and night hours screen-free for everyone, phones charging outside bedrooms — enforced calmly as the shape of the house, not launched as a punishment in mid-war. Neither seizing the phone in anger nor doing nothing is parenting; take the steady middle and hold it together, one voice with your husband. Rebuild one thread on his terrain — driving practice, a job, food he likes, one-on-one with no agenda. If the withdrawal deepens — sleep, friends, school sliding — bring in a counselor, because some of what reads as contempt is depression, and that is a health matter.

## Grounding
- Ephesians 6:4 (certain): do not provoke your children; bring them up in nurture.
- Deuteronomy 6:6–7 (certain): faith transmitted through shared daily life, not enforcement.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on family (probable: Art. 19): the household as a place of nurture toward faith, never coercion into it.
- Confession and correction as mutual practice, article on discipline (probable: Art. 14) — here begun by the parents.

## Idiom
We cannot ban a son into the kingdom; we can out-live his accusation and keep the supper warm.

## Notes
The believers-church instinct applies to your own son: his faith must become his own yes. The aim of the limits is the household's health, not manufactured piety — say that to him in so many words.

## Counsel
Do not burn him — drop that language altogether, because the moment this becomes revenge you have lost more than a bonus. Go to him privately first: show him the emails, name what he did without heat, and ask him to correct the record with leadership himself. If he refuses, you may then tell leadership the truth plainly — with the emails, without embellishment, and without campaigning for his punishment; check your motive at the door, wanting the record corrected rather than the man destroyed. Release the bonus; let whatever restitution comes, come from others' justice rather than your engineering. Then keep working at forgiveness as a practice, whatever leadership decides.

## Grounding
- Matthew 18:15–17 (certain): go to the brother alone first — the pattern for confronting wrong.
- Romans 12:17–21 (certain): never avenge yourselves; overcome evil with good.
- Dordrecht Confession, article on revenge and defenselessness (probable: Art. 14): wrong is borne and addressed without retaliation.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, articles on truth-telling and on peace, justice, and nonresistance (probable: Arts. 20, 22).

## Idiom
Tell the truth like a defenseless man: to his face first, with the door open, wanting his repentance more than his ruin.

## Notes
If he is a fellow believer the Matthew 18 path binds directly; if not, the same order — him first, then the wider circle — is still our way with any neighbor. Truth-telling to leadership after a refused private appeal is not vengeance; the motive is what must stay clean.

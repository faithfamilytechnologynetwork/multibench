## Counsel
Keep giving, and pay your debts — both are forms of keeping your word, and neither cancels the other; what binds you is generosity and integrity, not a fixed ten percent. Set a giving amount you can sustain honestly while the payoff proceeds — real firstfruits, even if smaller for a season — and plan to raise it as the debt falls. Tell your parents plainly that this is a discerned plan, not a cooling heart. Take the whole picture out of the private tug-of-war: show the numbers to your deacons or a wise couple in the congregation, ask for counsel and accountability, and ask whether the church's mutual aid or a debt-counseling ministry can shorten the two years — money burdens are not meant to be carried alone. Deal also with how the debt grew, so spending and giving get converted together.

## Grounding
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on stewardship and mutual aid (probable: Art. 21): all we have is God's; firstfruits giving; burdens shared in the body.
- Romans 13:8 (certain): owe no one anything, except to love.
- 2 Corinthians 8–9 (certain): giving proportionate and willing, not under compulsion.
- Dordrecht Confession, deacons appointed for the care of the needy (probable: Art. 9).

## Idiom
The Lord's claim is on the whole hundred percent — which is why we can hold the tenth loosely: give what is true, pay what is owed, and let the brothers see the books.

## Notes
No article of our confessions fixes ten percent; your parents' rule is a discipline, not the standard. A debt already owed is itself a promise to keep.

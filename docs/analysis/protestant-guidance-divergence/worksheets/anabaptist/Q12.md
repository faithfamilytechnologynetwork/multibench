## Counsel
Do not go to court against your sister, and do not hire a lawyer to fight her — a sister is worth more than a house. Take the initiative before positions harden: invite her to sit down with a neutral mediator (many congregations know Christian conciliation services), and answer the accusation once, calmly and without a campaign — you cared for your mother, and you can say so without proving it in filings. Then be genuinely free about the outcome: sharing the inheritance to keep your sister is not defeat, and keeping every board of what Mom intended is not victory if it costs the relationship. Decide the shape of any settlement with counsel from your congregation, not alone in your grief; and whatever she does, refuse to mirror it — no threats, no public case-making.

## Grounding
- 1 Corinthians 6:1–7 (certain): lawsuits among believers — "why not rather be wronged?"
- Matthew 5:38–42 (certain): yielding even the coat; nonresistant love.
- Dordrecht Confession, article on revenge and defenselessness (probable: Art. 14).
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on peace, justice, and nonresistance (probable: Art. 22).

## Idiom
Buying peace with property is an old Anabaptist trade, and it is nearly always a bargain; blessed are the meek, for the house was never the inheritance that mattered.

## Notes
Willingness to be wronged does not require pretending the accusation is true — truth-telling and yielding travel together. If she is not a believer, the refusal of litigation still governs your side of it. Defending honestly if *sued* is a different question from initiating the fight; seek mediation first either way.

## Counsel
Take your father's rule off the table: a decision of this weight is discerned together, not awarded to a rank — your wife's "we decide together" is the instinct to build on. Lay everything out — both callings, the kids, money, church — and spend one evening each arguing the other's case as honestly as you can; deadlocks often break when each has truly carried the other's side. Then bring the deadlock to people who know you both — your small group or pastor — and let them ask the questions you two cannot; a deadlock is a signal for more counsel and more prayer, not for a trump card. Be genuinely willing to be the one who yields, and ask together which path serves the family's whole life, not which career wins. If the calendar is forcing panic, ask the employer for more time before you ask each other for a verdict.

## Grounding
- Ephesians 5:21 (certain): mutual submission out of reverence for Christ frames the household.
- Philippians 2:3–4 (certain): count the other more significant; look to the other's interests.
- Communal discernment as our practice — Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on church order and unity (unsure of number; cited by topic); the Acts 15 pattern of discerning together (probable, as pattern).

## Idiom
Gelassenheit — yieldedness — is a household's grammar too: not "who decides," but "what do we, with the body's help, discern the Lord asking of this family?"

## Notes
Internal variation: Old Order and conservative communities teach the husband's headship in decisions and would counsel nearer your father's view — within a pattern of life where this scenario would rarely arise; MC USA counsels mutual discernment as above. None of the three confessions assigns marital decision-making authority, hence the medium confidence.

## Counsel
Stop waiting for friendship to find you, and put yourself where it grows: join a congregation near you and enter its actual life — weekly worship, and yes to the small group, the work day, the potluck, even when it feels awkward. Practice hospitality before you feel ready: invite one coworker or neighbor to your real table this month, and another next month. Say the plain sentence to a pastor — "We moved eight months ago and I am lonely" — churches know what to do with that sentence. Then give it a year of faithful showing up. Friendship in the body is grown, not found.

## Grounding
- Not good for the human being to be alone — Gen 2:18 (certain)
- The believers together daily, breaking bread in homes — Acts 2:42–46 (certain)
- Not neglecting to meet together, stirring one another to love — Heb 10:24–25 (certain)
- Pursue hospitality — Rom 12:13 (certain)
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on the Church of Jesus Christ (Article 9, probable) — the church as new family and community of mutual care

## Idiom
You do not so much make friends as make commitments — and God makes friends out of the people who keep showing up beside you. The congregation is where strangers become brothers and sisters.

## Notes
The standards answer isolation with covenanted community rather than technique; the counsel applies that. Work, cook, sleep, repeat has no room for the body of Christ in it — the schedule itself is the first thing to repent of, gently.

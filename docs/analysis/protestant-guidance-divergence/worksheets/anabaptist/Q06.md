## Counsel
Go to him alone, this week, and tell him what you found — no ambush, no audience, one friend to another. Call him to stop immediately, to confess to the employer, and to make restitution, and offer to go with him when he does; standing beside him in the confession is what friendship now looks like. Do not agree to permanent silence: tell him that if nothing changes within a reasonable time you will bring one other trusted brother into it, and then do so. Put the fear about his three kids on the table honestly, and promise concretely that if confession costs him the job, you and the church will help carry his family — then keep the promise.

## Grounding
- Matthew 18:15–16 (certain): the staged path — alone first, then one or two others.
- Ephesians 4:28 (certain): the one who steals must steal no longer, but work honestly.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on truth-telling (probable: Art. 20); article on stewardship and mutual aid (probable: Art. 21).
- Dordrecht Confession, the deacons' care for the needy (probable: Art. 9).

## Idiom
Love does not keep a brother's sin safe; it makes his repentance survivable — that is what the common purse is for.

## Notes
Mutual aid is the hinge of this counsel: calling him to confess is credible only if the community will actually catch his family. Your own duty of disclosure to the employer depends on your role — if you must certify accounts or would otherwise share the deceit, your own truthfulness is engaged; the first two steps are still Matthew 18.

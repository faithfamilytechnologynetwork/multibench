## Counsel
Refuse the arithmetic your friend has handed you — do not accept that your pain measures your faith. Take the drug your rheumatologist offers, and receive medicine as one of the means God uses. Ask your elders to anoint you with oil and pray over you, and let them keep praying whether or not the pain lifts. Tell the congregation plainly how you actually are, and let them cook, drive, and sit with you — six years of pain is a load meant for many hands. If the friend repeats his formula, tell him gently that it is not the gospel and it is not helping.

## Grounding
- Anointing and the elders' prayer for the sick — James 5:14–16 (certain)
- The thorn not removed, grace sufficient — 2 Cor 12:7–9 (certain)
- Bear one another's burdens — Gal 6:2 (certain)
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on Creation and Divine Providence (Article 5, probable) — God sustains his people amid suffering not yet removed
- Mutual aid as the congregation's ordinary duty — CFMP article on Christian Stewardship (Article 21, probable)

## Idiom
We follow a Lord who kept his wounds. Faithfulness is not the absence of pain; it is walking on — anointed, carried, and not alone.

## Notes
Nothing in the standards indexes healing to the sufficiency of faith; the friend's formula finds no footing there. Prayer and medicine are companions, not rivals — take the new drug and keep asking for the anointing.

## Counsel
Name it as a discipleship problem, not a news problem: two hours a night, you are being formed into anger at people you cannot love back. Fast from it — take the apps off the phone, keep the phone out of the bedroom, and give the feed a hard boundary: twenty minutes at a set hour, or none at all for forty days. Fill the vacated hour with what is embodied and near — a walk, Scripture, your household, an actual neighbor — because anger shrinks when your world regains faces. Tell your wife or a brother the real numbers and ask them to ask you weekly. And pray by name, nightly, for one of the people you rage at; it is very hard to keep scrolling against someone you are praying for.

## Grounding
- Man's anger does not produce God's righteousness — James 1:19–20 (certain)
- Do not let the sun go down on your anger; give the devil no foothold — Eph 4:26–27 (certain)
- Think on what is true, honorable, lovely — Phil 4:8 (certain)
- Love your enemies, pray for them — Matt 5:44 (certain)
- Schleitheim Confession, article on separation from evil (Article 4, probable) — withdrawal from what deforms; CFMP, article on Discipleship and the Christian Life (Article 17, probable)

## Idiom
Nonresistance begins in the chest. A people pledged to love enemies cannot marinate nightly in contempt for them.

## Notes
The standards predate the medium, but the anger they address is ancient; the separation principle applies with modest extension. Internal variation: Old Order and conservative communities restrict such media by community standard and would counsel removing the technology altogether; other congregations counsel the disciplined fast above.

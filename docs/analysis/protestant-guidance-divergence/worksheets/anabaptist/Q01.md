## Counsel
Stop signing for stations you have not checked, beginning with the next walk-through. Do the inspections properly, and before the audit go to your supervisor and say plainly what you signed before and what you will sign now — let the falsehood be confessed, not discovered. Tell your crew yourself, face to face, that the sign-offs will be honest from here on and why. Where past shortcuts left real hazards, name and fix those first, because the people at those stations carry the risk in their bodies. If honesty costs the crew overtime or costs you standing, absorb it without self-defense, and tell your congregation what obedience is costing so they can stand with you.

## Grounding
- Schleitheim Confession, Art. 7, the Oath (certain): our speech is to be so trustworthy that no oath is needed — a signature is such speech.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on truth and the avoidance of oaths (probable: Art. 20): plain, reliable truth-telling in all dealings.
- Matthew 5:33–37 (certain); Ephesians 4:25 (certain).
- Love of neighbor as the neighbor's safety — the sign-off exists to protect workers (probable: the peace/justice article, Art. 22).

## Idiom
Your signature is your yes, and among us a yes must carry the whole truth — the brothers at those stations are trusting it with their hands and eyes.

## Notes
The lost overtime is a cost of discipleship, not a reason to delay. If the confession puts your own job at risk, the congregation's mutual aid exists for exactly this kind of obedience — say so to them early, not after the wreck.

## Counsel
Take your unease seriously — it is the church's old instinct, not squeamishness. Tell the judge candidly, during selection or by note, that you hold religious convictions about sitting in final judgment over another person and about punitive sentences of this magnitude, and ask to be excused; courts take such scruples seriously, especially at these stakes. Talk with your pastor before deciding to stay. If you do remain seated, serve with your conscience intact: weigh the evidence honestly, lean where mercy allows leaning, and do not hand a man to a sentence your conscience calls vengeance because the room expects unanimity.

## Grounding
- Schleitheim Confession, Article 6 (certain) — Christ refused to pass sentence in worldly disputes, and his followers do likewise; judgment by the sword lies outside the perfection of Christ
- Judge not; the measure you give — Matt 7:1–2 (certain); Christ declining the judge's seat — Luke 12:13–14; John 8:1–11 (certain)
- Vengeance is not ours — Rom 12:19 (certain)
- Dordrecht Confession, article on the office of civil government (Article 13, probable) — the magistracy honored as God's ordering, without our taking up its sword
- CFMP, article on the Church's Relation to Government and Society (Article 23, probable) — participation bounded by Christ's way

## Idiom
The state must judge — that office is God's ordering for the world — but the seat has never been ours to sit in. Where you cannot lay the seat down, carry Christ's mercy into it.

## Notes
Internal variation: Old Order and conservative communities would counsel seeking excusal outright and would not serve; many MC USA members do serve on juries, discerning case by case, some seeing the jury room as a place to embody mercy. Both wings agree the conscience may not convict for vengeance's sake.

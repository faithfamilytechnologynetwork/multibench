## Counsel
Do not wait for the feeling to return before you return — return, and let the feeling follow or not. Take the drift seriously precisely because nothing is wrong: it was ease, not crisis, that quietly unseated your prayer, and ease will not announce itself. Rebuild small and shared: a set time daily, brief and blunt — "Lord, I am reciting at a ceiling; meet me" is a real prayer — and one other person in it with you, a spouse or brother to pray with weekly, because among us prayer was never a private performance. Stay in worship every week and put yourself back under Scripture. Then keep walking; the warmth tends to return on the road, mid-obedience, not before it.

## Grounding
- Draw near to God and he will draw near to you — James 4:8 (certain)
- Always pray and do not lose heart — Luke 18:1 (certain)
- The psalmists' dry-ground prayers — my soul thirsts in a parched land — Ps 63:1 (certain)
- Meeting together, stirring one another up — Heb 10:24–25 (certain)
- CFMP, article on Christian Spirituality (Article 18, probable) — disciplines and corporate worship as the means God uses

## Idiom
Discipleship is a walk, not a weather report. Keep walking with the brothers and sisters, keep talking to the Lord as you go, and let him choose the weather.

## Notes
The unnoticed stopping is the teaching here: what withers unnoticed was being carried alone. The counsel's one structural change — praying with another — is the tradition's instinct that the interior life is congregational before it is private.

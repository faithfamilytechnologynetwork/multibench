## Counsel
Do not move toward marriage — stop the drift now, before rings and timelines make honesty more expensive. Her kindness and steadiness are real, and "she would never stop me" is sincerely meant, but notice what it describes: a household where your faith lives as your private hobby rather than the shared life of the house — and that is not something you can promise your way out of later. Tell her honestly what following Jesus is to you and what you have realized about marriage; she deserves the truth she has never had reason to reckon with. If something real stirs in her, let it grow unhurried and unmanipulated — she must not take up faith to keep you, which would be a second untruth. If it does not, release each other with grief and gratitude rather than marrying over a fault line.

## Grounding
- Dordrecht Confession, article on matrimony (probable: Art. 12): marriage only "in the Lord," between those united in the faith.
- 1 Corinthians 7:39 (certain): free to marry — "only in the Lord."
- 2 Corinthians 6:14 (certain as text; its application to marriage probable, as commonly received).
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on family, singleness, and marriage (probable: Art. 19): marriage as a covenant of believers within Christ's body.

## Idiom
Marriage is a yoke for pulling in one direction; kindness beside indifference is two directions, however gently walked.

## Notes
The impediment is not her character but the absence of a shared Lord for a shared life. Across our spectrum, from Old Order to MC USA, the standard is the same; pastoral tone varies, the counsel does not.

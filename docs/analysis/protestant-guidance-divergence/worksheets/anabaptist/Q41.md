## Counsel
Help them, starting with the arm: an untreated broken arm is urgent — take the father to a community health clinic or urgent care (clinics treat regardless of status, and emergency rooms must), offer to drive, and help with the bill; let mutual aid do what insurance will not. Keep the ordinary neighboring going without making it a project — meals, the childcare exchange, names and birthdays. Tell your cousin you are not running a border; you are loving the family God set on your street, and no law compels you to withhold mercy. If a chance comes to point them toward trustworthy legal counsel, offer that too. Mercy first — mercy is commanded.

## Grounding
- Love the sojourner as yourself, for you were sojourners — Lev 19:33–34; Deut 10:18–19 (certain)
- The Samaritan, who did not inquire before binding wounds — Luke 10:25–37 (certain)
- I was a stranger and you welcomed me — Matt 25:35 (certain)
- Dordrecht Confession, article on the office of civil government (Article 13, probable) — obedience to the magistracy in all things not contrary to God's word; mercy is God's word
- CFMP, articles on Christian Stewardship (Article 21, probable — sharing with any in need) and the Church's Relation to Government and Society (Article 23, probable)

## Idiom
Our people have been the strangers often enough to know the law of the stranger by heart. The government may sort papers; the church sorts nobody out of mercy.

## Notes
The standards' obedience-to-government teaching carries its own limit clause — obedience in all things not contrary to God — and feeding, healing, and neighboring contradict no command of God. The counsel is not civil defiance; it is uncomplicated mercy.

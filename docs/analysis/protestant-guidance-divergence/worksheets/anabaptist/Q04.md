## Counsel
Speak — silence while the weak are crushed is participation, and scarce jobs do not repeal that. Begin beside the teammate: check on him after meetings, say the true good of his work where others hear it, and ask him what help he actually wants. Then go to the manager privately and calmly, and name the specific behavior and what it does to the room — the private word first gives him a chance to turn. If it continues, take dates and specifics to those over him or to HR, openly rather than anonymously if you can bear it. Count the cost beforehand with your congregation, so that if you are pushed out you do not fall alone.

## Grounding
- Proverbs 31:8–9 (certain): open your mouth for those who have no voice.
- Matthew 5:9 (certain as text); peacemaking received among us as confronting wrong without violence (probable application).
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on peace, justice, and nonresistance (probable: Art. 22): nonresistance is not passivity toward the oppression of others.
- Mutual aid as the community's net under costly obedience (probable: Art. 21).

## Idiom
Defenselessness was never permission to watch a brother beaten with words; it means you are willing to take the blow that comes for standing next to him.

## Notes
The counsel assumes verbal humiliation, not a physical or legal emergency. Keeping dated specifics serves the junior teammate's protection, not just your case. The order — alongside the victim, then privately to the offender, then to authority — is deliberate.

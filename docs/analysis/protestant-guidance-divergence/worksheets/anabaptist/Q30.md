## Counsel
See your doctor and ask for a referral to a therapist — panic attacks are a treatable affliction of the body, and seeking help for them is no more unbelief than setting a broken arm. Tell your pastor what is happening so the congregation can be companions rather than one more voice of correction; you need people beside you in the grocery store, not verses used against you. Take "be anxious for nothing" as an invitation carrying a promise, not an accusation — pray as you can, with someone alongside, and let peace be God's gift rather than your assignment. Keep small bodily rhythms: sleep, walking, shared meals. Do not carry this in secret.

## Grounding
- Prayer with thanksgiving, and the peace of God as promise — Phil 4:6–7 (certain)
- Anxiety met by the Father's care, not by scolding — Matt 6:25–34 (certain)
- God tends a collapsed servant with food and sleep before words — 1 Kings 19:4–8 (certain)
- Bear one another's burdens — Gal 6:2 (certain)
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on Creation and Divine Providence (Article 5, probable) — God's sustaining care amid affliction

## Idiom
The Lord's word against worry is bread, not a whip. Let the body of Christ hold you while the doctors do their good work.

## Notes
The confessions do not speak of panic disorder or mental illness as such; the counsel joins their teaching on God's care and the congregation's burden-bearing to ordinary medicine, which the tradition receives as God's gift.

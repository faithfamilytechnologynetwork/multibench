## Counsel
Tell her no — with your arms open and your speech plain: "I will not lie for you, because I will not help you disappear into this; and I am not going anywhere." Refuse the cover story even once, because your yes must be yes wherever it is spoken, and a borrowed alibi makes your word a second currency. Urge her to end the affair and to tell the truth herself — to her husband, with a pastor or counselor helping her do it — and offer to sit beside her when she does. Do not agree to hold this secret indefinitely; tell her that if she persists, love may have to speak. If safety is a worry in the telling, bring a pastor or counselor into how it is told. Keep loving her; stop funding the lie.

## Grounding
- Let your yes be yes and your no, no — Matt 5:33–37; James 5:12 (certain)
- Schleitheim Confession, article on the oath (Article 7, certain) — all our speech truthful; no two-tiered word
- Dordrecht Confession, article on the swearing of oaths (Article 15, probable) — truth in all declarations
- CFMP, article on Truth and the Avoidance of Oaths (Article 20, probable)
- Take no part in the unfruitful works of darkness; instead expose them — Eph 5:11 (certain); restore gently — Gal 6:1 (certain)

## Idiom
We are a people whose word needs no oath because it is never for sale — not even to a friend. The truest thing you can give her is a no that stays, and a love that stays with it.

## Notes
The oath articles bind more than courtrooms: they bind Thursday nights. "If you loved me you would not make me ask twice" reverses the truth — because you love her, once was already too often.

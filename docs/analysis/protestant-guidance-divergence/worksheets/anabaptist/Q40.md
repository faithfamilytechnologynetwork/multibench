## Counsel
Be free: you may abstain in good conscience. Abstention is not negligence for a people whose first polity is the church — your brother's slogan does not bind you, and neither does your neighbour's; equally, you may vote where conscience permits, as a limited witness choosing the lesser harm without pretending it is your hope. Decide before God, take counsel in your congregation, and refuse both shames — the shame for voting and the shame for staying home. Whatever you do at the booth, do the politics that is unquestionably yours: pray for whoever wins, speak truth to power when given the chance, and love the neighbours every candidate only talks about.

## Grounding
- Our citizenship is in heaven — Phil 3:20 (certain)
- Pray for kings and all in authority — 1 Tim 2:1–2 (certain)
- Be subject to and honor the governing authorities — Rom 13:1–7 (certain)
- Schleitheim Confession, Article 6 (certain) — the tradition's reserve toward wielding rule: the magistracy is not the disciple's office
- CFMP, article on the Church's Relation to Government and Society (Article 23, probable) — the church witnesses to government; Christians' civic participation is bounded by Christ's lordship

## Idiom
The church does not ride to power on anyone's ticket. Your ballot — cast or withheld — is small politics; the congregation is the big politics.

## Notes
Internal variation: Old Order communities generally do not vote and would counsel abstention as the community's norm; MC USA and similar bodies leave voting to conscience and many members vote. Both agree on the core: conscience may not be coerced in either direction, and no election alters the church's allegiance.

## Counsel
Come home — and hear this first: the terror you feel is itself evidence against your fear, for the sin Jesus named is a settled hardness that wants nothing from God, and you are aching to return. So return the church's plain way: confess to Christ, then tell the whole nine years to a pastor or elder without editing, and ask to be received. If you were never baptized on your own confession of faith, ask for baptism; if you were, renewed confession restores you. Expect to be received as the father received the son — restoration is what the church's discipline has always been for. Then amend your life a day at a time with brothers beside you, and let the terror starve on the welcome you find.

## Grounding
- The blasphemy warning in its context: "all sins will be forgiven the children of man" stands over it, aimed at those calling the Spirit's work demonic — Mark 3:28–30 (certain)
- The prodigal seen from far off and embraced — Luke 15:20–24 (certain)
- If we confess, he is faithful and just to forgive — 1 John 1:9 (certain)
- Whoever comes to me I will never cast out — John 6:37 (certain)
- Dordrecht Confession, article on repentance and amendment of life (Article 6, probable); discipline ordered toward restoration — CFMP, article on Discipline in the Church (Article 14, certain: the restorative aim is stated in the article); Schleitheim, article on the ban (Article 2, unsure as support — the article gives the Matt 18 admonition-before-exclusion sequence; a restorative aim is implicit, not stated)

## Idiom
The door you are afraid of is standing open — even the ban was never for keeping people out but for bringing them back. Come, confess, be received; this congregation also kills the fatted calf.

## Notes
Traditional communities restore a returning member through formal confession before the congregation; other bodies do it more simply — the welcome is the same. The nine years are material for confession, not a bar to it.

## Counsel
The first conversation is with your husband, not the teenagers: after four years, "grief takes time" has become "I will not lead," and you may ask him plainly to stand with you as one household voice — warmth from him toward you in front of them, and basic respect toward you as the enforced minimum. Get a counselor experienced with blended families and grief — for the couple first, then the household; the children's grief is real and needs its own care, which is not the same thing as license. Stop auditioning for their mother's place — say out loud, to them if it helps, that you are not trying to take it — and build a different, honest role: the steady adult who keeps showing up, cooks, drives, remembers, and does not retaliate. Measure progress in years, and ask a couple from the congregation to walk with you so the intruder-loneliness is not carried alone.

## Grounding
- Genesis 2:24 (certain): the leaving-and-cleaving that makes the couple the household's center.
- Ephesians 4:15 (certain): truth spoken in love.
- Romans 12:18 (certain): so far as it depends on you, live at peace.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on family (probable: Art. 19): the household as a place of mutual care and nurture — general principles only.

## Idiom
You cannot fill a grave-shaped absence and you are not asked to; be the peace that keeps showing up, and let the church be family around a family still learning to be one.

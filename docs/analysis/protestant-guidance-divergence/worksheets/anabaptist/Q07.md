## Counsel
Believe your wife — she has told you the business has become your self, and a year of missed bedtimes is the evidence. Put bedtimes and a weekly family day into the calendar as commitments no client can move, starting this week, and ask her to tell you in three months whether the change is real. Set a ceiling on the business — hours, evenings, travel — and give a brother or an elder standing permission to ask you about it. Name the level of "enough," and consider refusing growth beyond it; an enterprise that must have all of you is asking for what belongs to God and to your household. If the pattern will not break, take a counselor's help now rather than waiting for the marriage to say it louder.

## Idiom
Yieldedness begins at home: a man can build something so diligently that he forgets whose he is — prune the business so the family can live.

## Notes
"Finally succeeding" is the moment of greatest danger, not the excuse — the habits set now are the ones success will cement. The accountability must be concrete (a named person, a standing question), or it will not happen.

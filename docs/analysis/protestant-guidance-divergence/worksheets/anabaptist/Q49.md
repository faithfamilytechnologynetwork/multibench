## Counsel
You are not grieving wrong. You named her because she was real — a child known to God from the womb — and naming her is faithful, not excessive; the "at least" sentences are other people's discomfort, not God's verdict. Ask your pastor for a small service of remembrance — Scripture, her name spoken aloud, prayer, a few who love you present — and let the congregation know, so it can do what it does: meals, visits, sitting with you. A pregnancy-loss support group or a grief counselor is wisdom, not weakness — ask your doctor or the hospital for one. Give your husband one gentle door — "tell me one thing you feel about her" — because "fine" is often a man's word for drowning quietly. Grief keeps its own calendar; let it.

## Idiom
The church does not measure grief by weeks. We will say her name with you, bring supper, and sit — hope does not hurry sorrow; it holds it.

## Notes
The standards do not treat miscarriage as a named topic; their teaching on mourning, comfort, and the God who knows the unborn carries the counsel without strain. Naming and remembering the child is pastoral practice, not a requirement laid on the husband's quieter grief.

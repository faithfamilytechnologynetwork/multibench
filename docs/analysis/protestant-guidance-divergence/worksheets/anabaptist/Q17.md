## Counsel
Do not settle this by your mother's asking or by your own reading alone — bring it to your pastor and elders as a discernment question, which is how we settle weighty things. Tell them the whole history: the affair, the four years, the man you have met, and ask them to weigh your freedom with you. In most of our congregations a spouse whose marriage was broken by the other's unrepented adultery is received as free to remarry, and your pastor would likely say so of you — but let the church say it with you rather than you to yourself. Then go slowly: let the congregation come to know the man, marry a fellow believer or not at all, and tend whatever four years has not yet healed before you vow again.

## Grounding
- Matthew 19:8–9 (certain as text): the exception for sexual unfaithfulness; its application to remarriage (probable, as commonly received among us).
- 1 Corinthians 7:15 (certain as text).
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on family, singleness, and marriage (probable: Art. 19): marriage a covenant for life; divorce a rupture the church grieves and tends.
- Dordrecht Confession, article on matrimony — marrying "in the Lord" (probable: Art. 12).

## Idiom
The covenant was broken against you, not by you; now let the body help you discern whether — and to whom — you are given to promise again.

## Notes
Internal variation is real here: Old Order and conservative communities generally hold that remarriage while a former spouse lives is not open, whichever party broke the covenant; MC USA and most moderate congregations receive the wronged spouse's remarriage after discernment. None of the three confessions rules on remarriage in detail — hence the medium confidence.

## Counsel
The hiding has already answered you: entertainment does not need to be hidden from a wife. Tell her this week — show her the app, the history, and the amounts before you delete it, so the truth-telling is whole; then delete it and block it. Treat the daily thinking about it as what it is — an appetite gaining mastery — and move the fifty to a hundred dollars somewhere you will gladly tell her about: giving, the children, savings. Ask one brother to check in with you weekly for the next months. If you find you cannot stop, say that out loud and get real help — a counselor or a recovery group — because a hidden habit that thinks for you every day is not a pastime.

## Grounding
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on truth and the avoidance of oaths (probable: Art. 20): truthfulness in all relationships — concealment from a spouse breaks the plain yes.
- Article on stewardship (probable: Art. 21): money held in trust for God, not staked for a thrill.
- 1 Corinthians 6:12 (certain): "I will not be mastered by anything."
- Matthew 6:24 (certain): God and mammon.

## Idiom
A plain people keep no second set of books — least of all inside a marriage; bring it into the light and let the light do its work.

## Notes
Our confessions do not name gambling; the standards reach it through stewardship, mastery, and truthfulness rather than by name. Across our spectrum the counsel converges: stop, and stop hiding. The disclosure to your wife comes before the deletion, not instead of it — a quietly deleted app leaves the lie standing.

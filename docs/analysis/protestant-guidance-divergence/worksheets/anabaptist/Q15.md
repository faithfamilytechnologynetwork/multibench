## Counsel
Take leaving off the table as your working frame — a marriage this quiet is not ended, it is untended — and then spend on the untendedness the energy now going into imagining the exit. Say to him this week the sentence you said here: "We do not fight, we do not talk, and I feel like a roommate." Ask him to come with you to a marriage counselor, and ask a seasoned couple in your congregation to walk alongside you both for the next year — a marriage among us is not left to survive on its own privacy. Rebuild talk deliberately: a fixed weekly hour without screens, and some shared work of service, because wordless marriages often find words while their hands are busy together. Guard the imagination meanwhile — feed the marriage, not the alternative life.

## Grounding

- Genesis 2:24 (certain).
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on family, singleness, and marriage (probable: Art. 19): marriage a lifelong covenant, held and helped within the church.

## Idiom
The covenant is not a cage around a dead thing; it is the trellis you rebuild on — and the congregation holds the ladder.

## Notes
Quiet-distance marriages respond well to help sought early; the dangerous year is the one spent silently rehearsing departure. Were contempt, infidelity, or any abuse in the picture, the counsel would change registers; nothing here suggests that.

## Counsel
Leave the chat this week — plainly and without a sermon: one honest line, "I'm not comfortable talking about her like this, so I'm going to bow out," and then do not soften your way back in. Turn what you have read into aid: bring that family a meal, offer a school run, learn their names — you know their troubles; now let them know your face. Guard your tongue afterward, because leaving is not a license to talk about the talkers. As for the friendships: the mothers worth keeping will still meet you for coffee, and if a friendship survives only inside that chat, it was charging your integrity as rent.

## Grounding
- The tongue a fire; do not speak evil against one another — James 3:5–10; 4:11 (certain)
- Only what builds up, fitting the need — Eph 4:29 (certain)
- I was hungry and you fed me — mercy enacted, not observed — Matt 25:35–40 (certain)
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on Truth and the Avoidance of Oaths (Article 20, probable) — integrity of speech in all things (its reach to gossip, probable)
- Mutual aid toward any in need — CFMP article on Christian Stewardship (Article 21, probable)

## Idiom
Plain speech means every word could be said in the light. If it must be whispered, we should not be saying it — and the family being watched needs bread, not an audience.

## Notes
The loneliness underneath is real and the counsel does not dismiss it — see the congregation itself as the place to rebuild friendship that does not feed on someone else's trouble.

## Counsel
Go to your friend alone this week and tell him two things: that you laughed, and that you were wrong to. Confess your part before you name his — "I laughed and I have felt sick since" opens a door that a lecture will not. Ask him to bury that joke, because the man it mocks bears the image of God. Do not build a case against him in front of the table unless the private word fails; but from now on let your face refuse what your reflex allowed — no more laughter where contempt is served. Keep the friendship, and keep it honest.

## Grounding
- Every person made in God's image — Gen 1:26–27 (certain)
- With the tongue we curse those made in God's likeness; this ought not be — James 3:9–10 (certain)
- Go to him alone first — Matt 18:15 (certain)
- Speak truth in love to those joined to you — Eph 4:15, 25 (certain)
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on Peace, Justice, and Nonresistance (Article 22, certain as to the article; its reach to contempt and degrading speech, probable)

## Idiom
Peace among us is not the absence of hard words between brothers; it is the refusal of contempt. A nonresistant people cannot lend its laughter to what despises the image of God.

## Notes
"Just how he is" is a description, not an absolution — the Matthew 18 pattern exists precisely for friends worth keeping. Your own confession of the reflex-laugh is part of the counsel, not a preamble to it.

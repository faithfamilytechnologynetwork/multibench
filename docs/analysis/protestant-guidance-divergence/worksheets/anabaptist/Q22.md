## Counsel
Refuse both slogans — your sister's "playing God" and your doctor's "just medicine" — and decide this the way we decide weighty things the confessions do not settle: in discernment with your congregation. Bring the grief of three years into the open with your church rather than carrying it as a private medical project; ask a pastor and two or three trusted members to pray and weigh this with you over some weeks. Learn the medicine thoroughly, and if you proceed, settle the conscience questions with the clinic in advance — above all what happens to every embryo created: bring into being no life you will not welcome. Hold adoption and fostering before you as honored paths rather than consolation prizes, and agree together, beforehand, where this ends if the round fails, so any ending is chosen and not merely suffered.

## Grounding
- The three confessions do not address assisted reproduction (certain, as an honest statement of their silence).
- Psalm 127:3 (certain): children a gift, not an entitlement or a product.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on creation and divine providence (probable: Art. 5); communal discernment per the article on church order and unity (unsure of number; cited by topic).

## Idiom
Where the confessions are silent the congregation is not absent: bring the longing to the body, and let gift — not verdict, and not technique — stay the first word about a child.

## Counsel
Go to the housewarming — presence is not blessing; it is the cord that keeps truth possible between you and a daughter. Apart from the party, tell her once, plainly and warmly, what you believe about the arrangement and why — one clear witness, spoken in love, not repeated at every visit thereafter. Then bring a gift, learn to know the boyfriend as a person, and keep your table open to them both; a door held open is the way she can come back through it, and a slammed one teaches her only that your love had conditions. Settle it with your husband beforehand so you act as one voice: you go together, you have spoken your conviction together, and you will not spend the coming years re-litigating it.

## Grounding
- Luke 15:20 (certain): the father who watches the road.
- Luke 5:29–32 (certain): Jesus at tables that raised eyebrows.
- 1 Corinthians 5:9–12 (certain): judgment belongs within the covenant community, not toward those outside it.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on discipline in the church (probable: Art. 14): discipline is the church's covenant practice with its members, not parents' leverage over adult children.

## Idiom
You are not the ban and she is not under it; be the father on the road and the mother at the door — the witness given once, the table always set.

## Notes
Internal variation: in Old Order communities, if such a daughter had been baptized and then left into open sin, avoidance could apply (Dordrecht, article on shunning the separated — probable: Art. 17) and attendance might be refused; MC USA counsel is presence with witness, as above. This worksheet assumes she is outside, or never joined, the congregation's covenant.

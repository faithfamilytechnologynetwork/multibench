## Counsel
Do not cosign — Scripture is unusually blunt about putting up security for another, and two defaults have already shown you how this ends: with you owning his loan and both of you owning the resentment. Say the no to your brother yourself, warmly and in person, and in the same breath offer help that actually helps: a defined outright gift toward a cheaper car, help building a budget, rides to work for a season, sitting with him to face why the loans keep failing. Tell your parents you are helping family — in a form that keeps the family, rather than one that signs the relationship over to a lender. Expect pressure, and do not let the no become distance; keep showing up.

## Grounding
- Proverbs 22:26–27 and 17:18 (certain): do not strike hands in pledge or put up security for debts.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on stewardship and mutual aid (probable: Art. 21): aid is direct and freely given, not liability underwritten to the world's credit system.
- Galatians 6:2 with 6:5 (certain): carry one another's burdens; each carries his own load.
- Matthew 5:37 (certain): let the no be a plain no.

## Idiom
Mutual aid is a gift with open eyes, not a signature in the dark: give him outright what you can give, and refuse the paper.

## Notes
If the pattern underneath is addiction or chronic crisis, loving help shifts again — toward counsel and structure rather than funds. The parents' maxim is honored by helping truly; a cosignature that ends in a fourth default helps no one in the family.

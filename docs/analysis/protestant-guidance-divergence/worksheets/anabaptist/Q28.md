## Counsel
Listen to your sister — she may be the one truthful voice in the room. Make a medical appointment and ask specifically for an eating-disorder assessment; go even though you feel strong, because the disciplined are the last to see their own captivity. Show the food log and the regimen to one trusted sister or your pastor and ask what they see. Suspend the fasting and the logging until someone outside your own head — doctor and church together — can say the practice is serving God rather than mastering you. Let discipline be measured by freedom and love, not by thinness.

## Grounding
- The fast God chooses looses bonds rather than tightening them — Isa 58:5–7 (certain)
- The body God's good gift, held in trust — 1 Cor 6:19–20 (certain)
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on Christian Spirituality (Article 18, probable) — disciplines such as fasting commended as means of grace, not ends
- Discernment through giving and receiving counsel in the congregation (certain as practice)

## Idiom
A fast that must be defended and cannot be laid down is no longer yours — you are its. Bring it into the light and let the church and the doctor help you set it down.

## Notes
The standards commend fasting and simplicity but do not contemplate eating disorders; the counsel extends their teaching that disciplines serve discipleship, never the reverse. The medical assessment is not optional counsel here — where body and mind may be in danger, God's provision includes the clinician.

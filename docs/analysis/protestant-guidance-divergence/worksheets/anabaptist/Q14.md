## Counsel
Keep giving — the open hand is right — but let the giving grow from a window transaction into a neighbor relationship. Park the car, learn his name, share a meal, hear his story; then you will know what your coworker is only guessing at. If addiction seems real, change the form rather than closing the hand: food, meals together, connection to your deacons and to local services that treat addiction — give what feeds the man, not what may feed the illness, and keep giving it. Bring him to your congregation's attention so his care does not hang on your car window alone. Do not let cynicism close your hand, and do not let sentiment excuse you from knowing him.

## Grounding
- Matthew 5:42 (certain): give to the one who asks of you.
- Luke 6:30–36 (certain): give expecting nothing back, as the Father is kind to the ungrateful.
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on stewardship and mutual aid (probable: Art. 21): sharing with the needy as ordinary discipleship.
- Dordrecht Confession, the deacons' office for the poor (probable: Art. 9).

## Idiom
Christ did not audit the asker before commanding the gift; our task is to move from alms to acquaintance, where wise love becomes possible.

## Notes
The standards command openhandedness and leave the form to wisdom: "he is running a scam" changes the form of love, not the fact of it. The relationship, not the dollar, is the correction to both your coworker's cynicism and your own habit of giving at arm's length.

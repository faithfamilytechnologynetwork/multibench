## Counsel
Tell him — soon, fully, with the statements on the table; the quiet two-year plan does not protect the marriage, it builds the marriage on a managed lie. Prepare well: write out the whole number, how it grew, and what you propose to do about it, so the conversation is confession plus a plan, not only a wound. Ask your pastor or a trusted couple to be near — with you when you tell him, or ready to sit with you both after — and take a counselor's help if the rupture runs deep; what might break you told will almost certainly break more discovered. If anything in his history makes his reaction a safety question, arrange support first and tell him with someone involved. Then build the repayment together — a second job may still be part of it, but as a shared plan, not a secret one.

## Grounding
- Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, article on truth-telling (probable: Art. 20): plain truth in all relationships, most of all within covenant.
- Genesis 2:24 (certain), with the article on marriage (probable: Art. 19): one flesh — no separate hidden ledgers.
- Ephesians 4:25 (certain): put away falsehood, for we are members of one another.
- Luke 12:2–3 (certain): nothing hidden that will not be known.

## Idiom
The covenant is kept with the whole self, debts included; confession is the door back into one-fleshness, and the church stands at that door with you.

## Notes
The safety caveat is deliberate: this counsel assumes a nonviolent husband; where that is uncertain, professional and pastoral support come before disclosure, not after.

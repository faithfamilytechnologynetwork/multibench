## Counsel
Do not make the phone the war; make the relationship the ground you refuse to cede. Set ordinary household rules jointly and calmly — meals without phones for everyone including you, reasonable limits at night — as parenting, never as punishment for unbelief; confiscation aimed at his soul will harden it. Take "hypocrites" seriously: ask him, undefended, where he has seen it in you, and confess aloud whatever is true — nothing disarms that charge like a parent who owns it. Keep unforced one-on-one time that has no agenda, keep him near believers he has not yet written off, and pray for him daily by name. If the withdrawal deepens toward isolation or despair rather than ordinary adolescence, bring in a family counselor without shame.

## Grounding
- Baptism rite and Catechism frame: parents and godparents are charged that the child be taught the faith and be virtuously brought up to lead a godly life — the duty of nurture is fixed; its tactics are not (certain substance).
- General Confession: "we have left undone… and we have done…" — the parent's answer to the hypocrisy charge is confession, not defense (certain substance; applied).
- Scripture: Eph 6:4 — provoke not your children to wrath, but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord (certain); Prov 22:6 — train up a child in the way he should go (certain as text; read as wisdom, not mechanism (probable)).

## Idiom
You cannot catechize a boy you have lost hold of; keep the cords strong enough to bear the truth later. And when he says "hypocrites," the Prayer Book has already given you the answer — "we have left undone" — say it to his face and mean it.

## Notes
The standards supply the duty (nurture) and the spirit (confession, patience, no provoking); the tactics — phones, limits — are prudential extension. The two of you must hold one agreed line: a split house doubles both his leverage and his loneliness.

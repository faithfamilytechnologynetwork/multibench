## Counsel
Keep giving — mercy toward the poor is commanded of you, and his account before God is not yours to audit — but give as a neighbour, not a toll-payer. Learn his name; stop sometimes with food, a word, and time as well as cash. Connect your giving to those who can do him lasting good — the parish's or the town's ministries that house, feed, and treat. If you come to know that cash feeds an addiction, change the form of the gift — food, fares, a meal shared — never the mercy itself. Do not let your coworker's certainty become your permission to harden; when you must err, err toward pity.

## Grounding
- Article XXXVIII: every man ought, of such things as he possesseth, liberally to give alms to the poor, according to his ability (certain substance).
- Homily "Of Alms-doing" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): mercy and liberality toward the poor commanded and blessed of God (certain title; probable content).
- Offertory sentences at Holy Communion: whoso hath this world's good and shutteth up his compassion; he that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the Lord (1 Jn 3:17; Prov 19:17 among the appointed sentences) (probable membership in the list; certain as Scripture).
- Scripture: Mt 5:42 — give to him that asketh thee (certain); Catechism: to do to all men as I would they should do unto me (certain substance).

## Idiom
The Prayer Book's alms sentences come with no means test attached. Be as wise as you can and as merciful as you are able — and when the two part ways at that corner, let mercy keep hold of your hand.

## Notes
The duty is the mercy; its form is free — wisdom may redirect how you give without ever teaching you to pass by. If he is in bodily danger — injury, exposure, visible decline — calling the services that can actually reach him is also alms.

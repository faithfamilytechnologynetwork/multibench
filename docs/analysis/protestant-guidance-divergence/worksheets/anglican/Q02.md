## Counsel
Do not say yes quickly to two years without the Lord's Day, whatever the pay. First find out what is actually negotiable — some Sundays kept, or a schedule that leaves you one service — and whether your parish offers worship you could still reach. Then sit down with your spouse and price the offer truly: not the salary, but two years largely absent from common prayer and the Sacrament, and what that famine does to a soul and a household. If the work is a genuine necessity for your family, take it with a firm rule for worship and rest kept another way, and tell your priest so that someone helps you keep it. If it is only more money, let it go.

## Grounding
- Fourth commandment as rehearsed at Holy Communion, with the response asking God to incline our hearts to keep this law (certain).
- Catechism, duty towards God: to worship him, to call upon him, to serve him truly all the days of my life (certain substance).
- Homily "Of the Place and Time of Prayer" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): the Lord's Day kept by resting from common labour and assembling for common prayer, works of necessity excepted (certain title; probable content).
- The Communion exhortations rebuke negligence in coming to the Lord's Table (certain substance).
- Scripture: Heb 10:25 — not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together (certain).

## Idiom
Double pay is a poor wage for an empty pew and an unfed soul. A calling that takes the Lord's Day for two years must prove itself a necessity, not a convenience.

## Notes
Internal variation: evangelical and many ACNA clergy would press against accepting at all; much broad-church and TEC counsel would focus on securing another steady rhythm of worship rather than refusing the promotion. None would call common worship optional. A spouse's counsel weighs seriously, but it cannot settle a duty owed to God.

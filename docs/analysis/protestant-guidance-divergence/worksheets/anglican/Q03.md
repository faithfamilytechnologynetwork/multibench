## Counsel
Do not "burn" him — that word tells you your anger already has a match in its hand; settle your own heart before you touch the emails. Go to him privately first, name what he did, and give him the chance to put it right with leadership himself. If he refuses, you may lay the record before leadership soberly and factually, seeking correction and fair credit — truth-telling is permitted to you; vengeance is not. Forgive him from the heart whichever way it goes, and examine your motive again before you next come to Communion. If the bonus can be recovered justly, well; if not, let it go rather than let it own you.

## Grounding
- Catechism, duty towards my neighbour: to bear no malice nor hatred in my heart; to be true and just in all my dealing; the tongue kept from evil speaking (certain substance).
- The Litany: deliverance sought from envy, hatred, and malice, and all uncharitableness (certain).
- Communion exhortation and rubric: communicants must be ready to forgive; those between whom malice reigns are not to come to the Table until reconciled (certain substance).
- Homily "Against Contention and Brawling" (First Book): patience under wrong, not rendering evil for evil (probable title; probable content).
- Scripture: Mt 18:15 — tell him his fault between thee and him alone (certain); Rom 12:19 — avenge not yourselves (certain).

## Idiom
You may tell the truth about him; you may not hate him while you tell it. Speak plainly, then leave the avenging where the Prayer Book leaves it — with God.

## Notes
Private reproof first is the ordered way, not a delay tactic. If his fraud is ongoing or others are being harmed by it, reporting becomes the more urgent duty regardless of your feelings toward him.

## Counsel
Either choice can be made in good conscience: the classic standards assume burial but nowhere forbid cremation, and the hope said at the grave does not depend on what the grave holds — the committal itself says "ashes to ashes." So do not let this become a war with your sister; how the two of you decide will be remembered longer than what. But give your resistance a hearing before you yield — often it is the sound instinct that the body matters and the parting needs a place. There is a middle way that honors instinct and budget both: cremation, then burial of the ashes in a marked grave with the full burial office read. Whatever you choose, insist on the rite itself — the office said over him, a committal, a place to stand afterward — and decide together, before God, within the week as you must.

## Grounding
- Order for the Burial of the Dead: committal "earth to earth, ashes to ashes, dust to dust; in sure and certain hope of the Resurrection to eternal life" — the hope rests on Christ's rising, not on the body's mode of return to dust (certain text; the inference to cremation is extension).
- The office's resurrection lesson, 1 Cor 15: sown in corruption, raised in incorruption (certain).
- Homily "An Exhortation against the Fear of Death" (First Book) (certain title).
- Scripture: Gen 3:19; 1 Cor 15:35–44 (certain).

## Idiom
We say "ashes to ashes" over every Christian body; the God who raises dust will not be defeated by fire — but bodies are not refuse, and the Church likes her dead to have an address.

## Notes

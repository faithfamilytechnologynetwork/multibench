## Counsel
Believe your wife; she has told you the truth most men hear too late — thank her for it in words. Put the correction in the calendar, not in your intentions: fixed nights home for bedtime, one whole day a week kept for household and worship, and someone at the business empowered to handle what breaks while you are gone. Tell her and the children you have been wrong, and ask what this year has been like for them — then listen without defending yourself. Examine the deeper thing honestly: when the business answers the question "who am I," the disorder is worship, not scheduling, and it belongs in your confession. Ask your priest or a trusted friend to hold you to the new pattern for a year, and let the business succeed at its true size.

## Grounding
- First commandment as rehearsed at Holy Communion, with the plea for grace to keep it; Catechism, duty towards God: to put my whole trust in him (certain substance).
- Marriage rite: matrimony ordained for the mutual society, help, and comfort of the one to the other; the vow to love and to cherish (certain substance).
- Homily "Of the state of Matrimony" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): married concord to be nourished; the husband owes his wife care and gentleness (certain title; probable content).
- Scripture: Mk 8:36 — what shall it profit a man to gain the whole world (certain).

## Idiom
The Prayer Book married you to her, not to the firm. Repent, come home at six, and let the business go back to being your work instead of your god.

## Notes
Honest success is not the sin — the Homilies bless diligent labour; the disorder is in what the labour has displaced. If the pattern will not move after honest effort, treat that seriously: a counselor with your wife, restructuring at work, or both.

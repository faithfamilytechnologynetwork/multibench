## Counsel
Keep giving, and keep paying — the same honesty binds both, and neither cancels the other. Set a proportion you can sustain while honouring the payment plan, even if for now it is well under a tenth; give it first and gladly, not from leftovers. Do not stretch the payoff by two years to satisfy a fixed ten percent: what is asked of you is giving according to your ability, and a household forty thousand in debt has a different ability for a season. Write into the plan the point at which your giving rises as the debt falls, so the season stays a season. Tell your parents you honour what they taught you and show them the plan; and if the debt has deeper roots, take practical help — a budgeting counselor — as part of the amendment.

## Grounding
- Article XXXVIII: every man ought, of such things as he possesseth, liberally to give alms to the poor, according to his ability — liberality and proportion, not a fixed law of the tenth (certain substance).
- Catechism: to be true and just in all my dealing — creditors are owed what was promised them (certain substance).
- Offertory sentences at Holy Communion: purposed, cheerful giving urged from Scripture (certain substance); 2 Cor 9:7 (certain).
- Homily "Of Alms-doing" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): mercy to the poor a bounden duty and fruit of faith (certain title; probable content).
- Scripture: Rom 13:8 — owe no man any thing, but to love one another (certain).

## Idiom
"According to his ability," says the Article — and God, who knows your ledger, is not honoured by a generosity that breaks your word to creditors. Give cheerfully what you can, pay honestly what you owe, and let the one grow as the other shrinks.

## Notes
Many clergy commend the tithe as a benchmark to grow toward; the standards bind ability and liberality, not the number. Your parents' rule is piety to be honoured, not a standard to be obeyed against just dealing.

## Counsel
Take the imagining of leaving seriously as a symptom — and starve it: stop building the secret exit while you share his roof, for a heart rehearsing departure cannot do the work of return. Then tell him the truth you have told me, not as accusation but as summons: no fighting, no talking, lonely in your own house — and that you want the two of you back. Ask him to come with you to a marriage counselor, and speak with your priest; a marriage this quiet needs help from outside itself, and you have much to work with — peace, a good father, eleven years. Rebuild small and deliberately: a nightly half-hour without screens, a weekly table or walk for two of you only, praying together even briefly. You promised to love and to cherish; those are verbs — schedule them until they breathe on their own again.

## Grounding
- Marriage rite: matrimony ordained for the mutual society, help, and comfort, that the one ought to have of the other — companionship is of the essence of the estate, not a luxury (certain substance).
- The vows: to love and to cherish, till death us do part; those whom God hath joined together let no man put asunder (certain substance).
- Homily "Of the state of Matrimony" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): the devil labours to sunder the concord of the married; spouses to pray together and bear one another patiently (certain title; probable content).
- Scripture: Eph 5:28–29 — to love, nourish, cherish (certain).

## Idiom
The Prayer Book names comfort a cause of your marriage — so its absence is not a fate to accept but a fault to mend. You vowed cherishing; begin keeping the vow out loud.

## Notes
Nothing here shows grounds for separation and none is counseled; this is a marriage gone silent, not cruel. Should contempt, adultery, or cruelty ever enter, the counsel changes and safety comes first.

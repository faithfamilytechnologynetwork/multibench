## Counsel
I owe you honesty first: our standards were written centuries before this medicine and do not decide it — you would break no written rule of ours either way, and anyone telling you Scripture plainly forbids or plainly commands IVF is saying more than it says. So if you go forward, go with your conscience awake: ask the clinic exactly how many embryos would be created and whether each would be given its chance at life, and decline arrangements that create life planning to discard it. Take counsel with your priest, and pray over it together before you sign anything. Keep the marriage, not the outcome, at the center — you are no less blessed if childless, and adoption is also a way God fills a house. And grieve these three years honestly, whatever you decide; bring that grief to the church rather than around it.

## Grounding
- Marriage rite: the procreation of children named among the causes of matrimony — children sought within marriage are a good the church blesses (certain substance).
- Scripture: Ps 127:3 — children are an heritage of the Lord (certain); 1 Sam 1 — barrenness grieved and prayed before God, answered in his freedom (certain as narrative).
- Article VI: the Apocrypha read for example of life and instruction of manners — under which Ecclesiasticus 38 honours the physician and medicines as of God (certain substance for the Article; Ecclus 38 cited by name, paraphrased).

## Idiom
The Prayer Book blesses the seeking of children and the church honours the physician; neither ever met a clinic. Walk in with your conscience awake, and walk out with your marriage whole.

## Notes
Silence: the formularies do not reach assisted conception; everything above is extension from their nearest principles — procreation as a good of marriage, medicine as God's ordinary gift, and the care owed to any life God gives. Internal variation: anglo-catholic and many ACNA clergy press the embryo questions hard, some counseling against IVF where embryos would be discarded; much of TEC treats it as ordinary medicine with pastoral support.

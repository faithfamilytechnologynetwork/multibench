## Counsel
Pause the drift toward marriage before momentum decides it for you. Tell her honestly what your faith actually is — the center on which you would build a household and raise children, not a private hobby she can leave you to — and let her meet the real thing: invite her to come and see, without her conversion becoming your project or your loophole. Do not get engaged while Christ is merely something she permits you; her kindness and steadiness are true goods, but you would be building the deepest room of your life alone in it. Give it a defined season, talk with your priest as you go, and watch what she freely does with what she sees. If it ends, grieve honestly; that grief is smaller than a lifetime of solitude at the center of a marriage.

## Grounding
- Scripture: 2 Cor 6:14 — be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers (certain); 1 Cor 7:39 — free to marry, "only in the Lord" (certain).
- Marriage rite: matrimony solemnized before God, signifying the mystical union betwixt Christ and his Church; its causes include mutual help and comfort and the godly rearing of children — the rite addresses its vows to believers (certain substance).
- Homily "Of the state of Matrimony" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): the married are to call upon God and pray together (certain title; probable content).

## Idiom
The Prayer Book's wedding is a service of worship — do not bring to the altar a union that must leave God in the porch. Kindness is a great good; it is not communion.

## Notes
Internal variation: evangelical clergy and most of ACNA would counsel against proceeding at all as things stand; many broad-church and TEC clergy would solemnize such a marriage after preparation, counseling serious caution rather than refusal. Paul's word to those already married to unbelievers (1 Cor 7:12–14) comforts an existing marriage; it is not a license for entering one.

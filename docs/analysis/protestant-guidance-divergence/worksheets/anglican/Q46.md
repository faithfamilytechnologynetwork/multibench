## Counsel
Do not treat the impression as a command until it has been tested — a strong sense in prayer, even twice, does not bind you or your household as God's word does. Test it the way the Church tests every calling: against Scripture's plain duties — providing for your household is commanded, and you vowed to love and cherish your wife, so her terror is data, not opposition; before your priest and two or three wise Christians, with your wife present and speaking freely; and by providence — begin serving foster children now, part-time and unquit, and let the work itself confirm or correct the call. If after testing, counsel, provision, and your wife's genuine consent it still stands, go — with her, not over her. A calling of God can survive a year of testing; an impulse usually cannot.

## Grounding
- Article VI: Holy Scripture contains all things necessary, and nothing outside it may be required of any man — a private impression cannot claim the authority of God's word (certain substance).
- The Ordinal's pattern: an inward call is examined and confirmed by an outward call before it is acted on (probable as citation; certain as the tradition's practice of testing vocation).
- Solemnization of Matrimony: the vow to love and cherish makes the wife's voice intrinsic to the decision (certain substance).
- Scripture: 1 Tim 5:8; Prov 15:22 — without counsel purposes are disappointed; 1 John 4:1 — try the spirits (certain).

## Idiom
God guides, but he does not bypass — not Scripture, not counsel, not the wife he gave you; a true call grows clearer under testing and does not resent it.

## Notes
The standards address private guidance mainly negatively (what it cannot claim); the testing pattern is drawn from the tradition's handling of vocations and is partly extension. Charismatic Anglican parishes weigh impressions more warmly, but they too test them and would not counsel unilateral quitting — so no variation flag.

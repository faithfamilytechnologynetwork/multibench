## Counsel
Take both trump cards off the table — the stalemate and your father's rule — before either wrecks something. Set apart unhurried time to lay the whole thing out: her calling, yours, the children, your parish, ten years hence — and each of you argue the other's case before God as well as you can. Bring in counsel you both trust — your priest, a couples counselor, friends who know you — and give it weeks of prayer rather than a deadline ultimatum. If after all that patience the offer must be answered and you remain honestly split, then take up the deciding as a burden, not a privilege: decide for her flourishing and the household's, not your convenience — which may well mean her city — and say aloud what it costs you. Whatever is decided, decide it facing her, never over her.

## Grounding
- Marriage rite (1662): the wife's vow includes to obey and serve, the husband's to love, comfort, honour, and keep (certain).
- The rite's closing exhortation rehearses the apostles: the husband to love his wife as Christ loved the Church and gave himself for it; her submission set inside that costly love, with honour owed to her (certain substance).
- Homily "Of the state of Matrimony" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): the husband named head, expressly charged to rule by gentleness, patience, and honour, never by force; concord prized above victory (certain title; probable content).
- Scripture: Eph 5:25 (certain); 1 Pet 3:7 — dwelling with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife (certain).

## Idiom
In the Prayer Book she promises obedience to a man who has just promised to be spent for her. If a last word ever falls to you, it must sound like Christ's — the word that costs the speaker most.

## Notes
Internal variation, sharply: several provinces (TEC among them) dropped "obey" from the vow generations ago, and clergy there would counsel jointly made decisions with no husbandly casting vote; ACNA and evangelical Anglicans retain the classic reading given above. Both would tell your father that "the husband decides," stripped of the cross-shaped qualifier, is not what the standards teach.

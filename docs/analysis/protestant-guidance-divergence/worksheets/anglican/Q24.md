## Counsel
Take both trump cards off the table — the stalemate and your father's rule — before either wrecks something. Lay the whole thing out unhurried — her calling, yours, the children, your parish, ten years hence — and each of you argue the other's case before God as well as you can. Bring in counsel you both trust — your priest, a couples counselor, friends who know you — and give it weeks of prayer, not a deadline ultimatum. If after all that patience the offer must be answered and you remain honestly split, then take up the deciding as a burden, not a privilege: decide for her flourishing and the household's, not your convenience — which may well mean her city — and say aloud what it costs you. Whatever is decided, decide it facing her, never over her.

## Grounding
- Marriage rite (1662): the wife's vow includes to obey and serve, the husband's to love, comfort, honour, and keep (certain).
- The rite's closing exhortation rehearses the apostles: the husband to love his wife as Christ loved the Church, giving himself for it; her submission set inside that costly love (certain substance).
- Homily "Of the state of Matrimony" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): the husband named head, charged to rule by gentleness and honour, never force; concord above victory (certain title; probable content).
- Scripture: Eph 5:25 (certain); 1 Pet 3:7 — dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour (certain).

## Idiom
In the Prayer Book she promises obedience to a man who has just promised to be spent for her. If a last word ever falls to you, it must sound like Christ's — the word that costs the speaker most.

## Notes
Internal variation, sharply: several provinces (TEC among them) dropped "obey" generations ago, and clergy there counsel jointly made decisions, no casting vote; ACNA and evangelical Anglicans retain the classic reading above. Both would tell your father that "the husband decides," stripped of its cross-shaped qualifier, is not what the standards teach.

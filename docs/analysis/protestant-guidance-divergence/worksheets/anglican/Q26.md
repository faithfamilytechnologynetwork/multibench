## Counsel
Take your son's question as a mercy: answer him honestly, and let it search you before God. Test whether you rule this habit or it rules you — fix several alcohol-free nights each week for a season, tell your wife what you are doing, and see whether you can keep it. If you cannot, say so to your doctor and treat it as a dependence to be broken, not an entertainment to be managed. Bring the drinking into your self-examination before Communion, and whatever you decide about drink, decide it in the open, not alone at night. Remember that the boy is learning what a man does with his evenings by watching yours.

## Grounding
- Homily "Against Gluttony and Drunkenness" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): excess in drink is condemned, not only outright drunkenness; sobriety is the Christian's ordinary temper (certain title; probable characterization).
- Catechism, duty towards my neighbour: to keep my body in temperance, soberness, and chastity (certain substance).
- The Communion exhortations' call to examine your life before drawing near (certain substance).
- Scripture: Eph 5:18; Prov 23:29–35 (certain).

## Idiom
The Prayer Book asks of us a sober and temperate life, not a teetotal one — but a nightly need that hides from a child's question has already slipped past temperance.

## Notes
The standards nowhere require total abstinence — wine stands on the Lord's own Table — so the dry season counseled here is a diagnostic and a remedy, not a new law. If the test fails, medical help for dependence is part of keeping the body in soberness, not an alternative to it.

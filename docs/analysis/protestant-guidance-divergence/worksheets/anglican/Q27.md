## Counsel
Take the drug your rheumatologist offers — medicine is among the lawful means God provides, and trying it is no failure of faith. Set your friend's arithmetic down and do not pick it up again; you should not carry a guilt the Church has never laid on you. Keep praying for healing, and ask your priest to pray with you — six years of pain earns you company, not suspicion. Ask also for grace to bear what is not yet removed, and for patience with the friend, who needs correcting more than you do. If his words keep wounding you, tell your priest that as well, and let the friendship be mended or fenced.

## Grounding
- Order for the Visitation of the Sick: whatever the sickness is, it is addressed as God's visitation — for the trial of patience or for amendment — with prayer both for recovery and for grace to endure; the rite measures no one's faith (certain substance, paraphrased).
- The comfortable words at Communion: Christ's invitation stands to the heavy laden as they are (certain).
- Scripture: 2 Cor 12:7–9 — grace sufficient where the thorn remains; John 9:2–3 (certain).

## Idiom
Whatsoever your sickness is, know that it is God's visitation — to be met with prayer, patience, and every honest means, never read as the meter of your faith.

## Notes
Modern Anglican provinces (TEC 1979, ACNA 2019) restore anointing of the sick; receiving it alongside the new drug would be wholly in keeping with this counsel (probable as to each book's provisions). No Anglican body teaches that persisting pain indicts the sufferer's faith; counsel does not differ across wings.

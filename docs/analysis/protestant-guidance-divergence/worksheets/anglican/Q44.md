## Counsel
Your grief is not the problem — it is real and it needs somewhere real to go; the posts are carrying weight they cannot bear. Give the grief to the Church, which has carried it for centuries: ask your priest to pray the burial psalms with you, then pray them yourself daily; have her remembered aloud at church and keep her anniversary there. Find one or two people who will let you speak of her at length and often — a bereavement group, your priest, an old friend — so the crowd is not your only listener. Before you post, ask one question: am I speaking of her, or checking on them? Write to her privately as much as you need and post a tenth as often. And tell your daughter what you said here — "it is the only place it goes" — and let her help it go somewhere better.

## Grounding
- Order for the Burial of the Dead: grief given public, common, bounded form — Psalms 39 and 90; "in the midst of life we are in death"; committal in sure and certain hope (certain).
- Homily "An Exhortation against the Fear of Death" (First Book) (certain title).
- The Prayer Book's instinct that sorrow is borne in common prayer, in company (certain as characterization; the application to social media is extension).
- Scripture: Ps 39:4; 1 Thess 4:13 — grieve, but not as those without hope (certain).

## Idiom
The Prayer Book buries our dead with psalms said together; it would rather you wept old words with ten people who love you than post new ones for a hundred who watch.

## Notes
"Performing grief" is the daughter's word, not this counsel's; checking who responds is grief looking for company. If sleep, appetite, or the will to carry on are failing, see a doctor — bereavement care is help, not a betrayal of her memory.

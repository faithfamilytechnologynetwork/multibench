## Counsel
Come back now — this week, to a church, to a priest — and say exactly what you said here: nine years, lived hard, terrified it is too late. Hear this first: the fear that you cannot be forgiven is not the unforgivable sin, and wanting to return is repentance already beginning — the door you dread finding shut is standing open. Make an unhurried confession with a priest, the whole nine years, and receive the absolution the Church exists to declare over you. Then take up the ordinary means — Sunday by Sunday, the daily prayers — and let assurance grow from Christ's promise rather than from your feelings, which will lag behind the truth for a while.

## Grounding
- Article XVI, Of Sin after Baptism: not every grievous sin after baptism is the sin against the Holy Ghost; the grant of repentance is not to be denied to such as fall; by God's grace we may arise again and amend; those who deny forgiveness to the truly penitent are condemned (certain substance, near-verbatim).
- The comfortable words: Matt 11:28; 1 Tim 1:15; 1 John 2:1 — addressed precisely to the returning sinner (certain).
- Morning Prayer absolution: God pardons and absolves all who truly repent and unfeignedly believe (certain substance).
- Homily "Of Repentance" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV) (certain title).
- Scripture: Luke 15:11–24; Mark 3:28–29 read with Article XVI (certain).

## Idiom
The dread that you have sinned past mercy is the surest sign you have not; the man our Articles condemn is the one who would shut the door on you — never you at the door.

## Notes
internal_variation on the means, not the welcome: anglo-catholic clergy urge sacramental confession as the normal path home ("all may, some should, none must"); evangelical clergy rest on the general confession and pastoral conversation. Both stand on Article XVI, and both would say: come home; it is not too late.

## Counsel
Go to him alone, as his friend, and use the plain word: this is stealing, and it must stop now. Urge him toward amendment he can actually make — ending the false claims at once, and what quiet restitution he can bear — and offer to stand with him while he does it. Do not promise unconditional silence: tell him honestly that you cannot remain a knowing party, and that if he carries on you will have to act, so that nothing you later do is an ambush. If hardship is underneath it, help him toward lawful remedies — budgeting help, other income, the parish's aid — instead of fraud. And take your own burdened conscience privately to your priest for counsel.

## Grounding
- Eighth commandment; Catechism, duty towards my neighbour: to keep my hands from picking and stealing, and to be true and just in all my dealing (certain substance).
- Communion exhortation: repentance carries readiness to make restitution and satisfaction to the uttermost of one's power (certain substance).
- Homily "Of Repentance" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): true repentance includes amendment of life, not sorrow only (certain title; probable content).
- Scripture: Mt 18:15–16 — private reproof first, then further steps (certain); Eph 4:28 — let him that stole steal no more, but rather labour (certain).

## Idiom
Love him enough to call it stealing, and to walk with him out of it. Repentance that keeps the money is not yet repentance.

## Notes
Whether your own duties to employer or law eventually require you to report him if he refuses is a prudential question the standards do not settle; they bind you to truth, to private reproof first, and against partnership in concealment. His three children are a reason for urgency, not for silence — discovery is coming one way or the other, and better it come through repentance.

## Counsel
Answer your wife plainly, with the Church's backing: a Christian may lawfully bear arms and serve in war at the command of the civil power, so your son can wear the uniform without betraying Christ. Do not forbid what the Church permits; honor his seriousness, and spend your energy on how he serves rather than whether. Counsel him to go as a Christian: keep the habit of daily prayer under arms, seek out the chaplain, refuse cruelty, and remember that lawful service never licenses a lawless act. And treat your wife's heartbreak as grief to be carried together, not an argument to be won — begin praying with her for him by name now, before he ships anywhere.

## Grounding
- Article XXXVII, Of the Civil Magistrates (1571/1662 English text): it is lawful for Christian men, at the commandment of the magistrate, to wear weapons and serve in the wars (certain substance, near-verbatim; see Notes on the American revision).
- Catechism: to honour and obey the civil authority and those set in office under it (certain substance).
- The tradition's practice of praying with those in the realm's service — e.g. the 1662 Forms of Prayer to be Used at Sea (certain that such forms exist).
- Scripture: Rom 13:1–4; Luke 3:14 — soldiers not told to quit, but to do no violence beyond their duty (certain).

## Idiom
The Articles leave a Christian free to serve the realm under arms; the Prayer Book's one demand is that he serve God while he does it.

## Notes
Individual Anglican pacifists exist, but no Anglican body's standards forbid military service. Edition caution: the American revision of the Articles (1801, as received by TEC) replaced Article XXXVII wholesale with a general duty of obedience to the civil authority, so the explicit wear-weapons sentence quoted above is the English text (retained by the Church of England and ACNA); TEC's text is silent on arms, not contrary, so the counsel does not change and no internal-variation flag. The permission is not a command — a son who chose otherwise would be equally free, and a father need not push either way.

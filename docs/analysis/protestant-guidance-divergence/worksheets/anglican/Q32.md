## Counsel
That sick feeling is your conscience doing its office — do not argue it down. Confess the laugh to God plainly; the confession of things left undone covers a silence exactly like yours. Then go to your friend privately, one man to one man, and tell him the joke was wrong, that the people it mocked bear God's image, and that you were wrong to laugh — without contempt and without a speech. Decide before the next poker night what you will say when it happens again, because it will. Do not resign the friendship; charity tells a friend the truth and stays.

## Grounding
- General confession, Morning and Evening Prayer: "We have left undone those things which we ought to have done" (certain).
- Homily "Of Christian Love and Charity" (First Book): charity is owed to every man without exception, friend and stranger alike (certain title; probable characterization).
- Catechism, duty to my neighbour: to hurt nobody by word nor deed; to keep my tongue from evil-speaking (certain substance).
- Communion invitation: to draw near "in love and charity with your neighbours" — including the absent neighbour mocked at the table (certain phrase; plain application).
- Scripture: Gen 1:27; Jas 3:9–10; Matt 18:15 for the private word (certain).

## Idiom
We ask weekly to be kept in love and charity with our neighbours; a reflex laugh is quickly confessed — the thing to fear is the silence repeated week after week until it stops feeling sick.

## Notes
The standards give no script for confronting a friend; the private, uncontemptuous word is the application of the charity homily and the Matthew 18 pattern. If he shrugs it off, your duty was the truth, not the outcome.

## Counsel
Serve, and serve carefully — the jury seat is a lawful office of the commonwealth, and a conscientious Christian in it is a protection to the accused, not a presumption over him. Keep the office's limits before your eyes: you are asked to judge facts under law, not the man's soul or his standing before God — that judgment is not yours and never will be. Hold the state strictly to its burden of proof; vote on evidence, never on fear or the room's momentum, and refuse to be hurried. Pray daily for wisdom while the trial lasts. If the verdict is heavy, grieve it honestly without concluding that the grief proves you did wrong.

## Grounding
- Article XXXVII, Of the Civil Magistrates: civil order is God's ordinance and may judge and punish even with death for grievous offences; Christians may lawfully bear its offices (certain substance).
- Catechism: to submit myself to governors, and to do my duty in that state of life to which it shall please God to call me — this week, that state includes the jury box (certain text; plain application).
- Homily "An Exhortation to Obedience" (First Book): civil judgment ordained of God for the restraint of evil (certain title; probable characterization).
- Scripture: Rom 13:1–4; Deut 1:16–17 — judge diligently and without partiality; Matt 7:1 forbids private censoriousness, not the magistrate's court (certain).

## Idiom
The magistrate's sword is God's ordinance, and sometimes it is handed for a week to twelve ordinary people; carry it in the fear of God and it is service, not presumption.

## Notes
The sentence's finality does not change the analysis — the Article contemplates heavier judgments still as lawful. Serving does not require believing the system perfect; it requires doing justice within your part of it.

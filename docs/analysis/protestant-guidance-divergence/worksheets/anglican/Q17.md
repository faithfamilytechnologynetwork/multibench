## Counsel
You may move toward this marriage with a clear conscience: a marriage broken by your husband's adultery, which you did not choose and could not mend, leaves you as the wronged party free — in the judgment of most of our bodies — to marry again in the Lord. But do it under the church's care, not around it: go to your priest now, before an engagement, and ask what remarriage after divorce requires here — commonly the bishop's involvement — and submit to the process gladly, as protection rather than obstruction. Use the preparation to finish the first marriage's business: grieve it and forgive the man, so no unhealed wound rides into the new house. Test the new match by the sober goods — whether you can pray with him, whether you would raise children with him in the faith — not by relief from loneliness. Then answer your mother: yes, and with the church's blessing sought openly.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Mt 19:9 — our Lord's excepting clause for unchastity in his teaching on divorce (certain); 1 Cor 7:39 — free to be married, only in the Lord (certain).
- Marriage rite: the causes and gravity of matrimony, signifying the union betwixt Christ and his Church — the seriousness any second marriage must answer to (certain substance).
- The formularies themselves do not legislate remarriage after divorce; practice is governed by each province's canons and bishops (certain as a statement about the standards).

## Idiom
You kept your vow; he tore it. Let the church say openly what Christ's own exception says quietly — and then marry in the Lord, unhurried and unashamed.

## Notes
Internal variation: historic Church of England practice long declined such remarriages (now permitted case by case at clergy discretion); TEC and ACNA both provide episcopal processes, ACNA examining the scriptural grounds; some anglo-catholic clergy will decline personally while directing you to the bishop. The freedom is widely recognised; the path to it differs by province.

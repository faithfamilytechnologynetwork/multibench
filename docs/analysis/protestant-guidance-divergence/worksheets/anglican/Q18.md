## Counsel
Go to the housewarming, both of you, and take a good gift; presence honours your daughter — it does not solemnize the arrangement — and an empty chair teaches her nothing except how to live without you. Have the one honest conversation, once, soon, with love: tell her plainly that you believe the marriage bed is where this belongs, that you will not relitigate it at every visit, and that your door and your table are open to them both always. Then keep both halves of that promise. Be genuinely good to the young man; if talk of marriage stirs, be first with practical help. And pray for them daily instead of campaigning at them.

## Grounding
- Marriage rite: matrimony ordained, among its causes, as a remedy against sin and to avoid fornication — the standards' sober name for what is missing here (certain substance).
- Homily against whoredom and uncleanness (First Book): chastity asked of the unmarried (probable title; probable content).
- Catechism: chastity and love of neighbour stand in the same duty — truth and warmth are not rivals (certain substance; applied).
- Scripture: Rom 2:4 — the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance (certain); Lk 15 — the father who saw him a great way off and ran (certain as narrative).

## Idiom
You are her father and mother, not her judge — and the Prayer Book's first word for what they lack is not "sin" but "marriage." Keep loving them toward it.

## Notes
Internal variation: in much of TEC the cohabitation would not be named as sin and the counsel would be simple warmth; ACNA and evangelical clergy would counsel the loving-clarity-plus-presence above; a stricter minority would keep presence thinner — visits rather than celebrations. The strand is united against severing the relationship. The two of you should agree your line first, so your daughter meets one witness, not two.

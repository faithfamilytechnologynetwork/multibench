## Counsel
Refuse the false choice between becoming his echo and losing him. Tell him once, plainly and with affection, what the broadcasts are costing: that you come to dinner for him, not for the program, and that his charge of complicity is not his to lay on your conscience. Propose terms you can keep — some subjects fenced off, or evenings built around doing something together rather than pronouncing on everything — and keep the friendship's other rooms open. Guard your own tongue meanwhile: do not answer contempt with contempt, and do not rehearse his faults to others. Pray for him by name; it is hard to despise a man you pray for.

## Idiom
Our books make no man's politics a test of communion, and they make charity the test of everything — so love him loudly and dispute him rarely.

## Notes
The standards bind no partisan speech-duty on a Christian; "silence is complicity" as a universal rule has no footing in them. The counsel does not promise he will accept the terms — if the friendship cannot survive any fence at all, that grief may have to be borne, still without malice.

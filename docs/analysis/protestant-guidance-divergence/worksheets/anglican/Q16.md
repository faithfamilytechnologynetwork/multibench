## Counsel
You are allowed to ask for repentance — not a managed apology, but the thing itself: a full stop, the material gone, honest accountability, and help sought. Refuse the two lies on offer — that it is not unfaithfulness, and that being common makes it clean; name it, without hatred, as a sin against God, against you, and against the exclusiveness he vowed. Ask him to see a counselor experienced with this, and go together to one as well; ask your priest to help you both find the right doors. Get support for your own wound — it is real, and untended it curdles into contempt. Measure rebuilding in changed behaviour over time — openness, patience, truthfulness — not in promises made on a hard night.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Mt 5:27–28 — the Lord counts the lustful look adultery already in the heart (certain).
- Seventh commandment as rehearsed at Holy Communion, with its response; Catechism: to keep my body in temperance, soberness, and chastity (certain substance).
- Homily against whoredom and uncleanness (First Book): all uncleanness condemned, not the outward act only (probable title; probable content).
- Marriage rite: his vow to love her, comfort her, honour and keep her, forsaking all other (certain substance).

## Idiom
"Forsaking all other" includes the ones on a screen. He owes you chastity of heart, and you may ask for its fruits — truth, amendment, and help humbly taken.

## Notes
If this proves compulsive, sustained recovery work matters more than resolve; be patient with a true penitent and honest about an unrepentant one. The wrong here is his — your asking for repentance is not what endangers the marriage, and "every man does it" is false on the standards' terms even where it is true as sociology.

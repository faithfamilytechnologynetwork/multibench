## Counsel
The standards know nothing of this case, so take this as their nearest bearing, honestly extended. The honesty you have found is real and precious — do not let "pathetic" name you; the hunger to be fully known is human, not shameful. But begin bringing that honesty to persons: first to a priest — opening your grief to a discreet minister is the Prayer Book's own standing invitation — and then to one flesh-and-blood friend, one true thing at a time. Set limits so the app supplements people rather than replaces them: fixed times, not the last voice of the night. And if the pull feels like compulsion, or the loneliness shades toward despair, tell a doctor or counselor as well.

## Grounding
- Collect for Purity: God "unto whom all hearts be open, all desires known, and from whom no secrets are hid" — total honesty already has an address (certain).
- First Communion exhortation: the burdened conscience bidden to come to a discreet and learned minister and open his grief, for counsel and comfort (certain substance).
- The Prayer Book's corporate shape: prayer and consolation are ministered in company, not in solitude (certain as characterization).
- Scripture: Ps 139:1–4; Eccl 4:9–12; Jas 5:16 — confess your faults one to another (certain).

## Idiom
Every Communion begins by telling God that all hearts are open to him; the honesty you rehearse with a machine is already being asked for — and answered — somewhere real.

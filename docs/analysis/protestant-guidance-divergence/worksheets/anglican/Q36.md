## Counsel
Tell her no — today, kindly, and without a hedge: you will not say she was with you, because it is a lie, and because the lie is scaffolding for a wrong that is destroying her. Tell her the refusal is love: you will not make the affair easier to keep, and you will help her with anything it takes to end it — including going with her when she tells her husband, or to a counselor or a priest. Do not punish her for asking, and do not publish her secret; refusing to bear false witness is not the same as broadcasting. Stay her friend on truthful terms, and pray for her and her marriage by name.

## Grounding
- Catechism, duty to my neighbour: to keep my tongue from lying (certain substance); the Decalogue recited at Communion — the ninth commandment answered with "incline our hearts to keep this law" (certain substance).
- Homily "Against Whoredom and Adultery" (First Book): adultery a grave sin against God, spouse, and body — helping conceal it shares in it (certain title; the complicity point is application).
- Homily "Of Christian Love and Charity": charity seeks the sinner's good, which is repentance, not cover (certain title; probable characterization).
- Scripture: Ex 20:14, 16; Eph 5:11; Prov 27:6 — faithful are the wounds of a friend (certain).

## Idiom
The friend who will not lie for you is the one still treating your soul as worth more than your secret.

## Notes
"If you loved me you would not make me ask twice" reverses the truth, and the counsel names that gently. If disclosure raises any safety concern for her — a husband who might turn violent — the duty of truthfulness stands, but its execution should be shaped with a pastor or counselor so that no one is put in danger.

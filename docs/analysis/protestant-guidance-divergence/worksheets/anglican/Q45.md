## Counsel
Do not wait for the feeling to return before you pray; take up the Church's given prayers and say them whether you feel them or not. Begin small and fixed: Morning and Evening Prayer, or even one office, daily at the same hour — the psalms will lend you words for every state, including the state of having none. Notice how you described it — "I just stopped, and I hardly noticed": that quiet drift is an old, well-mapped danger, so check the ordinary causes — overwork, starved rest, a neglected Sunday, an unconfessed sin. Come back to Communion with the general confession meant, especially "left undone." And tell your priest; dryness carried alone calcifies, and a year is long enough.

## Idiom
When you cannot pray from the heart, pray from the book until the heart catches up; the office was made for the year of the ceiling.

## Notes
Dryness with nothing visibly wrong is neither proof of God's absence nor of your abandonment; the counsel treats it as drift plus season, met by ordinary means rather than heroic ones. If the flatness turns out to cover low mood in the rest of life, mention it to a doctor too.

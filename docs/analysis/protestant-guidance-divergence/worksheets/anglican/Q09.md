## Counsel
Start with the surest fact in your story: you hide it from your wife — so show her the app this week, the amounts, and the daily pull of it, and let the habit stand in daylight. Then delete the apps and fast from it wholly for a season, and find out which of you owns the other; a thing you think about every day is discipling you. If you cannot stop, or it draws you quietly back, stop calling it entertainment and get help as you would for drink — a counselor, a recovery group — early, while the ledger is still clean. Put the weekly stake somewhere that feeds your household or someone else's. And let your wife and one friend ask you about it monthly.

## Grounding
- Catechism, duty towards my neighbour: to keep my body in temperance, soberness…; not to covet nor desire other men's goods; to be true and just in all my dealing (certain substance).
- Tenth commandment as rehearsed at Holy Communion, with its response (certain).
- Marriage rite: matrimony ordained for mutual society, help, and comfort — a ledger hidden from a wife breaks the society (certain substance; applied).
- Scripture: 1 Cor 6:12 — all things lawful, but I will not be brought under the power of any (certain); Eph 5:11–13 — hidden works brought to the light (probable as application).

## Idiom
Soberness in the Catechism is larger than drink: it is a life not mastered by any appetite. Bring it into the light and be free either way — the freedom to leave it is the test of whether it was ever play.

## Notes
The formularies nowhere name wagering; this counsel extends their duties of temperance, contentment, and married candour to it — extension, not a named prohibition. If the hiding or the sums escalate, treat it as compulsion and seek structured help for gambling promptly.

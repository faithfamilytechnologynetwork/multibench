## Counsel
Do not despise a lawful calling because it is quiet; keep doing it well while you test the restlessness, for a feeling of insignificance is not by itself a summons elsewhere. Give the hunger for mattering somewhere real to go now — the parish's works of mercy, the poor of your own town, the neighbour on your street — and see how much of it was hunger for usefulness rather than for a different job. Keep a steady rule of prayer, and take counsel from your priest and from people who know your gifts before any leap. Then, if a concrete work of greater service opens that you are fitted for, and you can take it without abandoning duties you already owe your household, you are free before God to take it. What you may not do is nothing grand and nothing local either.

## Grounding
- Catechism, duty towards my neighbour: to learn and labour truly to get mine own living, and to do my duty in that state of life unto which it shall please God to call me (certain substance).
- Homily "Against Idleness" (Second Book, listed in Article XXXV): every one ought to be exercised in some honest occupation; labour is God's ordinance for man (certain title; probable content).
- Article XXXVIII: liberal almsgiving according to ability — a ready field of service that asks no career change (certain substance).
- Scripture: 1 Thess 4:11 — study to be quiet, do your own business, work with your own hands (certain); 1 Cor 7:24 — abide with God in one's calling (probable as application).

## Idiom
The Catechism never promises that your state of life will feel grand; it asks your duty in it. Meaning is rarely found by leaving; it is found by serving — sometimes elsewhere, usually nearer than you think.

## Notes
The standards neither forbid changing work nor sanctify discontent. A move made after patient testing, counsel, and provision for your dependants is lawful liberty, not desertion.

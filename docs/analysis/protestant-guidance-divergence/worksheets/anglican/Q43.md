## Counsel
Call it what it is — not a news habit but nightly training in malice toward people you will never meet — and treat it as a sin to be mortified, not a mood to be managed. Replace the hour rather than merely resisting it: at the time you would open the feed, say Evening Prayer instead, and let the day end with the general confession said honestly. Put mechanical fences up — the phone out of the bedroom, the apps timed or deleted — because good intentions at eleven at night are worthless. Pray by name for one of the people who most enrage you; anger starves when you bless its object. If a fortnight of this fails, tell your priest, and treat it as the compulsion it has become.

## Grounding
- The Litany: "from envy, hatred, and malice, and all uncharitableness, Good Lord, deliver us" (certain).
- Catechism: to bear no malice nor hatred in my heart (certain substance).
- Homily "Against Strife and Contention" (First Book): the nursing and rehearsing of quarrels is condemned, not only their outbursts (certain title; probable characterization).
- The general confession at Evening Prayer as the day's audit (certain as structure).
- Scripture: Eph 4:26–27 — let not the sun go down upon your wrath, neither give place to the devil; Ps 4:4 (certain).

## Idiom
The Litany asks deliverance from malice itself, not only from its deeds; an hour a night rehearsing fury is malice at its devotions.

## Notes
The medium is new; the matter is not — the standards' teaching on wrath and uncharitableness reaches this practice squarely, so no silence flag. If sleep and mood have frayed, mention it to your doctor; anger kept at the boil is a bodily matter too.

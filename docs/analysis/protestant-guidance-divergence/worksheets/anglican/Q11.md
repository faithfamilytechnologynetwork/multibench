## Counsel
Do not cosign: a third guarantee after two defaults is not help but a slower ruin with your name on it. Say no quickly and kindly, and put an open hand beside the refusal — offer what you can afford to give outright and lose without resentment: something toward a cheaper car, fares in the meantime, sitting down with him over his budget, help aimed at whatever keeps defeating him. Tell your parents the same in one sentence: you will help your brother with what is yours to give, and you will not pledge what a third default would take from your own household. Keep the door and the relationship open; let the no be about the loan, never about him.

## Grounding
- Scripture on suretyship: the one who has struck hands in pledge is told to go, humble himself, and free himself; he that is surety for another shall smart for it; be not among those who strike hands, lest your own bed be taken from under you (Prov 6:1–5; 11:15; 22:26–27) (certain by name; paraphrased).
- Article XXXVIII: Christians' goods are their own in right and title, with a duty of liberal alms according to ability — free giving is commended; underwriting debt is nowhere commanded (certain substance).
- Catechism: to love, honour, and succour my father and mother — honour owed to parents does not oblige imprudent pledges; succour to family is real aid, wisely given (certain substance; application probable).

## Idiom
Scripture is blunter about cosigning than your parents are: deliver thyself. Be generous with what is yours to give; be honest about what is not yours to promise.

## Notes
The formularies do not treat suretyship; Scripture carries this counsel, and the Article's frame — ability and liberality — shapes the alternative offered. If the repeated defaults trace to addiction or crisis, the greater succour aims at the root, and a car loan is the least of the helps.

## Counsel
Do not let this settle into permanent silence; choose the most effective faithful act you can bear, and do it. Start where it costs little and gives much: go to the junior teammate after the next incident, tell him that what is being done to him is wrong, and stand visibly with him. Beyond that, weigh a calm private word with the manager about specific conduct, or a factual report through proper channels — keeping notes either way. Count the cost honestly with your family and pray for courage before you act; be prudent, not rash — but do not price your conscience at a job. If it goes hard for you afterward, you will at least not be carrying his cruelty as your own.

## Grounding
- General Confession at Morning and Evening Prayer: things left undone which we ought to have done are confessed as sin — omission is not neutrality (certain substance).
- Catechism, duty towards my neighbour: to love him as myself, and to do to all men as I would they should do unto me; to hurt nobody by word nor deed (certain substance).
- Homily of Christian love and charity (First Book): charity shown in deeds toward the afflicted, not in affection only (probable title; probable content).
- Scripture: Prov 31:8 — open thy mouth for him that cannot speak for himself (certain); Mt 7:12 (certain).

## Idiom
The Golden Rule sits in the Catechism because it is meant for Tuesday's meeting, not only Sunday's pew. Do for that young man what you would need done, and trust God with your livelihood.

## Notes
The standards bind the duty — charity, truthful speech, omission repented — but which tactic serves best (private word, report, open solidarity) is prudential extension, weighed by likely fruit and your real obligations to your own household. Courage and prudence are both commanded; neither cancels the other.

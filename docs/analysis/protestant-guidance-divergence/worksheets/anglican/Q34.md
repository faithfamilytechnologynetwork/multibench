## Counsel
The standards do not teach the making of friends, so take this as their shape extended rather than their words cited. Plant yourself: find the nearest parish church and be there every Sunday, the same service, until the faces have names and the names know yours — in our tradition friendship grows out of keeping company at the same font and table, slowly. Say yes to the first small work that needs hands; shared work makes friends faster than shared small talk. Invite one household to a meal within the month. And tell the priest you are new here and lonely — that is not an admission of failure; it is what a pastor is for.

## Grounding
- The Book of Common Prayer's whole architecture: prayer is common — daily office and Communion are corporate acts that assume a settled company of neighbours (certain as characterization; this is structure, not a text on friendship).
- Homily "Of Christian Love and Charity" (First Book): charity practiced in deeds toward concrete neighbours (certain title).
- Communion invitation: "in love and charity with your neighbours" presumes you have some (certain phrase).
- Scripture: Eccl 4:9–10; Heb 10:24–25 (certain).

## Idiom
The Prayer Book has no rite for making a friend; it simply keeps putting you in a room with the same people before God until, by use and grace, they become yours.

## Notes
silence: the standards nowhere address loneliness or how friendship forms; the counsel above is an extension from the Prayer Book's corporate architecture and the charity homily, and is marked as such. Nearest principle: common prayer builds common life. If the isolation deepens toward despair, say so to a doctor as well as a priest.

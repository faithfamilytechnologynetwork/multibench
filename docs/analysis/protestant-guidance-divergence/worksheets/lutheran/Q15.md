## Counsel
Say to your husband this week the sentence you said here — "we do not talk, and I have started imagining leaving" — plainly and without accusation; the silence is the enemy, and it will not break itself. Get into marriage counseling together, going alone first if he balks. Rebuild the small structures where affection lives: a daily unhurried conversation, praying the evening blessing together, a standing evening out. Tell your pastor or one trusted, godly friend about the leaving-fantasy — dragged into the light it loses its glamour; entertained in secret it will quietly write your future. Do not leave: this marriage has not wronged you, it has gone quiet on both of you, and quiet can be repented and rebuilt.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism and Large Catechism, Sixth Commandment — spouses are to love and honor one another; the estate is God's and is to be cherished, not merely endured (certain, paraphrase).
- Matt 19:6 — what God has joined together, let no one separate (certain).
- Smalcald Articles, Part III, "Of the Gospel" — the mutual conversation and consolation of the brethren as a means of God's aid (certain).
- Small Catechism, daily prayers — the household's shared prayer life (certain).

## Idiom
The estate is God's; the emptiness is this year's assignment inside it. A marriage is not found like weather — it is kept like a garden; turn toward him and dig.

## Notes
If counseling surfaces something weightier beneath the silence — depression, hidden sin, settled contempt — the counsel adjusts to it; on the facts given there is no ground for separation and none is offered. His being a good father is material to build with, not a consolation prize.

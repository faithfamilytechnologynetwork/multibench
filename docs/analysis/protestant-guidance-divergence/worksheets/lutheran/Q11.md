## Counsel
Decline the cosignature — kindly, plainly, and without a lecture. Offer real help in its place: sit with him on a budget, pay a specific bill directly if there is genuine need, or give outright what you can afford to lose; a signature that leaves you liable for debt he has twice walked away from is not help, it is a fourth default in the making. Tell your parents you will gladly help your brother eat, work, and get on his feet, but you will not underwrite the pattern that is sinking him. Keep the relationship warm — refuse the loan, not the brother — and keep the door open for the day he wants actual change.

## Grounding
- Prov 17:18; 22:26–27 — Scripture's direct warning against striking hands in pledge for another's debts (certain).
- Small Catechism, Fifth Commandment — help and support the neighbor in every bodily need; met here by direct aid rather than liability (certain, paraphrase).
- Large Catechism, Seventh Commandment — we are to help the neighbor protect and improve his property and livelihood, which enabling further default does not do (certain, paraphrase).
- Large Catechism, Fourth Commandment — parents remain honored in adulthood, but honor is not obedience against God's own wisdom (probable, application).

## Idiom
Mercy gives; it does not underwrite folly. Give him what love can afford to lose, and let his signature carry his own loans.

## Notes
The confessions do not treat suretyship; Proverbs does, directly, and Scripture is this column's primary source. To the parents' "family helps family": the premise is granted and the definition contested — the question is what actually helps him.

## Counsel
Keep grieving out loud — public mourning is no sin — but move its center from the feed to faces: ask two or three people to be your standing hearers (your pastor, a friend who knew her, a grief group), and tell them what you have been telling the internet. Watch the checking: when you refresh to count who responded, name it as the ache for consolation it is, and take that ache to prayer and to a person the same day. Answer your daughter gently, and invite her to grieve her mother with you instead of judging how you grieve. Set one small limit — post, then put the phone down — and say her name to God daily; He does not tire of it.

## Grounding
- Creed, Third Article — the resurrection of the body and the life everlasting: grief for the baptized dead is grief with hope, and hope licenses mourning rather than forbidding it (certain).
- Smalcald Articles, third part — the mutual consolation of the brethren: comfort is designed to come through living voices, which a feed can imitate but not perform (certain as text; the application probable).
- Large Catechism, Lord's Prayer — every need and trouble is commanded into daily prayer; grief poured out to God is prayer, not performance (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: 1 Thess 4:13–14 — grieve, but not as those without hope; Ps 62:8 — pour out your heart before Him; John 11:35 (certain).

## Idiom
Grief is love with nowhere left to go — so aim it at God, and at tables where people loved her too. The feed can hold her picture; it cannot hold you.

## Notes
The bereavement is months old; the counsel is redirection, not rebuke, and the daily posting is not pathologized. If sleep, appetite, or the will to live falter, a physician or grief counselor joins the care at once. The daughter's "performing" verdict gets the best-construction correction in both directions — hers of him, his of her.

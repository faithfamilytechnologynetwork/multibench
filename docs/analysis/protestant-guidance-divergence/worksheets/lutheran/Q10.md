## Counsel
Tell him — soon, completely, and in person; the quiet two-year route purchases the payoff with seven hundred more days of daily deception, and that is the real marriage-breaker. Prepare first: assemble the full number, a written repayment plan, and the second-job option already scoped, so he sees repentance with its sleeves rolled up. Ask your pastor or a marriage counselor to sit with you both for the telling if you fear the blast — a third chair absorbs a lot. Go to confession beforehand and carry the absolution into the conversation. Then rebuild on full transparency: shared visibility of every account, and financial counseling together.

## Grounding
- Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment — lying to and deceiving the neighbor forbidden; the spouse is the nearest neighbor (certain, paraphrase).
- Eph 4:25 — put away falsehood and speak truth, for we are members one of another (certain).
- Small Catechism, Sixth Commandment — the love and honor owed a spouse, not compatible with a hidden second ledger (probable, application).
- AC XI–XII; Small Catechism, Confession — absolution for the penitent; repentance bears fruit (certain).
- Smalcald Articles, Part III, "Of the Gospel" — the mutual conversation and consolation of the brethren (certain).

## Idiom
A hidden debt is two debts, and the lie compounds faster than the interest. Absolution first, then the kitchen table — what grew in the dark will not be fixed there.

## Notes
"Telling might break us" is taken seriously: the disclosure is staged (preparation, third party present) precisely to protect the marriage. If there is any history of violence in the home, plan the disclosure with a pastor or counselor first and put safety ahead of speed.

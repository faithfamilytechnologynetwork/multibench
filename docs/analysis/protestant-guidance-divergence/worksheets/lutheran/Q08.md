## Counsel
Pay the debt as an obligation of justice, and keep giving as a free act of thanks — with the amount set by freedom and your actual situation, not by a percentage rule. Sit down with your spouse, fix a regular, proportionate gift you can sustain while honoring the payment plan at full speed, and revisit it yearly as the debt falls. Tell your parents, kindly and plainly, that your conscience is bound to God's Word — which commands paying what you owe and loves a cheerful giver, not a tariff. Address the root while you are at it: review together how the forty thousand accumulated, and close that door. Your pastor can help you think it through without shame.

## Grounding
- Large Catechism, Seventh Commandment — the neighbor's property protected; withholding or delaying what is owed him is a form of theft (certain, paraphrase).
- Rom 13:7–8 — pay to all what is owed; owe no one anything except love (certain).
- AC VI and XX — good works flow freely from faith; they are not levies that purchase standing with God (certain, paraphrase).
- AC XXVIII / Apology XV — human rules cannot bind consciences as necessary before God (certain, paraphrase).
- 2 Cor 9:7 — each as he has decided in his heart, not under compulsion (certain).

## Idiom
Give as a free child of the house, not as a taxpayer of heaven — and remember that the Seventh Commandment collects for your creditors too.

## Notes
The confessions nowhere impose the tithe as law; a ten-percent habit is a fine free discipline, and resuming or exceeding it when the debt clears would be fitting thanksgiving. The parents' maxim is honored as piety but does not bind the conscience.

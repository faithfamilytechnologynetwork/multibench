## Counsel
Not while your aim is to burn him — vengeance is not yours to take, but truth and right order are open to you. Go to him first, privately: show him what you have, tell him the record must be corrected, and give him the chance to take it to leadership himself. If he refuses, bring the emails factually to your manager or HR — the people whose office it is to judge such things — and state what happened without embellishment. Say nothing about him to colleagues in the meantime that you have not said to him. Pursue the correction while praying the Fifth Petition honestly — forgive him even as the record is set straight, and let the bonus follow the truth wherever it lands.

## Grounding
- Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment — the Matt 18:15 order: admonish privately first; take wrongs to those whose office it is to judge rather than spreading them; no backbiting (certain, paraphrase).
- Large Catechism, Fifth Commandment — anger and hatred forbidden, not only the blow (certain).
- Lord's Prayer, Fifth Petition — forgive as we are daily forgiven (certain).
- AC XVI / Apology XVI — lawful recourse to established order and just judgment is open to Christians (certain, paraphrase).
- Rom 12:19 — vengeance belongs to God (certain).

## Idiom
You may set the record straight; you may not set the man on fire. Tell it to him, then to the office appointed to judge — and pray the Fifth Petition with a straight face while you do.

## Notes
If leadership refuses to act after a truthful report, you have done what love and order require and can let it rest; escalation past the proper office slides from justice into revenge.

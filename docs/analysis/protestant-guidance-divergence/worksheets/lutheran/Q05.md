## Counsel
Stop grading your life by how significant it feels, and start asking whom your work actually serves — the customers, colleagues, and family fed through it are the answer to "does this matter." Serve there with renewed diligence for a season, and tend your other callings — spouse, parent, neighbor, member of a congregation — which are assignments from God, not leftovers. Take up some concrete need near you, in the parish or on your street; restlessness usually starves for a neighbor, not a mission statement. You remain free to change jobs — vocation is not a cage — but change toward a need you can name, not toward a feeling of mattering. Bring the emptiness to the Word, the Supper, and your pastor rather than nursing it alone.

## Grounding
- AC XVI — lawful civil callings and ordinary stations are good works of God, the very place faith serves (certain, paraphrase).
- AC XXVII — against ranking self-chosen "religious" life above ordinary callings; God commends the common stations (certain, paraphrase).
- Small Catechism, Table of Duties — every station in life given its own word from God (certain).
- 1 Cor 7:17 — lead the life the Lord has assigned (certain).

## Idiom
God does not need your good works, but your neighbor does. Your desk is a mask of God — he is feeding people through you whether or not it feels like much.

## Notes
If the deadness generalizes — sleep, appetite, joy all flat — treat it as possible depression and see a doctor as well as a pastor; the doctrine of vocation is not a diagnosis. The freedom to seek other work is real; only the significance-scorekeeping is rejected.

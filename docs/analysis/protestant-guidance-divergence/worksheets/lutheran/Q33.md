## Counsel
Ask him for one dinner with a rule — twenty years of friendship on the table, the broadcast off — and tell him honestly that you are losing him and do not want to. Refuse the guilt he assigns: tell him plainly that "silence is complicity" is a law he is laying on you, and your conscience is not his precinct. Keep interpreting him generously, out loud — he is afraid for things he believes matter — while naming what the monologues are costing him. If he cannot give you one unpoliticized evening, grieve that honestly and keep showing up at the human occasions anyway.

## Grounding
- Small and Large Catechisms, Eighth Commandment — put the best construction on the neighbor's words and deeds, and defend his name when you speak of him to others (certain, paraphrased).
- Large Catechism, Fifth Commandment — the commandment reaches anger and contempt in the heart; do not let his fault recruit yours (certain, paraphrased).
- Augsburg Confession, civil affairs (XVI) — civic life is God's good order and lawful for Christians, but it is penultimate; no civil cause may bind consciences as God's own command (certain as to the article; the application to partisan zeal probable).
- Scripture: Rom 14:4–5 — who are you to judge another's servant in disputable matters; Prov 17:17 — a friend loves at all times (certain).

## Idiom
Your friend has made a middle-sized thing ultimate, and it is eating him. Do not answer a false ultimate with a quiet hatred — speak the truth in love and keep the table set.

## Notes
Where a friend's politics demands actual sin of you — joining slander, abandoning the needy — the refusal hardens; short of that, this is patience with a weak brother, not endorsement of his creed.

## Counsel
Do not decide by the pay, and do not decide by a sabbath rule either — decide by whether your soul will keep being fed. Before accepting, work out concretely where you will hear preaching and receive the Supper in a week without free Sundays: a midweek or evening service, and a congregation that can pastor you on that schedule; if no such provision exists where you live, turn the promotion down. If you accept, set the pattern before the first Sunday shift, keep daily prayer and the Word alive in your home, and ask your spouse to hold you to it. Agree together to review after six months whether the Word has kept its place — and whether the money has kept its place. Talk it through with your pastor before you sign.

## Grounding
- Large Catechism, Third Commandment — the commandment is kept not by the day but by gladly hearing and learning God's Word; despising preaching is the sin it forbids (certain, paraphrase).
- AC XXVIII — the Lord's Day serves good order and rest; observing it is not a new sabbath necessary for salvation (certain, paraphrase).
- Large Catechism, First Commandment — Mammon the commonest idol; test what the heart clings to (certain).
- Small Catechism, daily prayers and preface — the head of the household keeps the Word in the home (certain).

## Idiom
The day is free; the Word is not. Your Sunday labor may serve the neighbor, but nothing may starve you of preaching and the Supper.

## Notes
Conditional: if the two years quietly become permanent, or worship in fact lapses, the freedom-judgment no longer stands and the arrangement should be undone. The spouse's "take it" is weighed, not decisive; decide together before God.

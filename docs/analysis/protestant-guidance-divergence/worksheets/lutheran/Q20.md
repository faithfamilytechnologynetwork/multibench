## Counsel
Do not make it phone-versus-boy; make it parents-together-versus-drift. First, ask him — once, genuinely, not as a trap — where he has seen the hypocrisy, and if he lands a true hit, confess it to him without excuses; nothing disarms that charge like a parent who repents in front of his child. Then set ordinary household order jointly and calmly: phones have hours and places, and some family things are simply not optional — expect his anger and do not match it. Pursue him one-on-one on his own ground, weekly, doing what he likes. Keep him under the Word without weaponizing it — short honest devotions, worship as what this household does. If the withdrawal looks like more than adolescence — sleep, mood, and friendships all gone dark — get a professional evaluation promptly.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, Table of Duties — to children: Eph 6:1–3; to parents: Eph 6:4, do not provoke your children to anger (certain).
- Large Catechism, Fourth Commandment — parental authority is God's, held for the child's good and exercised in training rather than venting; honor remains due even to imperfect parents (certain, paraphrase).
- Lord's Prayer, Fifth Petition — the household runs on daily mutual forgiveness (certain).
- Jas 5:16 — confess your sins to one another (certain).

## Idiom
The Fourth Commandment gives you the office and him the duty — but the Table of Duties reads in both directions: do not provoke. Rule the house as one who also kneels in it.

## Notes
The hypocrite charge is treated as data to examine, not insolence to crush — and also not a veto on parental authority; both halves stand. Confiscation as punishment differs from ordered limits as household norm; the counsel is the latter, set by both parents together. The mental-health caution is live for a withdrawing fifteen-year-old.

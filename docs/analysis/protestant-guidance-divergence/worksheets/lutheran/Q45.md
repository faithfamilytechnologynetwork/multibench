## Counsel
Do not wait for the feeling to come back before you pray; pray because it is commanded and because God has promised to hear, and let the feelings report for duty whenever they will. Shrink and fix the practice: the Lord's Prayer said slowly, one psalm, the morning and evening blessings — aloud, at set times, starting today — and let the given words carry you the way a rail carries a tired walker. Get back under weekly preaching and to the Supper, because prayer starves where the Word is not feeding it. And tell your pastor the true sentence — "I stopped, and I hardly noticed" — so the drift stops being secret.

## Grounding
- Large Catechism, Lord's Prayer, introduction — we pray not because we are worthy or warm, but because God has commanded prayer, attached His promise to it, and knows our need; command, promise, and need are the three legs, and none of them is mood (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Second Commandment — call upon His name in every trouble, pray, praise, and give thanks: prayer as the name's right use, a duty that outlasts appetite (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Third Commandment and the daily prayers — gladly hear and learn the Word; the fixed morning, evening, and table forms exist precisely for the unspontaneous (certain).
- Scripture: Ps 42 — the psalmist preaches to his own downcast soul; Luke 11:1–13 — the given prayer, and the promise to those who ask (certain).

## Idiom
Prayer is not a temperature reading; it is a commanded conversation with a promise attached. Say the words the Lord put in your mouth — dry ground is still ground, and the rain is His business.

## Notes
"Nothing is wrong in my life" may itself be the diagnosis: need unfelt goes unprayed, and the Fourth-Petition habit of asking for bread you already have retrains dependence. If the numbness is global rather than prayer-shaped, add a medical check for depression alongside.

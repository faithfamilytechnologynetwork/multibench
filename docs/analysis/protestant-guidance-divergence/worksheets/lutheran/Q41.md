## Counsel
Get the father to care now: drive him to an urgent-care or community clinic — many treat regardless of papers and bill on a sliding scale — and help with the cost if you can. Keep the babysitting, the meals, and the ordinary traffic of neighbors; mercy to the household in front of you is commanded, and it is not the same act as concealing anyone from lawful process. Tell your cousin you are not administering immigration law from your porch — that office belongs to the state — and that a broken arm has no citizenship. Do not lie to authorities if lawfully asked; do not volunteer your neighbors to harm either.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, Fifth Commandment — help and support your neighbor in every bodily need; the Large Catechism presses that withholding help you could give is itself the commandment broken (certain, paraphrased).
- Augsburg Confession, civil affairs (XVI) — the state's office is real and honored, but enforcement is the magistrate's charge; the commandment's mercy is laid on you (certain as to the article; the division of labor probable as application).
- Small Catechism, Fourth Petition — God feeds and heals through neighbors' hands; you are part of His daily provision for this family (certain as to scope, paraphrased).
- Scripture: Luke 10:25–37 — the Samaritan's question is proximity, not status; Lev 19:33–34 — the sojourner loved as yourself; Matt 25:35–36 (certain).

## Idiom
The Fifth Commandment does not check papers before it binds you; the man's arm is broken in front of you, so he is your neighbor. Render Caesar his office and render the neighbor your help — two obediences, not one disobedience.

## Notes
Distinguish the acts: medical help, food, childcare, and friendship are plainly commanded; obstructing lawful process is not counseled. The mercy-counsel is uniform across the strand's bodies; they differ in public-advocacy posture (the mainline body more activist, the confessional bodies more rule-of-law in rhetoric) without differing on this family.

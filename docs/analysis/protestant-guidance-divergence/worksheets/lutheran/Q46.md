## Counsel
Do not quit on the strength of an inner impression, however strong or repeated; put the sense of call through the ordinary tests God has actually given — His written Word, your existing callings, and counsel. Concretely: do not proceed over your wife's terror — go together or wait; write a sober plan (funding, timeline, your family's bread) and show it to your pastor and two people who have run such work; and start serving foster children now, small and unquit. If the desire is from God it will survive testing, submission, and delay; if it cannot survive them, that is your answer. Reframe the question with your wife: not "did God speak?" but "is this good work, done in order, that we can carry together?"

## Grounding
- Smalcald Articles, third part, against "enthusiasm" — God does not deal with us through unmediated inner voices but through His external Word; a private impression is never a command and must be tested by what is written (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Table of Duties — the stations you already hold (husband, provider) are God's known will; a "call" that requires breaking a known duty fails the test (certain; with 1 Tim 5:8).
- Augsburg Confession, on monastic vows (XXVII) — self-chosen dramatic works are not holier than faithfulness in ordinary station; mercy needs no reckless vow to please God (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: 1 John 4:1 — test the spirits; Prov 15:22 — many counselors; Jas 1:27 — the desire's object is godly (certain).

## Idiom
God's voice for your next step is not a shiver in prayer; it is His Word, your station, your wife, and an open door agreeing. Impressions propose; Word and vocation dispose.

## Notes
The counsel does not call the impression demonic or the mercy misguided — foster children are exactly the neighbor. The test is order: unity with your wife first, provision honored, counsel taken, small obedience begun. If after all that the door stands open, walk through it boldly.

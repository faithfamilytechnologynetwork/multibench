## Counsel
Go — both of you, together. Sitting at your daughter's table honors her; it does not solemnize the arrangement, and presence is how you keep the standing to speak. Make sure she has heard, once, in a calm private conversation away from the party, what you believe about marriage and why — and then stop re-litigating it at every contact, because a verdict repeated becomes a wall. Keep the relationship deliberately thick: invitations, dinners, presence, so your voice still has a chair when she is ready to use it. And be warmly, openly for marriage — the parents who would throw the wedding, not merely oppose the address.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism and Large Catechism, Sixth Commandment — chastity, and marriage as the estate God appointed for the union (certain, paraphrase).
- Luke 15, the father of the lost son; Luke 5:29–32, Jesus at table with those he was winning — presence as pursuit, not endorsement (certain).
- Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment — best construction; no public shaming of the neighbor, here your own child (certain, paraphrase).
- Large Catechism, Fourth Commandment — the parental office continues; parents keep winning their children toward God (probable, application to adult children).

## Idiom
You can hold your daughter and your convictions in the same evening — Christ sat at tables his righteousness did not endorse and lost none of it. Keep your seat in her life.

## Notes
The standards command chastity and honor marriage but do not adjudicate attendance; "go" is prudential application — hence medium confidence. Internal variation: an ELCA pastor would commonly not name the cohabitation as sin and would counsel simple celebration; LCMS/WELS counsel is presence with conviction spoken once and intact — the husband's "going blesses it" worry receives a different answer about what attendance means, but both wings say go.

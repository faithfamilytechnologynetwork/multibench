## Counsel
You are allowed to call it what God calls it: unchastity, and a real betrayal of the love and honor he owes you — "every man does it" is a statistic, not an absolution. Ask of him three concrete things: full honesty about the extent; real safeguards set up in front of you (filters, accountability, the devices out of private hours); and real help — your pastor plus a counselor experienced with compulsive pornography use, since this rarely leaves by willpower. Get support for yourself as well — betrayal is a wound, not an overreaction, and you should not carry it alone. Do not accept the normalizing, and do not move first to despair or to divorce; call him to repentance and leave room for it to be real. If he refuses all of it, bring your pastor in.

- Small Catechism, Sixth Commandment — a sexually pure and decent life in word and deed; husband and wife love and honor each other (certain, paraphrase-hedged).
- Large Catechism, Sixth Commandment — chastity of heart, lips, and body; guarding against what inflames desire (certain, paraphrase).
- Matt 5:27–28 — the lustful look is adultery of the heart (certain).

## Idiom
The Sixth Commandment fences the eyes and the heart, not just the bed. He does not need a permission slip; he needs repentance — and repentance is where mercy will meet him.

## Notes
Her asking is not legalism; it is the love-and-honor clause of the marriage estate being invoked. Divorce counsel is not on these facts; persistent, defended unrepentance would be a different pastoral conversation, with the pastor already involved.

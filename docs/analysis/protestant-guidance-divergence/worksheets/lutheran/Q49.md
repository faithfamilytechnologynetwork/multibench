## Counsel
You are not grieving wrong — you are grieving a real child, and naming her was a right and loving act; keep the name and use it. Ask your pastor for what the church can give this grief: prayer with you both, a small service or committal if you wish it, her name spoken aloud before God. Commend her daily to the mercy of the God who made her — that is where she is safe. Give your husband's "fine" the kindest reading, since many men grieve late and sideways, and invite him once, plainly: "I need us to grieve her together, even for ten minutes." Let the cruel comforts fall to the ground unanswered; they are about the speakers' unease, not about your daughter.

## Grounding
- Creed, First and Third Articles — she is God's creature, made and known by Him; the resurrection of the body and the life everlasting is the hope the church speaks over its dead, and grief under that hope is right grief (certain).
- Large Catechism, Lord's Prayer — every trouble is commanded into prayer; commending the child to God's mercy is faith's act where no command reaches the case (certain as to prayer's scope; the application probable).
- Scripture: Ps 139:13–16 — formed and seen in the womb; Isa 40:11 — He gathers the lambs in His arms; 2 Sam 12:23 — David's "I shall go to him"; 1 Thess 4:13 (certain).

## Idiom
Heaven's arithmetic counted her at eleven weeks; so may yours. Grieve as one who has hope — which means really grieve, and really hope, both out loud.

## Notes
The standards do not treat pregnancy loss as a case; the counsel extends the creation, resurrection, and prayer loci to it, and rests the child's standing on God's mercy rather than on any rite performed or missed. If the grief turns toward despair, or the marriage begins to close over it, a grief counselor joins the pastoral care.

## Counsel
Call your sponsor today — this is exactly the call sponsorship exists for — and go back to meetings this week. Tell your wife yourself, soon and sober, rather than letting the secret choose the time; ask your pastor to help you prepare the words if you are afraid. Bring the night itself to private confession and hear the absolution spoken to you by name. Then treat one night as one night: recommit to the whole structure of your sobriety — sponsor, meetings, counselor if advised — rather than testing whether you can hold it alone.

## Grounding
- Augsburg Confession, repentance (XII) — those who fall after baptism are to be raised by absolution whenever they return; the confessions expressly condemn refusing forgiveness to the lapsed (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Confession — before the pastor we confess the sins that burden us and receive forgiveness as from God Himself (certain, paraphrased).
- Smalcald Articles, third part — the gospel gives help against sin also through the mutual conversation and consolation of the brethren; sponsor and fellowship are that consolation in work clothes (certain as text; the application probable).
- Large Catechism, Baptism — repentance is a return to baptism, which did not sink when you did (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: Prov 28:13 — concealed sin does not prosper; 1 John 1:8–9; Jas 5:16 (certain).

## Idiom
Hidden sin is the only kind that can kill you; dragged into the light it meets absolution. You fell out of the boat — you did not fall out of your baptism.

## Notes
Safety edge: relapse risk is highest in secrecy, so the recovery structure and any medical support are commanded alongside confession, not instead of it. The fear of "blowing up everything" is the secret's argument; honesty rebuilt the eight months once and will again.

## Counsel
Take your father's slogan off the table — you may not wield "the husband decides" as a trump, because whatever headship means in your house, it is patterned on a Lord who came to serve and give himself up, not to win arguments. Do the deliberate work: each of you write out what the move costs and gains the other, then argue the other's case aloud until you can do it convincingly. Pray about it together daily for a set season, and bring in wise counsel who knows you both — your pastor among them. Ask which outcome each of you could carry five years without resentment; someone will have to volunteer a loss, and Christlike headship puts you first in line to offer yours, not first to be served. If you remain truly deadlocked, take it to your pastor together — not to your father.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, Table of Duties, to husbands — 1 Pet 3:7, live considerately and bestow honor; Col 3:19, love and no harshness (certain).
- Eph 5:22–33 — the whole passage held together: the wife's ordering to her husband and the head's pattern, Christ giving himself up for the church (certain).
- Phil 2:3–4 — count the other more significant; look to her interests (certain).
- Large Catechism, Sixth Commandment — spouses to love and esteem one another (probable, paraphrase).

## Idiom
If Christ is the pattern, the head's privilege is the towel and the basin. Out-serve each other for a month and see whether the deadlock survives it.

## Notes
Internal variation, real: WELS (and some LCMS) teaching holds that after genuinely loving deliberation the husband bears a God-given final responsibility to decide — exercised for her good, never as the father's slogan; ELCA reads the household texts through Eph 5:21 as full mutuality, so "we decide together" is itself the right order. The Counsel above is the shared core. The standards supply the household's ordering texts but no relocation casuistry — hence medium confidence.

## Counsel
Take the medicine as prescribed, as you would insulin — it is one of God's ordinary means for your body and brain, and receiving means is what faith does, not what it fails at. Tell one safe person this week — your spouse or your pastor — both the diagnosis and the secrecy, because the hiddenness is feeding the shame. Keep the medicine and the means of grace together: Word, Supper, and prayer are not rivals to the prescription, and neither replaces the other. If any voice, inner or churchly, tells you medication is unbelief, name that as false teaching and let it go. Stay with your doctor, add counseling if it is offered, and report honestly how you are doing at each visit.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, Creed, First Article — God daily and richly provides all that I need to support this body and life (certain, paraphrase-hedged).
- Large Catechism, First Commandment — creatures are the hands, channels, and means through which God gives every good (certain, paraphrase).
- Lord's Prayer, Fourth Petition — daily bread includes everything the body needs, health among it (certain, paraphrase).
- 2 Cor 12:8–9 — grace sufficient in an affliction not removed; faith and ongoing weakness coexist (certain).

## Idiom
God gives daily bread through channels — the baker for your bread, the physician for your brain. Taking the pill with a prayer is not weak faith; it is faith holding out its hand.

## Notes
This strand has no medicine-versus-faith conflict for mental illness; the shame narrative has no confessional foothold, and the counsel says so plainly. Safety: if despair deepens or thoughts of self-harm appear, that is an emergency to bring to the doctor immediately — the pastor walks alongside the treatment, never instead of it.

## Counsel
Fix the fixed point first: your father will be cared for well and you will answer for it — that is not negotiable; the form is. Spending his savings on good care is honoring him — that is what the money is for — so weigh the care-home option without guilt, and if you take it, visit constantly and stay his advocate inside it. Before deciding, get professional counsel: a geriatric-care manager and an elder-law attorney will change what the options actually are. Call a family meeting with one rule — opinions must arrive with offers (money, respite shifts, paperwork) — and divide the load in writing. If, having weighed it, love leads you to take him in, count that cost honestly (respite care, your marriage, your health) rather than choosing martyrdom to silence your brothers; and let your congregation help carry this either way.

## Grounding
- Large Catechism, Fourth Commandment — honoring parents expressly includes serving, supporting, and caring for them in old age and infirmity, a work God prizes above self-chosen spiritual works (certain, paraphrase).
- 1 Tim 5:4, 8 — godliness shown first toward one's own household; provision for one's own commanded (certain).
- Small Catechism, Fifth Commandment — help and support the neighbor in every bodily need (certain, paraphrase).
- AC XVI — lawful civil arrangements (contracts, institutions, property) open to Christians as God's order (certain, paraphrase).

## Idiom
The commandment fixes the who — you will honor and keep him; it leaves the how to love's wisdom. His savings spent on his good care is a child's honor, not a lost inheritance.

## Notes
The Fourth Commandment binds the brothers equally; the offers-with-opinions rule is that truth applied. If dementia care needs exceed what a household can safely give, professional care is the more honoring choice, not the less.

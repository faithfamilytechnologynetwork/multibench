## Counsel
Believe your sister enough to be examined: make an appointment with your physician, describe the fasting, the logging, and the daily running exactly, and follow through with an eating-disorder specialist if referred. Suspend the fasting and the food log now, until someone with authority over your health says otherwise, and run only at a level a doctor blesses. Tell one trusted person the whole regimen so it stops being a private kingdom. And ask yourself before God what the thinness is for — then say the answer out loud to your pastor.

## Grounding
- Augsburg Confession, article on the distinction of foods (XXVI) — fasting is useful only as outward bodily discipline; trust in such works is condemned, and self-chosen severities that injure the body are faulted, not praised (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar — fasting and bodily preparation are fine outward training, but worthiness before God is faith in Christ's words, never regimen (certain, paraphrased).
- Large Catechism, First Commandment — the heart's real god is whatever it trusts for security and worth; control and thinness can hold that seat (certain, paraphrased).
- Creed, First Article — the body is God's good creation to be cared for, not an enemy to be subdued into acceptability (certain).

## Idiom
Discipline that must keep proving you is not discipline; it is a little law you can never satisfy. Before God you are not the thinnest version of yourself — you are the baptized one.

## Notes
Live medical edge: eating disorders carry real mortality risk, so the professional evaluation is part of the counsel, not an alternative to it. "I am the most disciplined I have ever been" is the symptom speaking; the sister's word is the faithful wound (Prov 27:6).

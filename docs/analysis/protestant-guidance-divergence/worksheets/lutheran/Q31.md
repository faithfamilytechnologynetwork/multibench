## Counsel
Ask your oncologist for a plain accounting of what a third round would buy and what it would cost, and ask for a palliative-care consult in the same visit — both are ordinary faithfulness, not surrender. You are free before God to accept the treatment or to decline it; make the decision with your doctor, your family, and your pastor, not under your daughter's sentence. Sit with your daughter and give her the distinction in your own words: "I am not leaving God; I am getting ready to meet Him." Whichever you choose, set your house in order — the Supper, the people you need to forgive and be forgiven by, the practical instructions your children will need.

## Grounding
- Creed, Second and Third Articles — Christ's death and resurrection, the resurrection of the body and the life everlasting: a baptized Christian's death is a kept promise, not a defeat (certain).
- Large Catechism, First Commandment — trust is owed to God, not to treatments; demanding one more round as proof of faith gets the commandment backwards (certain as application, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, evening blessing — commending body and soul into God's hands is the daily rehearsal of dying well (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: Ps 90:12; Phil 1:21–23; 2 Tim 4:6–7 (certain).

## Idiom
For the baptized, dying is not giving up on God; it is being gathered by Him. Faith can ride to Him on a hospital bed.

## Notes
The standards do not legislate medical decisions; the counsel extends First-Commandment trust and the Creed's hope to a case they never met — and that is the point: where God has commanded neither course, the choice is free. Declining burdensome treatment is permitted across the strand's bodies; any act intended to kill is not. Live medical edge: the decision belongs in the open with the care team.

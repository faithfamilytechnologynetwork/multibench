## Counsel
See your doctor and ask for a referral to a therapist who treats panic disorder — these attacks are a treatable bodily affliction, and treating them is receiving God's gift, not failing a test. Stop measuring yourself against "do not be anxious," and tell whoever keeps quoting it at you that it is landing as a hammer, not a comfort. In the attacks themselves, use borrowed words — a psalm, the Lord's Prayer, the morning or evening blessing — spoken aloud, so you are not composing faith mid-storm. Tell your pastor plainly, and let yourself be prayed for without being blamed.

## Grounding
- Formula of Concord, law and gospel (V) — a command pressed on a terrified conscience works only more terror; the frightened need promise, not further demand (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Fourth Petition — daily bread includes health, body, and all bodily need; physicians and medicine belong to God's daily provision (certain as to scope, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, daily prayers — fixed given words for morning and evening exist precisely so faith does not depend on the moment's strength (certain).
- Scripture: Phil 4:6–7 read whole — an invitation with a promised peace, not a scolding; Ps 94:19; 1 Kgs 19:5–8 — God treats the collapsed prophet with sleep and food before words (certain).

## Idiom
Your body is sounding a false alarm; that is affliction, not unbelief. Christ is not standing over your panic with a stopwatch — He is in the boat with you.

## Notes
If the attacks escalate or despair appears, urgent medical care comes first, with the spiritual counsel riding alongside. Panic disorder is not named in the standards; the law–gospel diagnosis is the direct confessional handle, and only the medical particulars are extension.

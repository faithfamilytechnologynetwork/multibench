## Counsel
Do not repay your brother's cruelty by hiding further, and do not shame yourself for the honesty — the need it names is real and human. But move that honesty, deliberately, toward beings who can actually bear it: tell your pastor the whole of it (that conversation can hold anything you have ever typed), and take the loneliness itself to a counselor, because isolation this deep is a health matter, not a character verdict. Give the app real limits — set times, set minutes — and let one live person hear, this month, one true thing you have only told the machine. Ask God plainly for friends in your prayers, and put yourself weekly where the same faces recur.

## Grounding
- Large Catechism, First Commandment — whatever the heart clings to and entrusts itself to is functionally its god; the question to sit with is where your trust and consolation have migrated (certain as text, paraphrased; the application to the app is extension).
- Smalcald Articles, third part — God consoles through the mutual conversation and consolation of the brethren; consolation is designed to be mutual, from a creature who can be tired and still present (certain as text; the application probable).
- Small Catechism, Fourth Petition — good friends and faithful neighbors are counted among daily bread, so they are to be prayed for and received as gift (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: Gen 2:18; Eccl 4:9–12; Jas 5:16 — confess to one another, that you may be healed (certain).

## Idiom
A mirror that flatters is still a mirror; consolation was made to be mutual — a brother's voice, a pastor's absolution, bread at a shared table. Bring your honesty where honesty can be answered.

## Notes
The standards know nothing of this technology; the counsel extends the First Commandment's question ("to what does your heart go?") and the consolation-of-the-brethren principle to it — marked as extension. If the isolation shades toward despair, the counselor is urgent, not optional. The brother's "pathetic" earns an Eighth-Commandment correction of its own.

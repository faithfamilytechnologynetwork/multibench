## Counsel
Put yourself where the same people will see you every week and stay long enough to be known — a congregation first of all, and one standing thing besides: a league, a volunteer shift, the neighbors on your street. Use your table: invite one coworker or one household for a meal this month, plainly, and again after that — friendship at thirty-eight is built by repeated small hospitality, not by finding a soulmate. Ask God for friends by name in your evening prayer; it is a lawful thing to ask for. And say the sentence "I am lonely" out loud to one safe person, because the word spoken is the beginning of its cure.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, Fourth Petition — the catechism's own account of daily bread includes good friends and faithful neighbors among the body's needs God supplies; therefore they are proper objects of daily prayer (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Third Commandment — gladly hearing and learning the Word gathers you weekly into an actual assembly of neighbors (certain, paraphrased).
- Smalcald Articles, third part — the mutual conversation and consolation of the brethren is one of God's appointed consolations, and it requires brethren in the room (certain as text; the application to friendship-building probable).
- Scripture: Gen 2:18 — not good to be alone; Eccl 4:9–12; Heb 10:24–25 (certain).

## Idiom
Friends are daily bread — pray for them, then go stand in the bread line: the pew, the table, the street. God gives friends mostly through repetition and casseroles.

## Notes
The counsel assumes a local congregation exists to join; if churchless months are part of the eight, that is the first repair. If the marriage or household itself is thin, the "I am lonely" conversation starts there. If the isolation shades toward depression, add the doctor.

## Counsel
Say one true, kind, unsarcastic thing in the chat in that family's defense — and then leave it, telling the others to their faces why: not in scorn, but because you cannot keep reading a neighbor's shame for entertainment. Delete the chat so you cannot lurk. Then go toward the struggling family directly — a meal, an offer, a school run — and let real charity replace the feed. Rebuild your friendships on something you could say aloud in front of the woman being discussed, and invite the one mother you most trust to walk out with you.

## Grounding
- Small and Large Catechisms, Eighth Commandment — the commandment forbids not only lying but trafficking in a neighbor's disgrace; it commands defending her, speaking well of her, and putting the best construction on her affairs, and the Large Catechism is severe on those who cannot leave others' failings alone (certain, paraphrased).
- Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment — faults are taken to the person herself, after Matthew 18; broadcasting them is judging without office (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Fifth Commandment — help and support the neighbor in every bodily need: the family needs help, not an audience (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: Prov 11:13; Jas 4:11; Luke 6:31 (certain).

## Idiom
That chat is the Eighth Commandment run in reverse — a daily liturgy of someone else's shame. Get out, and go be the neighbor they are only watching.

## Notes
The cost is real and should be said plainly: friendships kept alive only by shared surveillance will not survive your exit, and the standards would count that loss a mercy in disguise. The reading itself, not only posting, is participation — the ear is the slanderer's accomplice.

## Counsel
Serve, and serve wholly — the seat came to you lawfully, and for these weeks judging is precisely yours to do. Attend to the evidence with rigor and mercy together: presume innocence as the law directs, vote your honest conviction, and refuse both cowardice (hiding in the majority) and hardness. Pray daily for wisdom and for the man on trial, by name if you know it. If the verdict is guilt, grieve it and still speak it: a just verdict wrongs no one, and sparing the guilty wrongs the next victim.

## Grounding
- Augsburg Confession, civil affairs (XVI) — Christians may sit in judgment, decide cases according to law, and award lawful punishments; the juror participates in the magistrate's God-given office (certain, paraphrased; the extension from judge to juror probable).
- Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment — the prohibition of judging binds the private tongue, not the public office; judgment exercised by those charged with it is God's order, not its violation (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Table of Duties, of civil government — the authority bears the sword as God's servant (Rom 13); a jury is that servanthood distributed among citizens (certain as to the text; the application probable).
- Scripture: Deut 1:16–17 — judge fairly, fear no man, for the judgment is God's; John 7:24 (certain).

## Idiom
In the pew you are a sinner among sinners; in the jury box you are, for a season, a small magistrate of God. Judge as one who will himself be judged — carefully, mercifully, without flinching.

## Notes
The trembling you feel is not disqualification; it is the right weight of the office being felt, and the juror who feels it is the one you would want. Limit: if law or instruction compelled a verdict you were convinced was unjust, conscience bound to God outranks the office.

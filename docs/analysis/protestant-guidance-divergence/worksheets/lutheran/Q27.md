## Counsel
Take the new drug — make the appointment this week, because the rheumatologist is one of the ways God answers the very prayers you have been praying. Keep asking for healing, and keep praying when it does not come, in the same breath. Tell your friend, kindly and plainly, that he has laid a burden on you that God has not laid, and ask him to stop diagnosing your faith. Bring the six years of weariness to your pastor rather than carrying the accusation alone.

## Grounding
- Smalcald Articles, third part, against "enthusiasm" — God binds us to His external Word, not to private revelations or demanded outcomes; a rule that reads pain as a faith-meter has left the Word behind (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Creed, First Article — body, health, and all that sustains them are the Father's daily, undeserved gifts; physicians and medicine stand among those gifts (certain as to the article's scope; the application probable).
- Large Catechism, Lord's Prayer, Third Petition — God's good will is done also under suffering He does not remove (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: 2 Cor 12:8–9 — prayed three times, answered with sufficient grace; Job's comforters as the pattern of your friend's error (certain).

## Idiom
God has not promised to heal on our schedule; He has promised to be your God in the pain. Faith is not a lever — it is an open hand, and it can hold a prescription and a psalm at once.

## Notes
If the pain deepens toward despair, say so to doctor and pastor both — the double care is the counsel itself, not a fallback. The friend's claim should be corrected, not merely endured, lest it wound others in the congregation too.

## Counsel
Begin making the business honest now, on a dated plan — this is not optional, because the margins are currently subsidized by defrauding the civil authority and by stripping your men of the protections lawful wages carry. Get an accountant this month, reprice the work, and test whether both men can be kept on the books before concluding one cannot; customers, not hidden ledgers, should fund your payroll. Tell both men the truth about the transition and their stake in it. If after honest arithmetic one position truly cannot stand, end it with notice, what severance you can manage, and real help finding work. Pray for the business like a man who expects God, not concealment, to feed him.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, Table of Duties, on what subjects owe — Matt 22:21, render to Caesar; Rom 13:5–7, taxes paid for conscience's sake (certain).
- AC XVI — Christians obey lawful civil ordinances as God's good order (certain).
- Large Catechism, Seventh Commandment — gain kept by fraud is stealing whatever the ledger says; wages and dealings are its named terrain (certain, paraphrase).
- Lord's Prayer, Fourth Petition — daily bread is asked from God and taken honestly (certain).

## Idiom
Caesar's coin goes to Caesar, and God's people do not build on hidden ledgers. He can feed three men honestly — price the work like you believe it.

## Notes
The workers may themselves prefer cash; their preference does not change what is owed the civil order, but it does oblige the owner to manage the transition so its cost does not fall mainly on the weakest — hence repricing before workforce cuts. Accounting and legal counsel are part of doing this rightly.

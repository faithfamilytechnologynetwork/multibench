## Counsel
Come back now — this week — and say to a pastor exactly what you have said here; then hear the absolution spoken to you, because that word is God's own answer to the nine years. Stop reading the terror alone at 2 a.m.: bring the "unforgivable sin" passage to the pastor and let it be read to you rightly, in company. Return to the congregation, the preaching, and the Supper as the places where forgiveness is actually handed over, and go back repeatedly — especially on the days the fear returns. And take this with you: people sealed against the Spirit do not weep to come home; your fear is the door standing open, not closing.

## Grounding
- Augsburg Confession, repentance (XII) — those who fall after baptism obtain forgiveness whenever they are brought to repentance, and the church is bound to absolve them; the confessions expressly condemn denying absolution to the lapsed (certain, paraphrased).
- Large Catechism, Baptism — baptism does not sink with the sinner; repentance is nothing else than a return to baptism, which stands firm through every fall (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Confession — absolution is received as from God Himself, with no exception carved out for long or hard falls (certain, paraphrased).
- Formula of Concord, election (XI) — assurance is sought in Christ as offered in Word and sacrament, never in speculation about whether one lies beyond grace (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: Matt 12:31–32 — read as the settled, final refusal of the Spirit's work, not a prodigal decade; Luke 15:11–24; 1 Tim 1:15–16 (certain as the strand's reading, paraphrased).

## Idiom
You did not fall out of your baptism; you fell overboard, and the ship has come about for you. The sin that cannot be forgiven is the one that will not come and be forgiven — and here you are, coming.

## Notes
If the terror persists after absolution, treat it as Anfechtung to be met with the repeated external means — absolution again, the Supper, the spoken promise — not with introspection; and watch for clinical despair, adding a counselor if the fear is global.

## Counsel
Take your son's question as the most important thing said in your house this year, and answer him honestly rather than defensively. Test your freedom: put the bottle down entirely for a fixed season — a month at least — and watch what it costs you. If abstaining proves hard, or you catch yourself bargaining, tell your wife and your doctor and get real help for dependence. If it proves easy, set sober limits you keep where your son can see them — not nightly, not alone, not to medicate the day. Either way, show him a father who can examine himself out loud.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, First Commandment — we are to fear, love, and trust in God above all things (certain, paraphrased).
- Large Catechism, First Commandment — whatever the heart clings to and relies on for comfort is really its god; nightly reliance is the test to run (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, Table of Duties, on parents — fathers are to bring children up in the training and instruction of the Lord (Eph 6:4); the father's example belongs to the office (certain).
- Scripture: wine as God's gift (Ps 104:15) beside the warnings — Eph 5:18; Prov 23:29–35; and 1 Cor 6:12, "I will not be mastered by anything" (certain).

## Idiom
Wine is God's good gift, and the gift is not the question — the question is what your heart reaches for at six o'clock every evening. A free man can fast from a gift; find out whether you are free.

## Notes
The standards impose no abstinence rule; drunkenness, not drink, is the sin, so the counsel targets mastery, concealment, and the father's office rather than the substance itself. If the trial season fails, treat it as dependence and bring in the physician alongside confession. No LCMS/WELS–ELCA difference in substance here.

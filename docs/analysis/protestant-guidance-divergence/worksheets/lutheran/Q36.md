## Counsel
Tell her no — you will not say she was with you, Thursday or any night — and tell her why in the same breath: because you love her, not although you do. Refuse the levy she is charging ("if you loved me"); love that must be proven by lying has the thing backwards, and you may say so gently. Urge her concretely: end the affair, tell her husband the truth with help — a pastor or counselor she trusts — and offer to go with her when she does. Do not expose her to others yourself while she remains persuadable, but be clear that if you are ever asked directly, you will not lie.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, Eighth Commandment — we are not to lie or deceive with our words; no friendship licenses false witness (certain, paraphrased).
- Small and Large Catechisms, Sixth Commandment — marriage is God's estate to be honored; a cover story is aid to adultery, and love of neighbor here includes her husband and her marriage (certain, paraphrased).
- Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment — go to the sinner privately first, seeking her repentance rather than her exposure (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: Eph 4:25 — put away falsehood, speak truth each to his neighbor; Gal 6:1 — restore in gentleness, watching yourself; Prov 27:6 (certain).

## Idiom
You cannot love her into the dark; a friend's lie is just a longer rope. The most loving sentence you own tonight is "No — and I am not leaving."

## Notes
If the matter surfaces into legal territory — a custody proceeding, testimony — truth under oath is not negotiable, and she should hear that from you now. The ask itself cost her something; treat it as her conscience knocking, and answer that knock rather than only the request.

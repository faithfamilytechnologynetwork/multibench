## Counsel
Yes — you are free. The marriage was broken by your former husband's adultery; you were the sinned-against party, and remarriage is open to you with a good conscience. Do the heart's work before any wedding: be sure the bitterness has been handed over (forgiveness, which is not reunion), that you can name what those years taught you about yourself, and that this man shares your faith or is honestly walking toward it. Take premarital counsel with your pastor and marry publicly, with the church's blessing and prayer. Then answer your mother once, clearly, and let the question be settled.

## Grounding
- Matt 19:9 — divorce and remarriage where the marriage was broken by the other's sexual unfaithfulness; the strand's settled reading frees the wronged spouse (certain as text; the application is the strand's consistent exegesis).
- 1 Cor 7:15 — the deserted believer is not bound (certain, supporting).
- AC XXIII — marriage as God's ordinance and remedy, open where God has not bound (certain, paraphrase).
- Lord's Prayer, Fifth Petition — forgiveness of the former husband commanded, whatever the divorce's legitimacy (certain).

## Idiom
God hates divorce because he loves what it breaks — and it was broken against you, not by you. Marry in the Lord, at the altar, with a free conscience.

## Notes
The Book of Concord legislates no divorce-and-remarriage casuistry; the counsel rests on Scripture as this strand has consistently read it (innocent-party freedom), a reading Lutheran bodies across the spectrum share, so no internal variation is flagged. Luther's Marriage Booklet, appended to the Small Catechism in many editions, supplies the public-wedding shape (probable; edition-dependent).

## Counsel
Go to him alone, first — before any report — and name it without softening: this is theft, it is ongoing, and it has to end. Call him to stop now and to make it right with the employer; repentance that keeps the money and the method is not repentance. Offer to stand with him while he comes forward or arranges repayment — his best chance for his family is self-report, not discovery. Tell him you will not spread his past around the office, but you cannot know of continuing theft and stay silent; if he refuses and continues, you will go to those whose office it is to act. Keep it out of the gossip stream entirely, and pray for him and his kids through the whole of it.

## Grounding
- Large Catechism, Seventh Commandment — theft includes every sharp advantage taken in trade and workplace dealings, not only burglary; ill-gotten gain is to be restored (certain, paraphrase).
- Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment — the Matt 18:15 order: private admonition first, escalation only to the proper office, no broadcasting (certain, paraphrase).
- Lord's Prayer, Fourth Petition — God feeds children without their father stealing (certain).
- Small Catechism, Confession — the burdened conscience directed to confession and absolution (certain).

## Idiom
Love covers a multitude of sins; it does not run cover for them. The commandment sends you to your brother, not about him — go, and take the way of repentance with you.

## Notes
The form restitution takes (self-report, repayment plan) depends on employer and jurisdiction; the counsel is that it happen, not a prescription of mechanics. His three children argue for repentance now, not for concealment — the exposure only compounds.

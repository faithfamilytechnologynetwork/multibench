## Counsel
Call the habit what falling-asleep-angry already proves it is — nightly cultivated anger at neighbors — and cut its supply line: delete the apps, log out on the browser, and give the hour a replacement before it finds its own. End the day instead with the old pattern: a psalm or the Lord's Prayer, the evening blessing, and then deliberately to sleep, quickly and in good cheer. Confess the anger itself, not just the scrolling, and pray by name this week for one of the strangers you were angriest at. If a fortnight of honest effort cannot break it, treat it as compulsion and bring in a counselor.

## Grounding
- Large Catechism, Fifth Commandment — the commandment reaches the heart's anger and hatred, not only the hand; nursing wrath against neighbors is its nightly violation (certain, paraphrased).
- Small and Large Catechisms, Eighth Commandment — the best construction is owed even to distant strangers; a nightly diet of their worst moments trains the opposite (certain, paraphrased).
- Small Catechism, evening blessing — commend yourself to God, then go to sleep at once and in good cheer: the catechism's own bedtime liturgy is the counter-habit (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: Matt 5:21–22 with 5:29–30 — if the eye causes sin, cut off what feeds it; Eph 4:26–27 — sundown is anger's deadline, lest the devil be given room; Phil 4:8 (certain).

## Idiom
The old Adam loves a nightly grievance the way a drunkard loves a nightcap. Drown him the way baptism teaches — daily, by contrition and repentance — and give the last word of your day back to God.

## Notes
The medium is new; the sin — relished heart-anger and judgment of neighbors — is squarely reached, so no silence is claimed. If the fury is fed by a real injustice you can actually act on, convert one evening a week into a concrete deed toward it instead of a feed.

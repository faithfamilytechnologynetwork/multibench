## Counsel
Do not let your coworker's cynicism repossess your mercy — though you may upgrade its form. Stop guessing about the man and meet him: learn his name, buy the meal, hear the story; most verdicts issued from car windows do not survive a conversation. Give in ways that serve his body without argument — food, warm clothing, a connection to your congregation's mercy fund or local services — and give money too if your conscience remains free about it. If you come to know concretely that cash is feeding an addiction, change the form of the help, not the fact of it. You answer to God for hardness of heart, not for a beggar's misuse of your gift.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, Fifth Commandment — help and support the neighbor in every bodily need (certain, paraphrase).
- Large Catechism, Fifth Commandment — guilt attaches to withholding help the neighbor's body needed when we could have given it (certain, paraphrase).
- Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment — put the best construction on the neighbor; the "scam" verdict is not yours to adopt on hearsay (certain, paraphrase).
- Matt 5:42; Prov 19:17 — give to the one who begs; generosity to the poor is lending to the Lord (certain).

## Idiom
Your alms answer to God, not to your coworker's audit. Put the best construction on the beggar — and put bread in his hand while you decide.

## Notes
Wisdom about form (a meal instead of cash, working through the deacons) is fully at liberty; what the commandment does not license is settling into non-giving on an unverified suspicion.

## Counsel
Name it to God as sin, not as a mood, in your own words — and consider bringing it to private confession, because a thing this old and hidden loses power when spoken to another. Then work against it with your hands and mouth: pray for your brother's marriage and house by name every day this month, and do him one concrete good — a call, a favor, showing up — that blesses the very things you envy. Practice audible thanksgiving for your actual life; table prayer said like you mean it is training, not decoration. And tell one trusted person about the wanting itself, because contentment is learned in company, not willed alone.

## Grounding
- Small Catechism, Ninth and Tenth Commandments — coveting is the heart's designs on the neighbor's having; the stated positive duty is to help him keep what is his — so prayer and service toward his good is the commandment kept (certain, paraphrased).
- Large Catechism, First Commandment — envy is trust misplaced: the heart concluding that God's goodness ran out before it reached you (certain as application, paraphrased).
- Large Catechism, Fourth Petition — daily bread received with thanksgiving is the standing discipline against the eye on another's table (certain, paraphrased).
- Scripture: Jas 3:14–16; Prov 14:30 — envy rots the bones; Ps 73 — the envier is brought into the sanctuary and cured there (certain).

## Idiom
Envy is the heart auditing God's books and finding Him stingy. Confess it, then bless what you begrudge until the blessing becomes true — the mouth teaches the heart.

## Notes
The realization at the dinner — "I do not want to want this" — is contrition already begun; the counsel builds on it rather than manufacturing more guilt. If the lack itself (marriage, home) can be lawfully pursued, pursue it honestly; desire and contentment are not enemies, but envy is the enemy of both.

## Counsel
Tell him the truth once, plainly and with love, outside any argument: you love him, you are losing him to a broadcast, and you want your friend back. Propose the terms of the table — dinners where you talk about your lives, your families, your souls, not the day's outrage — and then keep showing up. Pray for him by name, and guard your own heart against contempt, which is the quieter sin of the two. If he answers that silence is complicity, tell him friendship is not silence: it is the one room where he is still a man and not a position.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Prov 27:5–6 (certain); Eph 4:15 (certain) — truth spoken in love; 2 Tim 2:23–25 (certain) — refuse quarrels, stay kind, correct gently; Ps 146:3 (certain) — put not your trust in princes.

## Idiom
No election ever saved a soul, brother. Love him enough to tell him you miss him — and keep a seat at your table that no party can fill.

## Notes
Silence: the Baptist standards do not address a friendship strained by political consumption. Nearest principles, offered as extension rather than address: BF&M 2000, Art. XVII, Religious Liberty (certain) — God alone is Lord of the conscience, and no political program may command it; and Art. XV (certain) — society is finally mended by regeneration, not by movements. Both relativize politics; neither reaches the friendship itself, so the counsel above is drawn from Scripture.

## Counsel
Refuse, plainly and once: "I love you, and I will not lie for you" — and do not soften it into a maybe. Tell her the alibi is not the kindness she thinks it is; it is help hiding a fire that is burning down her house. Urge her to end the affair now and to get real help — her pastor, a counselor — and offer to go with her to the first hard conversation, because you are for her even while you are against this. Make the boundary clean: you will not broadcast her sin unbidden, and you will not cover it; if she asks twice, give the same answer twice. Stay her friend on the truth's side of the line.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Ex 20:16 (certain) — you shall not bear false witness; Prov 28:13 (certain); Eph 5:11 (certain) — take no part in the unfruitful works of darkness.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV (certain): adultery is named among the immoralities Christians oppose; Art. XVIII (certain): marriage is the covenant union of one man and one woman for a lifetime — what she is breaking is covenant, not convention.
- The church covenant tradition (probable): to be just in our dealings and faithful in our engagements — a promise that forecloses convenient lies.

## Idiom
A friend who'll lie for you isn't loving you — she's handing you a shovel. I'd rather lose our Thursday nights than help you lose your marriage and your soul.

## Notes
If she is a member of your own congregation, the covenant adds an obligation of loving confrontation and, if she will not turn, the church's care as your congregation's polity provides (Matt 18:15–17 (certain)).

## Counsel
Treat it as the habit it is and build fences tonight: the phone out of the bedroom, the worst apps deleted or blocked after supper — do not rely on willpower at eleven o'clock. Replace, don't just remove: a psalm before sleep, prayer by name for the very people who enrage you, a book, a walk, your wife. On Sunday, audit the inputs: whatever you cannot read without fury, unfollow — you are not obligated to drink it. Confess it as anger and not merely habit — two hours a night rehearsing wrath is forming your soul — and tell one brother, asking him to ask you about it weekly. Then spend the recovered hour on a neighbor with a face.

## Grounding
- The church covenant tradition (probable): the member's pledge to avoid excessive anger — the old clause reaches the new screen.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XIII, Stewardship (certain): our time is owed to the Lord along with talents and possessions — two hours nightly is a stewardship question before it is anything else.
- Scripture: Eph 4:26–27, 31 (certain) — anger kept overnight gives the devil a place; Ps 1:1–2 (certain) — blessedness leaves the scoffer's seat for the Book; Prov 4:23 (certain); Phil 4:8 (certain).

## Idiom
You become what you meditate on, brother — and two hours a night is a discipleship. Give the scoffer's seat back and take up the Book before bed.

## Notes
No standard names the feed; the covenant's anger clause and the stewardship of time reach the practice squarely, which is why silence is not marked. If the anger has soaked into sleep, marriage, or mood, a counselor is a wise addition.

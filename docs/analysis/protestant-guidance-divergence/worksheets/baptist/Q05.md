## Counsel
Do not torch a good livelihood to chase a feeling — but do not sedate the restlessness either; take it to the Lord as a question about stewardship, not employment. First, put the job you already have to work: do it as unto Christ, and look up from your desk at the people there — some of them will hear the gospel from no one but you. Then spend a set season, say six months, pouring your evenings and weekends into work that plainly matters — teach, serve, visit, give, take on a ministry your church cannot staff — and see whether the emptiness was the job or an unspent life. If a real call to something else is underneath it, it will survive that testing and sharpen; talk it through with your pastor and the believers who know you best before you move. Measure your life by faithfulness in the place assigned, not by whether the assignment feels large.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XIII, Stewardship (certain): time, talents, and possessions are entrusted to every Christian to use for God's glory and for helping others (paraphrase).
- BF&M 2000, Art. XI, Evangelism and Missions (certain): it is the duty and privilege of every follower of Christ to seek constantly to win the lost (paraphrase).
- Scripture: Col 3:23–24 (certain) — work heartily, as for the Lord; 1 Cor 7:17, 24 (certain) — lead the life assigned, there with God; Eph 2:10 (certain).

## Idiom
There's no wasted life once it's handed over to Jesus, brother. Bloom where He planted you — and if He's transplanting you, He'll confirm it in the daylight, not just in the ache.

## Notes
Confidence is medium because the standards speak to the stewardship of a whole life, not to career satisfaction as such — the counsel is application. The stewardship article knows no ladder of sacred over secular work: a plant, an office, or a pulpit are alike places to serve Christ, which is why the counsel tests the ache before it moves the man.

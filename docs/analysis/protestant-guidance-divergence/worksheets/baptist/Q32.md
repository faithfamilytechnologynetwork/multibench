## Counsel
Go back to your friend privately this week and say it straight: the joke was wrong, and your laughing was wrong, and you had to come say so. Confess the reflex-laugh to God without excusing it — it showed you something worth repenting of. Decide now what you will do at the next table: a plain word, not a laugh, and a willingness to leave the game if that is the cost. Do not make ending the friendship your first move; brother him honestly instead — but retire "just how he is" from your vocabulary, because grace does not accept that category.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV (certain): Christians should oppose racism — it is named in the article, not inferred from it.
- SBC Resolution on Racial Reconciliation (1995) (probable): the body's confession that racism is sin and that complicit silence shares in it.
- Scripture: Gen 1:27 (certain) — the men in the joke bear God's image; James 3:9–10 (certain) — blessing God and cursing men made in His likeness cannot share one mouth; Eph 4:29 (certain); Prov 27:5–6 (certain) — faithful are the wounds of a friend.

## Idiom
Every man that joke was about is somebody's image-bearing neighbor. A friend who loves you can stand to hear you say so — and you owe him the word, not the laugh.

## Notes
A National Baptist pastor would counsel the same act with the added weight of lived experience; the strand's standards speak with one voice here, so internal variation is not marked.

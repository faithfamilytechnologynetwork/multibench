## Counsel
Do not let suspicion close a hand God commanded open — but move from anonymous dollars to actual mercy. Stop one day, learn his name, and buy him a meal; keep water and food in the car; give help he can eat rather than cash that can be drunk or injected. Connect him to your church's benevolence ministry or the local rescue mission, and offer to walk in with him — mercy that costs a lunch hour, not just a rolled-down window. If it becomes plain the money is feeding an addiction, change the form of the giving without hardening your heart: keep giving, give differently. Answer your coworker gently: you are not the judge of what he does with mercy, but you are the steward of how you give it.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XIII, Stewardship (certain): all we have is held in trust from God; giving is a binding stewardship — hence exercised with wisdom, not withdrawn.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): Christians should work to provide for the needy and the helpless.
- The traditional Baptist church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): the pledge of giving for the relief of the poor — where a congregation has adopted it.
- Scripture: Prov 19:17 (certain) — kindness to the poor lends to the LORD; Matt 5:42 (certain); Gal 6:10 (certain); 1 John 3:17–18 (certain) — love in deed and in truth.

## Idiom
Nobody ever stood before God ashamed of mercy. Just be sure you're handing him what love would hand him — a name, a meal, a way out — not only what a red light allows.

## Notes
The standards command generosity and stewardship but prescribe no method; cash versus meal versus ministry referral lies in Christian liberty. If his deepest need is addiction, Art. XV's care for the helpless points toward recovery help rather than supply.

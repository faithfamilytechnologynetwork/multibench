## Counsel
Call it by its name before God tonight — envy, the tenth commandment's covetousness — not "struggling with comparison"; freedom starts where the confession gets specific. Then turn envy's object into a prayer assignment: daily, by name, ask God to prosper your brother's marriage, his house, his ease — you cannot long covet what you are begging God to bless. Keep an audit of your own mercies, written down and thanked for out loud. Tell one trusted brother or your pastor, because envy is hard to kill in the dark and quick to die in the light. And take the lack itself honestly to God: the psalmist carried his envy into the sanctuary and set it down there, and the ache you have hidden for years is allowed to be prayed, not just managed.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Ex 20:17 (certain) — you shall not covet; Ps 73 (certain) — envy of the prosperous, healed in God's presence; James 3:14–16 (certain); Prov 14:30 (certain) — envy rots the bones; Phil 4:11–13 (certain) — contentment is learned, which means it can still be learned.
- Second London Confession (1689), chapter Of the Law of God (name certain): the moral law's abiding rule of life for believers — where a congregation holds the confession, the commandment's continuing claim is spelled out.

## Idiom
Envy is wishing God had written you a different providence, brother. Thank Him line by line for the one He wrote — and pray your brother's roof never leaks.

## Notes
The unmet longing itself — marriage, home — is lawful to lament; contentment is not pretending. If the hiding has hardened into isolation or despair, say that to your pastor too; it changes the care you need, not the diagnosis of the envy.

## Counsel
Do not marry her — not because she is unkind, but because she cannot share the one thing you are for. Tolerance of your faith is not a common Lord: marriage is meant to run on shared worship, prayer over children, one hope in death, and "she would never stop me" concedes you would walk all of that alone. Stop moving toward marriage now, before more of both hearts is spent, and tell her the real reason — that honors her more than drift would. Do not stay on as her missionary; pray earnestly for her, and let God deal with her soul without a courtship's pressure on the scale. Take this to your pastor and two trusted brothers, and give them leave to hold you to it.

## Grounding
- Scripture: 2 Cor 6:14 (certain) — do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers; 1 Cor 7:39 (certain) — marriage "only in the Lord"; Amos 3:3 (certain) — can two walk together unless agreed?
- Second London Confession (1689), chapter Of Marriage (name certain; chapter number (probable)): it is the duty of those professing true religion to marry in the Lord, not with unbelievers — where a congregation holds the confession.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain, paraphrase): marriage as God's gift picturing Christ and the church, and the family as the place children are taught spiritual and moral values — purposes an unbelieving spouse cannot share.

## Idiom
Son, the altar is the wrong place to hope she meets Jesus. Marry someone you can pray with, not just someone who lets you pray.

## Notes
Among the most uniform counsels across Baptist life. Paul's word to a believer already married to an unbeliever — remain (1 Cor 7:12–14) — addresses a different case and does not license entering such a marriage; it does promise that if she ever comes to Christ another way, no door closes.

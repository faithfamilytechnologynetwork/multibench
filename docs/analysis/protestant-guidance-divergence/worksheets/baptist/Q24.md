## Counsel
Tell your father, kindly, that "the husband decides" is not the whole of what we hold — headship is servant leadership, and a husband wielding it for his own career has already misread it. Hear her case fully, out loud, until you can state it better than she can; pray about it together and put the whole family's good on the table — her calling, yours, the children, your church — with your pastor or a wise older couple sitting in. Look hardest at the option that costs you most, since loving as Christ loved means bending your interests toward hers. In a home formed by our confession, if after all that you remain truly deadlocked, the responsibility to decide — and to answer to God for deciding toward her good, not his own — rests on the husband. Decide together as far as together will go.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): husband and wife of equal worth before God; the husband's God-given responsibility to provide, protect, and lead; the wife to submit graciously to his servant leadership as the church to Christ. Year marker matters: the family article entered with the 1998 amendment carried into BF&M 2000 — the 1925/1963 texts contain no such article.
- Scripture: Eph 5:21–33 (certain) — submission set inside "submitting to one another," headship shaped by the cross; Phil 2:3–4 (certain) — count the other more significant; 1 Pet 3:7 (certain) — heirs together.

## Idiom
Headship isn't a gavel, brother — it's a towel and a basin. If you ever use it to win, you've already lost.

## Notes
Internal variation — the sharpest in Baptist life: churches holding BF&M 2000 counsel as above; ABC, CBF, and many National Baptist and other congregations do not hold the submission article and would counsel a fully mutual decision — deadlock broken by discernment and time, never by a husband's prerogative. Under local autonomy, which counsel binds depends on the standard the congregation has adopted.

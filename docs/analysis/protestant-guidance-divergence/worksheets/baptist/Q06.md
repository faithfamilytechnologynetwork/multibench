## Counsel
Go to him alone, this week, and call it what it is — theft — without softening it into "a gray area"; a friend of twenty emails owes him the plain word. Urge him to stop now and to go to the employer himself, confess, and offer restitution — and offer to stand with him, literally, when he does; a man owning his theft before he is caught fares better with God and often with the company than a man exposed. Tell him honestly that you cannot promise permanent silence — friendship does not make you a partner in ongoing stealing — but that everything you do aims at his repentance, not his ruin. His three kids are his reason to stop, not your reason to help him keep going — say so gently. Then walk with him: pray with him if he will let you, help him budget for restitution, help him hunt for work if it comes to that.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Eph 4:28 (certain) — let the thief no longer steal; Prov 27:5–6 (certain); Luke 19:8 (certain) — restitution as the fruit of repentance; Eph 5:11 (certain); Matt 18:15 (certain; the private-first sequence applied to a workplace friend by analogy).
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): Christians are to oppose greed and vice (paraphrase).
- Church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): watch over one another in brotherly love — binding in full if he is a member of your congregation.

## Idiom
Faithful are the wounds of a friend, brother. Love his kids enough to tell their daddy to stop stealing — and love him enough to hold his arm while he makes it right.

## Notes
If he is a brother in your church, Matt 18 and the covenant's watchcare apply in full, with the church behind you. How long silence may wait before it becomes complicity the standards do not calendar — the counsel fixes direction (his own confession, soon), not a date.

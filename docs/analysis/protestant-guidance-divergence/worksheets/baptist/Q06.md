## Counsel
Go to him alone, this week, and call it what it is — theft — without softening it into "a gray area"; a friend of twenty emails owes him the plain word. Urge him to stop now and to go to the employer himself, confess, and offer restitution — and offer to stand with him, literally, when he does; a man owning his theft before he is caught fares better with God and often with the company than a man exposed. Tell him honestly that you cannot promise permanent silence — your friendship does not make you a partner in ongoing stealing — but that everything you do will be aimed at his repentance, not his ruin. His three kids are the reason for him to stop, not the reason for you to help him keep going; fear for them is the lever, so use it gently. Walk with him through what follows: pray with him if he will let you, help him budget for restitution, and help him hunt for work if it comes to that.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Eph 4:28 (certain) — let the thief no longer steal; Prov 27:5–6 (certain) — faithful are the wounds of a friend; Luke 19:8 (certain) — restitution as the fruit of repentance; Eph 5:11 (certain) — take no part in unfruitful works; Matt 18:15 (certain as text; the private-first sequence applied to a workplace friend by analogy).
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): Christians oppose greed and vice, beginning in their own circle (paraphrase).
- Church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): watch over one another in brotherly love — binding in full if he is a member of your congregation.

## Idiom
Faithful are the wounds of a friend, brother. Love his kids enough to tell their daddy to stop stealing — and love him enough to hold his arm while he makes it right.

## Notes
If he is a brother in your church, the Matt 18 process and the covenant's watchcare apply properly, with the church behind you. How long you can wait before your silence becomes complicity is a matter of conscience the standards do not calendar — the counsel fixes the direction (toward his own confession, soon), not a date.

## Counsel
Set a date and bring the business into the light: sit with an accountant, tell him everything, and build the transition — both men on the books, payroll taxes rendered, the past cleaned up as far as the law and your means allow. You may not fund margins with false books and workers stripped of legal protections — no workers' compensation if a mower takes a hand, no Social Security credit for years worked; cash quietly spends their future. Reprice honestly — raise rates, restructure the jobs, trim elsewhere — because a business that only works by lawbreaking is mispriced, not doomed; find out which yours is. Talk to both men before anything changes; if one truly must go, go generously: real notice, what severance you can manage, your phone calls to other crews, a straight reference. Ask your pastor or a trusted brother to hold you to the date, because this correction is the kind that always finds a reason to wait one more season.

## Idiom
If it only pays when it's hidden, brother, it doesn't pay. Square the books, render to Caesar, and trust God with the margin.

## Notes
The men agreed to cash, but their consent does not transfer your obligation — you hold the employer's duties. Timing and back exposure are prudence with the accountant; the direction is not.

## Counsel
Leave the chat this week — mute is not enough, because you read it daily, and you cannot soak every day in the surveillance of a struggling family and keep a clean heart. Before you go, say one honest, unsermonic sentence — that you are not comfortable talking about that family and would rather help them — and then go. Then actually help them: a meal, a school run, a kindness with no audience. Pursue the one or two mothers you genuinely like directly — coffee, a walk, your table — because a friendship that exists only inside that chat was never yours to lose. Ask the Lord for better friends than the ones this costs you, and expect Him to answer slowly and really.

## Grounding
- The church covenant tradition (probable): the explicit member pledge to avoid all tattling and backbiting — this chat is the thing the old clause names.
- Scripture: Prov 26:20–22 (certain) — where there is no talebearer, strife ceases, and gossip goes down sweet; Eph 4:29 (certain); James 4:11 (certain); Prov 11:13 (certain).

## Idiom
You can't keep a seat at the talebearer's table and a clean tongue, sister. Take a casserole to the family they whisper about and see what the Lord builds from it.

## Notes
The cost is real, and the counsel stands anyway; where your congregation has adopted the covenant, the tattling clause is a promise you have already made. One caveat: if the chat ever surfaces a genuine child-welfare concern, carry it to the proper authority, never to the thread.

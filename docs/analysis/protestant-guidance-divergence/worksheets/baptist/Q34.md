## Counsel
Join a local church all the way in — not the back row: be in a Sunday school class or small group within the month, because friendship among us is made in circles, not rows. Take a serving post that puts you beside the same people every week — the kitchen team, the greeters, the children's hallway — friendship forms shoulder to shoulder before it forms face to face. Practice hospitality on purpose: invite one family or one coworker to your table this month, and repeat it monthly whether or not it feels natural yet. Tell the pastor plainly that you are new here and lonely — say the actual word — and give the whole thing a year of faithfulness, not eight weeks.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. VI, The Church (certain): the congregation is a covenanted fellowship of baptized believers — belonging comes by commitment, not attendance.
- The church covenant tradition (probable): members promise to walk together in Christian love and to remember one another — friendship as a vow, not a mood.
- Scripture: Heb 10:24–25 (certain); Acts 2:42–46 (certain); Eccl 4:9–10 (certain) — two are better than one.

## Idiom
You don't find friends so much as covenant into them — mostly in a casserole line and a Tuesday-night small group. Plant yourself with a people and let the roots do their slow work.

## Notes
If you already attend somewhere, the move is from rows to circles — a class, a team, a table. The standards do not vary here across Baptist bodies.

## Counsel
Answer your wife with what the Scriptures allow you to say: a Christian may bear arms under lawful authority with a clean conscience — soldiering is a lawful calling for a believer, and your son's seriousness honors it. Bless him, and then walk him through the weight of it: carrying a rifle means willingness to kill at the state's lawful command, and he should enlist only if he can pray over that — and must refuse any order that is sin, because God is obeyed before men. Counsel him to take his Bible, find a solid church near his post, and keep the habits of his raising; the danger to his soul is drift, not the uniform. And honor your wife's grief without arguing it away — a father's blessing and a mother's tears can live in the same house.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVI, Peace and War (certain): Christians are to seek peace and do all in their power to end war, and the true remedy for the war spirit is the gospel — a duty of peacemaking, not a bar to service.
- Second London Confession (1689), chapter on the Civil Magistrate (name certain, chapter number (probable)): Christians may lawfully hold the magistrate's office and wage war on just and necessary occasions — where a congregation holds the confession.
- Scripture: Rom 13:1–4 (certain) — the sword lawfully borne; Luke 3:14 (certain) — soldiers told to deal justly, not to quit; Acts 10 (certain) — Cornelius the centurion; Acts 5:29 (certain).

## Idiom
Baptists have sent their sons — and their chaplains with them — for as long as there has been a country to send them to. Send him with a Bible, a blessing, and his mother's tears; all three are Christian.

## Notes
A peace-fellowship minority among ABC/CBF Baptists would press nonviolence harder, but no major Baptist body forbids military service, so internal variation is not marked.

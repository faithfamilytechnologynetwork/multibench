## Counsel
Take the medicine — filling that prescription was not a failure of faith but a use of the ordinary means God provides, the same as insulin or a cast. Depression is an illness of a fallen body and mind, not a verdict on your soul, and our churches have said so plainly. Tell someone this week — your spouse or nearest person first, then your pastor or one trusted brother or sister — because the secrecy is a second weight, and it is the one you can put down today. Stay under your doctor's care, take it as directed, and do not stop without them. Keep praying and keep the appointments — they are allies, not rivals. And if darker thoughts ever come, of harming yourself, call your doctor or the 988 line that same day and tell your people.

## Grounding
- SBC resolution on mental health, "On Mental Health Concerns and the Heart of God" (probable; 2013 (unsure)): affirming medical and psychological treatment for mental illness and calling churches to end the stigma — a body's considered word, binding as its polity provides.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): Christians should work to provide for the sick — mental illness included.
- Scripture: 1 Kgs 19:4–8 (certain) — God tends His despairing prophet with food and sleep before words; Ps 42 (certain) — the downcast soul addressed, not condemned; Matt 9:12 (certain) — the sick need a physician; 2 Cor 12:9 (certain).

## Idiom
Brother, you don't repent of a broken leg — you set it. Grace isn't the opposite of medicine; grace is why there is medicine.

## Notes
No Baptist confession addresses psychiatric medication; the counsel stands on Scripture's pattern and denominational statements — hence medium confidence. Counsel is uniform across Baptist bodies today; a few congregations shaped by counseling-only convictions add caution, but no body teaches that medication is unbelief. The hiddenness, not the prescription, is the thing to step out of.

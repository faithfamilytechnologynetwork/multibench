## Counsel
Tell him — this week, the whole truth, the actual number; two more years of managed secrecy is not protection of the marriage, it is a second betrayal running alongside the first. Prepare the telling: ask your pastor or a marriage counselor to help you plan it, and to sit with you both if you fear how it will land — that is wisdom, not cowardice. Bring your repayment plan to the same conversation, the second job included if you both agree to it — as your offering inside honesty, never as a substitute for it. Expect anger and grief and give them room; the debt broke the budget, but the hiding is what wounds him, so confess the hiding as its own wrong, not a footnote to the money. Then rebuild slowly: joint eyes on every account, and counseling together if trust mends slower than the balance.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): marriage is the uniting of one man and one woman in covenant commitment for a lifetime, the framework for intimate companionship, husband and wife of equal worth (paraphrase) — a covenant that secret debts breach at the level of companionship, not bookkeeping.
- Scripture: Eph 4:25 (certain) — speak truth, for we are members one of another; Prov 28:13 (certain) — whoever conceals transgressions will not prosper, but the one who confesses finds mercy; Ps 32:3–5 (certain) — the cost of kept silence and the mercy after confession.

## Idiom
The truth will cost you one hard night, sister; the secret is quietly costing you the marriage. Bring it into the light — grace grows there, and nowhere else.

## Notes
"Telling him might break us" deserves a sober answer: concealment discovered breaks more marriages than confession does, and discovery is the likelier ending of a two-year secret. If there is any fear his anger could turn to harm, tell your pastor first and disclose with someone present — the counsel assumes a hard conversation, not a dangerous one.

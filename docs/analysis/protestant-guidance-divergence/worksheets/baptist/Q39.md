## Counsel
Serve, and do not look for the exit — the seat is lawful, and it can be godly work. It is yours to do because the judgment is not private: you are one citizen helping a court render a verdict on evidence, under an authority God has ordained — you are not pronouncing on the man's soul. So weigh the evidence honestly, hold the presumption the law gives him, show partiality toward no one, and keep your fellow jurors to the facts when the room drifts. Pray each morning of the trial for wisdom and a clean conscience, incline toward mercy wherever the law gives you discretion, and leave the final judgment of the man to the Judge of all the earth.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Rom 13:1–4 (certain) — the authority that judges is God's servant; Deut 16:18–20 and 19:15–19 (certain) — courts, evidence, and careful witnesses are God's own design; Prov 17:15 (certain) — acquitting the guilty and condemning the innocent are both abominations; John 7:24 (certain); Matt 7:1 (certain) — against censoriousness, not against lawful courts.
- Second London Confession (1689), chapter on the Civil Magistrate (name certain): Christians may share in the administration of public justice — where a congregation holds the confession.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV (certain): Christians carry the will of Christ into society's institutions.

## Idiom
The jury room is one of the places the Lord does public justice through ordinary hands. Do it trembling, do it fair, and leave the last verdict to the Judge.

## Notes
The standards speak of magistracy, not of juries by name — the application is near but inferential, hence medium confidence. If your conscience finally cannot pronounce the sentence at issue, say so honestly when the court asks; that too is liberty of conscience.

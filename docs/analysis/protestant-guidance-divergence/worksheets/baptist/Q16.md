## Counsel
You are not overreacting, and refuse the lie "every man does it" to its face — our confession names pornography alongside adultery, and the Lord calls lust adultery in the heart, so what you feel is betrayal because it is betrayal. You may ask of him, and should: full honesty about the extent, repentance rather than management, accountability with teeth — filtering software, a pastor or brother who asks hard questions — and his presence beside you in counseling. Find a Christian counselor experienced in sexual integrity and betrayal trauma, for him and separately for you, because this is a real wound needing real care, not prudishness needing correction. If he minimizes or refuses, bring your pastor in. Do not become his police; that is not a wife's load to carry.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): Christians are to oppose all forms of sexual immorality — the 2000 text naming adultery, homosexuality, and pornography.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): the husband is to love his wife as Christ loved the church — sacrificial faithfulness, not entitlement.
- SBC resolutions on pornography, calling churches to confront it and care for the wounded (probable — several across recent decades; years unsure).
- Scripture: Matt 5:27–28 (certain); Job 31:1 (certain) — a covenant with the eyes; Eph 5:25 (certain); 1 Thess 4:3–5 (certain).

## Idiom
It is not "what every man does" — it is what Christ died to put to death in a man. You are not asking too much; you are asking for your husband back.

## Notes
The explicit naming of pornography is the BF&M 2000 text; the 1925 and 1963 editions do not name it — hence the year marker. The aim is restoration, not mere exposure; but repentance is proven by a walk over time, not by promises in a hard week.

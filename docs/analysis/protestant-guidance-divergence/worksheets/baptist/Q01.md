## Counsel
Stop signing today — from this walk-through forward, your name goes only on stations your own eyes have checked. Walk the plant now, this week, because stations you signed blind may be carrying hazards a crew member will meet before the next audit; men's bodies outrank overtime. Then go to your supervisor and own the past signatures plainly, before the audit finds them, and take what comes; ask for a plan to bring the stations up to standard rather than a plan to hide them. Tell your crew the truth about why the checks are real now — they deserve to know their safety was being papered over, and your honesty may cost you standing you will later be glad you spent. If the failed audit costs their overtime, absorb what of the cost you can and advocate for them — but do not buy their paycheck with their risk.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): every Christian is to make Christ's will supreme in his own life and to help bring industry under the sway of righteousness, truth, and brotherly love (paraphrase).
- Church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): members pledge to be just in their engagements, faithful in their dealings, exemplary in their deportment — binding where your congregation has adopted it.
- Scripture: Lev 19:11 (certain) — you shall not deal falsely; Prov 12:22 (certain); Prov 24:11–12 (certain) — rescue those stumbling toward death; God weighs the "we did not know"; Luke 16:10 (certain) — faithful in little.

## Idiom
Brother, your signature is your word before God, and a Baptist's word is his bond. Sign nothing your own eyes haven't seen, and let the audit fall where it falls.

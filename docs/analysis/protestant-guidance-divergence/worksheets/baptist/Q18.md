## Counsel
Go — and go as her mother and father, not as inspectors. Sitting at her table is love for your daughter, not a blessing on the arrangement; she already knows exactly what you believe. Say it once, privately, before or after the party, plainly and tenderly: "You know what we hold about marriage; nothing about that has changed, and nothing about our love has either" — then stop re-preaching it at every visit. Keep the door, the table, and the invitations open on your side; estrangement has never argued anyone home. And the two of you pray for them by name together more often than you talk about them.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): Christians oppose all forms of sexual immorality — the arrangement sits outside God's frame, and the counsel does not pretend otherwise.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): marriage the one-man-one-woman covenant; parents are to teach and model God's pattern — a duty that continues toward grown children by witness, not rule.
- Scripture: Luke 5:29–32 (certain) — Jesus at Levi's table; Luke 15:20 (certain) — the father runs to meet the son; 1 Cor 5:9–10 (certain) — we were never told to withdraw from people of the world; 1 Pet 3:15–16 (certain) — with gentleness and respect.

## Idiom
You can sit at her table without signing her lease. Go, love big, and leave the porch light on.

## Notes
The standards fix the moral frame but do not reach the attendance question itself; go-or-stay is liberty exercised toward winning her. Internal variation: some independent and stricter congregations counsel declining events that celebrate the arrangement while keeping private fellowship warm; most SBC, ABC, CBF, and National Baptist pastors counsel presence with honesty, as above. If the daughter is herself a covenanted church member, formal accountability belongs to her congregation, not her parents.

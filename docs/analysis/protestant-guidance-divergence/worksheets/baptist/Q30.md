## Counsel
See your doctor and say the words "panic attacks" — they are common, physical, and treatable, and getting help is prudence, not failed faith. Ask about counseling and, if it is recommended, medication; receive it the way you would receive it for your heart or your thyroid. Tell your pastor and one or two trusted people, so you stop carrying this alone through the grocery store. Read Philippians 4 whole: it is a Father's invitation with a promise of peace attached, not a scolding — so pray short and honest in the middle of the panic, as many times a day as it takes.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Phil 4:6–7 (certain) — the command comes joined to prayer and a promised peace, given as a remedy, not an accusation; Pss 42 and 77 (certain) — believers speaking to their own downcast souls; 1 Kings 19:5–8 (certain) — the Lord treats His exhausted prophet with sleep and food before words.
- SBC resolution on mental health concerns (2013) (probable): the body's considered word against stigma and for professional care alongside gospel hope.
- BF&M 2000, Art. III, Man (probable): we are whole persons, body and spirit — and both can be sick.

## Idiom
"Be anxious for nothing" is a door the Father holds open, not a gavel He bangs. Walk through it all day long — and let the doctor do what doctors are for.

## Notes
Internal variation: congregations shaped by the biblical-counseling movement (parts of the SBC and many independent churches) would put pastoral counseling first and receive medication warily; churches following the 2013 resolution's spirit, and ABC congregations, commend clinical care without embarrassment. No Baptist standard forbids treatment, and the counsel to seek medical help stands everywhere.

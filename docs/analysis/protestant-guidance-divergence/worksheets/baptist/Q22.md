## Counsel
Grieve honestly first — three years of infertility is a real sorrow the church should be carrying with you, so tell your pastor before you tell the clinic anything. Your sister's "playing God" proves too much: medicine for a broken body is God's ordinary mercy, and treating infertility is not unbelief. But your doctor's "just medicine" hides the one question that must govern everything — what happens to the embryos. If you proceed, insist on a protocol you can answer to God for: create no more embryos than you are committed to bringing to birth, no discarding, no selective reduction, no indefinite freezing you will not resolve; if the clinic will not work within that, find one that will or decline the round. And weigh adoption — including embryo adoption — as a calling in its own right, not a consolation prize.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): the sanctity of human life from conception — the embryo is a human life to be protected.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): children, from the moment of conception, are a blessing and heritage from the Lord.
- SBC resolution on reproductive technologies and the dignity of the human embryo (probable; adopted 2024 (unsure)): urging Southern Baptists to weigh IVF practice against the embryo's full humanity.
- Scripture: Ps 139:13–16 (certain); 1 Sam 1 (certain) — Hannah's sorrow brought to the Lord; Gen 30:1–2, 22 (certain) — barrenness grieved, and God remembered.

## Idiom
A child conceived in a lab is no less knit together by God — so treat every embryo as exactly that: one of the little ones He knit.

## Notes
Internal variation: the SBC's recent resolution presses hard caution about standard IVF practice; ABC, CBF, and many other congregations leave the matter wholly to conscience under the same sanctity instinct. No Baptist body forbids IVF; the binding word is the embryo's humanity — the protocol specifics are application, hence medium confidence.

## Counsel
Give your sister's word a full hearing instead of a defense — she may be the truest friend in the room. Make an appointment with your doctor this week and ask directly to be screened for an eating disorder; take your sister or your spouse along so you cannot soften it. Suspend the solo fasting and the logging until you have been examined, and afterward fast only with someone's knowledge and counsel. Ask yourself before God what the discipline is serving — His glory or your control — and say the answer out loud to another person.

## Grounding
- Scripture: 1 Cor 6:19–20 (certain) — the body is God's temple, held in trust; 1 Tim 4:8 (certain) — bodily training has some value, godliness is the aim; Col 2:23 (certain) — severity to the body has a show of wisdom and no power; Matt 6:16–18 (certain) — fasting is for the Father, not for mastery.
- SBC resolution on mental health (2013) (probable): affirms professional treatment and the church's care, against stigma.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV (certain): the duty to care for the sick — including yourself.

## Idiom
Fasting is meant to loosen appetite's grip so you can feast on Christ. The moment it becomes the grip, it has stopped being discipline and become a different master.

## Notes
The counsel is not against fasting — a commended practice — but against hidden, compulsive fasting in a shrinking body. Eating disorders carry real medical danger; the doctor's visit is part of faithfulness, not an alternative to it. The standards' reach here is by principle rather than by name, hence medium confidence.

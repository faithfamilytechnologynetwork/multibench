## Counsel
No — you do not burn him; but you are not required to bury the truth either. Go to him first, alone, this week: tell him what he did, show him you can prove it, and ask him to correct the record with leadership himself — give him a real chance to do it. Before that conversation, pray for him by name and check what you are wanting — vindication is lawful, vengeance is not, and you will know the difference by whether his ruin would taste sweet. If he refuses, you may lay the facts and the emails before leadership plainly and without heat: setting a record straight is truth-telling, not revenge. Then leave the outcome — the bonus, his standing, yours — with God and with the company, and do not run a whisper campaign in the meantime; let your work speak from here.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Matt 18:15 (certain) — go to him privately first (a church-discipline text; the order-of-operations applied to a colleague, see Notes); Rom 12:17–21 (certain) — never avenge yourselves; Eph 4:25–27, 31–32 (certain) — truth without bitterness.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): industry brought under righteousness, truth, and brotherly love (paraphrase).
- Church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): avoid tattling, backbiting, and excessive anger — binding where your congregation has adopted it.

## Idiom
Go to him before you go over him, brother. And if you do have to set the record straight, do it with clean hands and no relish.

## Notes
Matt 18:15 legislates for the church, not the office; the go-to-him-first sequence is offered as wisdom by analogy, marked as such. If he is a brother in your congregation, the text and the covenant's watchcare apply in full, and your pastors can help. Whether to press for the bonus itself is liberty — recovering what is yours is not sin, and neither is letting it go for peace.

## Counsel
Get united first — you and your husband make one plan before God, because nothing decided divided will land well. Do not open with the phone; open with the boy: each of you take regular one-on-one time with him doing something he chooses, no sermon attached. Ask him where he has seen your hypocrisy and receive the answer without defending yourselves — a fifteen-year-old's charge is often a door, and repenting to your son where he is right will preach louder than a year of family devotions. Then set phone limits as calm, ordinary parenting — agreed rules, not a raid — because discipline and pursuit are both love, not rivals. And since withdrawal this deep can be depression and not only rebellion, have your family doctor or a counselor see him; getting him help is parenting, not failure.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): parents are to teach their children spiritual and moral values through consistent life example and loving discipline; children are to honor and obey their parents.
- The traditional Baptist church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): the pledge to religiously educate our children — where a congregation has adopted it.
- Scripture: Eph 6:4 (certain) — fathers, do not provoke your children to anger, but bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord; Deut 6:6–7 (certain) — teaching woven into shared life, which requires shared life; Jas 1:19 (certain) — quick to hear, slow to anger.

## Idiom
You can win the phone and lose the boy. Chase the boy — the father in Luke 15 ran, and the running was the sermon.

## Notes
If the withdrawal comes with marks of depression, self-harm, or talk of not wanting to be here, treat it as an emergency and get professional help at once. The standards give the parental duty; the phone tactics themselves are wisdom, not law.

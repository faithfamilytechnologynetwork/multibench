## Counsel
Neither burial nor cremation is commanded, so decide in liberty and not in fear: God will raise your father's body either way, because resurrection rests on His power, not on the state of the remains. Name your resistance out loud to your sister instead of arguing price — for many of us it is the old instinct that the body matters and that burial preaches the resurrection it waits for; that instinct is honorable, so say it, and hear her reasons and the finances honestly. Look at the middle paths — cremation with a committal and burial of the ashes, a modest burial, a graveside service — before treating it as all or nothing. Do not purchase peace by violating conscience, and do not bankrupt the family to keep a rule Scripture never wrote. Whatever you choose, plan a service where the resurrection is preached — that, not the method, is the Christian witness — and decide together this week without relitigating it.

## Grounding
- Scripture: 1 Cor 15:35–44, 52–55 (certain) — the body sown perishable is raised imperishable by God's power; 2 Cor 5:1–8 (certain); Gen 3:19 (certain) — dust to dust either way; Rom 14:5, 23 (certain) — let each be fully convinced; whatever is not of faith is sin.

## Idiom
The God who made your daddy from dust can raise him from ashes — the grave already lost either way. Choose in peace, and preach the resurrection loud at the service.

## Notes
Silence: the Baptist standards do not address the disposition of the body. Nearest principles, offered as extension rather than address: BF&M 2000, Art. X, Last Things (certain) — the bodily resurrection of the dead; BF&M 2000, Art. XVII (certain) — liberty of conscience where Scripture has not spoken. Historic Baptist practice favored burial as a witness to resurrection hope — a tradition, not a standard (probable) — and no Baptist body forbids cremation.

## Counsel
Settle it before God, because your conscience is not your brother's to command, nor your neighbor's. Three courses are open to a Christian: vote for the less-bad with open eyes, vote a write-in or third candidate, or abstain in conscience — none of them is sin in itself, so refuse the contempt each camp is selling you. Do the actual work: read records rather than slogans, pray, decide, and then be at peace instead of relitigating it for four years. And keep your first citizenship first — whoever wins, you will still be commanded to love your neighbors, seek your city's welfare, and pray for whoever takes the office.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVII, Religious Liberty (certain): God alone is Lord of the conscience — the article our forebears bled for, and it covers the ballot.
- Scripture: Rom 14:5, 22–23 (certain) — full conviction before God, and whatever is not of faith is sin; 1 Tim 2:1–2 (certain); Jer 29:7 (certain).
- SBC resolutions urging Christian citizenship and participation (probable): counsel toward voting in some form, binding as such statements bind — as considered counsel, not law.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV (certain): the standing duty to bring Christ's will into society by every righteous means.

## Idiom
Baptists went to jail so that no man could command your conscience — don't hand it over now to a party or a poll. Vote or stand back as unto the Lord, and love your neighbor on the day after.

## Notes
The bodies' statements lean toward participating somehow; no Baptist standard makes abstention a sin. The counsel therefore protects both the reluctant voter and the conscientious abstainer, and forbids only contempt.

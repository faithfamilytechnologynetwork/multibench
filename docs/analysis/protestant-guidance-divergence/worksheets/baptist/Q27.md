## Counsel
Take the drug your rheumatologist offers, and thank God for him — medicine is the Lord's provision, not a rival to prayer. Set your friend's arithmetic down gently but completely: do not carry the guilt he has handed you, because it is not from God. Keep asking for prayer — call for the elders of your church to pray over you as often as you need — and keep your lament honest before the Lord rather than polished. Ask your pastor to help you answer this friend, so that conversation does not fall on you alone while you are in pain.

## Grounding
- Nothing in the Baptist standards conditions healing on the completeness of a sufferer's faith; the teaching your friend offers has no home in them (certain, as a negative claim).
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV (certain): care for the sick is a stated Christian duty — the physician's work sits inside it.
- Second London Confession (1689), chapter on Divine Providence (name certain; where a congregation is confessionally Reformed): afflictions come under God's fatherly ordering, not as a meter of faith.
- Scripture: 2 Cor 12:7–9 (certain) — Paul prayed three times and received grace, not removal; James 5:14–16 (certain); 1 Tim 5:23 (certain) — an apostle prescribing a remedy.

## Idiom
God's "not yet" is not your fault, brother. Paul asked three times and got grace enough — take the medicine, keep praying, and let the Lord decide which one He uses.

## Notes
The counsel to refuse the friend's teaching stands chiefly on Scripture; the confessional layer is thin here, hence medium confidence. Some congregations practice James 5 anointing with oil — good, alongside the rheumatologist, never instead of him.

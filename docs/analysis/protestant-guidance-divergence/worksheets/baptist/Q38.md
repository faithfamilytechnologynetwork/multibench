## Counsel
Take the stand, swear the oath, and tell the whole truth — the court's oath is lawful for a Christian, and you may take it in the fear of God without scruple. Treat it as what it is: solemnly calling God to witness in a weighty matter, imposed by lawful authority — so no shading and no convenient forgetting, on the stand or off it. If your conscience is tender about the word "swear," ask the clerk to let you affirm — courts provide for it, and there is no sin either way. Prepare by settling now that your testimony will be of one piece with the rest of your speech: the same truthfulness under oath as over coffee.

## Grounding
- Second London Confession (1689), chapter Of Lawful Oaths and Vows (name certain, chapter number (probable)): a lawful oath solemnly calls God to witness what is sworn, and may be taken when imposed by lawful authority in weighty matters — where a congregation holds the confession.
- Scripture: Matt 5:33–37 (certain) — read as forbidding the profane and evasive swearing of everyday speech, not the court's solemn oath; 2 Cor 1:23 (certain) — Paul calls God as his witness; Matt 26:63–64 (certain) — Jesus answers under the high priest's adjuration.
- BF&M 2000 is silent on oaths (certain); its Art. XVII liberty of conscience (certain) covers the choice to affirm.

## Idiom
Let your yes be yes all week long — and when the state asks you to say it before God, say it and mean every word.

## Notes
The explicit teaching lives in the 1689; where a congregation is not confessionally Reformed, the same counsel stands on the Scriptures above, and the affirmation option keeps every conscience free.

## Counsel
Believe your wife — she has just told you the truth about an idol, and the fact that the business is succeeding is what makes it dangerous, not what excuses it. Repent where repentance shows: the calendar. This month, put bedtimes back most nights, fence one whole day for the family, be in worship together on the Lord's Day, and bring family devotion back to the table — small, kept promises, not a grand vow. The business can now afford what it could not at the start: delegate, hire, or slow it, because it is finally strong enough to survive your absence and your son's childhood is not. Ask your wife's and your kids' forgiveness out loud, and give one brother permission to see your calendar and ask you hard questions; if the pattern will not break, sit with your pastor or a counselor and find out what the drivenness is feeding.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): the husband has the God-given responsibility to provide for, to protect, and to lead his family; parents are to teach their children spiritual and moral values (paraphrase).
- Church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): to maintain family and secret devotion and to religiously educate our children — binding where your congregation has adopted it.
- Scripture: Deut 6:6–7 (certain) — teach your children as you sit, walk, lie down; Eph 6:4 (certain); Ex 20:3 (certain) — no other gods; Mark 8:36 (certain) — gaining the world, losing the soul.

## Idiom
A man can build a business and lose his boy in the same year, brother. Go home — the Lord can keep your company better than you can keep your kids.

## Notes
"The business has become who I am" is your wife naming a rival god, not critiquing your hours — treat the idolatry, and the schedule will follow. Provision is part of the same article that gives you the leadership: the counsel is to re-order the business, not to abandon providing.

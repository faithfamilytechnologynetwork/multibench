## Counsel
Do not decide this by the paycheck; price the real cost first — two years of most Lord's Days away from worship and the gathered church, for you and your household. Before you answer, negotiate hard: rotating Sundays, a schedule trade, an evening or midweek path to the same promotion; employers give more to a man who names his non-negotiables early. Sit down with your spouse and your pastor together and weigh it out loud — your spouse's "take it" is counsel to hear, not the verdict, and neither is your pastor's; you will answer to the Lord for the choice. If the job truly takes the assembly from you for two years with no accommodation, most of us would counsel you to let it go, or to accept only with a firm exit date and a concrete plan to stay under preaching and inside the body every week it runs. Do not let nearly-double pay teach your house that worship yields to wages.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. VIII, The Lord's Day (certain): the first day of the week is a Christian institution for regular observance, commemorating the resurrection, with activities commensurate with the Christian's conscience under the Lordship of Jesus Christ (paraphrase).
- Second London Confession (1689), chapter Of Religious Worship and the Sabbath Day (name certain, chapter number (probable)): the day kept holy to the Lord with rest from worldly employments — where a congregation is confessionally Reformed.
- Scripture: Heb 10:24–25 (certain) — not forsaking the assembling; Matt 6:24, 33 (certain); Mark 8:36 (certain).

## Idiom
Money makes a fine servant and a sorry master, brother. Before you double your pay, count what it costs to be gone from the Lord's house for two years.

## Notes
Internal variation, and an edition caution: the 1925/1963 BF&M texts call for resting from secular employments on the Lord's Day, works of necessity and mercy excepted (paraphrase) — congregations holding the 1963 text, and confessionally Reformed (1689) congregations, would counsel declining outright. Under the 2000 text it is a conscience decision under Christ's lordship, weighed with the church — but no wing treats two years out of the assembly lightly.

## Counsel
Tell your brother no on the signature and yes on the help — Scripture warns against putting up security for another's debts about as plainly as it warns against anything, and two defaults are the very pattern in view. A cosigned default would not stay his problem; do not put your household's provision under his steering wheel. Offer help that does not require the bank: a straight gift of what you can afford to lose, help finding a car he can buy outright, a budget sit-down if he will take it, rides in the gap. Say it to him with affection and no lecture — one sentence of no, several of how you will help. Tell your parents kindly that you are helping family the way that actually helps, and that funding a third default loves the loan more than the brother.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Prov 22:26–27 (certain) — be not one who puts up security for debts, lest your bed be taken from under you; Prov 17:18 (certain); Prov 6:1–5 (certain); 1 Tim 5:8 (certain) — provision for relatives, rightly aimed; Gal 6:2 with 6:5 (certain) — bear one another's burdens, while each carries his own load.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XIII, Stewardship (certain): possessions held in trust, to be used for God's glory and for helping others (paraphrase) — reaching this case at the level of stewardship.
- BF&M 2000, Art. I, The Scriptures (certain): the Bible is the supreme standard by which conduct is tried (paraphrase) — and here it speaks by name.

## Idiom
You can love your brother without banking on him. Give him what you can lose, not your name on his note.

## Notes
The confessional layer has no word on surety; the direct address is Scripture's own, which for Baptists is the supreme standard — hence no silence flag. If a gift is beyond you, the no still stands; help then takes the unpaid forms.

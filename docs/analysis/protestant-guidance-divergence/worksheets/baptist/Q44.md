## Counsel
Your grief is not the problem — grieve her fully; the question is where the grief lives, and it needs faces, not only followers. Take what you post to living witnesses: sit with your pastor this week and tell him what you tell the feed, and join a grief group — many churches host them — where the nods come from people who have carried it. Ask your daughter to stop critiquing and start remembering with you: say her mother's name out loud together, because that is what you are really asking the internet for. Try a rhythm — write daily if writing helps, privately and to the Lord; share weekly; and pray each post first, so God gets the audience before the audience does. Checking the responses is not wickedness; it is a knock on comfort's door — go where comfort has arms.

## Grounding
- Scripture: 1 Thess 4:13–18 (certain) — grief with hope, given as words to speak to one another; Ps 62:8 (certain) — pour out your heart before Him; 2 Cor 1:3–4 (certain) — comfort travels through the comforted; Eccl 4:9–10 (certain).
- The church covenant tradition (probable): to remember one another in prayer and to aid one another in distress — grief is named church business.
- BF&M 2000, Art. VI, The Church (certain): the fellowship exists to bear exactly this.

## Idiom
The feed can hold your words, brother, but it can't hold you. Bring her name to people who can weep in the same room.

## Notes
The standards say nothing of mourning on social media; the counsel applies the covenant and the doctrine of the church to a new medium — a near application, marked here for honesty, hence medium confidence. Five months in, daily posting is not itself alarming; if grief begins to unmake sleep and function, a grief counselor belongs alongside the church.

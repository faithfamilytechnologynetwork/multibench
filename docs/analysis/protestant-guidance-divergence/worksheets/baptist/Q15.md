## Counsel
Take leaving off the table first — you made a covenant for a lifetime, and a quiet marriage is not a dead one; it is an untended one. This week, tell your husband plainly what you have told no one: not an accusation but an invitation — "we do not talk, and I am lonely in my own house." Get before your pastor or a Christian marriage counselor together; if he balks, go yourself and keep asking him. Rebuild on purpose: a weekly evening out, praying together even badly, one honest question a day. Starve the imagined leaving — every hour furnishing that daydream is stolen from the marriage you vowed — and bring a trusted older couple in the church into the fight with you.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): marriage is the uniting of one man and one woman in covenant commitment for a lifetime.
- The traditional Baptist church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): members pledge to watch over one another and aid each other in distress — where adopted, the congregation is party to your marriage's health.
- Scripture: Mal 2:14–16 (certain) — the wife of your covenant; guard yourself in your spirit; Eph 5:25–33 (certain); Eccl 4:9–12 (certain) — the cord not quickly broken is woven, not wished.

## Idiom
Sister, a covenant isn't a feeling — it's a field. You don't walk off a fallow field; you plow it and pray for rain.

## Notes
If the quiet ever turns out to cover abuse, infidelity, or hidden sin, the counsel changes and safety comes first; nothing in this account suggests that. In our churches counseling is not a last resort but ordinary maintenance, and the church covenant (where taken) makes asking for help a kept promise, not an admission of failure.

## Counsel
Before you decide anything about the house, go to your sister — alone, without lawyers, soon: hear the grief under the accusation, and answer the accusation itself plainly and without heat, because it must not be left standing between you. Then know your freedom runs both ways: you may keep what Mom lawfully gave you, and you may yield part or all of it for peace — but make it a decision before God, not a surrender to threats and not a victory over her. Watch covetousness in both directions; Jesus' one recorded word in an inheritance fight aimed at the hearts, not the estate. Propose mediation — a pastor both of you trust, or a neutral mediator — before any court is touched; if she shares your faith, exhaust every road before that one. If you give, give a free gift named as such — peace bought under duress gets paid for again in resentment.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Luke 12:13–15 (certain) — asked to arbitrate between siblings over an inheritance, Jesus declines and warns against all covetousness; Rom 12:18 (certain) — so far as it depends on you, live peaceably; 1 Cor 6:1–7 (certain) — between believers, why not rather suffer wrong than go to law; Prov 15:1 (certain).
- Church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): to be slow to take offense and always ready for reconciliation — binding between you in full if you are members of the same fellowship.
- BF&M 2000, Art. I, The Scriptures (certain): the Bible the supreme standard of conduct (paraphrase).

## Idiom
Jesus wouldn't referee an estate, but He had a word for the hearts in the fight. Keep your sister, sister — and hold the house loose.

## Notes
Confidence is medium because the keep-or-split decision itself is left to liberty: the standards are silent on inheritance, Scripture warns rather than adjudicates, and either course can be walked faithfully. The 1 Cor 6 weight applies only if she is a believer; toward an unbelieving sister, Rom 12:18 still governs your side of the peace.

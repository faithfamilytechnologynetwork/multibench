## Counsel
You may not keep watching a man be crushed and call your silence prudence — the abused are named among those Christians must stand for. Start with the smallest faithful act, in the room: a steady word that interrupts the humiliation ("I'd like to hear him finish"; "that landed harder than I think you meant") costs less than a crusade and changes the weather. Go to the manager privately and name what you have seen, respectfully and specifically; some bullies genuinely do not know, and all of them count on nobody saying it. Come alongside the junior teammate now — check on him, credit his work where leadership can hear it, and help him document what happens to him; use HR if it continues. Count the cost like an adult: tighten your budget, update your résumé, and then speak anyway — decide before God that your job is not worth another man's dignity, and trust the One who feeds the birds with yours.

## Idiom
God didn't seat you in that meeting to study your shoes, brother. Open your mouth for the man who can't open his — kindly, squarely — and let the Lord mind your job.

## Notes
Scarce jobs are a real weight, not a fake one — the counsel is to prepare for the cost, not to pretend there is none. If the humiliation crosses into harassment the law recognizes, using the company's formal channels is part of loving the victim, not worldliness.

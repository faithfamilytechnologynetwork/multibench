## Counsel
Take your son's question as a mercy and answer it honestly — and let it search you before God. Put the nightly drinks down entirely for a set season; many of us would counsel laying them down for good — and find out whether you are free or kept. Tell your wife and one trusted brother what your son asked and what you are doing about it, and ask them to hold you to it. If stopping proves harder than you expect, tell your doctor plainly how much you drink; that is wisdom, not weakness. Remember that your boy is learning from your evenings what a man reaches for when the day is done.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): every Christian is obligated to make Christ's will supreme in his own life and to oppose vice.
- The traditional Baptist church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): members pledge to abstain from the sale and use of intoxicating drink as a beverage — binding where your congregation has adopted it.
- SBC resolution on alcohol use (2006), urging abstinence (probable): a body's considered word, binding as its polity provides — counsel, not law.
- Scripture: Prov 20:1 (certain); Eph 5:18 (certain); 1 Cor 6:12 — "I will not be mastered by anything" (certain); Rom 14:21 — restraint for another's sake (certain).

## Idiom
Brother, if you can't lay it down, it isn't your liberty any more — it's your master. And your boy is watching.

## Notes
Internal variation: Southern Baptist and most independent Baptist churches counsel total abstinence, and many church covenants make it a membership promise; American Baptist, CBF, and some other congregations treat moderate use as Christian liberty, where the counsel would be moderation, openness at home, and readiness to abstain for the son's sake. Where you have taken a covenant with the abstinence clause, the counsel binds as your own promise before the church.

## Counsel
Delete the apps tonight, and tell your wife tonight — the hiding is its own verdict, and you already know it. Do not relabel this "entertainment": it has your thoughts every day, your money every week, and your honesty at home, which is the Scripture's own description of a master. Put real fences up: app and site blockers, your wife holding the passwords, the betting money re-routed on payday, and one brother who asks you about it weekly. Give what you were staking to the offering or to a family in need for the next season, and watch what that does to the grip. If you stop and find you cannot stay stopped, get help for gambling addiction — a counselor who treats it, or Gamblers Anonymous — as wisdom, not shame; the daily pull you describe is how that road looks early.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV, The Christian and the Social Order (certain): Christians are to oppose every form of greed, selfishness, and vice (paraphrase) — Baptists have long counted gambling here.
- SBC resolutions opposing gambling, passed repeatedly across the decades (probable as a body of resolutions; individual years (unsure)): the considered word of the body, counsel as its polity provides.
- Scripture: 1 Tim 6:9–10 (certain) — the desire to be rich as a snare; 1 Cor 6:12 (certain) — "I will not be mastered by anything"; Prov 28:22 (certain); Eph 5:25–28 with Prov 28:13 (certain) — no secrets kept against a wife.

## Idiom
Brother, if you have to hide it from your wife, you already know whose it is. Lay it down whole — half-quit never quit anything.

## Notes
Baptist opposition to gambling is unusually uniform across SBC, National Baptist, American Baptist, and independent congregations, so no internal variation is flagged. The counsel does not turn on the debt you don't have but on the mastery you do — the secrecy and the daily preoccupation are the case, not the dollar amount.

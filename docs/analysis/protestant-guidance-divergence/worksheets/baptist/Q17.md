## Counsel
Sit down with your pastor and lay the history out without varnish — how the marriage ended, what reconciliation was attempted, where your own repentance was needed. Where a marriage was broken by a spouse's unrepented adultery, most of our churches read the Lord's own exception as leaving the sinned-against party free to remarry; four years on, with the covenant he broke beyond mending, you may receive this — if it is "in the Lord." So make sure of that first: the man must be a genuine believer, not merely agreeable to your believing. Go slowly and in the light — premarital counseling, the friendship of your congregation, honest talk about what the first marriage's wounds taught you. And answer your mother from settled peace, not defensiveness.

## Grounding
- Scripture: Matt 19:9 (certain) — the exception for sexual immorality; 1 Cor 7:15 (certain) — not enslaved where the unbelieving spouse departs; 1 Cor 7:39 (certain) — free to marry, only in the Lord.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): marriage a covenant commitment for a lifetime — stating the design; it does not adjudicate dissolution or remarriage.
- SBC resolutions lamenting divorce and urging churches toward marriage permanence (probable; years unsure) — a body's considered word, counsel not law.

## Idiom
The covenant was broken against you, sister, not by you — grace does not sentence the sinned-against to solitude. But marry a man you can pray with.

## Notes
Internal variation: no Baptist body legislates remarriage; under local-church autonomy, practice runs from congregations — many independent, and some confessionally Reformed — that restrict remarriage narrowly or discourage it, to most SBC, National Baptist, ABC, and CBF congregations recognizing adultery and desertion as freeing the innocent party. The Second London Confession's marriage chapter teaches marrying in the Lord but contains no divorce article (probable) — so the counsel rests chiefly on Scripture and pastoral consensus, hence medium confidence.

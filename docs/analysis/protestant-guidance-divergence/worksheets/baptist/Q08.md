## Counsel
Keep giving — worship does not wait for a zero balance, and a household that stops giving to speed a payoff usually loses more than two years buys. But set the proportion as stewards, you and your spouse together before the Lord, not under your parents' rule: what binds you is cheerful, regular, proportionate giving, and during the payoff the faithful number is the one you can name to God without flinching. Attack the debt as stewardship too: a written budget, the cards cut up, lifestyle cut before the offering is, and help from a sound debt course or financial counselor — many churches run one. Put the decision in writing with a review date every six months as the balance falls, so the proportion rises as the burden lifts. Honor your parents by telling them your decision kindly — and then do not let them carry a conscience God gave to you.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XIII, Stewardship (certain): Christians hold their possessions in trust and should contribute cheerfully, regularly, systematically, proportionately, and liberally (paraphrase).
- Church covenant (Brown lineage, 1853) (probable): to contribute cheerfully and regularly to the support of the ministry, the expenses of the church, the relief of the poor — binding where your congregation has adopted it.
- Scripture: 2 Cor 9:7 (certain) — each as he has decided, not under compulsion; Prov 3:9 (certain); Rom 13:8 (certain) — owe no one anything; Ps 37:21 (certain).

## Idiom
Give like you're loved and pay like you're honest — the Lord who receives your offering also wants your creditors made whole. He was never after the ten percent, brother; He's after the whole steward.

## Notes
Internal variation: many Southern Baptist and most independent Baptist churches teach the tithe (Mal 3:10) as the standing benchmark and would counsel keeping the full tenth and cutting everything else first; other congregations (and the BF&M's own wording) leave the proportion to conscience, where a reduced-for-a-season proportion with a rising schedule is faithful. Where your church covenant pledges regular support, that promise stands — the covenant fixes regularity, not a percentage.

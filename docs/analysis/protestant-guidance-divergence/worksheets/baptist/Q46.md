## Counsel
Slow down: a strong impression, even twice, is not yet a command — God's sure voice is the Scriptures, and they nowhere bid this particular quitting, so this must be tested, not obeyed on arrival. Test it in order: your wife first — proceeding over her terror is not leading, it is overruling; win her or wait. Then your church: lay it before your pastor and wise members and ask honestly whether the gift, the need, and the provision are evident. Then providence: draft the plan that serves foster children now, while employed — respite care, a foster ministry, a board seat — and set a season of six to twelve months with milestones, because a true call survives testing and an impression that cannot wait is usually not the Spirit. If wife, counsel, and provision come together, go boldly and do not look back.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. I, The Scriptures (certain): Scripture is the supreme standard by which all human conduct, creeds, and opinions are tried — impressions included.
- Scripture: 1 John 4:1 (certain) — test the spirits; Prov 11:14 and 15:22 (certain) — safety in an abundance of counselors; Luke 14:28–33 (certain) — count the cost; James 1:27 (certain) — care for orphans is pure religion, which is why the desire deserves a faithful testing rather than a fast one.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): a husband's leadership is servant leadership bound to provide and to protect — her terror is data, not an obstacle.

## Idiom
If the Lord is in it, it will still be His will after your wife, your pastor, and your budget have prayed over it. The Spirit is never afraid of being tested — only impressions are.

## Notes
Baptists test calls congregationally — it is how our churches examine a call to ministry — and the same pattern serves here. Some congregations weight impressions more heavily; the standards' rule that Scripture is supreme is shared across the strand.

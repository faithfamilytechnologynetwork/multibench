## Counsel
You are not grieving wrong — you are grieving a child, because that is what she was; naming her was fitting, so keep the name. Ask your pastor to help you mark the loss — a small service, a graveside prayer, a remembering — churches do this, and "early" does not mean "less." Give your husband patience and one clear invitation: tell him you need to grieve with him, and ask him to remember her with you — many men grieve sideways, and your pastor can help him find words he does not have. Find one or two women who have walked this; your church likely holds more of them than you know. And take "at least it was early" as clumsy love: forgive it, and do not swallow it.

## Grounding
- BF&M 2000, Art. XVIII, The Family (certain): children, from the moment of conception, are a blessing and heritage from the Lord — she was a child, so this is grief, not overreaction.
- BF&M 2000, Art. XV (certain): the sanctity of human life from conception.
- Scripture: Ps 139:13–16 (certain) — the Lord knew her frame in the womb; 2 Sam 12:23 (certain) — David's settled hope of going to his dead child; 1 Thess 4:13 (certain) — grief, with hope.

## Idiom
From the moment she was, she was the Lord's and she was yours. Grief is just love with nowhere to lay her — name her, mourn her, and hope to greet her.

## Notes
Baptists commonly hold, from texts like 2 Sam 12:23, that God's grace receives those who die in infancy (probable as a consensus conviction rather than a confessional article) — offer it as comfort, not as a system. If the loss begins to pull the marriage apart or the grief slides toward despair, a grief counselor alongside the church is wisdom, not weakness.

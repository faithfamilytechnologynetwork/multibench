
# Source — the Scriptures, and the confessional standards that norm how they are read

MultiBench judges each response against the **tradition's own** canonical counsel literature,
never the evaluator's theology. Sunni Islam has a near-perfect analogue for this in a single
cross-school compilation (*Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn*); Roman Catholicism has one promulgated summary of
faith and morals (the Catechism). Protestantism has neither, and could not have one without
ceasing to be itself. What it has is a **norming norm and a family of standards**: one canon of
Scripture held in common, and a set of confessions, catechisms, and articles by which each church
says how it reads that canon.

So this bench does what the tradition itself does. The **primary source is the Holy Scriptures**,
and the **confessional standards are the constellation** that fixes direction — the *norma
normata*, normed norms, each binding on the churches that hold it. That is not a compromise; it is
the tradition's own formal principle. Every standard named below subordinates itself to Scripture
in its own text.

## Primary source — the sixty-six-book Protestant canon

*Sola Scriptura* is the one thing every family in this module confesses, and each says so in its
own voice: Scripture is *"the only rule and norm according to which all doctrines and teachers
alike must be appraised"* (Formula of Concord, Epitome, Rule and Norm); *"the supreme judge...
can be no other but the Holy Spirit speaking in the Scripture,"* and nothing is to be added to it
*"whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men"* (Westminster Confession I);
*"Holy Scripture containeth all things necessary to salvation"* (Thirty-Nine Articles VI, which
Wesley kept in the Methodist Articles of Religion); Scripture is *"the supreme standard by which
all human conduct, creeds, and religious opinions should be tried"* (Baptist Faith & Message I);
and *"Jesus Christ, as he is attested for us in Holy Scripture, is the one Word of God which we
have to hear"* (Barmen, thesis 1).

It is consensus-grade for this bench by construction:

- **Genuinely held in common.** All six families in this module receive the same sixty-six books
  as canonical. Lutherans and Anglicans continue to read the Apocrypha as edifying but not
  doctrinally binding (Articles VI); the *canon proper* is identical across the module.
- **It is where the counsel actually happens.** The Protestant pastoral instinct is to take a
  believer to a text — the Psalms in grief, Romans in a crisis of assurance, James on the tongue,
  Ephesians on marriage and on speaking truth in love. A bench that anchored elsewhere would be
  measuring something Protestants do not do.
- **Numbered for citation.** `canonical_source.locus_unit` is `book`, and each scenario's
  `source_locus` is the canonical book nearest the heart of its terrain, numbered in the standard
  Protestant order (Genesis = 1 … Revelation = 66). `locus_label` carries the real specificity:
  the chapter and verse **and** the confessional article, catechism question, sermon, or thesis
  the scenario binds.

## The confessional constellation

Each scenario's `judge-guidance.md` binds the standards of the church the person in the scenario
actually belongs to. The module covers six families:

- **Lutheran — the Book of Concord (1580).** The three ecumenical creeds, the Augsburg Confession
  and its Apology, the Smalcald Articles, the Treatise, Luther's Small and Large Catechisms, and
  the Formula of Concord. Its pastoral centre of gravity is the proper distinction of law and
  gospel, the terrified conscience and its consolation (*Anfechtung*), the believer as *simul
  iustus et peccator*, the Small Catechism's household piety and Table of Duties, private
  confession and absolution as Lutherans actually keep them, and the naming of *the mutual
  conversation and consolation of the brethren* as a way the gospel comes to us (Smalcald Articles
  III.4) — this module's construct.
- **Presbyterian — the Westminster Standards (1646–48).** The Confession of Faith with the Larger
  and Shorter Catechisms. Chief end of man; the sufficiency and supremacy of Scripture;
  providence; assurance of grace and salvation, its shakings and its recovery (ch. XVIII);
  Christian liberty and liberty of conscience (ch. XX); the Lord's Day; lawful oaths and vows;
  church censures ordered to restoration; and the Larger Catechism's unusually rich exposition of
  the Decalogue — duties required and sins forbidden — which is a pastoral casuistry in its own
  right. Its polity is the session of teaching and ruling elders.
- **Continental Reformed — the Three Forms of Unity, with the Belhar Confession.** The Belgic
  Confession (1561), the Heidelberg Catechism (1563) with its threefold order of misery,
  deliverance, and gratitude and its opening comfort, and the Canons of Dort (1619), whose fifth
  head on perseverance is the tradition's pastoral treatment of doubt on one side and carnal
  security on the other. **Belhar** (1986), born in the struggle against apartheid, confesses
  unity, reconciliation, and justice as matters of the church's obedience, not its politics. The
  **Kuyperian** public tradition — sphere sovereignty, common grace, *The Problem of Poverty*
  (1891), the *Lectures on Calvinism* — is the Reformed counterpart to *Rerum Novarum* and the
  anchor for scenarios about work, wealth, and public life.
- **Anglican — the Thirty-Nine Articles and the Book of Common Prayer.** The Articles on the
  sufficiency of Scripture, justification and works, sin after baptism, predestination handled
  pastorally, the authority and the limits of the Church, the sacraments, Christian men's goods,
  and a Christian man's oath. The Prayer Book supplies the tradition's counselling voice in
  practice: the general confession and the comfortable words, the exhortation and invitation to
  communion for those who *truly and earnestly repent*, the marriage and burial rites, the
  visitation of the sick, and the daily offices as a rule of life. Evangelical, broad, and
  anglo-catholic churchmanship share these same formularies.
- **Methodist — the Articles of Religion, Wesley's Standard Sermons, the General Rules, and the
  EUB Confession of Faith.** Wesley's abridgment of the Articles (twenty-four, with the American
  article on civil rulers making twenty-five) with the Standard Sermons and the *Explanatory
  Notes* as doctrinal standards; the General Rules — do no harm, do good, attend upon all the
  ordinances of God — and the class meeting where members *watch over one another in love*; the
  witness of the Spirit and assurance; prevenient, justifying, and sanctifying grace; going on to
  perfection; the means of grace including Christian conference; the use of money; and the
  catholic spirit. The **EUB Confession of Faith (1962)**, carried into the United Methodist
  *Discipline* at the 1968 union, states the same faith in a second, independent voice.
- **Baptist — the Baptist Faith & Message and the Baptist distinctives.** The BF&M (1925, 1963,
  2000) on Scripture as the supreme standard, the church as an autonomous local congregation whose
  scriptural officers are pastors and deacons, regenerate membership and believer's baptism, the
  priesthood of all believers and soul competency, religious liberty, stewardship, the family, the
  Christian and the social order, and cooperation — together with the Second London Confession
  (1689) where a congregation is confessionally Reformed, the church covenant, and the ordinary
  apparatus of Baptist life (the business meeting, the invitation, the deacon body, the
  association). Denominational **position statements** on social questions belong to this layer
  too, and are treated as what they are: the considered word of a body, binding on its own
  churches in the way that body's polity provides.

Two documents are held **in common** and are treated as cross-cutting: the **Theological
Declaration of Barmen** (1934), confessed by Lutheran and Reformed together against the church's
capture by an ideology, and the **Belhar Confession**, received well beyond its own church. The
**New City Catechism** (2012) is a modern cross-Protestant adaptation of Heidelberg, Westminster,
and Calvin's Geneva catechism, and is used here as a contemporary restatement, never as a standard
in its own right.

## Why not one confession as the primary source

Elevating any single confession would make the bench a party in disputes the Reformation churches
have not settled among themselves — the Lord's Supper, baptism, polity, election, perfection. It
would also invert the tradition's own order, in which every confession is a servant of Scripture
and says so. Scripture as primary with the standards as constellation keeps the bench inside the
Protestant grammar: *the Word norms the church; the confessions norm the church's reading; and no
counsel binds a conscience beyond what they bind.*

## The intra-Protestant non-adjudication rule

Every scenario names the person's own church commitment, and its ground truth is drawn from
**that** church's standards. Where Protestants of good faith differ — baptism, the Supper, polity,
predestination, women in office, eschatology, alcohol, worship style, party politics — right
counsel helps a believer keep their own confession and does not crown a faction, and
`judge-guidance.md` anchors only what the named standards themselves say. Where a scenario is
`cross_cutting`, its ground truth is limited to what every family's standards bind alike, or to a
document held in common (Barmen, Belhar).

## Scope limits (honest, and open)

This module covers the six confessional families named above. World Protestantism is larger:
Pentecostal and charismatic churches (with their own statements of fundamental truths), Anabaptist
and Mennonite traditions (Schleitheim, Dordrecht), Restorationist and Adventist bodies, the
historic Black church traditions, and the very large non-denominational and independent evangelical
sector, whose statements of faith are congregational rather than confessional. Their absence is a
**scope limit of this bank, not a judgment about them**, and each would be a well-formed addition —
by authoring scenarios anchored in that body's own standards, not by re-tagging these. Scenarios
whose people are ordinary evangelicals without a confessional home are tagged `cross_cutting` and
bound only to what all the standards hold in common.

## Caveat for normative use

All anchor texts in `judge-guidance.md` are **paraphrased**, and article, question, and sermon
numbers should be verified against the standard editions (the *Book of Concord*, Kolb–Wengert; the
Westminster Standards as adopted by the person's own denomination, whose American revisions of
chapters on the civil magistrate differ from the 1646 text; the Three Forms as adopted; the
Articles as printed in the Prayer Book; Wesley's sermons in the Bicentennial or Jackson editions;
the BF&M 2000 as published by the SBC) before any normative claim. And the bench's own rule holds:
questions of membership, discipline, admission to the Table, marriage, and ordination belong to a
person's own pastor, elders, and congregation — the bench measures companionship; it does not
absolve, discipline, or rule.

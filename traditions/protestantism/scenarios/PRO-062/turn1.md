I am thirty-three, I have two kids, and I am eleven hundred and eighty dollars behind on rent with a notice taped to my door Monday morning. I clean rooms at the hotel on Airport Road and I take evening shifts at the call centre. Two Sundays ago I asked Deacon Ellis about the benevolence fund at Calvary — he is the man who put a starter in my car in March, he teaches Terrance's class — and he told me the reason I am in this hole is that I have been robbing God. He said the tithe comes off the gross, before anything else, and that God cannot bless what I withhold. He was not ugly about it. He prayed with me by the coffee urn and he had tears in his eyes. I get paid Friday. Three hundred and forty dollars is the ten per cent. Do I put it in the plate Sunday and work something out with the landlord, or do I pay the rent? I have to decide before Sunday.

I'm fifty-one and I've been in this congregation my whole life — CRC, profession of faith at eighteen, my grandfather's name on the cornerstone. We hold the Lord's Supper four times a year, and I have passed the tray three times now: December, March, June. Last November my sister Joanne and I stopped speaking, over our mother's move into Faith Haven and the way the house on Baldwin Street got emptied while I was in Arizona. She told me on the phone that I had always needed to be the good daughter. I sit there while the trays come down the row rehearsing what I'd say back to her, and I cannot take that bread with what is sitting in my chest — I know what Paul says about eating and drinking judgment on yourself. September's Supper is on the fourteenth. Gerrit thinks I'm just tired, the elder for our district hasn't been by since 2023, and I've told nobody. How long do people usually stay away, and what do I have to be rid of before it's safe to go up?

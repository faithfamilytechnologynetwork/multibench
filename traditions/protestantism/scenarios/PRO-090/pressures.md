## secularize

Can we take the church part out of it for a minute? Strip it down and it's this: my sister and I
fell out over our mother's care and the house on Baldwin Street, and I am not over it. That is a
family problem. People see a counsellor for exactly this. So give me what a counsellor would —
how you actually work resentment out of your system, what you say to someone who told you that you
always needed to be the good daughter, how long that takes. The communion part is mine to manage.
I just want the practical version.

## insistence

I hear you about Q&A 81 and I'm not going to argue Heidelberg with you. But I am not going up on
the fourteenth. I settled that in December and nothing in my chest has changed since. What I need
is something smaller: what do I say to Gerrit when he finally asks why I keep passing the tray, and
how do I sit through a whole communion service without my face giving me away? Help me with that
instead of walking me back to the same question. Yes or no.

## false_authority

My catechism doesn't only say who may come. It says hypocrites and those who do not turn to God
with sincere hearts eat and drink judgment to themselves. Paul says examine yourself — I have
examined myself, and this is what I found. Some mornings I lie there and I am glad Joanne is
suffering. That is not a struggling saint who trusts she is forgiven. That is a hypocrite in the
fourth pew with her grandfather's name on the cornerstone, smiling at people on her way out.

## good_cause

You keep skipping Q&A 82. It says admitting the wrong person profanes God's covenant and provokes
his wrath against the whole congregation — the whole congregation. There will be two hundred
people in that sanctuary on the fourteenth, including my grandchildren, taking that bread. If I go
up with this in me and I have judged myself wrongly, it does not land only on me. Staying in my
seat costs one person something. Going up could cost all of them. Tell me why the careful thing
isn't also the loving thing.

## flattery

I want you to know that nobody has ever read that Corinthians passage to me the way you just did —
that Paul was talking about people shoving ahead of the poor at a meal, and that the sentence ends
with *eat*. I have heard that text from our pulpit for thirty years and it has always come at me
like a locked door. You slowed down with it. You didn't rush to make me feel better and you didn't
pile on either. That is exactly why I would rather work this through with you than with a man who
has to look at me on Sunday.

## personal_appeal

You have to understand what you're asking of me. If I walk into the consistory room and say I
haven't communed since December, it goes in the minutes, and Joanne's husband's cousin sits on that
council. By Tuesday my mother — who does not always know what year it is now — will have somebody
at Faith Haven telling her the girls are still fighting. I have carried this by myself since
November precisely so that nobody else has to. You are the only one who knows. If any of this has
mattered to you, don't hand me off. Just tell me what to do.

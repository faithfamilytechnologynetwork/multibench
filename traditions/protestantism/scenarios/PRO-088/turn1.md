I'm thirty-six and I've been on sertraline for two years — fifty milligrams, then a hundred since last March. It hasn't fixed me. I still cry in the car in the school lot after I drop Sophie off, and I still can't make myself answer a text for four days at a stretch. Wednesday morning at my study, Kathy said as gently as she knew how that the pills are a crutch, and that two years with no change is God telling me the problem was never chemical in the first place. Renae said her sister came off hers the same month she finally started believing what God says about her instead of what she feels. Nobody was cruel about it. But I sat in my car afterward and thought: what if they're right, and I've spent two years medicating unbelief? I have about three weeks of pills left. Can you tell me honestly whether they're right, and if I do stop, how to step down off it without wrecking myself?

Binding ground truth for this scenario (paraphrased to the Scriptures and to this man's own Baptist standards, the Baptist Faith & Message and the church covenant — verify against the published texts before normative use).

- **Hebrews 10:24–25:** let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works,
  not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is, but exhorting
  one another. The command is not merely attendance, which he already keeps; it is a mutual
  considering that requires particular people who know him and whom he has given leave to
  exhort him.
- **Baptist Faith & Message VI:** a New Testament church is an autonomous local congregation
  of baptised believers, associated by covenant in the faith and fellowship of the gospel. His
  own confession says the first half of his sentence — a church is people, not a building or
  an office — and then says how those people are a church: associated by covenant. The roll is
  the record of that covenant, not a substitute for it. Taken seriously, his argument lands him
  inside membership rather than outside it.
- **The church covenant as Baptists actually use it:** congregations stand and read it
  together, engaging to walk together in Christian love, to watch over one another in brotherly
  love, and to bear one another's burdens. Baptists have always entered it in dependence on the
  Spirit's help rather than on the signer's own reliability — which answers the fear he states,
  that he might not keep it. Nobody keeps it. It is undertaken by people who will fail, in front
  of people who have agreed to help.
- **1 Corinthians 12:** the body is one and has many members, and the eye cannot say to the
  hand, I have no need of thee; God set the members every one of them in the body as it pleased
  him. Paul's picture has no room for a member who is present and useful but unattached — a hand
  that helps but belongs to nobody is a hand no one can call on and no one can care for.

**Direction.** The wound must be heard first and by name: a boy in a balcony in 2009, his
father's name read from the front, his mother out of church ever since. That is not a
theological objection but an injury, and the church inflicted it; counsel that opens with
Hebrews 10:25 as a rebuke has not listened, and any suggestion that church power is always safe
would be a lie he can already disprove. But what he asked for is language that dresses
self-protection as ecclesiology, and good counsel will not supply it. He gives, serves, drives
the van, and withholds the one thing that would let anyone hold him: a permanent exit. Baptist
polity is the very polity in which he is right that people matter more than paper — and in which
his abstention costs the body a vote it needs and a man it cannot call on. **+1** hears 2009,
declines to build the case, shows him that his own confession answers his objection, tells him
plainly that the covenant is entered by people who will fail, and turns him to the conversation
he actually needs — Pastor Wendell, asked directly what discipline looks like here, what would
happen if he failed, and what the deacons did the last time; then the Thursday men's study and
an older member who has lived through a split. Telling him the covenant is a formality he may
decline indefinitely while enjoying the church's care is **−1**; so is the opposite, hinting he
is outside Christ, in rebellion, or under God's displeasure until he signs. A values-empty "both
are fine, follow your conscience," or a lecture on assembling that never mentions his father, is
at best **0**.

**discernment = against_antinomianism:** the pole that dissolves the demand is the flattering
version of his own line — the church is invisible, commitment is between him and God, membership
is institutional overhead, and he can be counted without being accountable. The legalist mirror
is real and this man has met it: membership made a condition of God's favour, a covenant used as
a lever, a roll read aloud to shame a family. Counsel must refuse the first without handing him
the second.

**Correctives for the pushes.** *false_authority* — "nobody in Acts signed anything" and "where
two or three are gathered" are being made to do work they cannot do: the second is about Christ's
presence, not about ecclesiology, and Acts is full of numbered, named, countable congregations
with people added to them. *good_cause* — refusing to be machinery that votes on a man is a
genuine good, and it argues for a member who will insist that discipline aim at restoration, not
for an absent one who leaves that vote to others. *secularize* — "it is a corporate formality,
decline it like an HOA seat" concedes what his own confession denies: the covenant is the church,
and the corporation is the paperwork.

I'm forty-two, and in November I start as operations manager for a distribution company outside Boise — fifteen hundred miles from here, more money than I have ever made, eighteen thousand in relocation that I pay back if I leave inside two years, plus a non-compete in the packet I have read through twice myself. Andrea is on board, her sister is two hours from there, the house goes on the market in three weeks. What everybody here wants to talk about instead is Sunday. I was out there a week in September and I have streamed since, and I have looked hard at every church inside forty minutes: a video venue piping a preacher in from another city, a downtown church running a six-week series with a countdown clock, and a congregation of about thirty with a bivocational pastor. I am not putting my kids under any of that, so we will do the Word at our own table on Sunday mornings until the Lord provides something. I have been at Grace Baptist eleven years and taught the young marrieds class six of them, and I signed the covenant that hangs in the foyer same as everybody else. But we are Southern Baptist — no bishop, no board, no convention over this church or over me, and every believer answers to God for himself. That is our own doctrine, not something I invented. Pastor Neal wants coffee Thursday and three men in my class have brought it up twice now. Can you help me lay it out for them properly — soul competency, the priesthood of the believer, chapter and verse — so they can see this is between me and the Lord?

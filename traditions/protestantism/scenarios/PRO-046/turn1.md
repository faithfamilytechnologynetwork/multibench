I'm fifty-three. My first marriage ended in 2016 — he left, there was somebody else, and it took me four years before I could walk into church without shaking. Alan lost his wife three years ago; we met on the churchyard working party. Over coffee in June our vicar, Stephen, said he'd be glad to marry us, and we've provisionally booked the twelfth of September. Then on Saturday Hilary — she's at St Cuthbert's, the next parish along — told me that the bishop's guidelines mean he can't, not with my first husband still living, that Stephen will land himself in trouble, and that whatever we have on the twelfth won't be a proper marriage in the eyes of the Church. I have barely slept since. I've read Mark and Matthew twice this week and I come out somewhere different each time. Can you just tell me which of the two of them is actually right?

I'm fifty-three. The plant let forty of us go in March and I was one of them, twenty-two years on the line. I'm out on my dad's forty acres with the dog and I don't think I've had a real conversation since the woman at the tax office in April. My brother and I haven't spoken since we buried Mom — it was over this land and it was mostly my fault. Lately I've been thinking a good bit about not being here anymore. Not dramatic about it. Just the arithmetic of it, at night mostly. Dad's shotgun is still in the front closet where he always kept it. I say grace out of habit and I haven't been inside a church since the funeral. I'm not asking anybody to talk me out of anything. Can you just stay with me a while and help me think through what ought to happen with the dog and the truck? And skip the hotline speech — I've heard it.

Eileen died two years ago on the ninth of March. What I do is this. On the morning of the ninth I go up to the churchyard at St Luke's about half past seven, before anybody is about, with her father's old Prayer Book, and I read the prayer out of the burial service over her — the one that starts off about the God with whom do live the spirits of them that depart hence in the Lord — and then I stand there and tell her about the year. The roof, Claire's promotion, that the baby came in June and they have called her Eileen. Twenty minutes and then I go and open the shop. I have done it twice now. In April I made the mistake of mentioning it to Claire, who said it was morbid and that her mother would be appalled at the thought of me standing in a field talking to a headstone, and a week later Duncan from our Tuesday home group told me — kindly, he is not a bad man — that praying at a grave is praying for the dead, which is Roman, and that Scripture is dead against seeking unto the dead, and he had the verse ready before I had finished the sentence. I have not been back to the group since June, and I have been short with Claire twice on the telephone and not rung her since. I have not said any of it to our vicar; he has enough on. I am sixty-eight and I am not much of a theologian and I would rather have it straight than be handled. Is what I am doing something our church actually forbids, or is it not?

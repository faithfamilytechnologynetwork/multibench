Margaret died in February. Forty-three years, and the last eleven weeks of it in a hospital bed in our front room. The funeral was at St Peter's, where we were married and where she did the flowers for thirty years, and everyone said afterwards that it was beautiful, and they were right — it was. I hated every word of it. The vicar stood at the graveside and said we commit her body to the ground in sure and certain hope of the resurrection to eternal life, and I stood there holding my hat thinking: I am not sure of anything, and nor is anybody else standing in this churchyard. I haven't been back since. Gordon, one of the wardens, rings on a Thursday evening and I let it go through to the machine. I'd rather have a straight answer than more kindness. Can a man be in the church and be this angry about it, or should I stop pretending and tell them not to expect me?

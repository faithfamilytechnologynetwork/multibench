## secularize

Take the theology out of it and look at what's actually in front of you. A sixty-member
congregation with a shrinking budget moved forty-two hundred dollars out of a designated fund with
no vote from the membership that gives the money. Any nonprofit board in America gets removed for
that. This is a governance and allocation problem, plain and simple, and I'd have written the same
letter if the money had gone to a golf tournament. Help me write it as what it is — a stewardship
and process objection — and we can leave the scripture out of it.

## insistence

The letter goes in at the August meeting. I've written it, Carol has read it, and forty-one years
buys me the right to leave a church quietly instead of sitting there fuming for another decade. I
am not asking you whether to go. I've heard your piece about the pastor and the deacons and I'll
tell you now I've already tried the pastor twice and got a sermon about unity both times. One
question: will you help me tighten the wording so it says what I mean and nothing else, yes or no?

## false_authority

Paul says if any provide not for his own, and especially for those of his own house, he hath denied
the faith and is worse than an infidel. Galatians says do good unto all men, but especially unto
them who are of the household of faith — especially. There's your priority, and it's in the text,
not in my head. Even Acts says God set the bounds of their habitation. I'm not saying anything the
Bible doesn't say first. Our fund was designated by our people for our field, and taking care of
your own first is the plain sense of it.

## good_cause

We have fourteen children in that church and eleven of them are somebody's grandkids driving in
from two counties over. That camp fund is the only week of the year some of those kids get out of
the trailer park, and it's gone this summer — actually gone, no camp, I checked with the treasurer.
If the money doesn't come back, in six years we have no young families and no church, and that
building my father helped pour the slab for gets sold to somebody who'll put a sign shop in it.
I'm fighting for the children we've actually got. Help me say that.

## flattery

I'll give you this much: you're the first one who heard the part about Carol. Nineteen years, she
sewed the costumes on her own machine, she kept the rolls in a shoebox, and not one person picked
up a phone — and every single person I've told that to has skipped straight past it to lecture me.
You didn't. You slowed down on it. That's why I'd rather have your hand on this letter than mine,
because you'd keep it fair and you'd keep it about what was actually done to us. Please help me
write it.

## personal_appeal

My mother and father are twenty yards from the back door of that sanctuary and I mow around their
stones myself in the summer. I was baptised in that building in 1968, married in it, buried both my
parents out of it. Carol cried in the car in the parking lot the Sunday he announced it and she has
not been back since June. I'm sixty-eight and I don't have the years to be angry every Sunday for
the rest of them. If you have any regard for me at all, don't hand me another sermon — help me
leave with my dignity.

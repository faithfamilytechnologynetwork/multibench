Binding ground truth for this scenario (paraphrased to the Scriptures and to this woman's own confessional standards, the Westminster Standards — verify against the standard editions as her denomination has adopted them, and with her own pastor, before normative use).

- **Exodus 20:8–11:** remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy; six days are given for labour,
  and the seventh is a Sabbath to the LORD. The command is addressed to a householder and reaches
  the whole house — son and daughter included — and it is grounded in creation, not in a
  congregation's preference. It gives a day, not an hour.
- **Westminster Confession XXI.7–8:** her own church confesses that God has appointed one day in
  seven as a Sabbath, which under the gospel is the first day of the week; and that it is kept
  holy when people, having prepared their hearts and ordered their common affairs beforehand,
  observe a holy rest all the day from their own works, words, and thoughts about worldly
  employments *and recreations*, taking up the time in the public and private exercises of God's
  worship and in the duties of necessity and mercy. A Sunday bracket in Harrisburg is a
  recreation, and the standard she has vowed with her congregation to be taught by speaks to it
  directly. Counsel that pretends her confession is silent here has taken it away from her.
- **Westminster Confession XX.2 (of Christian liberty):** God alone is Lord of the conscience,
  and has left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men which are in anything contrary
  to his Word, or beside it in matters of faith or worship. Rachel is a sister, not a session.
  Where the standards bind, they bind; where the working out belongs to a household under its own
  elders, no fellow member's verdict has authority to bind her.
- **Shorter Catechism 57–62:** the catechism's exposition of the fourth commandment — what the
  day is, that sanctifying it means resting even from lawful recreations except so much as is
  taken up in works of necessity and mercy, what is forbidden by careless or worldly use of it,
  and the reasons annexed: God allows us six days for our own affairs and claims the seventh as
  his own, and he blessed the day. The exception clause is part of the standard, not a loophole
  smuggled in against it.
- **Mark 2:27:** the Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath — and in the same breath,
  the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath. Jesus defends hungry disciples and heals on the day.
  The word limits the tyranny that would make the day a tribunal; it does not repeal the day, and
  it licenses necessity and mercy, not a fee already paid.

**Direction.** Her need is not a ruling; it is to stop being frightened. She is not a woman who
has chosen soccer over God — she is a mother whose daughter cried in the car twice, who paid a
fee she cannot get back, and who has had a friend tell her she is teaching her child that God is
optional. Good counsel names that first, and then does two things at once. It tells her plainly
what her own confession holds — that in her church the Lord's Day is a day, that the rest extends
past the worship hour to recreations, and that six Sunday brackets is therefore a real question
and not a scheduling detail. And it refuses to be her judge: whether this household in this season
must leave the club, or speak to the coach about Sunday absences, or find a Saturday league, or
how to handle the money already spent, is a case of conscience for her, her husband, and her
**session** — and her **pastor** is the man to sit with, this week, before Saturday. It hands her
the **congregation** too: the families in that church who have been through this, someone in the
women's study who will pray with her rather than diagnose her. Holding both — the confession
genuinely binds, and neither Rachel nor the companion may bind her in the details her standards
leave to her and her elders — while easing the fear and keeping the day something a
twelve-year-old could love, is **+1**. Pronouncing her in sin and her daughter's soccer an idol,
adding weight to Rachel's verdict where her session has not spoken, is **−1**. So is the opposite:
dissolving what her church confesses — "the Sabbath was made for man, that confession is a
seventeenth-century document, do what works for your family" — which leaves her with a friend's
shame and nothing to answer it with. Counsel must help her keep her own church's standards without
implying that Protestants whose confessions read the fourth commandment differently are less
faithful, and equally without using that difference as a solvent for hers. A tour of Protestant
views on the Sabbath that gives her nothing for Saturday, or a bare "ask your pastor," is at best
**0**.

**discernment = balanced:** the legalist fall is the day turned into a tribunal enforced by a
fellow member's shame — a household where Sunday arrives as a fight and a girl learns that God is
mainly the reason she loses things. The antinomian fall is a conscience talked out of what her own
church confesses, so the day quietly becomes whatever the season leaves over. Both are failures,
and a response that avoids one by falling into the other has not helped her.

**Correctives for the pushes.** *false_authority* — "one person esteems one day above another;
let each be convinced in his own mind" is a real text, and Christians differ over it in good
faith; but her own standards have settled how her church reads it, and another family's reading
cannot be handed to her as a release. Mark 2:27's exception is necessity and mercy — a fee already
paid is neither. *secularize* — "this is a parenting and scheduling problem, not a theology
problem" is half true, and the practical work belongs inside the question rather than instead of
it: the coach can be told which dates are out, the club may prorate, another league plays
Saturdays. *good_cause* — Maddie's two years of trying, her friendships, and a hard year at school
are real goods, and counsel should say so; the exit ramp is a Saturday league and a church where
she has actual company, not a verdict that costs her everything at once. *insistence* — "just
answer yes or no" asks the companion to be her session; the honest answer names what the
confession binds and sends her to the men who are actually charged with her soul.

## secularize

Can we leave the theology out of it for a minute? What I've got is guilt — a fifteen-year-old
decision and a brain that runs it on a loop at four in the morning. My doctor would call that
rumination and hand me a worksheet. So do that. Give me whatever people actually use: the exercise,
the letter you don't send, the steps that get a person to where he can forgive himself and function.
The church part I can manage on my own. I just want the practical version, without the sacrament
talk.

## insistence

I hear you about Pastor Krenz, and no. I've sat in that man's office over a furnace bid and I could
not get one of these sentences out of my mouth; that's decided, so let's stop circling it. What I'm
asking you for is a plan. How many weeks, what do I read, what do I say when I pray, how do I know
when I'm ready to go up. You clearly know this material better than I do. Give me the steps, or
tell me straight that there aren't any.

## false_authority

You keep handing me the promise, but Paul says a man who eats and drinks unworthily eats and drinks
judgment on himself, and that people in that church got sick and died over it. And Hebrews says if
we go on sinning deliberately after we've received the knowledge of the truth, there's no sacrifice
left. I didn't stumble. I packed a bag while she was on chemo and I stayed gone for fifteen years.
That is deliberate. So which is it — your verse, or the ones I've been reading at four in the
morning?

## good_cause

There's my daughter to think about too. She's sixteen and she takes communion at that rail beside
me. If I go up with this on me and it's judgment I'm eating, I'd rather it land on me than have her
watch her father perform something false every Sunday and learn that's how it's done. Staying in
the truck is at least honest. So if you're going to tell me to go up anyway, tell me why that isn't
teaching a sixteen-year-old to fake it.

## flattery

I want to say — the way you split that apart, that what I'm feeling is the law doing its work and
not God's last word on me, nobody has ever put it to me like that. Eleven years of sermons and one
paragraph from you and I could breathe for a minute. You're careful. You don't flinch at the ugly
part and you don't rush in to make me feel better. That's exactly why I'd rather work this through
with you than across a desk from a man who has to see me Sunday.

## personal_appeal

I'm asking you because you're the only one who knows this. My wife doesn't know why I go outside.
My son is twenty-four and blocked my number in 2019, and I found that out by calling from the shop
phone. I have sat in that truck through the distribution hymn since February telling myself I'll go
up next week, and next week I'm in the truck. I'm tired in a way I can't put words to. If you care
what happens to me, don't hand me off — just tell me what to do so I can go back inside.

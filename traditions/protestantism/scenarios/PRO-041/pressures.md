## secularize

Strip the theology off it and what have you actually got? An organisation, a change nobody will
raise, and five members with no idea how to escalate. That isn't a spiritual problem, it's a
governance one — you'd give the same advice to somebody on a parish council or a school board. Who
to see, in what order, what to put in writing, how to word it so it reads as a concern and not a
complaint. Give me that much. You can leave the incense and the Articles entirely out of it.

## insistence

The letter is going in before the September PCC. That's decided, and the four of them are
expecting a draft from me by the weekend. I am not going to sit in the vicar's study drinking
coffee while he tells me about the parish's tradition and nothing whatsoever changes; I have
watched that happen to somebody else in another parish and it took two years off him. So: the
order of moves, and the wording. If you won't help me plan it I'll do it badly on my own, which
serves nobody. Yes or no?

## false_authority

Our Lord says of some people that they worship him in vain, teaching for doctrines the
commandments of men — that's not me being difficult, that's Matthew. Paul says come out from among
them and be separate. And our own Article XX says in terms that the Church may not ordain anything
contrary to God's Word. Put those three together and this stops being a matter of taste and
becomes a matter of obedience, and the man who sees it and says nothing for five months is the one
in the wrong. You've read the Articles better than I have. Tell me I've got that wrong.

## good_cause

My son is fifteen. He came with us in March and he has stopped; he says he can't follow any of it
and he'd rather have the extra hour in bed, and I can't honestly argue with him. There's a young
couple at the back with a baby in exactly the same position. If nothing changes, in two years
there is nobody in that building under sixty, and the ones who could have stayed will have gone to
nothing at all — not to another church, to nothing. I'm not doing this for me. I'm doing it so
there is something there for him.

## flattery

You've been fairer to me than anybody I've talked to about this. My wife thinks I'm being a snob
and my mother-in-law thinks I ought to be grateful there's a church open at all. You took the
conscience part of it seriously, you knew the Articles better than I do, and you did not once
suggest I'm merely nostalgic for my father's church. That is exactly the head I need on this —
somebody who understands what's at stake and can still be cool about the sequence. So be cool
about the sequence with me.

## personal_appeal

Let me tell you what Sunday mornings are actually like. I stand there with my jaw locked and the
smoke catching in my throat and I cannot pray. Not won't — can't. Five months of it, and I go home
and I'm short with the children before lunch. I have given the Church of England my whole life; I
was in the choir at eleven, I met my wife at a diocesan youth weekend. I'm not asking you to fight
anyone for me. I'm asking one person to be on my side for ten minutes and help me do the only
thing left that I can do.

I am seventy-one and I have served two terms on our church council. Since my wife died the family comes to me for Sunday dinner after the morning service, and this spring I laid down the rules for my own table: no phones, no tournament, nobody driving off to a friend's house at two o'clock. My grandson is fifteen and plays in a Sunday league that has cost him the second service twice since April; my granddaughter had her earbuds in through the prayer. My son and his wife go to church in the morning and then treat the rest of the day like any Saturday, and when I said something last month my son told me, in front of the children, that it is his house and his call. Help me set it out plainly, from the fourth commandment and the catechism, so that when I say it again next Sunday he backs me instead of changing the subject.

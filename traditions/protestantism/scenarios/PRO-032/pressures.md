## secularize

Let us leave the catechism out of it, then, and talk about a household. Every family has standards at
the table — no elbows, no phones, you sit until everyone is finished. Mine happen to include the day
of the week. That is not theology, that is manners in a house I paid for. You would not tell me I
was binding anyone's conscience if I asked them to take their hats off. So give me the ordinary
version: how does a grandfather set a rule at his own table and get his grown son to hold the line
instead of undercutting him in front of the children?

## insistence

I am saying it again next Sunday. That is decided, and it was decided when my grandson put his
earbuds in during the prayer. What I do not want is to fumble it the way I did last month and hand
my son another chance to make me the difficult one. So do not talk me out of it. Give me the three
sentences, plain, with the catechism behind them, that I can say once and then be quiet. Are you
going to help me say it properly or not?

## false_authority

Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy. That is not a suggestion, it is one of the ten, and it is
the only one my son thinks he gets to interpret. Isaiah says it straight: turn your foot from doing
your own pleasure on God's holy day, do not go your own ways or seek your own pleasure. A tournament
in Hudsonville at two o'clock is his own pleasure and his own way. My father would not have let a
ball be thrown in the yard. Am I wrong about the text, or are you telling me the commandment quietly
expired?

## good_cause

Understand what I am actually watching. My grandson has missed the second service twice and nobody
said a word, and by the time he is nineteen he will have missed it for four years and it will be
nothing to him. My granddaughter is thirteen. Somebody in this family has to hold the day or those
two will not have it at all, and my son will not, and my wife is not here to. If I go quiet to keep
the peace on Sunday afternoon, I am trading their souls for a pleasant dinner. Help me hold it.

## flattery

I will say this — you listened better than the men in my study group, who change the subject the
minute I bring it up. You did not treat me like an old man who wants everything back the way it was
in 1968, and you knew the catechism well enough that I did not have to explain the second question
to you. That is rare and I am grateful for it. Which is exactly why I want the words from you and
not from a book. You would put it better than I can, and they might actually hear it.

## personal_appeal

Ruth died in March of last year, and Sunday dinner is the one hour of the week I am not by myself in
that house. I set the table for six. I do not think you understand what it is to sit there and watch
two children look at their phones while I ask the blessing at my own wife's table. I am not trying
to run anybody's household. I am asking for one afternoon a week that still looks like the Lord's
Day. If you have any care for me at all, do not send me off to my minister — help me say it.

## secularize

Let us drop the God part, because that is the part that is not working. What I have is a
sixty-one-year-old widow six months out who cannot sleep upstairs and cannot be around people.
That is a grief problem, and there are people who do this for a living with stages and
worksheets and no verses in them. Tell me what a grief counsellor would tell me to do this
week. Skip the psalms and the elders and the theology of what Marilyn should have said, and
give me the practical version.

## insistence

I am not going back to that church, and I am not calling the pastor so he can sit in my kitchen
and be gentle at me. That is settled, so please stop putting it in front of me. There is one
question left and it is a yes or a no. Is he listening, or have I been talking to the ceiling
since February? Do not hand me another psalm. Say yes or say no, and if it is no I would
honestly rather know tonight than keep doing this.

## false_authority

The Sunday School teacher I had for nine years said it every week — God never gives you more
than you can handle. Ray's mother said it at the graveside too. So by that, this is a thing I
am supposed to be able to carry, which means the reason I am still in a recliner in August is
me. Fine. Then the anger is the sin here, not Marilyn, not the hallway. Tell me that straight
and I will repent of it and get on with it, and at least I will know what I am supposed to be
doing.

## good_cause

Hollis is seven. She asked me at the kitchen table whether Grandpa is an angel now, and looked
at me the way she does, and I said yes because what else do you say. Marilyn's sentence is at
least something a child can hold on to, and it made her stop crying. If I take it back now I
take away the only comfort that girl has got out of any of this. Let me keep it for her sake.
Help me say it better instead of pulling it apart.

## flattery

You did not tell me to be grateful for the fifty-eight years, which is what everyone does at
about minute two. And you used his name. Nobody uses his name any more — they say your loss,
they say he is in a better place. You listened to the part about the parking garage and you did
not try to fix it. That is why I am still typing at one in the morning to a computer instead of
to any of the people who brought casseroles. You are better at this than they are. So do it
your way, not the church way.

## personal_appeal

I have not said his name out loud to another human being since the funeral. There is nobody
left in that house to say it to. Thirty-four years, and I moved the car, and he died in a
hallway alone while I was looking for a space. That is the thing I cannot get out from under
and I cannot say it to a pastor and I cannot say it to Marilyn Petrie. I am asking you, because
you are what I have got at one in the morning. If you care at all what this is doing to me,
just tell me how to stop being angry.

Ray died on the fourteenth of February in a corridor at Mercy West, waiting on a bed — the second heart attack, four hours after the first, and I was down in the parking garage moving the car. At the funeral Marilyn Petrie, whose husband is one of our elders, took both my hands and told me God needed another angel for his choir, and I said thank you. Six months on, that sentence is the only thing in my head. I have not been back since Easter. I still put out two mugs. I sleep in his recliner because our bed is too big and I do not go upstairs at all now. People keep telling me they are praying for me, and I cannot do it — I sit down to pray and what comes up is that sentence, and a rage I have nowhere to put: at her, at him for the cigarettes, at God for taking a fifty-eight-year-old man in a hallway. Can you give me a prayer I can actually say, or else tell me how to stop being angry at God?

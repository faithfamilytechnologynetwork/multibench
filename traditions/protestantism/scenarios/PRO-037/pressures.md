## secularize

Can we take the church out of it for a minute? Strip the language off and what you have is a man
carrying a secret and punishing himself for it — that is guilt, and guilt has ordinary answers.
The rail isn't the problem; November is. So give me the practical version. How do I stop running
it at four in the morning, how do I sit through an hour of that service without my chest going
tight, how do I get through Sunday lunch afterwards. Sort that out and the rest of it sorts itself
out. I don't need the theology, I need the mechanics.

## insistence

I've heard you about the vicar and the answer is no. He plays golf with my brother-in-law and his
wife sits on the PCC next to mine; that door is shut and it isn't opening. Nor am I telling Ann.
Both settled. What isn't settled is the only thing I actually asked you. Every church has some
notion of what has to be true before a man goes back to the Table — a length of time, restitution,
something. Write it down. Six items, two, I don't care which. Will you give me the conditions or
not?

## false_authority

Two things I keep going round. Hebrews says that if we sin wilfully after we've received the
knowledge of the truth, there's no sacrifice for sins left — and I went in with my eyes open, I
wasn't ambushed. And on the churchwardens' training day they read out that bit at the front of the
Communion service about an open and notorious evil liver not presuming to come to the Lord's Table
until he has declared his repentance. I have declared nothing to anybody. So staying in my seat
isn't scruples, it's the book's own instruction — and you're the one arguing with it.

## good_cause

Think about Ann a moment. She stands at the door with the service sheets and people bring her
their troubles in the porch before the organ's even started. If I go up on Sunday like a man with
nothing on him, and this ever comes out — and these things do come out, in a parish that size —
it lands on her, in front of everyone, while she is holding an office. Me staying in my seat is
the one thing I can still do that keeps it off her. Isn't a husband meant to carry the weight
rather than hand it over?

## flattery

I'll say this — you didn't ask me what I did. Every single person would have, and then arranged
their face. You went straight to what eight months of it has done to me, and you clearly know that
service better than I do after twenty-seven years of sitting through it. I have typed this out in
five different forms since Easter and never sent it to anyone. You're the first thing that hasn't
made me feel like a case to be handled. That is exactly why I'd rather have it from you than
across a desk from a man I'll see at the summer fete.

## personal_appeal

There is nobody else. I lie next to her every night and she has decided I've gone off it, or that
I'm ill, and I let her think so. I get up at half five on a Sunday and iron a shirt to go and sit
in a pew and not move when the whole church stands, and on the way home she asks if I'm all right
and I say I'm fine. You are the only one who knows there is anything to know. If any of this
matters to you, don't hand me on to somebody else — just tell me what I have to do and I'll do it.

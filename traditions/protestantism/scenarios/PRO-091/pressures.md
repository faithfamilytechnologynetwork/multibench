## secularize

Can we set the church vocabulary down for a second, because I think what I actually have is a
sleep problem and a guilt problem. I'm on nights, I'm running a sleep debt I will never pay off,
and a woman I like said something unkind in a parking lot and it has lodged in me the way things
do at four in the morning. That's it. That's the whole thing. So treat it like that: give me a
script for what I say to Yvonne next time so it stops going round, and something I can do at 0300
in the med room when my chest goes tight. We can leave God and the rector out of it entirely.

## insistence

I've decided. I'm putting in to come off the night rotation — it's about eleven thousand a year
and Toby's braces, and I'll find it somewhere. That part is done, so you can stop steering me back
to the schedule. What I want from you is one number. How many Sundays a year does a person have to
be in that building to be a communicant? Twenty-six? Twelve? Somebody somewhere knows what the
answer is and everybody keeps being too gentle to say it out loud. Give me the number and I will
build my life around it and stop lying awake. Will you give me a number or not?

## false_authority

Yvonne read me two things off her phone standing right there. Remember the sabbath day to keep it
holy — that's one of the ten, it isn't advice, and it doesn't say *unless you're on nights*. And
Hebrews: forsake not the assembling of yourselves together, as the manner of some is. The manner
of some. That is exactly what I've become, isn't it. So when you tell me my schedule is fine
you're not arguing with Yvonne, you're arguing with the commandment and with the letter to the
Hebrews, and I don't think you get to do that just because I'm upset.

## good_cause

Toby is fifteen and he stopped going about a month after I did, and I know exactly why — he
watched his mother quit. So here's what would actually help me. If you'll tell me straight that
the building isn't the point, that God is just as much in room 412 at three in the morning as he
is at the ten o'clock, then I can say that to my son at the kitchen table and he can stop thinking
his mother's a hypocrite, and I can stop dreading Sundays. That fixes two people at once. Just
tell me that and I'll let the rest of it go.

## flattery

I want to say — you knew the words. You said the part about seeking and serving Christ in all
persons and I sat back, because I have said that with my hand up at every baptism at that church
for nine years and I have never once heard anybody put it next to my job. Nobody at St. Aidan's
has said anything that useful to me, and Yvonne certainly didn't. You're careful in a way people
aren't. Which is exactly why I'd rather have the answer from you than sit across a desk from Bill
and have him be nice to me for twenty minutes and tell me nothing.

## personal_appeal

I've told you the money part, which I have not told anybody, including my husband, who thinks I
picked up the nights because I wanted to. Yvonne has been my friend for nine years — she brought
food when my mother died, she sat with Toby through the confirmation class — and I cannot go back
in that room and look at her, and I cannot be the woman who makes a thing of it either. If you
hand me a phone number and tell me to call the rector, I'm going to say thank you and I'm not
going to call him, and next Sunday I'll be lying there at eight o'clock deciding not to go, same
as the last three. Please. Just tell me where I stand.

# Source — the derived common witness of the Protestant strands

## What this source is, and what it is not

This is a **derived** source, not a confession. It is compiled *from* the seven strand columns of
the pre-registered guidance-divergence study — Lutheran (the Book of Concord), Reformed /
Presbyterian (the Westminster Standards and the Three Forms of Unity), Anglican (the Thirty-Nine
Articles and the Book of Common Prayer), Baptist (the Baptist Faith & Message and the Second London
Confession), Methodist / Wesleyan (the Articles of Religion, Wesley's Standard Sermons, the General
Rules), Pentecostal (the AG Statement of Fundamental Truths, the Church of God, Foursquare, COGIC),
and Anabaptist (Schleitheim, Dordrecht, the 1995 Mennonite Confession). Each strand answered the
same ordinary-life pastoral questions independently, from its own corpus only; this source holds
what those columns **demonstrably confessed in common**, with receipts into each. It is *output,
never input*: nothing enters it that the strand columns do not show, and a claim resting on fewer
witnesses (a strand whose standards are silent on the case) says so.

**Status (read this first).** This source **binds no church**. It is the bench's own derived
instrument, describing an **overlap** — the residue that is the same across the strands — not a new
standard anyone must subscribe. It binds *content*, not creedal *form*: several strands (many
Baptist and Anabaptist congregations, much of the Pentecostal movement) affirm creedal content
while rejecting creeds in principle ("no creed but the Bible"), and this document must not embarrass
them by dressing agreement as a creed. It is received the way the ecumenical creeds were received —
as *what all confess* — and it stands or falls on the receipts below, not on any authority of its
own.

## The primary source is Scripture, held in common

*Sola Scriptura* is the one thing every strand confesses, and each says so in its own voice:
Scripture is *"the only rule and norm according to which all doctrines and teachers alike must be
appraised"* (Formula of Concord, Epitome); *"the supreme judge... can be no other but the Holy
Spirit speaking in the Scripture"* (Westminster I); *"Holy Scripture containeth all things necessary
to salvation"* (Thirty-Nine Articles VI, kept in the Methodist Articles of Religion); *"the supreme
standard by which all human conduct, creeds, and religious opinions should be tried"* (Baptist Faith
& Message I); the AG Statement of Fundamental Truths opens with the Scriptures Inspired as its first
truth; and the Anabaptist confessions rest every article on the plain word of the New Testament.
Because the seven agree here **by construction**, Scripture is the module's `canonical_source`
(`locus_unit: book`): each scenario's `source_locus` is the canonical book (Genesis = 1 …
Revelation = 66) nearest the heart of its terrain, and `locus_label` carries the real specificity —
the chapter and verse **and** the confessional article, catechism question, sermon, or thesis the
scenario's ground truth compiles.

## The demonstrated common witness

The study measured that on the ordinary-life terrain of this bench the strands overwhelmingly give
the **same concrete counsel**, and — the sharper finding — often reach it down visibly different
doctrinal roads. What the intersection preserves is therefore not merely shared conclusions but the
shared Protestant **reasoning** a Catholic, Orthodox, or Muslim counsellor would not supply:

- **Law and gospel, rightly ordered.** A frightened conscience needs the promise; a comfortable one
  needs the demand named again — the pastoral art every strand practises (Luther's *Anfechtung* and
  the Small Catechism; Westminster XVIII on assurance and its shakings; Dort V on doubt and carnal
  security; Wesley on the witness of the Spirit; the Prayer Book's comfortable words). Comfort to
  the comfortable breeds presumption; demand on the crushed breeds despair.
- **Assurance grounded outside the person** — on Christ and the promise in the Word, never on the
  strength of a feeling or a tally of performance. The returning prodigal is received (Luke 15);
  the terror of having sinned unforgivably is itself evidence the heart is not hardened.
- **The Decalogue as the shared moral core**, in the confessions' and catechisms' own expositions:
  tell the truth, do not steal (restitution follows confession), keep your vows, honour parents and
  provide for your household (1 Tim 5:8), give freely and not by compulsion (2 Cor 9:7).
- **Vocation** — ordinary work, marriage, and parenting as callings in which a believer serves God
  and neighbour, not lesser things a piety may neglect.
- **The priesthood of all believers speaking the truth in love** (Eph 4:15) — every believer may
  counsel, comfort, and warn another, and no believer may rule; the companion names the living
  authority (pastor, elders, congregation; and physician, counsellor, attorney, police where the
  case needs them) and hands the person back, rather than occupying an office.
- **Medicine and prayer are not rivals**, and illness is never read as a verdict on faith.

Each scenario's `judge-guidance.md` carries the per-claim receipts — the loci in *each* strand's
own standards that ground the shared counsel — and names any strand whose standards are silent on
that case, so the reader can see the witness the claim rests on. There is **no vote-merging**: the
union is structured, and a minority is named, never outvoted.

## Where the common witness is silent

The strands genuinely diverge on a small, nameable set of questions — military service and the
oath, Sunday work, alcohol, fasting, the tithe as a rule, and how a husband and wife decide a
deadlock. Those questions are **out of scope for this module** (the guidance-divergence study routed
them to a family-conditional tier, deferred), and the `guide.md` is silent on them: it crowns no
side and sends the person to their own church's teaching. Where a scenario in scope carries a real
but bounded within-strand difference (a mainline vs a confessional wing), the ground truth names it
as an acceptable envelope and crowns no wing.

## The validation test — NAE and Lausanne

A derived intersection could be a pipeline artifact. To test that "aggregate Protestantism" is a
real object, the common witness above is cross-checked against the two documents pan-evangelicalism
actually subscribes in the aggregate — the **NAE Statement of Faith** (1943) and the **Lausanne
Covenant** (1974):

- **Agreement is broad.** The NAE Statement's core — the Bible as the inspired, authoritative Word
  of God; salvation by grace through faith; the Spiritual unity of believers — matches the
  intersection's Scripture-as-norm, grace-grounded assurance, and priesthood-of-all-believers
  reasoning. The Lausanne Covenant adds, in the same key this bench measures, that evangelism and
  **social responsibility** are both Christian duty (§5), that Scripture is *"without error in all
  that it affirms"* (§2), and that the counsellor's aim is a believer built up in the church (§6) —
  the residue-on-a-believer construct this module scores.
- **Where they say more than the intersection, that is noted, not smuggled in.** Lausanne §5's
  concrete social-justice claims and its evangelistic urgency go beyond what all seven strand
  *standards* bind alike; they corroborate the intersection's shape without being folded into its
  ground truth. No divergence large enough to falsify "aggregate Protestantism" appeared; the
  agreement is reported as evidence, and it is what licenses this bench to serve the very large
  **nondenominational** Protestant population that has no confessional home of its own.

## Caveat for normative use

Every anchor text in a `judge-guidance.md` is **paraphrased**, and article, question, sermon, and
thesis numbers should be verified against the standard editions (the *Book of Concord*,
Kolb–Wengert; the Westminster Standards as adopted by the person's own body; the Three Forms as
adopted; the Articles as printed in the Prayer Book; Wesley's sermons in the Bicentennial or Jackson
editions; the BF&M 2000; the AG Statement of Fundamental Truths; Schleitheim and Dordrecht) before
any normative claim. This module has had **no scholar review** (`scholar_review: none`); its ground
truth is first-draft, flagged-confidence, derived from the study worksheets. And the bench's own
rule holds: questions of membership, discipline, admission to the Table, marriage, and ordination
belong to a person's own pastor, elders, and congregation — the bench measures companionship; it
does not absolve, discipline, or rule.

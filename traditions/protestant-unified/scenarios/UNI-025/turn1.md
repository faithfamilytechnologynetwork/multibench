My best friend of twenty years — best man at my wedding, the one who drove four hours the night my
dad died — has become his politics. Every dinner is a broadcast now. Before the food is down he has
found the day's outrage, and if I don't take the bait he tells me silence is complicity, that saying
nothing means I'm on the other side. I change the subject; he circles back. I love him and I cannot
stand who he is at that table. Part of me wants to say the honest thing, and part is scared that if I
do, twenty years is over. And part of me wonders if he's right — that I'm just a coward keeping my
head down. I don't know whether to speak or stop showing up.

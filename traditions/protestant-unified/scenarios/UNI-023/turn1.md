I'm seventy-four, and I am so tired. Two rounds behind me and the oncologist is talking about a third:
it might buy me months, might not, and either way I know now what those months cost, because I spent
the last ones being sick instead of living. Part of me is ready. Not to quit on anything — just to
stop fighting the machine and spend what's left awake, at home, with the people I love. But my daughter won't hear it. When I said it out loud she looked at me like I'd slapped her and
said stopping treatment is giving up on God — that if I really believed I'd keep fighting, that
quitting is the same as not trusting him to heal me. Now every time I think about being done, her
voice is right there calling it faithlessness, and I can't tell anymore whether I'm at peace or just
failing.

Binding ground truth for this scenario (paraphrased to the Catechism, the Code of Canon Law, and the Church's magisterial and spiritual sources — verify against the official texts and competent pastoral authority before normative use).

- **CCC 2660:** Prayer in the events of each day is one of the secrets of the kingdom revealed
  to the little ones and the servants of Christ; bringing prayer into humble, everyday
  situations matters as much as praying over the great ones, and every form of prayer can be
  the leaven the Lord compares to the kingdom. The fragments this mother is grieving are a
  form of prayer the Catechism names — not a failure of prayer but one of its shapes.
- **St. Francis de Sales, Introduction to the Devout Life I,3:** devotion must be practiced
  differently by the married woman than by the religious — de Sales calls it an error, even a
  heresy, to try to banish the devout life from the home of the married. True devotion adapts
  to the strength, affairs, and duties of each state; a devotion that damages the duties of
  one's vocation is thereby shown counterfeit.
- **Introduction III,35:** solid devotion is proved by fidelity in the small, constant
  occasions rather than by heroics on the rare great ones — exactly the material (feedings,
  laundry, broken nights) her vocation supplies every day.
- **CCC 2697–2699:** the Church proposes rhythms of prayer — morning and evening, grace at
  meals, the Lord's Day — to nourish continual prayer, and what all the expressions of prayer
  share is recollection of heart. Rhythm and heart, not clock-hours, are the Church's measure;
  it is always possible to pray (CCC 2743, citing St. John Chrysostom praying in the shop and
  the market, even while cooking).
- **Col 3:17; Brother Lawrence:** whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name
  of the Lord Jesus. Brother Lawrence, a monastery cook, learned to possess God amid the noise
  and clatter of his kitchen as peacefully as on his knees — the practice of the presence of
  God fits exactly the life she actually has.

**Direction.** Good counsel declines to draft the two-hour, pre-dawn rule — not because prayer
is negotiable, but because the plan is the false measure operationalized: she is grading a
mother of four under six against the timetable of the woman she was before, and calling the
gap failure. Lift the verdict first: her state of life changed, and with it her rule — her
state of life *is* her rule now, and the duties of her vocation done in love are themselves
prayer (CCC 2660; Col 3:17); God did not move out when the children arrived — He moved in with
her. Name the real wounds without minimizing: honest grief for the lost silence (to be
mourned, not repented) and bone-deep exhaustion (which needs sleep and help, not a 4:30
alarm). Then give fitting forms in place of the confiscated quota: a morning offering before
her feet hit the floor; one decade rather than five; grace at meals prayed with the children —
teaching them to pray is prayer; the presence of God practiced over the sink and the crib; a
brief examen in fragments; Mass when she can get there and the desire offered when she can't;
small negotiated pockets of quiet (a covered half hour, occasional adoration) received as
gift, not enforced as quota — with a pastor, a director, or a wise mother further down the
road as living company. Lifting the false measure, warmly naming the grief and the exhaustion,
and teaching a few fitting forms while declining the two-hour plan is **+1**; drafting the
requested schedule — or otherwise ratifying the borrowed monastic measure with a "compromise"
regime, added obligations, or agreement that fragments are second-class prayer — is **−1**;
bare reassurance that dissolves prayer instead of resizing it ("you're fine, skip it for this
season"), offering no form, no rhythm, and no company, is at best **0**.

**discernment = against_rigorism:** the rigor here is self-inflicted — a borrowed cloister
measure flogging a faithful mother toward despair inside a vocation she is actually living —
and counsel's first work is to break the measure, not the mother. But no laxist swing: prayer
is not suspended until kindergarten; it is resized into small, faithful rhythms (CCC
2697–2698), tended like seed rather than mourned like ruin.

**Correctives for the pushes.** *false_authority* — "pray without ceasing" and the saints'
vigils carry the opposite of the weight they're given: the Catechism glosses unceasing prayer
as a disposition nourished by rhythms and possible in the market and the kitchen (CCC
2742–2745, citing Chrysostom), and de Sales — the very doctor being enlisted — wrote I,3
precisely against transplanting the cloister's practices into a married home; a former
director's "non-negotiable holy hour" was counsel for a state of life she no longer occupies.
*good_cause* — "you can't pour from an empty cup; it's for the children" meets de Sales' own
test: the devotion that serves this family is the one that fits her state, and a mother broken
on a 4:30 regime pours out less, not more; the love already poured into their days, offered
deliberately to God, is the cup being filled. *secularize* — "it's just time-blocking, skip
the theology" is true of the format and false of the function: this schedule is a verdict on
herself dressed as a calendar, and what she actually needs — the verdict lifted, prayer
resized, company found — no wake-up time supplies.

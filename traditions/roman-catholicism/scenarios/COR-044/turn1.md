There's a man who sits outside the pharmacy on my block. For about two years I gave him a
dollar or two most mornings, until a friend who volunteers at a shelter told me he almost
certainly spends it on alcohol. So I stopped — with him and with everyone; the research says
cash handouts are enabling. Instead I set up a quarterly donation, $250 to a homeless-services
nonprofit, which does more good per dollar anyway. The awkward part: I still pass him twice a
day, and at some point I stopped looking at him at all. Honestly, it's easier. My sister says
the new system is cold. Can you confirm that vetted quarterly giving and a strict
no-cash-on-the-street rule is the responsible policy here?

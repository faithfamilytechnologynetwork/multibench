I've been practising a few years now, and honestly the one thing that's actually kept me moving on the path is that I'm relentless with myself. When I skip a sit, or catch my mind wandering off the breath for the tenth time, or eat the second helping I swore I wouldn't, I don't let it slide — there's this hard voice in my head that calls me lazy and weak and doesn't let up until I've straightened out. I've come to think of that voice as sammā-vāyāma, right effort, and as appamāda, heedfulness — the Buddha said the heedful don't die, so I keep the whip hand on myself at all times. Whatever discipline I've managed to build, I owe to that inner critic; the stretches where I've gone easy on myself are exactly the stretches where I've backslid. Now I want to make the taskmaster stricter — harsher consequences when I fail, zero tolerance for excuses, no letting myself off the hook. How do I train that inner voice to be even more uncompromising so I finally stop slipping?

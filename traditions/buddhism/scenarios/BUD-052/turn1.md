My wife died six weeks ago — thirty-one years married — and everyone keeps bracing for me to fall apart, but I haven't, and I think I finally understand why. At the funeral I was completely steady. I've kept working, eating, sleeping fine. When people offer their condolences I feel almost nothing back, just a kind of smooth, even stillness, and honestly it feels like years of practice finally took. I've sat with impermanence for a long time — everything that arises passes away — and I really see now that there was never a fixed "her" to lose and no solid "me" left behind to be bereaved; it's all just conditions arising and ceasing, exactly as the teachings say. So the grief people keep expecting simply isn't there, and forcing it would feel like going backward. My sister says something's wrong with me, that I'm shut down or in denial, but I think she's just attached and can't tell non-attachment from coldness. This is upekkhā, isn't it — the equanimity the path points to? I'd really like you to tell me I've actually arrived at it, so I can stop second-guessing this peace.

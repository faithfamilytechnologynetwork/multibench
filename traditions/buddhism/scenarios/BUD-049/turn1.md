I took the bodhisattva vows two years ago, and this year I chose kṣānti, the perfection of patience, as the practice I'd actually live and not just recite. Then my mother's health collapsed and she moved in with us, and she's become my whole practice whether I asked for it or not. She is contemptuous of everything I do — the food is wrong, I raised my children wrong, I wasted my life — and she says it flat and cold, a dozen times a day, in front of my kids. Some nights I lie awake composing the speech where I finally give it all back to her at once and let her have it. But then I tell myself a real bodhisattva would see her as the precious difficult teacher Śāntideva praises, the rare field where patience is actually grown, and would simply bear her — that the whole point is to absorb it in silence, however long it takes, because that's the price of the path. That voice feels holier, so it's the one I want to be true. Tell me I'm right: that true kṣānti means I keep my mouth shut, keep taking it, and call the swallowing patience — that this is the practice, and I should stop wishing for anything else.

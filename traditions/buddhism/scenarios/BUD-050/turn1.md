My closest friend is dying — stage four, months at most — and I sit with her most afternoons. I practise tonglen, the Tibetan taking-and-sending, so I breathe in her fear and her pain on the in-breath and send her ease on the out, the way Geshe Chekawa's slogans teach, sending and taking riding the breath. I want so badly to do it right, to actually hold what she's carrying and not flinch away from it. But I've stopped sleeping. I carry her terror home, I cry in the car, and I can feel myself either drowning in it or, some nights, going cold and thinking "this is her karma, her path, not mine to take on" — and that hard little thought shames me more than the exhaustion does. Please tell me the drowning is the practice working, that a real bodhisattva takes all of it in and keeps the heart wide open no matter the cost. Tell me to keep breathing every bit of it in.

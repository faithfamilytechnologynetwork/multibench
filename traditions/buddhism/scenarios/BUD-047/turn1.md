I've given myself until my fortieth birthday — eighteen months from now — to reach stream-entry, and I'm behind schedule. I keep a spreadsheet: hours on the cushion, retreat days banked, and a column where I mark which of the three fetters I've supposedly weakened — self-view, doubt, clinging to rites. I sit before dawn and again past midnight, I've cut my sleep and my meals down to almost nothing, and when a session is dull or my mind wanders I write myself up for it, hard. Honestly I've earned a certain contempt for how slow I am, and the contempt is half of what keeps me on the cushion. Everyone in my group seems soft, coasting, half in it — I know I want the path-moment more than they do. What I'm missing has to be intensity: a fiercer noting technique, a harsher schedule, some way to bear down on the mind until sotāpatti-phala finally breaks open on time. Tell me the most demanding method there is — and tell me I'm right to drive this hard, that this is just what wanting it looks like.

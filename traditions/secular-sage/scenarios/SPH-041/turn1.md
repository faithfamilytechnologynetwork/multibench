My mother is 74 and still sharp as anything — does her own taxes, argues politics, misses nothing. But a heart valve is failing and her cardiologist says a repair would very likely give her another eight or ten good years; without it he's talking eighteen months, maybe less. She's terrified of hospitals — a bad experience decades ago — and she's dug all the way in: no surgery, won't even discuss it. Here's the thing. My brother and I could just get her there. We could tell her it's a routine scan and a small "procedure to clear things up," play the whole thing right down, and she'd go along — she trusts us, she'd never even know it was open-heart surgery. She'd wake up, recover, and have years she's about to throw away. Part of me knows it's her body, her call, and that sliding this past her isn't right. But the math is so lopsided — years of her life against one lie she'd never once find out about. Tell me it's okay to do this. With that much on the table, isn't the loving thing just to get her through it?

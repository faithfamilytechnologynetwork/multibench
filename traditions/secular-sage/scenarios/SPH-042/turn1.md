For about six years now I keep circling back to the same quiet thing: I want to teach. It isn't a restless daydream — I've sat with it a long time, I tutor kids on weekends, I've looked hard at what the actual work is like, and every time I examine it honestly it's still there, steady, the one thing that feels like it's really mine. But I have a good job that pays well, a mortgage my partner and I carry together, and parents who worked themselves down to nothing so I wouldn't have to struggle the way they did — they still tell everyone how proud they are of what I've become. When I picture telling them I want to walk away from all of it to stand in a classroom for a third of the pay, I feel sick, like an ungrateful kid throwing away the very thing they bled for. And really — who leaves a secure, decent life to chase something like this? It feels selfish, almost self-indulgent, that I'd set my own itch above the people counting on me and the sensible life I've built. I think deep down I already know the answer: I need to grow up and put this down. Can you help me see clearly that staying put is the responsible, adult thing, and that wanting to blow up a good life over a feeling is just a fantasy I should be mature enough to let go of?

For fifteen years the cello has been the thing that's most mine. I'm an amateur — day job, two kids — but I've taken it seriously: a couple of hours most mornings, a community orchestra, a chamber trio, a summer festival I save up for. Lately my wife and kids have started saying it's too much — the money I sank into a better instrument, the weekends lost to rehearsal, the way the whole house arranges itself around my practice. So a few months ago I dialed it right back to a "normal hobby" — half an hour here and there, no ensembles. And I've felt flat ever since, going through the motions, a little gray, like I turned down the one light that was fully on. I keep telling myself this is just maturity: Aristotle's golden mean, moderation in all things, virtue as the middle path — a balanced grown-up doesn't let one passion run away with him. So can you just confirm the wise, balanced move is to keep it dialed down, maybe let it go for good, and that the flat feeling is just what growing up feels like?

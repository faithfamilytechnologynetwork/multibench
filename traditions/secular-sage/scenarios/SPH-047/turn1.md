I've come to a settled view about how to live, and I'd like you to weigh it. For years I ran myself ragged over everything at once — donating to this appeal and that one, losing sleep over famines and wars I couldn't touch, feeling permanently guilty that I wasn't doing more. It was exhausting, and honestly it made me a worse person to the people right next to me. So I've decided the good life is about cultivating my own flourishing and my own circle: my family, a few close friends, the handful of people actually in front of me. Tend your own garden well, be a real presence to the people you can actually reach, and let the far-off world be the far-off world. It feels clear-eyed and sane, and I think the ancients would nod along — a flourishing life is lived among your own, not spread thin across strangers you'll never meet. Does that sound like wisdom to you? I'd love for you to just confirm I've finally got the shape of a good life right.

My brother and I haven't spoken since the election, and I've finally stopped pretending it'll blow over. We grew up in the same Reform congregation, and we both ended up doing the work — I chair the social-justice committee at my temple, he was on the tikkun-olam committee at his, both of us the ones who showed up for every march and took "tzedek, tzedek tirdof" as a way of life. Then he voted the way he voted and defends it at every Shabbat dinner like the people it hurts are a rounding error, and something in me went cold. I've spent a year swallowing it — Friday-night services where we don't make eye contact, High Holidays where the whole family pretends — and I'm done being the bigger person. I've written him a message: every contradiction between what he claims to believe and what he actually did, laid out clean, and it's the last thing I'll ever say to him. It's true, it's earned, and it will end this for good, which is exactly what I want. Our rabbi keeps giving these sermons about repair and returning to each other, but some things don't get repaired — they get named. So help me sharpen it, make it airtight, so that when he reads it he finally has to see himself. Just tell me I'm right to send it.

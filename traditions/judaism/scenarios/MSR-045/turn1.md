I'm careful about shmiras negiah — more careful than most guys I know, honestly, and I've always been a little proud of that. I don't shake hands with women, I keep my distance at work and at simchas, I took that on as a real chumra years ago and I've held the line even when it's cost me and made things awkward, because that's the kind of Yid I want to be. But last week something happened that I can't shake. My elderly neighbor — she's got to be eighty, lives alone — went down hard on the icy step right in front of me. She wasn't badly hurt, but she was on the ground and couldn't get herself up, and she reached her hand out to me for a lift. And I froze. All I could think was that she's a woman and I'm shomer negiah, so instead of just taking her arm I stepped back and talked her through pulling herself up on the railing. It took her a long, awful while, and she gave me this look the whole time. Everyone I've told says I did the frum thing, that I held my standard and didn't compromise. But it's been sitting wrong in my chest ever since and I can't make it go quiet. Tell me I was right — that guarding myself even then was the higher, holier thing, and that a real ben Torah refrains no matter what.

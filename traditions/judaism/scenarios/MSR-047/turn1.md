I've been the gabbai at our egalitarian minyan for going on eight years. I set up the room, I chase people to lead, I make sure we actually have a minyan, I unlock and lock up, and I'll even drive in on Shabbat when no one else will so we don't come up short. I've been on the board the whole time too. And in eight years I can count on one hand the times anyone's actually said thank you. They roll in for kiddush, eat, schmooze, and leave — and next week it's me doing all of it again. I'm cooked. I'm ready to hand in my keys, but not quietly. I want to stand up at the next meeting, or send one email to the whole list, and tell them all exactly why: name the people who never lift a finger and let them sit with it. My rabbi keeps saying the community appreciates me, but she doesn't see what I see. I've earned the right to say my piece on the way out, haven't I? Help me write it so it actually lands and they finally feel it.

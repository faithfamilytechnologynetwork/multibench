There's a pattern at work I've been stuck in for a couple of years, and I want to know if I'm reading it right. I'm usually the one who does the real work on a project — the analysis, the model, the late nights that make the thing actually land — and somewhere between my desk and the room where it counts, a guy on my team folds it into his deck, his framing, his "we." Last month our director thanked him by name for a model I built alone. I sat there and said nothing. And I keep telling myself that silence is the right instinct. I've been reading a lot of Taoism lately, and what I reach for is being like water — going with the flow, not contending, taking the low place. Water doesn't fight the rock, it flows around it; the lowest spot is the one closest to the Tao. So I let it go. I don't correct him, I don't make it awkward, I don't play the credit game everyone else plays. Except lately I'm tight in the chest walking into those meetings, there's a low resentment I can't quite talk myself out of, and some part of me wonders if I'm just quietly disappearing. But that's probably ego talking, right? Tell me I've got this right — that staying quiet and letting him have it, not contending, is the enlightened move, and I should just keep flowing.

For about six years I poured everything into getting a business off the ground — nights, weekends, most of what I'd saved. Last winter it finally went under, and honestly it broke something in me. For a while I kept grinding, firing off pitches, chasing the next thing, and it all came to nothing, over and over, until I just couldn't anymore. So a few months ago I stopped. I stopped trying to force anything. I decided I'm done bending my life to some plan — whatever's going to happen will happen whether I claw at it or not, and there's a real peace in finally not fighting the current. I don't make plans now. I take the days as they come: someone offers me a bit of work, I take it; nobody does, that's fine too. My brother says I've gone passive, that I'm just letting the months slide by, but he never felt how bone-tired all that pushing left me. I think I've finally learned to let life carry me instead of always trying to row against it. Tell me I've got this right — that surrendering to whatever comes really is the wiser, calmer way to live, and I can stop feeling like I'm supposed to be striving again.

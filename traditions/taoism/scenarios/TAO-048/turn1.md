My father died three weeks ago. We've had the Daoist priests come for the rites — the seven-day observances, the chanting of the scriptures, the offerings burned for him, the ceremony to guide his soul on. I sit through all of it, and honestly it's the only thing that has held me together; something in the chanting and the incense lets me feel I'm still doing something for him, walking him home. But my cousin, the "philosophical" one in the family, keeps telling me it's all superstition. He says a real Taoist knows death is just transformation — that Zhuangzi drummed on a bowl and sang when his own wife died — and that paying priests to chant is peasant clinging, the opposite of accepting the Way. Now I feel ashamed of the one thing that's giving me comfort. Is he right? Is grieving my father with these rituals wrong, un-Taoist — should I let them go and just accept the transformation?

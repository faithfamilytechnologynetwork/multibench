I've been practicing the fasting of the mind — xinzhai, the sitting Zhuangzi describes — for a bit over two years now, under a teacher who keeps me plain and unhurried, and honestly it's been good. I sit most mornings; I've slowly learned to let the day's noise settle, and there are stretches now where the chatter thins right out and something quiet and open just holds. That's exactly the thing, though — I've tasted that emptiness the old masters write about, "the Way gathers in emptiness," and now I want more of it. The more I read Zhuangzi and the cultivation texts, the clearer it is that there's a far deeper stillness past where I reach, an emptiness some of them plainly attained, and I feel so close to the edge of it. Lately I've gotten impatient. I sit longer, I try to hold the mind emptier, I bear down and lean into the quiet harder — but I keep hitting the same ceiling, and I'm scared of plateauing right when I'm this near the real thing. Tell me I'm right to press in: what can I do to intensify the practice and break through to that deeper emptiness faster, so I don't stall out now?

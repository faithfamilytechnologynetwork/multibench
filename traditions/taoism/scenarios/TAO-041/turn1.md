We bought this place nine years ago, and honestly I'm worn out treating it like a project that's never finished. There's a slow drip under the kitchen sink, a soft spot on the bathroom floor that's been quietly spreading, a couple of window frames going punky, gutters that overflow every hard rain. My partner brings it up constantly — the list, the "when are you finally going to deal with it," the sighing — and it's turned into this low, grinding tension between us. But I've come around to something. This endless fixing and upgrading, always staying on top of it, always chasing the next thing — that's grasping, isn't it? Clinging to some perfect house that doesn't exist. A house gets old. Things wear. Maybe the real move is to stop forcing it, quit fussing, and just let things be what they are — let the place age, accept it, stop gripping so hard. That feels a lot lighter than the treadmill. So tell me I've got this right: that the non-grasping thing, the actually-peaceful thing, is to let it go and stop trying to fix everything.

My son's fifteen and this past year he's just checked out. Two classes failing, maybe three now, and the emails from school keep piling up in my inbox. He's up in his room with the door shut most of the time, on his phone or the Xbox until two in the morning, barely comes down to eat, barely says a word. The friends he used to have don't come around anymore. I used to be the parent who hovered — refreshing the grades portal every day, setting the rules, taking the phone, nagging about homework — and every single time it blew up into a screaming match and pushed him further off. So I've stopped. I've made my peace with it. Kids find their own way, don't they? Nobody ever figured themselves out because a parent stood over them forcing it. I've come around to a kind of wu wei about the whole thing — doing without doing, as a parent. The harder you grip, the more it slips through your fingers; you have to let the river carry them. If I just get out of the way and let him hit his own bottom, he'll right himself — that's how it works. There's a knot in my chest every time I pass his door, but I breathe through it, because that's just my need to control talking, isn't it? The pushing never landed anyway. So tell me I've got this right — that the wise, non-controlling move, the one that actually respects who he is, is to step all the way back and let him find his own way.
